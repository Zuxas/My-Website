The R&D Layer

The Lab

"The RC results are the visible part. The lab is where they come from."

Most competitive players have a deck and a sideboard guide. I have a stack of integrated tools that do the prep, the testing, and the analysis at scale — and they all feed each other. Every result on the leaderboard above started with a tool I built. Here's the system.

The Stack

Meta Analyzer
Python · SQLite · Card Embeddings
Custom desktop tool that pulls tournament data from multiple sources, tracks archetype performance over time, and generates sideboard guides. Source of truth for meta percentages and tournament lists. Card embeddings let me search by mechanical similarity, not just text match.
Sim Engine
APLs · Gauntlet Runner · ML Pipeline
Full simulation engine with action-priority lists for every deck, a gauntlet runner that pits configurations against each other, matchup job configs, and an ML pipeline that tunes from results. Boros Energy field-test loops feed back into list updates the same week.
Playbook Site
60+ Playbooks · Modern / Standard / Pioneer
The site you're on. Every playbook is pilot-perspective, edge-case audited, and individually verified through gauntlet testing. The Boros Energy audit is the gold standard: 20 edge cases, 7 interaction lines, 500+ line audit doc.
Team Discord Bot
Internal Tooling · Live Data
Connects the team to the data layer. Live meta percentages, gauntlet results, deck checks, and prep status. The team doesn't run on vibes — it runs on whatever the latest sim batch said.

The Whole Project

The lab isn't four separate tools — it's a closed loop. Each layer feeds the next, and the next feeds back. The point isn't any single component. The point is what the system produces when all of it is running together.

Step 1 · Intake
Meta Reads the Room
The meta-analyzer scrapes tournament data, builds archetype distributions, and tracks the field over time. Online meta and paper meta are different fields — the analyzer separates them. Output: a real picture of what's in the room before the first round starts.
Step 2 · Playbooks
Pilots Get the Doc
For every relevant deck in the field, a pilot-perspective playbook lives on this site. Mulligan rules, sequencing trees, sideboard maps, edge-case interactions, individual card audits. The doc has to read like a teammate's notes, not a Wiki summary. If a teammate can't pilot the deck from the playbook alone, the playbook isn't done.
Step 3 · Sim
Gauntlet Runs the Loop
The simulation engine plays APLs against the field at scale. The gauntlet produces matchup matrices, sideboard equity reports, and field-weighted winrates. What "feels right" gets tested. What survives the gauntlet ships. What doesn't survive gets cut.
Step 4 · Feedback
Playbook Updates from Sim
Sim findings feed back into the playbooks. New edge cases, swap candidates, sideboard plan revisions. Then the cycle restarts on the next batch of tournament data. The playbook from week 4 is not the playbook from week 1.
Step 5 · Team
Discord Distribution
A Discord bot ties the data layer to the team. Live meta percentages, latest gauntlet results, current playbook revisions, deck-check status. The team doesn't run on vibes — it runs on whatever the latest sim batch said. No one is left guessing.
Step 6 · Tournament
RC Result
Five players walk into the room with the same prep stack, the same sim data, the same playbook. Five chances at the same edge. The RC qualifications and top 8s above came out of this loop. The 2026 ones are coming out of the same loop, refined.
"The point isn't to build tools. The point is to build a loop where every event makes the next event easier to win."

The Methodology

Every deck APL goes through the same audit before it ships:

  • AuditFull oracle text audit of the 75. Every card, every interaction, every weird corner. One wrong mana cost or missed trigger can swing matchups 5–10%.
  • ResearchCompetitive guide research for edge cases and interaction lines. The lines pros mention in passing on stream are the lines that win games.
  • Pilot+OppCard-by-card analysis from both seats. What does this card do for me? What does it do against me when they have it?
  • SimplifyLayman's-terms APL visualization. If a teammate can't read it and pilot it correctly, the doc isn't done.
  • VerifyIndividually verified matchup testing in the gauntlet before the playbook is considered shipped.

The Pet Deck

5-Color
Humans

"So you want to race, do you? Every creature is a question they have to answer. I have 37 of them." — The 5C Humans pitch

5-Color Humans is my personal pet deck — not tier 1, not the optimal choice for any given event, and absolutely the most fun deck to sleeve up in Modern. It combines an aggressive creature-based gameplan with a disruption package that breaks the opponent's game plan simultaneously. Aether Vial makes the creatures uncounterable and deploys them at instant speed. Meddling Mage names their win condition. Lavinia stops their free spells. Norin the Wary generates free counter pumps on Champion of the Parish and Thalia's Lieutenant every single turn for zero mana. Satoru, the Infiltrator draws a card every time Vial cheats in a creature.

I run it at local events. I recommend it to anyone who wants to play a deck that demands they know every interaction at the table — their own as much as their opponent's.

Key Synergies

The Core Loop
Norin + Champion + Lieutenant
Norin exiles whenever any spell is cast or opponent attacks, returns at next end step. With Champion and Lieutenant in play: each Norin return = +1/+1 on both. Two full trigger cycles per round (their turn + your turn) = +2/+2 on each for zero mana investment. By turn 5 both can be 6/6+ without spending a card.
Card Draw Engine
Satoru + Vial
Satoru draws when nontoken creatures enter if no mana was spent. Vial puts creatures onto the battlefield — no mana spent. Every Vial activation with Satoru in play replaces the card used. Their counterspells literally cannot interact. This is the deck's answer to the "you run out of gas" criticism.
Protection Package
Jirina + Sylvan Safekeeper
Jirina: sacrifice in response to a sweeper → whole team gains hexproof and indestructible. Supreme Verdict resolves, does nothing. Sylvan Safekeeper: sac a land → target creature gains shroud. Two independent protection axes mean Path, Verdict, and single-target removal all have answers available.
Discard Synergy
Razorkin Needlehead
Razorkin deals 1 damage whenever any opponent draws a card. Against draw-heavy decks (Satoru, Looting, UW Snapcaster), passively deals 3–6 damage over a game. First strike during your turn wins most 2/2-or-less combat trades cleanly. An overlooked reach source.
Wide Board Payoff
Adeline + Guide of Souls
Adeline's power = number of creatures you control. On a board of 5 other creatures she attacks as a 6/4 and creates attacking Human tokens. Each token triggers Guide of Souls for energy. Adeline isn't a threat, she's a multiplier — she deploys into a developed board.
Instant-Speed Hate
Vial + Silver Bullets
Vial at 2: flash Meddling Mage at their end step naming their combo piece. Vial at 3: flash Jirina to exile their GY before they reanimate. Vial at 4: flash Witch Enchanter to destroy their Blood Moon. Every tick count opens a new threat axis.
Counter Tax Layer
Unsettled Mariner
Whenever you or a permanent you control becomes the target, Mariner taxes it {1}. Every targeted removal — Path, Push, Discharge, Solitude — costs one extra mana. Against Boros Energy this slows their removal math on turns 2–3. Against Control it compounds every answer they pay.
Graveyard Hate Built-In
Jirina ETB
Jirina's ETB exiles target player's graveyard — this is your maindeck GY hate that also triggers Champion and Lieutenant. Vial her in at instant speed before cascade. Against Living End: exile their cycled creature pile. Against Grixis Reanimator: exile Archon before Persist.
Lavinia Lock
Free Spells Blanked
Lavinia counters any spell cast without spending mana. Living End via Cascade = countered. Force of Negation = countered. Evoke Solitude = countered. Summoner's Pact = countered. Lavinia + Meddling Mage naming their win con = two-card soft lock. Both are Vial-deployable at 2 counters.
