Chapter 01

Deck
Identity

"A Delver deck with a reanimator backup plan. Every cantrip fills the graveyard for three different payoffs. Every discard is both a pump spell and a setup spell. Nothing in this deck does only one thing."

Dimir Oculus is Modern's premier tempo-control deck with a reanimator subtheme. It evolved from UR Murktide through three distinct generations: first, Psychic Frog replaced Ragavan as the two-drop engine (MH3, June 2024). Then Abhorrent Oculus replaced Murktide Regent as the primary finisher (Duskmourn, September 2024). Now the deck exists in a mature form where all three threats coexist, each consuming graveyard resources in different ways — creating the most demanding resource-management puzzle in Modern.

The deck's identity is defined by a single tension: every card in your graveyard is currency for three different banks. Psychic Frog wants to exile cards for flying. Abhorrent Oculus needs six cards exiled to hardcast (but bypasses this via Unearth). Murktide Regent delves instants and sorceries for +1/+1 counters. The elite pilot tracks their graveyard contents the way a poker player tracks the deck — every card accounted for, every exile deliberate.

The other half of the identity is the interaction suite. Counterspell, Force of Negation, Spell Snare, Fatal Push — 14+ interactive spells that protect your threats and deny the opponent's plan. You deploy a threat, then hide behind a wall of countermagic and removal. The opponent has to fight through your permission to answer your Frog or Oculus, and every turn they spend doing that is a turn your threats are generating card advantage.

The Three Threat Axes

Axis 1 — The Engine
Psychic Frog
{U}{B} · 1/2 Frog. Whenever it deals combat damage to a player or planeswalker, draw a card. Discard a card: put a +1/+1 counter on it. Exile three cards from your graveyard: it gains flying until end of turn. This is the deck's heart. The discard grows Frog AND fills the GY for Unearth/Murktide. The combat draw replaces the discarded card. The flying converts late-game GY resources into evasion. Every ability feeds another ability. Frog is The One Ring of this deck — once it starts generating value, the opponent drowns.
Axis 2 — The Unfair Angle
Abhorrent Oculus
{2}{U} · 5/5 Eye · Flying. Additional cost: exile 6 cards from GY. At beginning of each opponent's upkeep, manifest dread. The hardcast cost is real ({2}{U} + exile 6), but the primary play is Unearth ({B} sorcery: return creature MV ≤ 3 to battlefield). Oculus is MV 3. Unearth does NOT cast — the additional cost of exiling 6 from GY doesn't apply. A T2 Unearth on a milled Oculus = free 5/5 flying that generates 2/2 manifest tokens every opponent upkeep. Unearth (the card) has NO exile clause — unlike the Unearth keyword. The creature stays permanently.
Axis 3 — The Legacy Finisher
Murktide Regent
{5}{U}{U} · 3/3 Dragon · Flying, Delve. Enters with a +1/+1 counter for each instant/sorcery exiled via delve. Whenever an instant or sorcery leaves your GY, put a +1/+1 counter on it. Typically an 8/8 flying for {U}{U}. Demoted to 2 copies in modern builds because Oculus is more efficient, but Murktide is still the best standalone closer. Key tension: Frog exiling cards from GY for flying competes with Murktide's delve — exile lands/creatures with Frog, save instants/sorceries for Murktide.

Oracle Accuracy — Every Card, Every Edge Case

Psychic Frog — Critical Rulings
3 Abilities, All Instant Speed
All three abilities activate at instant speed. Combat draw: triggers on damage to player OR planeswalker — relevant when attacking an Oko or Jace. Discard: can discard in response to removal (Frog gets bigger, card goes to GY for later). Can discard multiple cards at once — each discard is a separate activation. Flying: exile 3 cards as one activation. Can activate multiple times per turn (exile 6 for two instances of flying — redundant for flying itself, but relevant for depleting GY before opponent's Surgical). Anti-Surgical play: opponent targets Oculus in your GY with Surgical Extraction → exile Oculus to Frog's flying ability in response → Surgical fizzles (target left zone). The exile is a COST, not an effect — opponent cannot respond to the exile itself.
Abhorrent Oculus — Manifest Dread Rules
Every Card Type Behaves Differently
Manifest dread: look at top 2, one enters face-down as 2/2, other goes to GY. The face-down 2/2 can be turned face up by paying its mana cost IF it's a creature card. Instant/sorcery manifested: stays face-down as 2/2 FOREVER — cannot be turned face up. Revealing it just shows it's not a creature. Murktide manifested: CAN be turned face up by paying {5}{U}{U} (its mana cost). Delve does NOT apply when turning face up — only when casting. DFC manifested (Sink into Stupor): enters face-down, cannot transform while face-down. Front face is an instant — cannot be turned face up. Land manifested: stays face-down as 2/2, cannot flip. Turning face up doesn't trigger ETB — the creature was already on the battlefield.
Unearth (the Card) vs Unearth (the Keyword)
CRITICAL DISTINCTION
Unearth the card ({B} sorcery): "Return target creature card with mana value 3 or less from your graveyard to the battlefield." That's it. No exile clause. No end-of-turn clause. No "if it would leave the battlefield, exile it instead." The creature returns permanently. An Oculus returned via the Unearth card stays on the battlefield forever unless killed/bounced normally. The Unearth keyword (on cards like Nethergoyf) HAS an exile clause: "If it would leave the battlefield, exile it instead. At end of turn, exile it." These are completely different mechanics that share a name. Modern Dimir Oculus uses the CARD, not the keyword. Your Oculus stays.
Oculus Additional Cost
When Does Exile-6 Apply?
Per Scryfall ruling (2024-09-20): "Abhorrent Oculus's additional cost must be paid even if it's cast 'without paying its mana cost' or for any alternative cost." This means: Cascade into Oculus — still pay exile 6. Collected Company hitting Oculus — CoCo puts it onto the battlefield, doesn't cast it, so NO additional cost. Unearth — returns to battlefield, doesn't cast, NO additional cost. Manifest dread — enters face-down, wasn't cast, NO additional cost. The additional cost ONLY applies when CASTING the spell from any zone.
Force of Negation — Turn Restriction
Free Only on Opponent's Turns
"If it's not your turn, you may exile a blue card from your hand rather than pay this spell's mana cost." Your upkeep: still your turn — can't free-cast. Your end step: still your turn — can't free-cast. Opponent's upkeep: their turn — CAN free-cast. This means casting spells during your opponent's upkeep dodges their Force of Negation (they can't free-cast on your turn either). Exile clause: FoN exiles the countered spell. This permanently removes flashback targets (Past in Flames), cascade targets (Living End), and escape cards. The exile is part of the counter, not a replacement — if the spell is already countered by something else, FoN's exile doesn't apply.
Harbinger of the Seas — Self-Damage
Your Own Mana Gets Hit
{1}{U}{U} 2/2 Merfolk Wizard. "Nonbasic lands are Islands." ALL nonbasic lands — yours too. Your Watery Graves, Polluted Deltas, Undercity Sewers, Gloomlake Verges = all Islands (only produce {U}). You lose access to {B} from all nonbasics. Only your basic Swamp (1 copy in most lists) produces {B} under Harbinger. Pre-fetch Swamp BEFORE deploying Harbinger if you need {B} for Fatal Push or Bowmasters later. Fetches under Harbinger: Polluted Delta is an Island under Harbinger — it CAN still be cracked to search for an Island or Swamp (the fetch ability isn't an activated ability of the land-type). You can fetch your basic Swamp through Harbinger.
Sink into Stupor // Undercity Sewers
MDFC — Spell and Land in One
Front: {1}{U}{U} instant — return target spell or nonland permanent opponent controls to owner's hand. Hits spells ON THE STACK (pseudo-counter that returns to hand) AND permanents in play. Back: Undercity Sewers — land, enters tapped, surveil 1, produces {U} or {B}. The deck counts this as land #20-21 even though it's also interaction. Edge case: If you Sink a spell on the stack, the spell returns to hand — it was never countered. This matters against "can't be countered" spells like Cavern of Souls creatures or Supreme Verdict — Sink doesn't counter them, it bounces them. Supreme Verdict says "can't be countered" but Sink doesn't counter — it returns to hand. This is a legal play.
Orcish Bowmasters — Army Stacking
Second Copy Grows the Existing Army
{1}{B} 1/1 Orc Archer · Flash. ETB + whenever opponent draws extra: deal 1 damage to any target, then amass Orcs 1. Amass Orcs 1: if you control an Army, put a +1/+1 counter on it. If you don't, create a 0/0 Orc Army token and put a counter on it. Second Bowmasters: the ETB trigger of the second one grows the EXISTING Army. You don't get two Army tokens. Over a long game a single Army can become a 5/5 or 6/6. Flash timing: deploy at opponent's draw step — their draw is the "extra draw" that triggers Bowmasters. Also triggers on: Wrenn and Six +1 (draw), any "draw a card" effect, opponent's Psychic Frog combat damage draw (in the mirror).
Counterspell vs Spell Snare vs Spell Pierce
When to Hold Which Counter
Counterspell ({U}{U}): hard counter, no conditions. Hold when you need certainty — their must-answer spell resolving loses the game. Requires 2 blue mana, which means T2 at earliest with correct lands. Spell Snare ({U}): counters MV 2 exactly. In current Modern: Bowmasters, Stoneforge Mystic, Wrenn and Six, Lightning Helix, Coppercoat Vanguard, Tarmogoyf. Best on T2 — deploy Frog AND hold Snare with {U}{B} + {U}. Spell Pierce ({U}): counter noncreature unless pay {2}. Best T1-T3 before opponent has excess mana. Falls off hard in late game. Board in against combo/control, board out against creature aggro.
Consider vs Thought Scour — Sequencing
Controlled vs Random GY Fill
Consider ({U} instant): surveil 1 (choose whether top card goes to GY), then draw. You CHOOSE what enters the GY. Use when you need a SPECIFIC card in GY (an Oculus sitting on top). Thought Scour ({U} instant): target player mills 2, draw a card. RANDOM — you don't choose what's milled. But mills 2 vs Consider's 1. Use when you need GY VOLUME (filling for Murktide delve or Oculus hardcast). Sequencing rule: if you have both, Consider first when you need to find something specific. Scour first when you need maximum GY count and plan to Consider afterward to filter. Thought Scour can target the OPPONENT — relevant for milling their key cards or denying a known topdeck after a scry. This comes up maybe 1 in 200 games but wins when it does.

Meta Position — April 2026

Dimir Oculus/Frog sits at approximately 3.5% of the Modern metagame when combining "Dimir Frog" (1.46% on MTGDecks) with "Dimir Midrange" (2.3% on MTGGoldfish). It's the highest-performing tempo deck in the format after the December 2025 bans removed The One Ring and weakened Boros Energy. The deck was unaffected by the ban announcement and gained equity as its worst matchups (pre-ban Boros with One Ring) lost their best card.

The current top tables: Boros Energy (16%), Izzet Affinity (8%), Jeskai Blink (7%), Eldrazi Tron (5.3%), Ruby Storm (5%). Dimir Oculus is favorable against spell-heavy decks (Storm, Living End), even against midrange (Jeskai Blink, mirrors), and unfavorable against fast aggro that goes under its interaction (Boros Energy on the play, Prowess with a nut draw).

Why play this deck: you want the highest decision density in Modern. Every game is different. You're rewarded for knowing every matchup, every edge case, every timing window. The deck's ceiling is the highest in the format — in the hands of an expert, it has no unwinnable matchups. The floor is also punishing: missequence your lands or mismanage your GY, and you lose games you should have won.

Why Play / Why Skip

Play This If
You Want Maximum Agency
You enjoy making 15+ meaningful decisions per game. You like tracking hidden information (GY contents, opponent's hand after Thoughtseize). You want a deck that rewards knowing every card in the format because your interaction suite needs to answer everything. You find the puzzle of GY resource allocation satisfying. You like winning games by outplaying, not out-topdecking.
Skip This If
You Need Autopilot Consistency
The deck is unforgiving of mistakes. Wrong land sequencing T1 can cost you the game on T4. Wrong counter target can lose the counter war. Wrong GY management means your Murktide enters as a 4/4 instead of an 8/8. If you're playing on 4 hours of sleep at a day-2 event, the decision fatigue is real. Also: graveyard hate is everywhere in Modern. Nihil Spellbomb, Surgical Extraction, Leyline of the Void — one well-timed piece of GY hate can collapse your entire game plan.

GY Resource Allocation Map

The Most Important Skill in the Deck
Every card in your graveyard is currency for three different threats. Track these allocations constantly:

Psychic Frog flying: exile ANY 3 cards. Prioritize lands and creatures (irrelevant to Murktide).
Murktide Regent delve: exile instants/sorceries ONLY (each one = +1/+1 counter). Never waste instants/sorceries on Frog flying if Murktide is coming.
Oculus hardcast: exile ANY 6 cards. This is the most expensive cost — only relevant in long games when Unearth isn't available.
Unearth target: needs a creature with MV ≤ 3 IN the GY. Don't exile Oculus or Bowmasters to Frog if you haven't used Unearth yet.

The heuristic: exile lands and creatures to Frog. Save instants/sorceries for Murktide. Keep at least one Unearth target in GY until you've used both copies of Unearth or hardcast your Oculus.
Chapter 03

Gameplan

"The first three turns define the game. T1 land sequencing. T2 threat or hold. T3 close the door or grind. Every decision after that flows from what you established in those three turns."

The Critical First Three Turns

From competitive primers: "I cannot emphasize enough how important the first three turns are and the decisions you make during those turns, starting with land drops." This is the framework:

Turn 1 — Always a Land Decision
Fetch → Surveil Land
Ideal: Fetch-land → crack → find Undercity Sewers (enters tapped, surveil 1). This removes the tapped land from your deck AND fills GY with the surveil. Do NOT lead with Consider or Thought Scour T1 even if you have them — the surveil land is more important because it takes a tapped land out of your deck for future draws. If no fetch: play Gloomlake Verge (untapped, produces {U}). If opening with Undercity Sewers in hand: play it tapped T1, surveil an Oculus to GY → set up T2 Unearth. Never play basic Swamp T1 unless you must — it's your only {B} source under Harbinger and you want it available later.
Turn 2 — Deploy or Hold
Frog vs Open Mana
Deploy Frog when: you have protection (Spell Snare, Spell Pierce backup), OR they tapped out T1, OR their deck can't efficiently answer a 1/2 (combo, Tron, Storm). Hold open when: they have {1} open for removal (Boros with Galvanic Discharge), OR you have Counterspell + threat for T3, OR you milled an Oculus and can Unearth it this turn instead. T2 Unearth on Oculus: the nut draw. Requires Oculus in GY from T1 surveil or a lucky Thought Scour. A 5/5 flying on T2 with mana open for protection is often game-winning.
Turn 3 — The Pivot
Discard Oculus → Unearth
The standard line: T2 Frog resolved. T3: discard Oculus to Frog (+1/+1 counter, Oculus goes to GY). Cast Unearth for {B} (Oculus returns permanently). Hold {U} for Spell Snare or Spell Pierce. Board state: 2/3 Frog + 5/5 flying Oculus + 1 open mana. Alternative: if no Oculus, T3 hold up Counterspell ({U}{U}) and attack with Frog. Draw a card off combat damage. Rebuild hand for T4 Murktide. Against fast decks: T3 might be Fatal Push + Spell Snare — pure defense, no threat deployment.

Mulligan Guide

Always Keep
Fetch + Frog + Interaction
Any hand with a fetch-land (for surveil sequencing), Psychic Frog (your primary engine), and at least one piece of interaction (counter, removal, or Force). Example: Polluted Delta, Watery Grave, Frog, Fatal Push, Counterspell, Consider, Thought Scour = snap keep. Even without Oculus or Murktide — Frog generates enough value to find your finishers.
Situational Keep
Unearth + Mill + Land
A hand with Unearth, Thought Scour, and 2 lands is keepable if you're willing to gamble on milling an Oculus or Harbinger. The T1 Scour → T2 Unearth line is powerful enough to justify. But if Scour misses all creatures, Unearth is a dead card until you find another way to fill the GY. Riskier on the draw than the play.
Mulligan
No Threat, No Cantrip
A hand of 3 lands, 2 Counterspells, Fatal Push, Force of Negation = mulligan. All interaction, no proactive plan. You can't win by countering forever — you need a threat to close. Also mulligan: hands with only Murktide as the threat and no way to fill the GY (no cantrips, no Thought Scour). Murktide without GY fuel enters as a 3/3 for 7 mana — unplayable.
The Swamp Trap
Basic Swamp in Opening Hand
From MythicMaria's guide: "This is a very awkward hand... mostly because of the basic Swamp. In most cases I don't want to fetch for it, so having it in a starting hand could seriously damage your spell-sequencing." The deck is heavily blue — your T1 and T2 plays are almost always blue. A Swamp in your opener means one of your first two land drops produces only {B}, which can't cast Consider, Thought Scour, Spell Snare, or deploy Frog's {U} half. Mulligan aggressively if Swamp is your only black source AND you have no fetch to find an Island.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T2 Frog Is the Line
On the play, T2 Psychic Frog is your highest-value action. It comes down before they can assemble interaction, and they have to answer it immediately or lose to the discard-grow loop. Mulligan more aggressively for hands with a T2 Frog play. A hand without Frog or a way to find Frog by T2 is a slow hand that gets outpaced against combo decks.
On the Draw
Counter-Mana Hands Are Fine
On the draw you see 8 cards and can keep counter-heavy hands (Force of Negation + Counterspell + land) since you have time to deploy them. T1 Spell Snare or Stern Scolding on their T1 threat is a legitimate draw-side opening. Against aggro on the draw, Fatal Push + Shoot the Sheriff hands are keepable even without Frog — you need to survive to T3 before deploying your threats.

When Disrupted

Frog Killed
Murktide as Backup Finisher
Frog answered: deploy Murktide Regent as the backup closer. Murktide is effectively a 4/4+ flying for {2}{U} after GY fills. It survives Fatal Push and Lightning Bolt. If Murktide is also unavailable, Orcish Bowmasters + opponent draw triggers creates a drain clock. You have three closing threats — exhaust their answers before scooping.
GY Hate Active
Tempo + Value Plan
Nihil Spellbomb or Surgical Extraction is in play. Pivot to Harbinger of the Seas + Bowmasters value: Harbinger locks opponents off multiple islands, Bowmasters generates bodies every time they draw. This plan doesn’t need the GY to function. Sink into Stupor bounces their permanents and generates disruption. You can win without any GY synergies in this mode.
Chapter 04

Core Engines

A
The Frog Value Loop
Deploy
T2 Psychic Frog
Psychic Frog enters as a 1/2 for {U}{B}. Immediately threatens: discard to pump (instant speed), combat draw on connection, GY exile for flying. The opponent must answer it NOW or it snowballs. Every turn Frog lives, it generates more cards, grows bigger, and depletes resources the opponent needs to track.
Generate
Attack → Draw → Discard
Attack with Frog. If it deals combat damage: draw a card. Now discard that card (or another) to put a +1/+1 counter on Frog AND fill the GY. Net result: Frog is +1/+1 bigger, GY has one more card for Murktide/Unearth, and you've filtered your hand. The drawn card replaces the discarded card — you never go down on cards. This is The One Ring on a creature body.
Close
Exile 3 → Flying
When ground blockers appear: exile 3 cards from GY for flying until end of turn. A 5/6 flying Frog represents lethal in 2 swings. Prioritize exiling lands and creatures (not instants/sorceries — save those for Murktide delve). If no Murktide in hand, exile anything. Flying activates at instant speed — declare attackers, opponent doesn't block thinking Frog is grounded, then activate flying after blocks.
B
The Unearth-Oculus Combo
Mill
Put Oculus in GY
Methods: (1) T1 surveil land puts Oculus in GY directly. (2) Thought Scour mills 2 — ~15% chance to hit Oculus if 4 copies in 53 remaining cards. (3) Consider surveil 1 — you CHOOSE to put Oculus in GY if it's on top. (4) Discard Oculus to Psychic Frog's pump ability — guaranteed, costs a card from hand but grows Frog AND sets up the combo.
Reanimate
Unearth for {B}
Unearth ({B} sorcery): return target creature MV ≤ 3 from GY to battlefield. Oculus is MV 3 ({2}{U}). The additional cost of exiling 6 from GY does NOT apply — Unearth returns, it doesn't cast. The creature stays permanently — Unearth (the card) has NO exile clause. A 5/5 flying for {B} that manifests dread every opponent upkeep. If you have Frog in play: T3 discard Oculus → Unearth → 2/3 Frog + 5/5 Oculus + 1 open mana.
Manifest
Free 2/2s Every Upkeep
Once Oculus is in play: each opponent's upkeep, manifest dread. Look at top 2, one enters face-down as 2/2, other goes to GY. The 2/2 can be turned face up if it's a creature (pay mana cost). If it's Murktide face-down: pay {5}{U}{U} to flip (delve doesn't work for face-up, only casting). If it's an instant/sorcery: stays as 2/2 forever. The GY card fuels future Murktide delve or Frog exile. Every upkeep = free board presence + free GY fuel.
C
The Murktide Delve Finisher
Fill
8+ Instants/Sorceries in GY
The cantrip suite (Consider ×4, Thought Scour ×4) plus interaction (Fatal Push, Counterspell, Spell Snare) rapidly fills the GY. By T4-5, you typically have 8-10 instants/sorceries in GY. Each one exiled via delve = +1/+1 counter on Murktide. A Murktide that delves 5 cards enters as an 8/8 flying for {U}{U}.
Cast
{U}{U} for an 8/8 Flier
Delve exiles cards from GY as part of casting. You choose WHICH cards to exile. Always exile instants/sorceries over lands/creatures — each one becomes a +1/+1 counter. Lands and creatures exiled to delve don't generate counters. If Frog has already exiled some instants for flying: Murktide enters smaller. This is the core GY tension — plan your Frog exiles around whether Murktide is coming this game.
Grow
Passive Growth
"Whenever an instant or sorcery card leaves your graveyard, put a +1/+1 counter on Murktide Regent." This triggers on: cards exiled by Psychic Frog (if they're instants/sorceries), cards exiled by opponent's Surgical Extraction, cards exiled by your own Nihil Spellbomb (if targeting yourself — rare but relevant). Murktide grows passively even after entering. A resolved Murktide with Frog in play gets bigger every time Frog exiles an instant/sorcery for flying.
D
The Harbinger Cheese Lock
Deploy
T2-T3 Harbinger
Harbinger of the Seas ({1}{U}{U} 2/2 Merfolk): "Nonbasic lands are Islands." Against greedy manabases (Boros Energy 0-1 basics, Domain Zoo, 4C): this locks them out of non-blue mana entirely. Castable T2 off Unearth if milled ({B} to return a MV 3 creature). T3 off natural mana. Even a T3 Harbinger against Boros can strand 3-4 cards in hand permanently.
Self-Manage
Pre-Fetch Your Swamp
YOUR nonbasics become Islands too. Before deploying Harbinger: crack a fetch for your basic Swamp. Now you have {B} from Swamp and {U} from everything else. If you didn't pre-fetch: you lose access to Fatal Push, Bowmasters, Unearth, Shoot the Sheriff — everything black. Harbinger is a symmetric effect that punishes you if you didn't sequence correctly. Fetch Swamp T1 or T2 if you see Harbinger in your opener.
Edge Cases
What Still Works
Fetches under Harbinger: Polluted Delta is an "Island" but still has its sacrifice ability (fetch abilities aren't land type abilities). You CAN crack it to find basic Swamp even under your own Harbinger. Gloomlake Verge under Harbinger: becomes an Island, loses its printed abilities, only taps for {U}. Undercity Sewers: already an Island Swamp — under Harbinger it's just an Island (loses Swamp). Surveil trigger still fired on entry (ETB, not a land type ability).

Engine Priority Order

Priority 01
Frog + Faithless Looting
Discard 2 cards = 2 counters on Frog in one activation. Frog becomes a 3/4 flying on T3 after a T2 deploy + T3 Looting. This is your fastest threat-growth engine. Execute whenever Frog is in play and Looting is in hand. The card selection is a bonus — the primary goal is growing Frog to a size they can’t race.
Priority 02
Bowmasters + Opponent Draw
Every card they draw creates a Rabbit token and deals 1 damage. Against draw-heavy decks (Storm, UW Control, Living End), Bowmasters generates massive value passively. Deploy Bowmasters before their big draw turn for maximum impact. This engine wins games where Frog has been answered — they can’t stop drawing and the damage accumulates.
Priority 03
Harbinger Tempo Lock
Harbinger of the Seas makes all islands legendary — opponent can only keep one. Against blue decks, this halves their mana immediately. Deploy when they’re tapped out to maximize the tempo hit. This engine is not a finisher but it creates a window: their mana is restricted, your threats connect. Follow Harbinger immediately with a threat or counter-up to capitalise on the tempo swing.

Speed Math

Frog Clock
How Fast Does Frog Kill?
T2 Frog (1/2 flying). T3 Faithless Looting → Frog is 3/4 flying. T4 swing for 3. T5 another discard outlet → Frog grows, swing for 4+. Starting from T3, Frog closes in 4–5 attack steps without buffs. With additional discard triggers (Sink into Stupor on their EOT), Frog can be 5/6+ by T4 — that’s a 3-turn clock at 20 life.
Bowmasters Clock
Passive Drain Rate
Against draw-heavy decks: T3 Bowmasters. They cast Brainstorm (draw 3, lose 3 life, 3 Rabbits). They cast Preordain (draw 2 more). By T4-5 in a draw-heavy game, Bowmasters has often dealt 8–10 damage passively. Combined with 1/1 Rabbit attacks, this is a realistic 5–6 turn clock that doesn’t require you to ever attack with Frog.
T2 vs T3 Threat Decision
When to Hold Mana
Deploy T2: They’re on an aggro or combo deck. Frog must hit play before they establish their clock.

Hold mana T2: They have 2 open mana and haven’t cast anything — Force of Negation read. Pass T2 with Force up, deploy Frog on T3 when they’ve used mana on their end step.

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Line 01 — Critical
Frog Triggers on ANY Discard
Psychic Frog gets a +1/+1 counter whenever ANY card is discarded — including cards you discard to hand size, Faithless Looting’s discard 2, and even opponent’s Thoughtseize stripping your hand. Each individual card discarded is one trigger. Faithless Looting = 2 triggers = 2 counters at instant speed. You can activate this on their combat step to make Frog large enough to survive blocks.
Line 02 — Critical
Harbinger Applies to ALL Islands
Harbinger of the Seas makes ALL islands legendary — including their Hallowed Fountain, Scalding Tarn after fetching, Flooded Strand after cracking, and any island-type dual. If they already have two islands in play when Harbinger resolves, they choose one and the other is sacrificed immediately. This is not a "enters tapped" effect — it’s an immediate sacrifice for the duplicate. Know which of their lands are islands before deploying.
Line 03 — Critical
Sink into Stupor at Their EOT
Sink into Stupor / Sinister Monologue is a two-card adventure: cast the adventure first (draw-then-discard + Frog trigger), THEN cast the sorcery next turn to bounce a permanent. You can also hold Sink into Stupor and cast the bounce on your main phase when they’re tapped out for maximum tempo. Casting on your own end step gets you the adventure; saving it for their permanents gets the bounce. Decide before T3 which you need.

Anti-Graveyard Hate Plays

vs Surgical Extraction
Exile Your Own Card to Frog
Opponent casts Surgical Extraction targeting Abhorrent Oculus in your GY. Response: activate Psychic Frog's flying ability, exiling Oculus (plus 2 other cards) as COST. Surgical fizzles — its target left the graveyard. The exile is a cost, meaning the opponent can't respond to the exile itself; by the time they get priority, Oculus is already in exile. Frog gained flying as a bonus. Note: this only works if you have 3+ cards in GY (need 3 total to activate flying). If GY has only the Oculus, you can't exile it to Frog.
vs Nihil Spellbomb / Rest in Peace
Prioritize Unearth Timing
If you suspect GY hate: use Unearth BEFORE they get mana to deploy hate. A T2 Unearth on Oculus resolves before their T2 Rest in Peace. Once Oculus is on the battlefield, GY hate doesn't affect it — it's already out. Against Nihil Spellbomb (sacrifice to exile GY): they can fire it in response to Unearth. Counter-play: hold Spell Snare (counters MV 2 artifacts on cast) or Counterspell for their Spellbomb trigger (the sacrifice-to-exile is an activated ability, not a spell — but the draw mode IS an activated ability you can't counter with Counterspell). Best answer: race them. Deploy threats faster than they can hate.

Sink into Stupor — When to Bounce vs Counter

Bounce a Spell on the Stack
Pseudo-Counter That Returns to Hand
Sink bounces a SPELL on the stack back to hand. Unlike Counterspell, the spell was never "countered" — it just returned to hand. This matters against: "Can't be countered" spells: Supreme Verdict says "can't be countered" but Sink doesn't counter it — it returns it to hand. Legal play. Cascade: bounce the cascade spell after Living End resolves (the cascade card goes back to hand, not GY). Suspend: bounce a spell cast from suspend — it returns to hand, not exile. Downside vs Counterspell: they get the card back. Against a control opponent with 10 mana, bouncing buys one turn. Counterspell denies the card permanently.
Bounce a Permanent in Play
Tempo Removal That Dodges Indestructible
Sink bounces any nonland permanent opponent controls. This hits: creatures (even indestructible ones), planeswalkers, artifacts, enchantments. Unlike Fatal Push which destroys (stopped by indestructible), Sink returns to hand regardless of indestructible, hexproof, or protection. Best targets: expensive permanents they spent their whole turn deploying (Karn, Primeval Titan, Murktide). Worst targets: cheap creatures they'll just recast next turn (Guide of Souls, Ragavan). Use on permanents where the tempo gain matters more than the card disadvantage.

1-in-5000 Edge Cases

Thought Scour Targeting the Opponent
Thought Scour says "target player mills two cards." It can target your opponent. Use cases: (1) They scried to top and you want to deny a known topdeck — Scour them to mill it. (2) In the mirror, milling their Oculus into their GY could be dangerous (they could Unearth it) but sometimes denying their draw step card matters more. (3) Against Lantern Control / top-of-deck manipulation: disrupting their known top card. (4) Against Tron: milling an Urza land they searched for. This comes up maybe once every 200 games but wins when it does.
Spell Pierce as Graveyard Fuel
Spell Pierce can be cast even when it won't counter anything — the opponent simply pays {2} and their spell resolves. But YOUR Spell Pierce still goes to the graveyard as a resolved instant. This feeds Murktide delve (+1/+1 counter). In late-game board stalls where you need one more counter for Murktide: cast Spell Pierce on their spell knowing they'll pay {2}. Your Murktide grows. The {2} they paid was worth less than the +1/+1 counter.
Manifested Murktide Regent — Can It Flip?
If Oculus's manifest dread puts Murktide face-down as a 2/2: YES, you can turn it face up by paying its mana cost {5}{U}{U}. However, delve does NOT apply when turning face up — delve only works when casting a spell. You must pay the full {5}{U}{U}. The creature enters the battlefield with 0 +1/+1 counters (the ETB counter ability triggers on entering, and Murktide entered face-down, so no counters were added). BUT: once face-up, Murktide's "whenever an instant or sorcery leaves your GY" trigger is active. Future Frog exiles of instants will grow it.
Manifest Dread with 1 Card in Library
Per Scryfall ruling: "If your library contains only one card when you manifest dread, you'll look at that card and put it onto the battlefield face down. You won't have the option to put it into your graveyard instead." You MUST manifest the one remaining card. If your library has 0 cards: manifest dread does nothing (no trigger failure, the ability just has no effect). You don't lose the game from an empty library during manifest dread — you only lose if you try to DRAW from an empty library.
Force of Negation During Your Own Upkeep
"If it's not your turn" — your upkeep IS your turn. You CANNOT free-cast Force of Negation during your own upkeep, even though it feels like you should be able to. If something triggers during your upkeep that you need to counter (opponent's Pact of Negation trigger, for example), you must pay {1}{U}{U} for Force. Conversely: casting your spells during the OPPONENT'S upkeep means THEY can't free-cast their Force of Negation either (it's their turn).

Role Assignment

"You're a tempo deck with three modes. The wrong mode loses. The right mode wins games you shouldn't."
Tempo
Your default mode. Deploy Frog T2, protect it, attack, draw cards, grow it. Counter their key spell, Push their key creature, attack through the hole. Every turn you're both threatening and protecting. This is the deck at its best — doing two things at once.
vs Boros Energy · vs Jeskai Blink · vs Midrange · vs Mirror
Control
When the opponent is faster than you (Prowess nut draw, Storm). Hold all interaction, don't deploy threats until you've stabilized. Counter their key spell, Push their attacker, then slam Oculus or Murktide on a clear board. You're playing draw-go until the coast is clear.
vs Storm · vs Prowess · vs Belcher · vs Fast Combo
Unfair
T2 Unearth Oculus. T2 Harbinger locking them off colors. When you have the nut reanimation draw, ignore the fair game and slam the unfair angle. This mode wins games that no amount of "good Magic" could win — against Tron, against combo, against anyone who stumbles for one turn.
vs Tron · vs Slow Combo · vs Greedy Manabases

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • 01
    Not growing Frog on opponent’s attack step. Faithless Looting is an instant. When they attack into Frog, you can cast Looting in response to damage — Frog gets 2 counters and may now be too large to trade with their attacker. Always ask “can I make Frog bigger before damage resolves?” on their attack step.
  • 02
    Casting Sink into Stupor on your own turn when you need the adventure. The adventure (Sinister Monologue) is a sorcery. You must cast it on your own main phase anyway. Stop holding it for their turn — the adventure and the bounce are separate cards. Cast the adventure main phase for the Frog trigger and card selection, then the bounce is a separate future decision.
  • 03
    Deploying Harbinger into non-island decks. Harbinger of the Seas is a 3/2 for {2}{U} with no text against Boros Energy, Jund, or Eldrazi Tron. Don’t bring it in and don’t play it in matchups where they have no islands. Check their deck’s manabase before playing Harbinger — it’s dead in some game ones.
  • 04
    Holding Force of Negation for the wrong threat. Force counters any non-creature spell for free — but it’s a 2-for-1 when you pay the alternate cost. Save it for game-ending threats (Grapeshot, Living End, Violent Outburst, Titan) not for turn-2 cantrips. Misforcing costs the game when the real threat arrives two turns later with no answer available.

Role-Switching Signals

Switch to Control Mode When
Your Threats Are Answered
Frog and Murktide are both answered. Bowmasters is in play. Switch: draw-go with counters + Bowmasters passive drain. Every time they draw a card you advance your position. Hold mana open every turn, counter the important spells, let Bowmasters accumulate damage over 6–8 turns. This is a real win condition.
Switch to Aggro Mode When
Frog Is Big and They’re Low
Frog is 5/6+ flying. They’re below 12 life. Stop developing, stop counterspelling — attack. Two swings with a 5/6 kills from 10 life. Don’t waste mana on extra interaction when the game is already decided by the air. Tap out, attack, hold Priority only to prevent exile effects.
Never Switch When
Frog + Counter Backup Is Online
You have Frog in play, Force of Negation in hand, opponent is at 12 or below. This position wins. Don’t pivot to anything else. Attack with Frog, Force anything that answers it. The counter backup converts a 60% position into a 90% position.
Identify Mode Before T1
Read Their Opening
Aggro deck: lead interaction (Fatal Push, Spell Snare), Frog at T2 to block. Combo deck: lead Force of Negation backup, Frog at T2 to race. Control deck: lead Harbinger at T3 to cut their mana, deploy threats when they tap out for a sweeper. The deck changes shape based on what they’re doing.

Heuristics

  1. 01
    T1 fetch into surveil land, not cantrip. Even if you have Consider in hand, play the fetch-land and crack for Undercity Sewers. The surveil puts a chosen card in GY AND removes a tapped land from your deck. Consider T1 mills 1 random card and draws — less controlled, doesn't thin tapped lands. Surveil land first, cantrips second.
  2. 02
    Exile lands and creatures to Frog. Save instants/sorceries for Murktide. When activating Frog's flying (exile 3 from GY), choose lands and creatures. Every instant/sorcery you exile is a +1/+1 counter Murktide doesn't get. Exception: if Murktide isn't in your hand or foreseeable future, exile anything.
  3. 03
    Unearth (the card) has NO exile clause. Your creature stays permanently. Unlike the Unearth keyword on creatures like Nethergoyf, the sorcery Unearth just returns the creature to the battlefield. No end-of-turn exile. No "if it would leave, exile instead." If someone tells you your Unearthed Oculus exiles at end of turn, they are wrong. Call a judge.
  4. 04
    Activate Frog's flying AFTER blocks are declared, not before. Declare Frog as an attacker (grounded). Opponent doesn't assign a blocker (Frog is "just a 3/4 ground creature"). After blocks: activate flying. Frog is now unblocked AND flying. If you activate flying before attacks, the opponent knows to assign a flier to block. The instant-speed activation is the trick.
  5. 05
    Pre-fetch Swamp before deploying Harbinger. Harbinger turns ALL nonbasics into Islands — including yours. If you don't have basic Swamp in play: you lose access to {B} permanently while Harbinger is alive. Fetch Swamp T1 or T2 if Harbinger is in your opener. A Harbinger without Swamp locks YOU out of Fatal Push, Bowmasters, and Unearth.
  6. 06
    Second Bowmasters grows the existing Army. Don't expect two tokens. If you already control an Orc Army token: the second Bowmasters' amass puts a +1/+1 counter on the existing Army. You don't get a second token. Plan your board state accordingly — one growing Army is better than two 1/1s in most situations.
  7. 07
    Frog can exile cards in response to Surgical Extraction. They target Oculus with Surgical → exile Oculus (plus 2 others) to Frog's flying ability as cost → Surgical fizzles. The exile is a COST — opponent cannot respond to it. But you need 3+ total cards in GY to activate (Oculus + 2 others). If your GY has only the Oculus, you can't save it.
  8. 08
    Sink into Stupor bounces "can't be countered" spells. Sink doesn't counter — it returns to hand. Supreme Verdict says "can't be countered" but Sink legally returns it to their hand. This is YOUR answer to uncounterable spells. Sink also bounces permanents in play — use it on expensive threats they tapped out to deploy.
  9. 09
    Manifested instant/sorcery stays as 2/2 forever. If Oculus manifests a Consider or Counterspell face-down: it's a 2/2 creature. It cannot be turned face up (it's not a creature card). It stays face-down permanently. This is free board presence but can't attack profitably into anything bigger than a 2/2. Use these tokens as chump blockers or sacrifice fodder.
  10. 10
    Thought Scour can target the opponent. "Target player mills two." Use against: known topdecks (they scried, you mill their chosen card), Lantern effects (disrupt their top-of-deck manipulation), mirror (mill their Oculus before they can draw it — risky, puts it in their GY for Unearth). 1-in-200 games, but game-winning when correct.
  11. 11
    Spell Pierce as intentional GY fuel. Cast Spell Pierce on their spell knowing they'll pay {2}. Your Spell Pierce resolves (goes to GY as an instant) — fuels Murktide delve (+1/+1 counter). The {2} they paid was worth less than the counter. Only do this in board stalls where one more Murktide counter = lethal flying damage.
  12. 12
    Cast spells during their upkeep to dodge their Force of Negation. FoN only works "if it's not your turn." Their upkeep IS their turn — they can't free-cast Force. Your spells during their upkeep are protected from free Force. Use this window to resolve key threats or interaction when they have Force in hand. They must pay {1}{U}{U} during their own turn.
  13. 13
    Don't deploy Murktide into empty GY. Without instants/sorceries to exile, Murktide enters as a 3/3 for {U}{U} (after delving lands/creatures for cost reduction). A 3/3 flier for {U}{U} is acceptable in emergencies but bad in most situations. Wait until you have 5+ instants/sorceries in GY before casting Murktide. An 8/8 wins games; a 3/3 trades down.
  14. 14
    Oculus's additional cost applies when cast from any zone — even "without paying mana cost." Cascade into Oculus? Still exile 6. Collected Company? CoCo puts it onto battlefield (doesn't cast) — no cost. Unearth? Returns (doesn't cast) — no cost. The distinction: "cast" triggers the additional cost. "Put onto battlefield" doesn't.
  15. 15
    Manifest dread with 1 card in library: you MUST manifest it. No choice — the last card goes face-down. With 0 cards: manifest dread does nothing (you don't lose). You only lose from drawing on an empty library, not from manifesting. If you're low on library cards, stop attacking with Frog (combat draw is mandatory if Frog deals damage). Manifest doesn't draw.

Tuning

Murktide Count — 2 vs 0

Keep 2 Murktide
When You Need an Unkillable Finisher
Murktide survives Fatal Push, Lightning Bolt, and most damage-based removal. An 8/8 flying that grows passively is the best standalone closer in the deck. Keep 2 copies when: the meta is midrange-heavy (Murktide outclasses their threats), when GY hate is low (Murktide delve is reliable), or when you need a card that wins the game in 2-3 attack steps without any additional support.
Cut to 0 Murktide
When GY Hate Is Everywhere
Some builds have dropped Murktide entirely. The argument: Oculus is a better payoff (Unearthable for {B}, doesn't need GY cards to be large), and Murktide competes with Frog for GY resources. In a meta full of Nihil Spellbomb and Surgical Extraction, Murktide often enters as a 4/4 or 5/5 — not the 8/8 you need. Replace with: 3rd Bowmasters + extra interaction, or Tamiyo, Inquisitive Student for a different card advantage axis.

Harbinger Count — 2 vs 3 Main

2 Main + 1 SB (Current)
Cheese Angle Without Dilution
2 main means you see Harbinger in ~25% of games. Against the right opponent (Boros, Domain) it's game-winning. Against the wrong opponent (mono-color, 2C with basics) it's a 2/2 for 3 mana. The SB copy comes in when you know Harbinger is live. This split balances the cheese potential against the risk of a dead card.
3 Main, 0 SB
All-In on the Mana Lock
In a meta where 70%+ of decks have greedy manabases: 3 main Harbinger increases your odds of the T2-T3 lock. The trade-off is more dead draws against Eldrazi Tron (they run Wastes/colorless basics) and mirror (both players are blue). Only go to 3 main if your local meta is dominated by Boros/Domain/4C and you want to maximize free wins from the cheese angle.

The Faithless Looting Splash

Grixis / Sultai Splash
Adding a Third Color
Some players splash red for Faithless Looting (unbanned Dec 2025): draw 2, discard 2 for {R}. This directly discards Oculus to GY (guaranteeing the Unearth combo) and fills GY faster for Murktide. Also enables Tamiyo, Inquisitive Student flips (draw 3 in a turn). The cost: a third color in a 19-land deck. You need red sources (Steam Vents, Scalding Tarn) which dilute your {U}{B} consistency. Verdict: powerful but risky. The mana is already tight — adding a third color increases your mulligan rate.
Straight Dimir
Maximum Consistency
The straight UB build never has color issues. Every land produces {U} or {B} or both. You never lose to your own mana. The trade-off: you rely on Thought Scour / Consider / surveil lands / Frog discard to put Oculus in GY — all less reliable than Faithless Looting. Verdict: better for an open field where you can't predict what you'll face. The consistency of never stumbling on mana outweighs the explosiveness of the Looting draw in most tournaments.

Register Conditions

Register When
Green Lights
Draw-heavy decks are well-represented (Storm, UW Control, Living End) — Bowmasters generates massive value passively. Many blue decks in the room — Harbinger tempo lock is live in more matchups. Low Surgical Extraction density in expected sideboards. Midrange-heavy meta where Frog is not a known target heading into the event.
Avoid When
Red Lights
Heavy graveyard hate meta — Nihil Spellbomb, Surgical Extraction, Rest in Peace shut off Murktide and GY synergies. Very creature-heavy field where Bowmasters drain is irrelevant — pure aggro decks rarely draw enough to matter. You’re not comfortable with the three-mode role switching — this deck punishes pilots who default to one plan.

SB Slot Priority Order

  1. 01
    Consign to Memory (3 copies) — Versatile: counters any spell AND exiles target GY. Goes in vs combo (Storm, Living End), Affinity (counter the combo piece), and any GY deck. The most flexible SB card. Never cut below 3.
  2. 02
    Nihil Spellbomb (3 copies) — Exile entire GY at instant speed + draw a card. Goes in vs Living End, Goryo’s Vengeance, Neoform, any reanimation strategy. Cast on their end step before their combo turn. The draw a card mode means it’s never a pure blank even without a GY target.
  3. 03
    Force of Negation (extra copies) — Free counter for non-creature spells. Goes in vs Storm (counter Past in Flames), Living End (counter cascade), Amulet (counter Titan). Never cut vs combo decks. Consider keeping in vs any deck where their 1-card win conditions matter.
  4. 04
    Harbinger of the Seas (2 copies) — Tempo piece vs island-dependent decks. Goes in vs Jeskai Blink, UW Control, any blue-heavy deck. Completely dead vs Boros, Jund, Eldrazi Tron. Check their manabase before bringing it in.
  5. 05
    Flex (Stern Scolding, Accursed Marauder, Spell Pierce) — Stern Scolding vs creature-light decks with small creatures. Accursed Marauder as a recursive threat vs grindy matchups. Spell Pierce vs non-creature spell decks. Adjust based on field composition.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand with 2 lands + interaction. Discard-heavy hands without threats are mulligans.
  • Role rule: Before T1: are you the aggressor or the control player? Frog + disruption = aggressor. Disruption only = control.
  • Frog rule: Never grant Frog flying unless the combat result is worth losing 3 GY cards.
  • Solitude rule: Evoke + Ephemerate for 3 exiles across 2 turns. Never waste Solitude on a 1/1.
  • Stack rule: Never cast Ephemerate first in a stack war. Let them commit, then respond.
  • Sideboard rule: Name the correct Meddling Mage target before G2. Write it on your deck box.
