Chapter 01

Deck
Overview

"Jeskai Control is an Azorius Control variant that splashes red to take advantage of how valuable that color is in many matchups — particularly the current meta centered around creatures and combat."

Jeskai Control runs zero creatures in the main deck — the entire 60 is removal, counters, and card draw. The win condition is Jeskai Revelation, which as an instant bounces a threat, deals 4 damage, creates two 1/1 Monk tokens with prowess, draws 2 cards, and gains 4 life — essentially resetting a board state and refueling simultaneously. Lightning Helix at instant speed deals 3 damage and gains 3 life, stabilizing against aggro. Inevitable Defeat exiles any nonland permanent, uncounterable, with life swing. Against creatures, this package is nearly impossible to fight through.

The deck's weakness is creature-light strategies — Izzet Prowess flood with tokens, Mono-Green's enchantment-based threats, and combo strategies that don't use creatures. The sideboard is built around filling those gaps (Torpor Orb vs ETB-heavy, High Noon vs aggro, Pyroclasm vs tokens).

Why Study This Deck

Jeskai Control is ~5% of the Cincinnati meta — you'll face it 1-2 rounds. As Dimir Midrange, this is your best matchup (64% WR per Untapped). Knowing their counterspell suite (No More Lies, Spell Snare), their sweepers (Day of Judgment, Ultima), and their uncounterable finisher (Inevitable Defeat + Jeskai Revelation) helps you sequence correctly — hold key spells through their removal window and deploy Surrak, Elusive Hunter (can't be countered) when they tap out.

Unlike aggro, this curve is designed around holding mana open. The 2-mana slot (Helix, Spell Snare) is deployed reactively. The 4-mana slot (Inevitable Defeat, Consult kicked) is the primary interaction. Always evaluate: "can I hold up a response this turn?" If yes, hold it. Card Notes

The Lifegain Removal
Lightning Helix
{R}{W} Instant. 3 damage to any target + gain 3 life. The best red-white spell in the format. At instant speed, it answers their T1-T3 creatures AND stabilizes your life total. Against Boros Aggro, 4 Helixes = 12 damage absorbed and 12 life gained — effectively a 24-point life swing. Against Prowess, it answers Slickshot Show-Off at any stage. The most important card in the deck against aggro.
The Uncounterable Exile
Inevitable Defeat
{2}{B}{W} Instant. Can't be countered. Exile target nonland permanent. Its controller loses 3 life, you gain 3 life. The answer to everything — Ojer Axonil, Mossborn Hydra, Sapling Nursery, Stormchaser's Talent. Can't be countered means it resolves through their Spell Pierce and Get Out. The life swing (+3 / −3 = 6-point swing) is meaningful in close races. 4 copies because this is the primary removal engine.
The Win Condition
Jeskai Revelation
{2}{U}{R}{W} Instant. Return target spell or permanent to hand + deals 4 damage to any target + create two 1/1 Monk tokens with prowess + draw 2 cards + gain 4 life. Five effects in one instant-speed card. Cast at end of opponent's turn after they've tapped out: bounce their key permanent, create two prowess threats, refuel your hand, gain 4 life. Those Monk tokens are your win condition — they grow with Stock Up and Helix triggers on your turn.
The Hard Counter
No More Lies
{U}{W} Instant. Counter target spell unless its controller pays {3}. If countered, exile it. The soft counter that's effectively hard when they're on curve. T2 Elusive Otter requires they pay {3} extra = it costs them {U}+{3} = {3}{U}. They can rarely pay {3} extra in the early turns. The exile clause prevents GY recursion for Esper Origins, Wild Ride Harmonize, and any flashback effects.
The Early Counter
Spell Snare
{U} Instant. Counter target spell with mana value 2. Hard counter for one mana specifically targeting the most dangerous turn-2 plays: Stormchaser's Talent, Warleader's Call, Shocking Sharpshooter, Earthbender Ascension. The {U} efficiency means you can hold it while deploying another spell. Only 2 copies because it's dead against 3+ mana threats.
The Sweepers
Day of Judgment / Ultima
Day of Judgment: {2}{W}{W} Sorcery — destroy all creatures. 2 main + 1 SB. The primary board reset. Ultima: {4}{W}{U} Sorcery — destroy all artifacts and creatures, then end the turn (exile all spells/abilities from stack, current player discards to max hand size, damage wears off, EOT effects end). Ultima destroys Warleader's Call (enchantment... wait — check: Ultima destroys artifacts AND creatures, not enchantments). Day of Judgment handles creatures only. Both are sorcery speed — must play proactively.
The Card Engine
Consult the Star Charts / Stock Up
Consult the Star Charts: {1}{W} Instant. Kicker {1}{U}. Look at top X cards (X = lands you control), put 1 (or 2 if kicked) in hand. With 6 lands, unkicked = look at 6 pick 1. Kicked with 6 lands = look at 6 pick 2 for {2}{U}{W}. Stock Up: {1}{U} Sorcery — look at top 5, put 2 in hand. Consult at instant speed enables reactive digging — find the sweeper or Helix you need on their end step.
The Mana Base
Thundering Falls / Mistrise Village
Thundering Falls: Island + Mountain, enters tapped, ETB surveil 1. Fixing + library manipulation. Mistrise Village: enters tapped unless you control Mountain or Forest. {U}{T}: your next spell can't be countered. The uncounterable clause on Mistrise is critical in the control mirror — deploy key Revelation or Inevitable Defeat through their countermagic. Note: Ultima can't be countered via Mistrise to blow up their board even through open blue mana.
Chapter 03

Game Flow

"Your job for the first 4 turns: don't die. Your job turns 5-8: answer everything. Your job after that: cast Revelation and win."

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turns 1–3: Survive
Hold Mana Open
Hold Lightning Helix and No More Lies in hand. Deploy land + hold up {R}{W} for Helix response on their attack step. Against aggro: kill their key threats at instant speed (Sharpshooter, Slickshot Show-Off). Against midrange: counter their value engines (Stormchaser's Talent, Sapling Nursery). Never tap out unless you're drawing a land and have nothing to hold up.
Turns 4–6: Reset
Sweep + Refuel
Once their board is threatening: Day of Judgment to wipe, then Consult the Star Charts kicked to refuel. Inevitable Defeat for their indestructible threats (Ojer Axonil) that Day of Judgment doesn't hit. Stock Up at instant speed on their end step before your turn. The goal: enter turn 7 with 3+ cards in hand and their board clear.
Turns 7+: Win
Revelation into Monks
Cast Jeskai Revelation at instant speed (end of their turn): bounce their key permanent, deal 4 damage, create 2 Monk tokens with prowess, draw 2 cards, gain 4 life. Now you have 2 prowess 1/1 threats on board. Cast Lightning Helix next turn while attacking: each Helix is also +1/+1 on each Monk until EOT. They become 3/3s after one Helix. Win in 2-3 swings.

Ultima's End the Turn Clause

Ultima ends the turn after resolution. This means: all spells and abilities currently on the stack are exiled (including Ultima itself). The current player discards to maximum hand size. All "this turn" and "until end of turn" effects end — including their prowess buffs, their creature's haste (they untap next turn), and their temporary pump spells. Use Ultima when they've tapped out to attack and pumped their creatures — you destroy everything and their pump effects vanish with the turn ending. Note: Ultima destroys artifacts AND creatures, not enchantments. Warleader's Call survives Ultima.

Chapter 04

Answer Suite

"Know which spell answers which threat. Running out of answers because you used the wrong tool is how Jeskai Control loses."
Creatures (any size)
Lightning Helix / Inevitable Defeat
Helix: instant, 3 damage, gain 3 life. Best against 1-3 toughness creatures early (all of Boros' 1/1-2/3 threats, Elusive Otter, Slickshot Show-Off). Inevitable Defeat: exile any nonland permanent, uncounterable. Use for: indestructible creatures, high-toughness threats Helix can't reach (Eddymurk Crab 5/5), planeswalkers, key enchantments. The rule: Helix kills efficiently (2 mana for 3 damage + 3 life), Defeat exiles anything that doesn't die to Helix.
Enchantments
Inevitable Defeat / Get Lost
Inevitable Defeat exiles any nonland permanent — the only main-deck answer to enchantments like Sapling Nursery, Warleader's Call, Stormchaser's Talent. Get Lost: {1}{G} instant — destroy target creature, enchantment, or planeswalker; controller gets 2 Map tokens. The Map tokens are a concession (they get minor card advantage), but instant-speed enchantment removal is critical. Use Get Lost when Helix's 3-damage clause is irrelevant and the enchantment must die immediately.
Counterspells
No More Lies / Spell Snare
No More Lies: soft counter at {U}{W}, hard counter at MV ≤ 3 effectively. Use for key threats you can't answer in play (combo pieces, uncounterable threats, things without answers in your main deck). Spell Snare: hard counter for MV 2 exactly — optimal against Stormchaser's Talent, Warleader's Call, Shocking Sharpshooter. Hold Snare when their T2 play is dangerous. Never tap out to cast a proactive spell when Snare is your only response.
Wide Boards
Day of Judgment / Ultima
Day of Judgment: {2}{W}{W} sorcery, destroy all creatures. Use when they have 3+ creatures and Helix can't answer them individually. Ultima: {4}{W}{U} sorcery, destroy all artifacts and creatures, end the turn. Use when they have artifacts AND creatures threatening simultaneously (Boros Aggro with artifact-matters, any multitype board). Remember: Ultima doesn't hit enchantments — Warleader's Call survives.

What Jeskai Cannot Answer in Main Deck

Main Deck Gap
GY-Based Strategies
The main deck has no graveyard hate. Izzet Spellementals using GY for Eddymurk Crab cost reduction, Reanimator returning large threats, Icetill Explorer playing lands from GY — all operate without main deck interference. Plan: counter or exile before they hit the GY. Post-board: Rest in Peace (hard exile everything) and Torpor Orb (stops ETB triggers from reanimated threats).
Main Deck Gap
Fast Token Floods
A resolved Stormchaser's Talent at level 3 generating tokens per spell is difficult to answer — each Inevitable Defeat on the Talent costs {4} and they've already gotten value. Day of Judgment clears the tokens but not the Talent. Annul from the SB counters it on the way in. Post-board: Annul for Talent, Torpor Orb blanks Crab and other ETB threats. The gap: no instant-speed enchantment removal in the main.
Chapter 05

Role Assignment

Jeskai Control is always the reactive player — you never have a proactive "I must do X by turn Y" plan. Your role is defined by the opponent:

vs Aggro
Life Total Manager
Use Lightning Helix at instant speed to both kill threats and gain life. Never tap out when they have open attack mana. Day of Judgment as the reset button. Goal: reach turn 6 with 8+ life, then Revelation stabilizes completely.
vs Midrange
Answer Engine
Inevitable Defeat for their key permanents. No More Lies for their value spells. Counter their draw engines before they generate card advantage. Revelation when they're topdecking — bounce their last threat and refuel.
vs Control Mirror
Resource War
Hold counterspells for their key spells. Mistrise Village makes your Inevitable Defeat uncounterable — use it to land the uncounterable exile. Revelation at instant speed EOT lets you maintain card parity while generating the Monk clock they can't answer.

Sideboard Guide

Flashfreeze ×2
{1}{U} Instant. Counter target red or green spell. Hard counter for one color — specifically designed for Mono-Green Landfall and Boros Aggro's red package. Counters Burst Lightning, Howlsquad Heavy, Voice of Victory (white, doesn't help), Earthbender Ascension (green), Sapling Nursery (green), Mossborn Hydra. At {1}{U} it's efficiently costed. Board in: Mono-Green, Boros (for their red spells), any matchup where Helix range misses key threats.
High Noon ×2
{2}{W} Enchantment. Each player can't cast more than one spell per turn. Slows down any strategy dependent on chaining multiple spells per turn — Izzet Prowess (their cantrip chain generates all their damage), Boros Aggro (limits their burn chains). Note: doesn't stop Mobilize tokens (those aren't spells), and doesn't stop triggered abilities. Board in: any combo or spell-chain strategy.
Rest in Peace ×2
{1}{W} Enchantment. All cards go to exile instead of GY. Hard GY hate. Stops Icetill Explorer from playing lands from GY, prevents Wild Ride Harmonize, blanks Eddymurk Crab's GY cost reduction. Board in: any GY-dependent strategy (Izzet Prowess, Spellementals, any Reanimator).
Wan Shi Tong, Librarian ×2
{2}{U} Legendary Creature 3/3. Knowledge is power — when you draw a card, you may put any card from hand on bottom of library. Provides a recurring threat that also refines your hand. Board in: any midrange or control matchup where the game goes long and you need a card-quality engine. A 3/3 body that also draws is significant in a deck with no other creatures.
Annul ×2
{U} Instant. Counter target artifact or enchantment spell. Hard counter for {U}. Stormchaser's Talent (1 mana), Sapling Nursery (X mana), Warleader's Call (3 mana), Earthbender Ascension (2 mana). The cheapest possible answer to their most dangerous enchantments. Board in: Izzet Prowess, Mono-Green, Boros Aggro (Warleader's Call), any enchantment-heavy strategy.
Torpor Orb ×2
{2} Artifact. Creatures entering don't cause abilities to trigger. Blanks all ETB-based strategies. Against Dimir: Tidebinder can't counter your abilities, Drowner can't stun, Kaito's ninjutsu trigger is blanked. Against Mono-Green: Badgermole Cub's earthbend doesn't trigger, Fecund Greenshell's ETB doesn't trigger. Note: Orb also prevents Jeskai's Revelation Monks from having prowess triggered by spells — they enter as 1/1 Monks that don't get prowess buffs. Board in carefully when the ETBs you want to stop outweigh your own ETBs lost.
Chapter 08

Heuristics

  1. 01
    Never tap out when they have open attack mana — hold response mana always. Control's golden rule. Tapping out for a sorcery-speed Consult or Stock Up when they have 3 open mana means they untap and attack into an unguarded Jeskai player. The loss condition is almost always "I tapped out and they attacked for lethal." Hold {R}{W} for Helix or {U}{W} for No More Lies every single turn.
  2. 02
    Inevitable Defeat is your primary removal — use it on indestructible, high-toughness, and enchantment threats that other spells miss. Helix answers toughness 3 or less. Inevitable Defeat answers everything else. Reserve Defeat for: Ojer Axonil (4 toughness, indestructible when transformed), Sapling Nursery (enchantment), Mossborn Hydra (large toughness), Eddymurk Crab (5 toughness). Don't waste Defeat on a Shocking Sharpshooter when Helix handles it.
  3. 03
    Jeskai Revelation's bounce is a tempo gain, not permanent removal — bounce their best permanent, not their most permanent. Revelation returns "target spell or permanent to its owner's hand." Bouncing a Sapling Nursery delays it one turn but they replay it next turn. Bouncing a planeswalker when they have the mana to re-cast is poor value. Best targets: bounce their spell in response to ETB before it resolves (tempo gain + 4 damage + tokens + 2 draws + 4 life), or bounce their key permanent when they're at 1 land and can't re-cast.
  4. 04
    Mistrise Village's uncounterable activation must be used the same turn you cast the spell — it applies to the next spell you cast this turn. Activate Mistrise Village ({U}{T}: next spell can't be countered) immediately before casting Inevitable Defeat or Jeskai Revelation in the control mirror. The activation is at sorcery speed — use it before moving to cast the spell you want to protect. The uncounterable window doesn't carry to next turn.
  5. 05
    Ultima ends the turn after destroying artifacts and creatures — your own combat damage and effects end too. If you had your own permanents with "until end of turn" effects, they end. More importantly: if you were planning to attack after Ultima, you can't — the turn ends immediately after Ultima resolves. Cast Ultima BEFORE your combat step if you want to destroy their board and still attack with your Monks. Or cast it on their end step to take out their board before untapping.
  6. 06
    Voice of Victory prevents opponents from casting spells during your turn — plan your removal before their attack step. When facing Boros Aggro, after Voice attacks and creates Mobilize tokens, you cannot cast Lightning Helix or Inevitable Defeat in response during their turn because Voice's ability prevents opponents from casting spells DURING their turn. You must deploy removal BEFORE they enter their attack step. If they attack with Voice and you're holding Helix, you've missed the window.
  7. 07
    Consult the Star Charts at instant speed is your most efficient card selection — use it on their end step. Consult: {1}{W} instant, unkicked = look at top (lands you control), put 1 in hand. Kicked {1}{U} = put 2 in hand. On their end step when they've passed priority: cast kicked Consult to dig 6 cards deep and take 2 into your full untap. This is your primary draw engine — it's better than Stock Up (sorcery) because you can respond and dig during their turn.
Chapter 09

Tuning Notes

Revelation: 3 vs 4 copies
4 is correct for RC
Jeskai Revelation is your win condition and your stabilizer. Some lists run 3 to fit more interaction, but 4 ensures you reliably find it in games 2-3 when they've boarded in more threats. The {2}{U}{R}{W} cost is heavy but you're running 26 lands with extensive fixing. Never cut below 3.
Day of Judgment: 2 main + 1 SB
Match the meta
2 main deck is correct for the current meta. In aggro-heavy metas (Boros Aggro up in meta share), run 3-4 main. In midrange-heavy metas, trim to 1 main and add more targeted removal. The SB copy gives access to 3 total against token-heavy strategies without dead draws against control.
Ultima: 2 main deck
Use vs artifact decks
Ultima's value is the artifact+creature wipe combined with ending the turn. If artifact decks (Krang, Izzet Artifacts) are absent from the meta, Ultima is usually worse than a 3rd Day of Judgment. The "end the turn" clause is uniquely powerful in some situations but mostly irrelevant in the current meta. Trim to 1 if facing mostly creature-only decks.
Counterspell ratio
6 total (4 NML + 2 Snare)
4 No More Lies + 2 Spell Snare is the correct ratio for a primarily reactive control deck. Against heavy countermagic metas (more control): add Get Out (modal — also returns your own permanents). Against creature-heavy metas: trim a Spell Snare for a Get Lost. The deck shouldn't run more than 8 total counterspells or it becomes too clunky in the aggro matchups.
Gloomlake + Godless Shrine splash
Black access for Dimir SB
The Gloomlake Verge + Godless Shrine package provides black mana access for the sideboard. Tishana's Tidebinder is {1}{U}{G} but some builds add Duress or Thoughtseize access. The splash is minimal and doesn't affect main deck consistency. If you cut black from the SB, trim the Gloomlake Verges to 0-1.
Torpor Orb: 2 copies
Anti-Dimir specialist
2 copies is enough against Dimir (your worst matchup). Their ETB package (Tidebinder, Drowner, Cub) is dismantled by Orb. Warning: Torpor Orb prevents your own Jeskai Revelation Monks from having prowess. Those 1/1 Monks remain 1/1s even while you cast spells. If you're relying on Monks as your win condition against Dimir, deploying Orb first means your Monks don't grow. Sequence: kill their ETB threats, then Revelation for Monks after or before Orb as appropriate.
