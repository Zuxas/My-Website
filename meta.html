the four archetypes — how we frame every deck

Every deck in Modern fits somewhere on this grid. Understanding where a deck sits — and where it sits in each matchup — is the foundation of role assignment and sideboard construction.

Archetype 01
Aggro
Win through speed. Low-cost threats, direct damage, and momentum. End the game before the opponent's plan comes online. Prowess is the current best example in Modern — Cori-Steel Cutter added a new dimension.
Archetype 02
Control
Survive, answer threats, close with a few powerful win conditions. Counterspells, removal, and card advantage. UW Control is the shell we're testing — best in combo-heavy fields.
Archetype 03
Combo
Assemble a specific sequence to win. Requires protection and setup. Grixis Reanimator (Glockulous) is our active combo test — midrange-combo hybrid, NOT a glass cannon.
Archetype 04
Midrange / Tempo
Efficient threats plus efficient answers. UW Blink and Jeskai Blink live here — tempo-control hybrids that shift roles by matchup. Most of our testing hours right now.

current meta read

Modern is wide open. The format rewards preparation and familiarity over raw deck power — which is why we invest in the gauntlet system.

Tier 1 — Boros Energy

Still the most consistent threat in the format. I qualified on this deck last Modern season — 17 RCQ Top 8s. Fast clock, resilient threats, efficient interaction. The core is Ragavan, Guide of Souls, Ocelot Pride, Ajani Nacatl Pariah, Phlage, and Goblin Bombardment. Punishes stumbles immediately.

  • Key cards: Ragavan; Guide of Souls; Ocelot Pride; Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury; Goblin Bombardment; Ajani, Nacatl Pariah
  • Wins by: Efficient threats + converting tokens into reach via Bombardment
  • Loses to: Deafening Silence, Wrath effects at the right moment, stabilizing life total

Tier 1 — Prowess (Izzet)

Fastest deck in the format. DRC, Swiftspear, Slickshot Show-Off, and Cori-Steel Cutter create explosive turns. Cori-Steel Cutter is the key two-drop — creates a 1/1 prowess creature with haste and trample every turn cycle, functions as an unkillable Monastery Mentor. Higher ceiling than Boros Energy, lower floor.

  • Key cards: Monastery Swiftspear; Dragon's Rage Channeler; Cori-Steel Cutter; Slickshot Show-Off; Lava Dart
  • Wins by: Explosive spell-dense turns pumping multiple creatures simultaneously
  • Loses to: Deafening Silence; High Noon; Trinisphere; Solitude at instant speed

Tier 1–2 — Jeskai / UW Blink

Most of our testing hours. The blink shell is powerful and hard to hate out cleanly. Complexity ceiling is high — reps matter. Jeskai adds Teferi TRav and Lightning Bolt at the cost of mana consistency. Correct when the field is 20%+ combo. UW is correct in aggro-heavy open fields.

  • Key cards: Ephemerate; Phelia; White Orchid Phantom; Solitude; Ocelot Pride; Guide of Souls
  • Wins by: Recursive ETB value, mana denial, Guide + Ocelot inevitability
  • Loses to: Very fast aggro before engines come online

Tier 1–2 — Grixis Reanimator (Glockulous)

Active combo test this season. Team Resolve pet name: Glockulous. This is NOT Goryo's Vengeance — it's a midrange-combo hybrid built around Psychic Frog, Abhorrent Oculus, Archon of Cruelty, and Emperor of Bones. The key insight: Archon entering off Persist at instant speed cannot be answered by the creature removal the field is calibrated for.

  • Key cards: Psychic Frog; Abhorrent Oculus; Archon of Cruelty; Emperor of Bones; Persist; Faithless Looting
  • Wins by: Reanimating Archon at instant speed through Persist — the field's removal doesn't interact with the stack
  • Loses to: Consign to Memory; Teferi, Time Raveler; graveyard hate

Tier 2 — UW Control

Classic shell, rewards deep format knowledge. Best in fields skewed toward midrange and combo. The challenge is Boros Energy and Prowess both play under its interaction — don't pick this if the field is aggro-heavy.

  • Key cards: Counterspell; Snapcaster Mage; Solitude; Teferi, Hero of Dominaria; Supreme Verdict; Consign to Memory
  • Wins by: Answering everything, generating card advantage, closing with protected Teferi Hero
  • Loses to: Decks that go under interaction; aggressive starts before Verdict mana

how we approach deck selection

At Team Resolve, we don't pick decks based on meta share alone. We run gauntlets.

Step 1 — Identify the Field

What does the expected metagame look like for the specific RC or RCQ we're preparing for? Local trends, recent large event results, and what qualified players in our region tend to play all factor in. We pull archetype frequency data through the MTG Meta Analyzer before building any prep package.

Step 2 — Run the Gauntlet

Structured gauntlets against the top 6–8 archetypes in the expected field — both pre- and post-board. Win rates are tracked against real match data, not estimated. If a deck can't hit acceptable numbers against the field we expect, it's not the right call regardless of how powerful it feels in isolation.

Step 3 — Lock the 75

Once a deck passes the gauntlet, we lock the 75. Not 73 with two flex slots undecided the morning of — 75, committed, with clear reasoning for every card. We build a full matchup guide before the event, not during. This process drove the consistency behind 17 and 23 RCQ Top 8 results last season.

Role Assignment

Before the first spell is cast, know your role: beatdown or control? Against Prowess you're control — stabilize, then close. Against Azorius Control you're beatdown — pressure early, force answers ahead of curve. Getting this wrong costs games. Getting it right consistently separates 4–1 from 3–2.
