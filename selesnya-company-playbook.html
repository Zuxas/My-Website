Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"Every creature enters doing something. Thalia slows them down. CoCo refills your board at instant speed at the end of their turn. They never have a clean window to answer everything."

Selesnya Company is Pioneer's premier creature-based midrange deck. The strategy is built around three interlocking pieces: mana acceleration through early elves, disruption through taxing creatures, and explosive board refills via Collected Company. No individual card is overwhelming, but the combination of consistent threat density, instant-speed surprise hits, and difficult-to-answer resilience makes the deck one of the most consistent performers in the format.

The engine starts with Llanowar Elves and Elvish Mystic — eight unconditional mana dorks that let you play three-mana threats on turn two. Thalia, Guardian of Thraben is the most punishing two-drop: her tax on noncreature spells slows down every other deck in Pioneer. Against UR Cutter, every cantrip costs {1} extra. Against Greasefang, every discard outlet and Monument activation costs {1} more. Against control, every counterspell and removal spell costs more. Thalia buys you a full turn of breathing room in matchups where tempo is everything.

Collected Company is the payoff. At instant speed for {3G}, you reveal the top six cards and put up to two creatures with MV 3 or less into play. With 26+ creatures in the deck, the hit rate is extremely reliable. The instant speed is the key — you cast CoCo at end of their turn, untap with two fresh creatures, and attack. They never get to see what's coming and can't tap out safely when you have four mana available.

Why Selesnya At 9% Pioneer

Resilience
Board Wipes Don't End the Game
Against a traditional midrange or aggro deck, a sweeper ends the game. Against Selesnya Company, a sweeper is answered by a CoCo end of their turn and two creatures entering play. Extraction Specialist returns another creature from your GY. Your opponent needs multiple sweepers in sequence to contain the board, and control decks rarely have that.
Disruption
Thalia Slows Every Deck
Thalia taxes all noncreature spells {1} extra. In a format where UR Cutter runs 20+ noncreature spells, Greasefang depends on discards, and control relies on cheap interaction, Thalia's tax generates 4-8 extra mana worth of tempo over the course of a game. She makes every opponent's turn more awkward from turn 2 onward.
Speed
T2 Three-Drops Every Game
Eight mana dorks make T2 three-drops consistent. A T1 elf into T2 Thalia or Voice of Resurgence is a strong clock. T3 Collected Company (hitting two creatures) puts you at 4-5 creatures on the board by turn 3. No other midrange deck in Pioneer can deploy this much threat volume this early while also disrupting the opponent.

Key Card Notes

The Payoff
Collected Company — Instant-Speed Two-for-One
Cast at end of their turn for {3G}: reveal top 6, put up to two creatures with MV ≤ 3 into play. With 28 creatures (MV 1-3) in the deck, expected hits: 2 creatures roughly 85%+ of the time. The instant speed is the key advantage — they can't tap out for a four-mana spell at end of their turn safely when you have {3G} available. Always ask: what's the best time to CoCo? Usually end of their turn 3 or 4.
The Taxer
Thalia, Guardian of Thraben — {1} on Everything
All noncreature spells cost {1} more for both players. Against UR Cutter: Vivi Ornitier, cantrips, and Treasure Cruise all cost more. Against Greasefang: Monument looting, discard outlets, everything costs more. Against control: counterspells, removal, and wraths cost more. Your deck runs almost entirely creatures — Thalia taxes them asymmetrically. The 2/1 first strike body is also difficult to profitably block.
The Value
Kellan, Daring Traveler — Draw on Attack
2/1 for {1G} that draws a card when it deals combat damage to a player. With mana elves enabling T2 Kellan, you often draw your first card on T2. Every time Kellan connects you pull ahead on cards. He's your card advantage engine in creature mirrors where both sides are trading resources. Small enough to CoCo into, large enough to trade with most one-drops.
The Recovery
Extraction Specialist — Return + 3/2 Body
3/2 for {2W}. When it enters, return a creature with MV 2 or less from your GY to play (tapped). This means your Llanowar Elves, Thalia, Voice of Resurgence, Kellan, and Skrelv never truly die — Extraction Specialist loops them back. It creates a recursive engine where removal never permanently answers your threats. A key CoCo hit in any late-game scenario.
Chapter 03

GAME
FLOW

"T1 elf. T2 Thalia. T3 attack, then CoCo on their end step. Untap with five creatures."
Ideal Curve
T1–T4 Board Dominance
T1: Llanowar Elves or Elvish Mystic. T2: Thalia (tapping the elf) + Voice of Resurgence. Opponent is now taxed and facing a 2/2 + 2/1 first strike. T3: Attack for 4, then cast Collected Company on their end step, hitting two creatures from 6. T4: Untap with 5 creatures, attack for lethal or near-lethal. This is a T4 kill doing nothing unusual.
Mulligan
Need a Mana Elf in Every Opening
Keep: any hand with a mana elf + 2-3 lands + at least one two-drop or CoCo. Mulligan: hands with zero mana dorks (you're a turn behind every other Pioneer deck without acceleration), hands with 5+ lands, and hands with CoCo but nothing to accelerate into it on T3. At 6: elf + land + any two-drop is keepable. 5-card hands: elf + land + Thalia is correct.
CoCo Timing
End of Their Turn, Not Your Main Phase
Always cast Collected Company at the end of the opponent's turn unless you need the creatures to block immediately. Waiting for their end step means: (1) you untap with the creatures, (2) they couldn't interact during their turn with full mana up, (3) they committed resources assuming you had no action. The surprise factor of instant-speed creatures is a major part of the deck's strength.
Voice of Resurgence
Force Them Off Instant-Speed Interaction
Voice of Resurgence creates an Elemental token when either player casts a spell during your turn. It punishes instant-speed interaction during your main phase — if they counterspell or remove a creature on your turn, they give you a token. This forces them to let your spells resolve or pass without interaction. Against control, Voice completely warps how they can deploy answers.
Chapter 04

CORE
ENGINES

"Mana elves front-load your curve. Thalia taxes every answer. CoCo refills at instant speed. Three interlocking systems."
Engine 1 — Acceleration
Eight Mana Dorks
4 Llanowar Elves + 4 Elvish Mystic = eight unconditional T1 one-mana green creatures that produce {G}. The math: with 8 elves in 60 cards and 28 creatures total, you have a ~78% chance of having a mana dork in your opening 7. Without them, you're a turn behind on every curve — with them, you're deploying three-mana threats on turn two consistently.
Engine 2 — Disruption
Thalia + Voice Tax Package
Thalia taxes noncreature spells. Voice of Resurgence punishes instant-speed interaction on your turn. Together they create a zone of control: opponents must cast spells at sorcery speed (to avoid Voice tokens) but Thalia's tax means those sorceries cost more mana. The two-card combination warps opponent sequencing more than either card alone.
Engine 3 — Resilience
CoCo + Extraction Loop
Collected Company refills your board from your library at instant speed. Extraction Specialist refills your board from your graveyard. Together, they make your threat supply essentially infinite in longer games. A sweeper leaves them tapped out; CoCo on your end step puts two more into play. Extraction Specialist on the following turn returns the best MV-2 creature from the GY.
Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Don't tap out for CoCo on your turn unless you need the blockers. Instant speed is the advantage."
Line 01 — CoCo Timing: End of Their Turn Unless You're Blocking
The default CoCo timing is end of opponent's turn. Exceptions: (1) you need blockers to survive an attack, (2) you need to answer a specific threat immediately. In all other cases, waiting until their end step means they committed to a line without knowing your board state. They attacked with five creatures assuming you had no defense; your CoCo at end of step puts two blockers into play and they don't get to undo their attacks.
Line 02 — Protect Thalia from Removal, Not from Combat
Thalia's tax is most valuable when she's alive. Use Skrelv (protection from a color of your choice) to protect Thalia from targeted removal, not to push damage. A protected Thalia generates more tempo value over multiple turns than one extra point of combat damage. The exception: if your Thalia is already dying to a sweeper, Skrelv doesn't help — focus on rebuilding with CoCo instead.
Line 03 — Knight of Autumn Modes — Gain 4 Life vs Remove an Artifact
Knight of Autumn has three ETB modes: gain 4 life, remove an artifact or enchantment, or become a 4/3. Default mode vs aggro: gain 4 life. Default mode vs Lotus Field: destroy Thespian's Stage or Lotus Field. Default mode vs most midrange: 4/3 body for aggressive pressure. The 4/3 mode makes it a legitimate beater for CoCo hits when neither of the other modes are relevant. The 4-life mode is often the CoCo hit that stabilizes you at exactly 1-2 life.
Line 04 — Voice of Resurgence Forces Sorcery-Speed Interaction on Your Turn
Voice creates an Elemental token whenever EITHER player casts a spell during your turn. This means: if they remove a creature on your turn (Path, Play with Fire, Fatal Push), they give you a token. If they counterspell on your turn, they give you a token. The correct response: cast your spells on your turn freely — the tax on their interaction is your profit. Don't slow-roll spells trying to "protect" them from being countered if Voice is in play.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"You're the proactive deck in most matchups. Be the one developing a board. Let them react."
vs Combo
Clock + Disruption
Against Greasefang and Lotus Field: deploy Thalia early to tax their setup, apply clock with creatures, and win before they execute. You're not trying to interact with the combo — Thalia buying one extra turn is often enough. Leyline of Sanctity (SB) prevents Thoughtseize from disrupting your hand. Rest in Peace (SB) shuts off Parhelion II recursion.
vs Aggro
Outclass Their Board
Against RDW: your creatures are larger and you have Knight of Autumn for lifegain. Gaining 4 life from Knight stabilizes against burn. Lathiel, the Bounding Dawn generates +1/+1 counters on life gain — a board that gains life and grows is difficult for burn to close against. Against UR Cutter: your bodies block their Monks profitably; Thalia slows their cantrips.
vs Control
Play Around Wraths
Against UW Control: deploy two threats, hold CoCo in hand. When they tap out for a wrath, CoCo their end step. Never overcommit more than 3-4 creatures unless you're killing them that turn. Voice of Resurgence makes them choose between tapping out for a sorcery-speed wrath or holding mana for interaction — either path benefits you.

SIDEBOARD
GUIDE

"Your sideboard answers combo and hate. Your main deck already beats fair decks."
P1 — vs Combo (Discard)
Leyline of Sanctity ×3
Prevents you from being targeted by discard spells, burn, and other targeted effects. Against Greasefang, Thoughtseize is their primary disruption — Leyline in your opening hand completely blanks that plan. Notably, Leyline is free if in your opening 7, making it perfect against hand disruption strategies that would normally strip your combo answers before you can cast them.
P2 — vs GY Strategies
Rest in Peace ×2
Enchantment that exiles all GY cards and exiles everything going to the GY afterward. A T2 Rest in Peace completely answers Greasefang (no Parhelion II to return), Arclight Phoenix (can't return from GY), and any other GY-based strategy. Your deck doesn't use the GY in game 2-3 after boarding — Extraction Specialist is cut. Rest in Peace is a one-sided lock in most GY matchups.
P3 — vs Combo (Mana)
Damping Sphere ×2
Artifact that makes lands only produce {C} if they produce more than one mana, and spells cost {1} extra per spell cast by that player this turn. Shuts off Lotus Field's mana generation completely. Also relevant vs any storm-style combo. Comes in alongside Thalia's tax for a double-disruption package against mana-intensive combo decks.
P4 — vs Removal-Heavy
Shapers' Sanctuary ×2
Enchantment: whenever a creature you control becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, draw a card. Against UW Control and any targeted removal deck, this converts their interaction into card draw for you. They can't answer Shapers' Sanctuary with their removal (it's an enchantment), and every removal spell they cast draws you a card. Creates an insurmountable card advantage gap in long games.

Chapter 09

HEURISTICS

"CoCo at end of their turn. Thalia on T2. Never overextend into a wrath."
  1. 01Always lead with a mana elf on T1 if you have one. The entire deck's curve advantage comes from T1 mana acceleration. A T1 elf into T2 Thalia is the deck's strongest opening. A T1 elf into T2 Voice + Thalia (with extra mana) is even stronger. Never wait on your elf.
  2. 02CoCo on their end step, not your main phase, unless blocking immediately. Instant speed is the point. Casting CoCo in your main phase throws away the deck's primary advantage — surprise, untapping with creatures, and denying them the ability to react during their turn.
  3. 03Protect Thalia with Skrelv against targeted removal, not in combat. Thalia's ongoing tax effect is worth more than one attack. Use Skrelv to give Thalia protection from whatever color their removal spells are. First strike makes Thalia hard to attack into, so combat kills are less common than targeted removal kills.
  4. 04Don't overextend against control — hold 2-3 creatures and a CoCo. Against UW Control or any deck with sweepers: keep at most 3 creatures in play. When they tap out for a wrath, cast CoCo. If you have 7 creatures in play and they wrath, you're rebuilding; if you have 3 creatures and CoCo in hand, you're fine.
  5. 05Voice of Resurgence: let them interact on your turn — it makes a token. Don't slow-roll your spells to "protect" them from instant-speed interaction when Voice is in play. If they remove your creature on your turn, they give you an Elemental token. You're profiting from their interaction.
  6. 06Knight of Autumn mode is context-dependent — decide before putting it in play. The three modes are: gain 4 life (vs aggro, burn), remove artifact/enchantment (vs Lotus Field, artifacts), 4/3 body (vs everything else or when you need offense). Decide the mode before declaring the ETB trigger — once you announce the trigger, you're committed.
  7. 07Extraction Specialist returns MV-2 or less — keep this in mind when making trades. Before trading a Thalia, Voice of Resurgence, Kellan, or Skrelv into their creature, note that Extraction Specialist can return any of them. Favorable trades that put a MV-2 creature in your GY are often correct because Specialist recovers it next CoCo hit.
  8. 08Against RDW: Knight of Autumn gain-4-life mode buys 2 full turns. RDW deals roughly 2-3 damage per turn in the mid-game. Gaining 4 life is equivalent to buying 1-2 full turns. In many games, a single Knight of Autumn gain-4 is the entire difference between winning and losing.
  9. 09Commune with Spirits digs for CoCo or a missing creature. Cast it to find CoCo when you're missing the payoff spell, or to find a specific creature (Thalia vs combo, Knight vs aggro). At 1 mana it's the cheapest way to smooth your draw. Cast it early in the game, not late.
  10. 10Rest in Peace shuts off Extraction Specialist — don't board it in if you need Specialist. After bringing in Rest in Peace, note that your own Extraction Specialist can no longer return creatures from your GY (they're exiled now). Plan your post-board games assuming the Specialist loop is offline.

TUNING
NOTES

"4 Elves, 4 Mystic, 4 Thalia, 4 CoCo are the locks. Everything else adjusts to the meta."
Lock — Never Cut
4 Llanowar Elves + 4 Elvish Mystic
Eight mana dorks is the minimum for consistent T2 three-drops. Cutting to 6 noticeably reduces your acceleration consistency, which is the entire reason to play this deck over a non-CoCo GW midrange build.
Lock — Never Cut
4 Thalia, Guardian of Thraben
Your best card in 4 of 5 matchups. First strike makes her difficult to kill in combat. 4 copies is mandatory — you need her in your opening hand or on T2 consistently.
Lock — Never Cut
4 Collected Company
The deck's entire late-game identity. CoCo is why you play 28 creatures instead of a more interactive build. Cutting even one reduces the frequency of your most powerful plays.
Flex 2–4
Voice of Resurgence
4 in creature-heavy or control metas. Can go to 2-3 in combo-heavy metas where the token creation is less relevant. Voice's anti-counterspell property is best vs control and tempo decks.
Flex 1–4
Commune with Spirits
Pure card selection that finds either a creature or CoCo. Increase to 4 in metas where finding CoCo consistently is critical (control-heavy). Decrease to 2 in metas where raw threat density matters more (aggro-heavy).
Meta Call
Lathiel, the Bounding Dawn
Best when burn (RDW) or lifegain matters. Grows your board with +1/+1 counters whenever you gain life. Cut entirely in metas where lifegain isn't relevant. Add copies when facing heavy burn or aggressive strategies.

Know Cold
CoCo Hit Rate Math
With 28 creatures in 60 cards and 6 cards revealed: expected 2-creature hits ~85%, 1-creature hits ~13%, 0 hits ~2%. Practical note: never mulligan a hand just because you don't have CoCo — you hit often enough that having acceleration + threats is correct. At 26 creatures, hit rate drops slightly (~80% for 2). Keep creature count at 26+.
Know Cold
Thalia's Tax Per Matchup
UR Cutter: 6-8 spells per game × {1} = 6-8 free mana of tempo. Greasefang: 4-6 discard/Monument activations × {1} = 4-6 tempo. UW Control: 6-10 interaction spells × {1} = your clock advantage. Calculate this at game start — knowing Thalia generates 6+ free mana of tempo tells you how aggressive to be with your clock.
