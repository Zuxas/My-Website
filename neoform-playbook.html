Chapter 01

Deck Identity

Simic Neoform is Modern's purest kill-you-before-you-react combo deck. One free creature, one two-mana spell, one 12/12 trampler with haste. Turn two. Good game.

Neoform's entire architecture points at a single moment: exile two green cards from your hand, drop Allosaurus Rider for free, cast Neoform or Eldritch Evolution, find Ghalta, Stampede Tyrant or Griselbrand, and close the game before most decks have established anything. The deck competes with Tameshi Belcher for the title of Modern's fastest consistent kill.

Unlike cascade-based combo decks, Neoform operates at sorcery speed — you must initiate the combo on your own turn, which means the entire game hinges on protecting a two-second window. Pact of Negation, Veil of Summer, and Consign to Memory are your armor. The deck's weakness is also its identity: it has almost no interaction. If you don't win fast, you lose slow.

At Pro Tour Edge of Eternities (September 2025), Neoform placed multiple copies in the top 32, cementing itself as a legitimate tier archetype. Modern lists now run Planar Genesis as an instant-speed land-finding tool that also digs for the combo, and MDFCs like Disciple of Freyalise and Bridgeworks Battle double as both exile fodder for Rider's alternate cost and mana sources.

Oracle Accuracy — Critical Interactions

Allosaurus Rider — MV 7
You may exile two green cards from your hand rather than pay this spell's mana cost. Power and toughness each equal 1 plus the number of lands you control. The alternate cost does not change the mana value. Rider's MV is always 7. Neoform therefore finds exactly MV 8 (Ghalta or Griselbrand). Eldritch Evolution finds MV ≤ 9 (any target, including Atraxa at MV 7).
Neoform — Sorcery speed. Critical timing restriction.
"Search your library for a creature card with mana value equal to 1 plus the sacrificed creature's mana value, put that card onto the battlefield with an additional +1/+1 counter on it." Neoform is a sorcery — you can only cast it on your own main phase. There is no instant-speed combo line in this deck. Protect your window or lose it.
Ghalta, Stampede Tyrant — ETB from hand, not library
"When Ghalta enters, put any number of creature cards from your hand onto the battlefield." The creatures come from your hand, not your library. You must have Xenagos, God of Revels (or another haste enabler) in hand at the time Ghalta resolves for the kill to happen on the spot.
Xenagos, God of Revels — at the beginning of combat, not on ETB
"At the beginning of combat on your turn, another target creature you control gains haste and gets +X/+X until end of turn, where X is that creature's power." Xenagos triggers at the beginning of combat — the creature must already be in play when the combat step begins. Because Ghalta's ETB puts Xenagos directly onto the battlefield, Xenagos's combat trigger fires immediately (since you move to combat after your main phase). Ghalta (13/13 with Neoform's counter) becomes 26/26 hasty trampler. Swing for lethal.
Consign to Memory — counters colorless spells AND triggered abilities only
"Counter target triggered ability or colorless spell." Consign to Memory counters Summoner's Pact (MV 0, colorless), Pact of Negation (MV 0, colorless), and triggered abilities like Goblin Charbelcher's activation... wait — Charbelcher is an activated ability, not a triggered ability. Consign to Memory cannot counter activated abilities. It cannot counter Neoform (green spell) or Eldritch Evolution (green spell). Your opponents' Consign to Memory can counter your Summoner's Pact or Pact of Negation — plan around this.
Veil of Summer — protects everything, then draws
"Draw a card if an opponent has cast a blue or black spell this turn. Spells you control can't be countered this turn. You and permanents you control gain hexproof from blue and from black until end of turn." One green mana makes your entire combo uncounterable by blue interaction. It also blanks Thoughtseize (black). Draw only triggers if they've cast blue or black before you cast Veil.
Summoner's Pact and Pact of Negation — win or die next upkeep
Both Pacts have upkeep payment triggers. If you use Summoner's Pact to find Allosaurus Rider and don't win that turn, you pay {2}{G}{G} at your next upkeep or lose. If you use Pact of Negation to protect your combo and survive, you pay {3}{U}{U} next upkeep or lose. These are not optional — if you cast a Pact and don't win, you must have the mana. Do not Pact on turn one unless you're winning turn two.
Planar Genesis — instant-speed dig
"Look at the top four cards of your library. You may put a land card from among them onto the battlefield tapped. If you don't, put a card from among them into your hand. Put the rest on the bottom of your library in a random order." Instant speed. Finds Hedge Maze (Forest Island, enters tapped), Bridgeworks Battle, Garden of Freyalise, or digs for a combo piece. Use end-of-opponent's-turn to set up next turn's combo.
Disciple of Freyalise // Garden of Freyalise — MDFC exile fuel + ramp
The creature side (MV 6): when enters, may sacrifice another creature — gain X life + draw X cards where X = that creature's power. The land side: pay 3 life to enter untapped, {T}: Add {G}. Both sides count as a green card to exile for Allosaurus Rider's alternate cost. Disciple on the creature side is also a fantastic follow-up after a Ghalta that gets answered — sacrifice a leftover token or creature to draw 4–8 cards.

Chapter 03

Gameplan

Every game is a countdown. Know your fastest line, know your protection, know when to go.

The Kill Line — Ghalta Route (Turn 2)

Requirements: Allosaurus Rider + two other green cards in hand, Neoform or Eldritch Evolution, Xenagos, God of Revels in hand, two mana (one green, one blue for Neoform or {1}{G}{G} for Eldritch Evolution).

Step 1 — Deploy the Rider
Cast Allosaurus Rider by exiling two green cards from your hand. Rider enters as a creature whose P/T = 1 + lands you control. Typically a 2/2 or 3/3 at this point — doesn't matter. It's just the sacrifice target.
Step 2 — Neoform or Eldritch Evolution
Sacrifice Rider (MV 7) to Neoform ({G}{U}) — find MV 8 (Ghalta). Or sacrifice Rider (MV 7) to Eldritch Evolution ({1}{G}{G}) — find MV ≤ 9 (Ghalta, Griselbrand, or Atraxa). Ghalta enters with +1/+1 counter from Neoform (13/13).
Step 3 — Ghalta ETB
Ghalta's ETB triggers: "Put any number of creature cards from your hand onto the battlefield." Put Xenagos, God of Revels onto the battlefield. You now have both in play simultaneously.
Step 4 — Beginning of Combat Trigger
Declare attackers step has a preceding "beginning of combat" step. Xenagos triggers: target Ghalta. Ghalta gains haste and gets +13/+13 (its power = 13 after Neoform's counter). Ghalta attacks as a 26/26 trampler. Opponent at 20 life. Lethal damage.

The Griselbrand Route — When You Lack Xenagos

If Xenagos is stuck in your library, find Griselbrand instead. Griselbrand (7/7 flying lifelink) doesn't kill immediately but generates overwhelming advantage. Use Nourishing Shoal — exile a large green card from hand (like Generous Ent at MV 6 = gain 6 life), then activate Griselbrand's "Pay 7 life: draw 7" repeatedly. You will eventually find Xenagos or another route to victory. Run the Griselbrand line when your opening hand has the combo but no Xenagos — the draw-7 usually finds one.

The Atraxa Route — Through Eldritch Evolution

Eldritch Evolution finds MV ≤ 9 — so Atraxa, Grand Unifier (MV 7) is a valid target. Atraxa's ETB reveals the top 10 cards and lets you take one of each card type. This often finds an instant (Veil of Summer, Pact of Negation), a creature (Xenagos), a land, and more — effectively rebuilding your entire hand. Atraxa then serves as a 7/7 flying vigilance deathtouch lifelink threat that demands an immediate answer. Use this route when you need to rebuild rather than kill immediately.

The Ureni Route — Against Creature-Heavy Boards

Ureni, the Song Unending (MV 8, 10/10 flying, protection from white and black) ETB deals X damage split among creatures and planeswalkers your opponents control, where X = lands you control. By turn 3+ with Eldritch Evolution, this can sweep an entire board. Ureni's protection from white and black makes it resilient to Solitude and Fatal Push. This is the route against Boros Energy or Esper Blink when you can't race but can clear the board and stabilize.

When to Go Vs. When to Wait

Go Now — Conditions to Combo Immediately
• You have the full combo in hand (Rider + 2 green + Neoform + Xenagos or Griselbrand)
• Opponent is tapped out or at 1-2 mana
• You're on the play and they have no known interaction
• Their deck has minimal main-deck disruption (Affinity, Domain, Storm)
• Pact of Negation or Veil of Summer in hand to protect
Wait a Turn — Set Up with Planar Genesis
• Missing one piece (Xenagos not in hand, or only one green card to exile)
• Opponent has shown counterspell mana open
• You need a land drop to make the mana work
• Use Planar Genesis end-of-opponent's-turn to find the missing piece
• Summoner's Pact the missing green creature on your turn, then immediately combo

Mulligan Framework

This deck mulligans to the nuts. A seven-card hand that doesn't contain a kill line or a clear path to one by turn three is a mulligan. Keep hands that have: Allosaurus Rider (or Summoner's Pact to find it), at least two other green cards to exile, and Neoform or Eldritch Evolution. The mana requirement is low — you need one green and one blue (or two green and one generic) plus a land to fetch either color.

Paris mulligans to five are common. The deck is designed to win from five cards if they are the right five. Never keep a hand that is all protection and no combo. Protection is worthless without a kill.

Lines and Tricks

Summoner's Pact Timing
Cast Summoner's Pact on your main phase immediately before comboing. Find Allosaurus Rider. Now cast Neoform with your remaining mana. If you win (you should), the Pact upkeep never arrives. Only Pact if you're winning this turn or have the mana to pay next upkeep.
Veil of Summer Before Going Off
If opponent has blue mana up, cast Veil of Summer first. It costs only {G}. Veil makes all your spells uncounterable this turn. Then cast Neoform into open blue mana with confidence. Veil also draws if they cast blue/black before it resolves.
Pact of Negation as Combat Protection
After Ghalta resolves and you're in combat, your opponent may try to Solitude or Path to Exile Ghalta. Hold Pact of Negation until that removal targets Ghalta in combat. Counter it. You're winning this turn anyway — the upkeep cost is irrelevant.
Disciple of Freyalise as Post-Disruption Recovery
If your Ghalta resolves but then gets answered (Solitude, etc.), you may still have creatures in play from Ghalta's ETB. Cast Disciple of Freyalise on the creature side, sacrifice your largest creature (Hooting Mandrills at 4 power = gain 4, draw 4). Find another combo line.
Generous Ent Forestcycling
Forestcycling for {1} searches a Forest — this includes Hedge Maze (Forest Island) and Breeding Pool (Forest Shock). Use Ent forestcycle to find colored mana in the early game even when not going for the kill yet. The Ent itself is excellent exile fuel.
Hooting Mandrills — Surprise Second Sacrifice Target
If Allosaurus Rider is stuck in your library, Hooting Mandrills (MV 6, delve) can be cast cheaply using graveyard cards. Sacrifice Mandrills (MV 6) to Neoform → find MV 7 (Atraxa). Or to Eldritch Evolution → find MV ≤ 8 (Ghalta). This is the backup combo line when Rider isn't available.
Chapter 04

Core Engines

Engine 01 — The Ghalta Kill
Setup
Hand contains: Allosaurus Rider, two other green cards (Generous Ent, Disciple, Bridgeworks, Veil, etc.), Neoform or Eldritch Evolution, Xenagos, God of Revels, and enough mana for the evo spell. Summoner's Pact can replace Rider in hand if you have mana to Pact and immediately combo.
Execution
Exile two green cards → cast Allosaurus Rider for free. Cast Neoform ({G}{U}), sacrificing Rider (MV 7) → search library for MV 8 → put Ghalta, Stampede Tyrant onto battlefield with +1/+1 counter (now 13/13). Ghalta ETB fires: put Xenagos, God of Revels from hand onto battlefield. Move to combat — Xenagos's beginning-of-combat trigger targets Ghalta → Ghalta gains haste and gets +13/+13.
Result
26/26 hasty trampling Ghalta attacks for lethal damage from 20 life. Game ends turn two (on the play) or turn three (on the draw). No spell your opponent casts at this point matters unless it targets Ghalta or gains life above 26.
Engine 02 — The Griselbrand Draw Engine
Setup
Hand has the Allosaurus Rider combo but lacks Xenagos. Or: you need to find a protection piece before going for Ghalta. Griselbrand is the flexible finisher for hands that have combo mana but an incomplete payload.
Execution
Neoform + Rider (MV 7) → Griselbrand (MV 8). Griselbrand enters as a 7/7 flying lifelinker. Pay 7 life → draw 7 cards. Cast Nourishing Shoal from hand by exiling Generous Ent (MV 6 = gain 6 life) or another large green card. Regain life. Pay 7 again → draw 7. Repeat until you find Xenagos (put him into play via another Ghalta, or just attack with Griselbrand repeatedly).
Result
Drawing through the deck finds all remaining Pacts for protection, extra Veil of Summer, and eventually Xenagos or another kill. Griselbrand also attacks for 7 lifelink — opponent at 20 life takes three hits minimum, but you'll usually find a faster close in the drawn cards.
Engine 03 — Planar Genesis + Pact Setup
Setup
End of opponent's turn 1 or 2 with {G}{U} available. You have most of the combo but are missing one piece — a land, a green card to exile, or a key creature. Instead of forcing a bad combo attempt, you set up.
Execution
Cast Planar Genesis at end of opponent's turn. Look at top 4 — if you see a land (Hedge Maze, Breeding Pool, Disciple of Freyalise land side), put it onto the battlefield tapped. Otherwise, take the best combo piece into hand. On your next main phase: cast Summoner's Pact to find Allosaurus Rider, then immediately combo with Neoform or Eldritch Evolution.
Result
You convert a "missing one piece" hand into a reliable turn-3 kill while denying your opponent the chance to set up interaction on their turn 2. The extra land from Planar Genesis also increases Allosaurus Rider's P/T — at 3 lands, Rider is a 4/4, making Neoform find MV 8 the same as always, but Eldritch Evolution's math doesn't change.

The T2 Kill Line — Step by Step

Step 1 — Free Creature
Allosaurus Rider for {0}
Exile two green cards from hand instead of paying {5}{G}{G}. Rider enters as a 1+*/1+* (1 + lands you control). On T1 with 1 land: it's a 2/2. The exile is an ALTERNATIVE COST, not an additional cost — you choose to pay either {5}{G}{G} or exile 2 green cards. You can exile ANY green cards: lands (if they're green, e.g. Dryad Arbor), creatures, instants. The cards go to exile, not GY.
Step 2 — Sacrifice + Tutor
Neoform Rider into Craterhoof
Neoform ({G}{U} sorcery): sacrifice Rider (MV 7) → search for creature MV 8 with +1/+1 counter. Eldritch Evolution ({1}{G}{G} sorcery): sacrifice Rider (MV 7) → search for creature MV ≤ 9. Both find Craterhoof Behemoth (MV 8). Neoform gives Craterhoof an extra +1/+1 counter (6/6 base). Evolution exiles itself after resolving.
Step 3 — Kill
Craterhoof = Lethal
Craterhoof enters: "creatures you control gain trample and get +X/+X where X = creatures you control." With just Craterhoof in play: X=1 → it's a 6/6 haste trample (or 7/7 with Neoform counter). That's not lethal alone — you need another creature. With a mana dork surviving: X=2 → Craterhoof is 7/7, dork is 2+/2+ → lethal if opponent is at 20. Multiple dorks = overkill.

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

Allosaurus Rider — Exile Cost Is Alternative, Not Additional
"You may exile two green cards from your hand rather than pay this spell's mana cost." This is an ALTERNATIVE cost. You choose: pay {5}{G}{G} OR exile 2 green cards. You cannot do both. If you choose the exile route and it's countered: the 2 green cards are exiled permanently (they don't return). The cards must be green — check the color indicator or color identity. Lands are colorless unless they have a color indicator (Dryad Arbor is green). Manamorphose is red AND green — it can be exiled.
Neoform vs Eldritch Evolution — Key Differences
Neoform ({G}{U}): finds MV EQUAL to sacrificed +1. Rider (MV 7) → must find exactly MV 8. Puts a +1/+1 counter on the creature. Does NOT exile itself — goes to GY. Evolution ({1}{G}{G}): finds MV LESS THAN OR EQUAL TO sacrificed +2. Rider (MV 7) → can find MV ≤ 9. No counter. EXILES ITSELF — goes to exile, not GY. Both: sacrifice is additional cost — if countered, you still lose the creature. Don't cast into open Counterspell mana.
Summoner's Pact — Lose the Game on Upkeep
{0} instant: search for a green creature, put in hand. At beginning of your NEXT upkeep: pay {2}{G}{G} or lose. The upkeep trigger goes on the stack — it CAN be responded to. If you combo off before your next upkeep (T2 kill): you never pay and never lose. If you use Pact and DON'T combo: you need {2}{G}{G} next upkeep or you're dead. Pact of Negation works the same way: {0} counter, pay {3}{U}{U} next upkeep or lose. Both Pacts are free protection during your combo turn.
Chancellor of the Tangle — Reveal from Opening Hand
"You may reveal this card from your opening hand. If you do, at the beginning of your first main phase, add {G}." The {G} is added at first MAIN PHASE, not upkeep. It doesn't use the stack — it just happens. This free {G} enables T1 plays that normally require 2 mana (like a land + Chancellor → {G}{G} for a 2-drop mana creature). Chancellor is also a 6/7 vigilance reach creature that can be hard-cast as a backup plan, but at {4}{G}{G}{G} that's almost never the line.
Simian Spirit Guide — Exile from Hand for {R}
"Exile this card from your hand: Add {R}." This is a mana ability — it doesn't use the stack and can't be responded to. Provides {R} for Manamorphose, Wild Cantor, or Grinning Ignus. The exile is permanent — the card goes to exile, not GY. In post-board games, Simian can be hard-cast as a 2/2 for {2}{R} in emergencies (almost never correct).
Manamorphose — Free Cantrip That Fixes Colors
{1}{R/G} instant: add 2 mana of any color combination, draw a card. Net mana: +0 (you spent 2, you get 2 back). Net cards: +0 (you spent Manamorphose, you drew 1). But it fixes your colors AND triggers Birgi ({R} on cast). With Birgi: Manamorphose becomes mana-positive ({1}{R/G} cast → Birgi gives {R} → Manamorphose gives 2 mana → net +1 mana and a card drawn). This is the combo fuel that chains spells together.
Grinning Ignus + Birgi — Infinite Mana and Storm
Grinning Ignus ({2}{R}): "{R}, return to hand: add {C}{C}{R}. Activate only as sorcery." Birgi: "whenever you cast a spell, add {R}." Loop: cast Ignus for {2}{R} → Birgi triggers (add {R}) → you have {R}. Activate Ignus: return to hand, add {C}{C}{R}. Pool: {C}{C}{R}{R} → recast Ignus for {2}{R} using {C}{C}{R} → Birgi triggers again → net +{R} each loop. Infinite mana, infinite storm count. Win with Walking Ballista (X = infinite) or Craterhoof.
Eldritch Evolution Exiles Itself — Can't Be Recurred
"Exile Eldritch Evolution." After resolving, it goes to exile permanently. Can't be Snapcastered, can't be E-Witnessed, doesn't fuel delve. The sacrifice is ADDITIONAL cost — if it's countered, you lost the creature AND the spell. Against open counter mana: Neoform is safer (doesn't exile itself, costs less). Evolution is better when you need the wider search range (MV+2 vs MV+1 exact).
Wild Cantor — Sacrifice for Any Color
{R/G} 1/1 Human Druid. "Sacrifice: add one mana of any color." The sacrifice is a mana ability — doesn't use the stack, can't be responded to. Cantor does double duty: it's a creature to sacrifice to Neoform/Evolution AND a mana source. Neoform sacrificing Cantor (MV 1) finds MV 2 creatures. Evolution sacrificing Cantor finds MV ≤ 3 creatures. Neither finds Craterhoof from Cantor alone — you need Rider.
Chapter 05

Roles

Neoform has one role in game one and three roles post-board. Know which game you're in.
Pure Aggressor
Game 1 vs. everything. Your job is to kill faster than they can react. You are not a control deck. You are not an interactive deck. You have one goal: find the combo, protect it with one piece, resolve a lethal attacker by turn two or three. Play your fastest line. Do not slow down to interact unless you have Consign to Memory up against an Eldrazi activation.
Protected Aggressor
Game 1 vs. known counters / Game 2-3 vs. blue decks. You know they have interaction — Solitude, Force of Negation, Thoughtseize. Wait one extra turn to hold Veil of Summer or Pact of Negation alongside the combo. The protected aggressor accepts a slightly slower kill (T3 instead of T2) in exchange for immunity to one piece of disruption.
Bait-and-Switch Aggressor
Games 2-3 vs. heavy disruption. Against decks loaded with Thoughtseize, Consign, and counterspells, you play Hexdrinker on T1 as a legitimate threat that levels up to protection from everything. Force them to answer the snake. While they do, find the combo in the background. Post-board Koma (can't be countered) is a similar trump card.

Positional Awareness

Neoform is almost never the control player. The one exception is against other all-in combo decks (Tameshi Belcher, Ruby Storm) where you are coincidentally faster — in those matchups you are both aggressors, and the first one to execute wins. Against those decks, play Consign to Memory only if you believe your combo is faster and you need to stop their kill before you set up yours.

Against midrange and control (Boros Energy, Esper Blink, Dimir), do not attempt to out-attrition them. You will lose that game. Your only game is the speed game. If they slow you down enough that you can't kill them by turn four, reassess whether you have an alternate Hexdrinker plan to fall back on post-board.

Chapter 06

Matchup Deep Dives

You don't win matchups. You win turns. Know what turn they can beat you, and kill them before it.

Boros Energy — 15.8% meta · Unfavorable

Boros Energy is your hardest non-blue matchup. They kill faster than you expect: Ocelot Pride on turn one can produce a lethal board by turn four, and Goblin Bombardment turns any token into a sacrifice damage outlet that bypasses combat entirely. Their main deck has no hard disruption (no Thoughtseize, no counterspells), so game one is winnable — you simply need to execute before they've assembled enough bodies to race your 26/26 trampler. The critical problem arrives post-board: they bring in Clarion Conqueror, which shuts off all activated abilities of artifacts, creatures, and planeswalkers. Note: Xenagos's trigger is a triggered ability (fires at the beginning of combat), not an activated ability. Clarion does NOT stop Xenagos from giving Ghalta haste and +13/+13. However, Clarion does stop Griselbrand's "pay 7 life: draw 7" activated ability entirely. Plan accordingly — if you see Clarion post-board, go for the Ghalta line, not Griselbrand.

OutIn
−2 Pact of Negation+2 Into the Flood Maw
−1 Nourishing Shoal+1 Dismember
−1 Griselbrand+1 Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite

Post-board plan: Pact of Negation is bad (they don't counter). Griselbrand is bad (Clarion shuts it). Into the Flood Maw buys a tempo turn vs their aggression. Elesh Norn via Eldritch Evolution wipes their entire board (-2/-2 to all opponent creatures kills most of their 1/1 and 2/2 tokens). Dismember cleans up a Ragavan or Ocelot Pride that slips through.

Key needle: Clarion Conqueror stops Griselbrand's draw ability but NOT Xenagos's combat trigger. If they resolve Clarion, switch to the Ghalta+Xenagos line exclusively.

Tameshi Belcher — 11.1% meta · Favorable

A pure speed race. Tameshi Belcher typically wins on turn four with a Whir of Invention into Lotus Bloom into Charbelcher activation. You win on turn two or three. Game one is heavily in your favor — they have almost no interaction (their disruption is the Disrupting Shoal / counterspell suite, but those cost mana they'd rather spend on setup). The key oracle note: Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities, NOT activated abilities. Goblin Charbelcher's win condition is an activated ability — Consign cannot counter it directly. However, Consign CAN counter Tameshi's ETB trigger (when Lotus Bloom enters from the graveyard via Tameshi's ability, that's a triggered ability). Use Consign to disrupt their engine, not their final activation.

OutIn
−1 Disciple of Freyalise+1 Pact of Negation
−1 Nourishing Shoal+1 Nature's Claim

Nature's Claim destroys Goblin Charbelcher (artifact) if you somehow need to interact — but mostly stay on plan and kill them first. Extra Pact protects your combo if they're on a counter package.

Key needle: You are faster. Kill them. Don't over-sideboard against a deck you beat by going under.

Eldrazi Tron — 10.5% meta · Even

Eldrazi Tron's danger comes from two cards: Chalice of the Void (at 2 counters, blanks both Neoform and Eldritch Evolution) and Thought-Knot Seer (MV 4, exiles a card from your hand on ETB). A turn-two Thought-Knot taking your only Neoform or Evolution can strand you completely. The good news: they're mostly colorless, their disruption is slow, and you can frequently combo before they set up. Karn, the Great Creator's static ability doesn't directly interact with your combo — it shuts opponent's activated abilities on artifacts, but you have none. Race before Tron assembles or Chalice lands.

OutIn
−1 Veil of Summer+2 Nature's Claim
−1 Nourishing Shoal+1 Endurance

Nature's Claim destroys Chalice of the Void for {G} (they gain 4 — irrelevant). Veil of Summer is weak here since they're colorless — it won't draw a card and hexproof from blue/black doesn't help vs Thought-Knot. Endurance can be cast via Eldritch Evolution as an alternate threat (flash 3/4, though at MV 3 it's Rider → Evo → find ≤5, not standard line).

Key needle: If they Chalice for 2, your only evo spell left is Eldritch Evolution (MV 3, not hit). Keep at least one Evolution in your opening hand if you see Chalice game 2.

Esper Goryo's — 5.7% meta · Favorable

Goryo's Vengeance requires Atraxa in the graveyard and a reanimation spell — typically a turn-three or turn-four kill. You're killing on turn two or three. Game one is a comfortable speed advantage. The matchup tightens post-board when they bring in Thoughtseize and Consign to Memory. Consign to Memory can counter your Summoner's Pact upkeep trigger (the "pay {2}{G}{G} or lose" is a triggered ability) — if they counter it, you lose on your next upkeep. Veil of Summer pre-emptively blanks their entire post-board package.

OutIn
−2 Generous Ent+4 Mystical Dispute
−1 Nourishing Shoal+1 Pact of Negation
−2 Planar Genesis

Mystical Dispute counters Goryo's Vengeance, Ephemerate, Force of Negation for {U}. Veil of Summer stays in — best card post-board vs their black/blue disruption package.

Key needle: Consign to Memory counters your Summoner's Pact upkeep trigger. If you Pact and they have {U} open, protect with Veil of Summer before you enter your next upkeep.

Izzet Affinity — 5.7% meta · Favorable

Izzet Affinity goldfishes to around turn four with a large Kappa Cannoneer. They have Metallic Rebuke as soft disruption (counter unless pay {3}) and post-board Force of Negation and Blood Moon. Force of Negation cannot be cast on your turn for free — they must pay {1}{U}{U} to counter on your turn. Blood Moon converts your duals to Mountains — before comboing, fetch your basic Forest and Island so your colored mana is protected.

OutIn
−1 Nourishing Shoal+2 Nature's Claim
−1 Disciple of Freyalise+2 Mystical Dispute

Nature's Claim destroys Blood Moon (enchantment) or Mox Opal. Mystical Dispute counters Force of Negation for {U}. Stay on the fast combo plan.

Key needle: Fetch basics before comboing if you suspect Blood Moon. Basic Forest + basic Island protects your mana completely against Moon.

Esper Blink — 5.7% meta · Unfavorable

Your hardest post-board matchup. Thoughtseize strips combo pieces on turn one. Solitude (evoke: exile a white card) removes Allosaurus Rider from the battlefield for free the moment it resolves — before you can cast Neoform. Fatal Push kills Rider once it's in play. Consign to Memory can counter Ghalta's ETB trigger (a triggered ability) even if the Ghalta resolves. Every step of the combo is vulnerable to a different piece of disruption. Veil of Summer on your combo turn blanks Thoughtseize (black) and all blue counters simultaneously. This is the matchup where Veil is mandatory.

OutIn
−2 Generous Ent+4 Mystical Dispute
−1 Nourishing Shoal+2 Into the Flood Maw
−2 Planar Genesis+1 Pact of Negation
−2 Disciple of Freyalise+1 Hexdrinker
−1 Bridgeworks Battle

Full disruption package. Hexdrinker T1 demands an answer (Thoughtseize, Fatal Push), buying time for the combo. Into the Flood Maw bounces Overlord of the Balemurk or Phelia before they value-blink them. Mystical Dispute counters Force of Negation and Consign to Memory for {U}.

Key needle: Cast Veil of Summer before Summoner's Pact or Neoform on your combo turn. It blanks Thoughtseize AND all blue counterspells for {G}. The {G} cost is the cheapest insurance available.

Izzet Prowess — 5.2% meta · Favorable

Prowess runs zero main-deck disruption for your combo. They're a pure aggro deck — Dragon's Rage Channeler, Monastery Swiftspear, Mutagenic Growth. Their kill is turn three or four on a good draw. You kill on turn two or three. Clean game one. Post-board they bring Consign to Memory (counter your triggered abilities) and Into the Flood Maw (bounce Rider). Veil of Summer answers both for one mana. Lava Dart flashback (sacrifice a Mountain) deals 1 damage — Rider at 2 lands is a 3/3 and survives.

OutIn
−1 Nourishing Shoal+2 Mystical Dispute
−1 Generous Ent+1 Endurance

Light sideboard — you're favored. Mystical Dispute counters Consign and Into the Flood Maw. Endurance disrupts DRC delirium if needed.

Key needle: Lava Dart flashback deals 1 damage — can't kill Rider unless you're at 0 lands (Rider would be 1/1). Always fetch a land before comboing if you suspect Dart.

Domain Zoo — 4.3% meta · Favorable

Domain Zoo presses hard with Territorial Kavu, Doorkeeper Thrull, and Leyline of the Guildpact. Their disruption is mostly Stubborn Denial (ferocious counter — requires a 4+ power creature in play) and Lightning Bolt. Bolt deals 3 — Rider at 2 lands is 3/3, survives. Stubborn Denial without ferocious is just a Force Spike (pay {1} to fizzle). Check whether they have a 4+ power creature before deciding to protect. You are cleanly faster than their clock.

OutIn
−1 Nourishing Shoal+1 Dismember
−1 Consign to Memory+1 Endurance

Minimal sideboarding. Dismember handles a Territorial Kavu threatening to win before you combo. Consign is weak here — they don't have many relevant triggered abilities.

Key needle: Stubborn Denial with ferocious counters your Neoform for free. Check their board. If they have a 4-power creature, hold Veil of Summer or Pact of Negation as protection before comboing.

Ruby Storm — 3.9% meta · Even

Pure race. Ruby Storm wins turn three or four via Desperate Ritual + Pyretic Ritual chains into Grapeshot or Empty the Warrens. You win turn two or three. On the play you're faster. On the draw it's genuinely even. Consign to Memory can counter Grapeshot's storm copies (which are triggered abilities placed onto the stack), but by the time you're countering storm copies you've already lost the race. The correct approach: kill them before they reach critical mass. Mystical Dispute is weak here — Ruby Storm runs almost zero blue spells. Endurance disrupts Past in Flames (exiles their graveyard). Veil of Summer draws a card if they've looted with Faithless Looting (red) — the draw trigger only fires vs blue or black spells.

OutIn
−2 Generous Ent+1 Endurance
−1 Nourishing Shoal+1 Pact of Negation
−1 Consign to Memory+1 Koma, World-Eater

Endurance exiles their graveyard at instant speed, blanking Past in Flames. Koma (this spell can't be countered, ward {4}) is an uncounterable mid-game threat if needed. Keep Mystical Dispute in the board — it's almost dead here (Ruby Storm is nearly monored).

Key needle: On the play, combo turn two without hesitation. On the draw, add one piece of protection (Pact or Veil) and kill turn three. Do not try to out-grind a deck that goes off explosively.

Amulet Titan — 2.7% meta · Favorable

Amulet Titan typically wins on turn three or four by chaining bounce lands with Amulet of Vigor into Primeval Titan finding Valakut. You kill on turn two or three. Game one is a clean speed advantage. They have almost no main-deck disruption for your combo — Force of Vigor destroys artifacts/enchantments (irrelevant vs you), Summoner's Pact finds green creatures (their tool), Defense Grid makes your opponents' spells cost {3} more but does nothing for them against you. If the game goes long, Ureni, the Song Unending (MV 8 via Eldritch Evolution) enters and deals X damage to all their creatures and planeswalkers where X = your land count, sweeping their battlefield.

OutIn
−1 Nourishing Shoal+1 Koma, World-Eater

Near-bye. Stay on combo plan. Koma as a backup uncounterable threat if the game drags. Don't sideboard heavily against a deck you outrace.

Key needle: Ureni (MV 8 via Eldritch Evolution — Rider MV 7 → find ≤ 9) deals X damage to all their creatures where X = lands you control. At 5 lands that's 5 damage, killing Primeval Titan (6/6 — barely not enough). More useful as a 10/10 flying blocker with protection from white and black.

Dimir Frog / Control — 2.3% meta · Unfavorable

Your hardest matchup. Psychic Frog discards your hand to pump itself while applying clock pressure. Thoughtseize strips combo pieces. Counterspell and Consign to Memory backstop everything. Murktide Regent threatens to close the game as a large flying threat before you reassemble. Post-board they add more interaction and you run out of combo pieces. The only winning line is Veil of Summer → combo on the same turn, or Hexdrinker leveling up to protection from everything while you find the combo in the background. Patience and sequencing are everything — do not tap out for Planar Genesis when Thoughtseize mana is up.

OutIn
−2 Generous Ent+4 Mystical Dispute
−1 Nourishing Shoal+2 Into the Flood Maw
−2 Planar Genesis+1 Pact of Negation
−2 Bridgeworks Battle+1 Hexdrinker
−1 Disciple of Freyalise

Mystical Dispute counters Counterspell, Force of Negation, Consign to Memory for {U}. Into the Flood Maw bounces Murktide or Psychic Frog. Hexdrinker is a sustained threat that eventually reaches protection from everything (level 8) — requires 8 mana total across multiple turns, but buys time.

Key needle: Cast Veil of Summer on your combo turn if they've cast any blue or black spell that turn. It makes your entire combo sequence uncounterable and draws if they used black (Thoughtseize) or blue (any counter). This is frequently the only way through their disruption.

Sideboard Guide

You have 15 cards to solve one problem: they know what you're doing. Make them wrong anyway.

Sideboard Card-by-Card

Mystical Dispute ×4
Your best post-board card vs blue decks. Counters any blue spell for {U} (or {1}{U} if they don't control an Island). Hits Force of Negation, Consign to Memory, Counterspell, Goryo's Vengeance, Ephemerate. Comes in vs Esper Blink, Dimir Frog, Esper Goryo's, and any blue-heavy deck. Stays out vs Boros Energy, Eldrazi Tron, Ruby Storm, Domain Zoo.
Into the Flood Maw ×2
Instant-speed bounce for a creature an opponent controls. Without gift: return target creature to hand. With gift (promise an opponent a 1/1 Fish): return any nonland permanent. Use to bounce a Murktide Regent, Psychic Frog, Solitude, or an aggressive creature attacking you before you can combo. Gives you one extra turn.
Nature's Claim ×2
Destroy target artifact or enchantment — opponent gains 4 life. One green mana. Destroys Chalice of the Void (the card that hard-stops Neoform at 2 counters), Blood Moon (prevents you making blue), Leyline of the Void (hates your GY-based lines), Stony Silence (shuts nothing relevant to you — but you might face it from opponent's board). Comes in vs Eldrazi Tron (Chalice), Izzet Affinity (Blood Moon), any artifact/enchantment disruption.
Pact of Negation ×1
A fourth copy of Pact for matchups where you need maximum counter protection. Counters any spell for free — pay {3}{U}{U} next upkeep or lose. Use only on the turn you're killing them or you'll lose to the trigger. Comes in vs heavy counter decks (Dimir, Esper) or matchups where you need four pieces of protection.
Endurance ×1
Flash 3/4 for {2}{G}. ETB: exile all cards from target player's GY, shuffle them into their library. Stops Past in Flames (Ruby Storm), disrupts Goryo's Vengeance setup (GY-dependent), and is a flash creature that can block. Also a valid Eldritch Evolution target — sacrifice a MV 1 creature to find MV ≤ 3 creatures including Endurance. Can't find Rider (MV 7) so doesn't fit into the main combo line.
Dismember ×1
Instant, {1}{B/P}{B/P} — pay 4 life and no mana, or {3}{B}{B}. Target creature gets -5/-5 until EOT. Kills Ragavan (2/1), Murktide Regent (before triggers), Psychic Frog, almost any threat. Colorless for payment purposes — you can cast it off a Forest or Island. Comes in vs Boros Energy, Dimir, Prowess when you need a removal answer.
Hooting Mandrills ×2
MV 6, 4/4 trample, delve. Alternate sacrifice target for Neoform (finds MV 7 — Atraxa) or Eldritch Evolution (finds MV ≤ 8 — Ghalta). Comes in when Allosaurus Rider is being heavily disrupted (Thoughtseize strips, GY exile, etc.) and you need a second reliable sacrifice target. Delve makes them cheap once you've cycled or discarded.
Hexdrinker ×1
MV 1, 2/1 snake. Level up {1}: Level 3–7: 4/4, protection from instants. Level 8+: 6/6, protection from everything. A legitimate alternate threat that doesn't care about Thoughtseize (it's already in play), Counterspell (already resolved), or removal at level 8. Costs 8 total mana to max (1 + 7 more level counters) but can be done across multiple turns. Buys time and forces answers.
Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite ×1
MV 7, 4/7 vigilance. Other creatures you control get +2/+2. Opponent creatures get -2/-2. Via Eldritch Evolution: sacrifice Allosaurus Rider (MV 7) → find MV ≤ 9 → Elesh Norn. On ETB, all opponent creatures with 2 or fewer toughness die instantly (kills most Boros Energy token board: 1/1 Cats, Voice of Victory 2/2). Alternative to Ghalta when you need a board wipe instead of a single attacker.
Koma, World-Eater ×1
MV 7, 8/12, trample, ward {4}. "This spell can't be countered." Via Eldritch Evolution: sacrifice Rider (MV 7) → find MV ≤ 9 → Koma. Koma is uncounterable — Consign to Memory, Force of Negation, and Counterspell all fail. Ward {4} makes it expensive to target after. Generates 3/3 Serpent tokens on combat damage. The go-to target when you know they're holding counterspells and Ghalta would be countered.

What Comes Out First

Always Cut vs Non-Blue Decks
Consign to Memory (1–2)
Consign counters triggered abilities and colorless spells. Against non-blue decks without triggered ability chains (Boros Energy, Domain Zoo, Ruby Storm), Consign is mostly dead. Cut 1–2 copies first.
Cut vs Non-Graveyard Decks
Nourishing Shoal (1)
Shoal's purpose is to regain life after Griselbrand activations. Against decks where you're not running the Griselbrand line (most post-board games where Griselbrand is too slow), Shoal is dead. Always the first cut.
Cut vs Interactive Decks
Planar Genesis / Generous Ent
Against heavy interaction, these slow pieces (setup tools that take a turn) become liabilities. Cut them for hard counterspells and threats. Against goldfish matchups keep them — they're your best setup tools.
Chapter 08

Heuristics

Sixteen rules that survive the pressure of the tournament table.
  1. 01
    Neoform is a Sorcery. Period. You cannot cascade into it. You cannot cast it at instant speed. Your combo window is your main phase. Protect that window before you open it.
  2. 02
    Veil of Summer costs {G}. Cast it before you cast anything else on your combo turn. If they've cast a blue or black spell, you draw a card too. If they haven't, it still makes everything uncounterable. One mana for total combo protection is always correct when you suspect interaction.
  3. 03
    Never Pact unless you're winning that turn or you have the mana. Summoner's Pact: pay {2}{G}{G} next upkeep or lose. Pact of Negation: pay {3}{U}{U} next upkeep or lose. Pacting into a turn-three kill when you can't pay on upkeep is a death sentence. Calculate your mana before activating.
  4. 04
    Xenagos must be in hand when Ghalta resolves. Ghalta's ETB puts creature cards from your hand onto the battlefield — not from your library. If Xenagos is in your library, find Griselbrand instead and draw into Xenagos or a second kill line.
  5. 05
    Allosaurus Rider's MV is always 7, regardless of how you paid. Alternate costs don't change mana value. Neoform finds exactly MV 8 (Ghalta or Griselbrand). Eldritch Evolution finds MV ≤ 9. Know the math before you sacrifice.
  6. 06
    Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities and colorless spells only. It cannot counter Neoform (green), Eldritch Evolution (green), or Allosaurus Rider (green). It CAN counter your Summoner's Pact upkeep trigger, Ghalta's ETB trigger, and any colorless Pact. Know what your opponent's Consign can and cannot do to you.
  7. 07
    Fetch your basics before comboing if Blood Moon is a possibility. Basic Forest gives you {G}. Basic Island gives you {U}. Once basics are in play, Blood Moon only hurts your Breeding Pool and Hedge Maze — your colored sources are safe. One fetch decision can protect your entire mana base.
  8. 08
    Clarion Conqueror does NOT stop Xenagos. Xenagos's combat trigger is a triggered ability, not an activated ability. Clarion only shuts activated abilities of artifacts, creatures, and planeswalkers. Clarion DOES stop Griselbrand's "pay 7 life: draw 7" activated ability. If they resolve Clarion, switch to the Ghalta+Xenagos line.
  9. 09
    Hooting Mandrills is your backup sacrifice target. Sacrifice Mandrills (MV 6) to Neoform → finds MV 7 (Atraxa). To Eldritch Evolution → finds MV ≤ 8 (Ghalta, Griselbrand). Use when Allosaurus Rider is disrupted, exiled, or stuck in your library. Delve makes Mandrills cheap — cast it for {G} or even free if you have 5 graveyard cards.
  10. 10
    Generous Ent's Forestcycling finds Hedge Maze. Hedge Maze is a Forest Island — it's a Forest. Forestcycling for {1} searches it out. Use this to fix your blue mana early when you have {G} available but no blue source yet.
  11. 11
    Against combo races, kill first — don't interact. Against Ruby Storm and Tameshi Belcher, your Consign to Memory is mostly irrelevant (Belcher's activation is activated, not triggered; Storm copies are triggered but you'd rather be killing). Play your fastest combo line. The deck that goes first wins.
  12. 12
    Disciple of Freyalise draws X cards where X = sacrificed creature's power. After a Ghalta that resolves but gets answered, you may have Hooting Mandrills (4 power) or another creature in play. Sacrifice it to Disciple: gain 4, draw 4. This refuels immediately and is often enough to find a second combo attempt.
  13. 13
    Planar Genesis at end of opponent's turn is your best setup action. It's instant speed — use it EOT to set up next turn's combo. Finding a Hedge Maze (enters tapped but still counts as a land for Rider's P/T tomorrow) or a key creature into hand is often better than any main-phase action.
  14. 14
    Against Eldrazi Tron: Chalice at 2 blanks Neoform and Eldritch Evolution. Eldritch Evolution (MV 3) is not hit by Chalice at 2. Wait — Chalice at 2 counters spells with MV exactly 2. Neoform is MV 2 (hit). Eldritch Evolution is MV 3 (not hit). If Chalice resolves at 2, switch entirely to Eldritch Evolution. Keep at least one in hand from game 2 onward.
  15. 15
    Koma, World-Eater resolves through counterspells. "This spell can't be countered." If they're holding Consign to Memory or Force of Negation, find Koma via Eldritch Evolution instead of Ghalta. They cannot counter Koma. Ward {4} protects it post-resolution. This is the correct call when you've seen multiple counterspells.
  16. 16
    Mulligan to the combo, not to protection. A hand with four pieces of protection and no combo wins zero games. A hand with Allosaurus Rider, two green exiles, and Neoform wins most games. Protection is the secondary consideration — find the kill first, then assess whether it needs armor.
Chapter 09

Tuning Notes

The decklist is a starting point. The meta is what determines the final 75.
If Blue Decks Are Prevalent
+1 Veil of Summer MD
A third main-deck Veil of Summer (or fourth) is justified when Dimir Frog, Esper Blink, and Esper Goryo's represent 15%+ of the field. One green mana making your combo uncounterable is too good to leave in the sideboard when blue is everywhere. Trim the 3rd Generous Ent or the Nourishing Shoal.
If Eldrazi Tron Is Dominant
+2 Nature's Claim MD
Chalice of the Void at 2 is your hardest non-blue hate card. If Eldrazi Tron makes up 12%+ of the field, consider moving 1–2 Nature's Claim main deck. Trim the Nourishing Shoal (already a 1-of) and possibly one Consign to Memory (weak vs colorless).
Speed vs. Resilience Dial
Pact Count (3→4)
Main-deck Pact of Negation count is a speed vs. resilience decision. Three Pacts means faster combo setup (less dead weight). Four Pacts means more protection in interactive fields. A field of fast aggro decks (Prowess, Domain) argues for 3. A field of blue midrange argues for 4.
Target Diversification
Ghalta Count (2→3)
Running 3 Ghalta instead of 2 increases the probability of finding her via Neoform when you're blind-searching. Consider if you're finding yourself frequently on the Griselbrand line and prefer the Ghalta kill. The tradeoff is one fewer exile fodder card (typically cut the 3rd Generous Ent).
Hooting Mandrills Main
1→2 If Rider Is Targeted
If opponents are sideboarding Thoughtseize and hand disruption heavily (Esper Blink, Dimir Frog meta), a second main-deck Hooting Mandrills gives you a second, cheaper sacrifice target. Mandrills (delve, often free) is harder to strip from your hand than Rider because it costs less and can be cast at near-zero mana cost.
Sideboard Flex Slots
Hexdrinker + Koma Count
The 1-of Hexdrinker and 1-of Koma occupy the "alternate threat" flex slots. In a field without heavy disruption, these are cuttable for a 4th Mystical Dispute, a 2nd Endurance, or a 3rd Nature's Claim. Adjust to what your local meta punishes hardest.
Chapter 10

Tournament Prep Sheet

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand with Allosaurus Rider + a green card to pitch, or Neoform + a 1-drop creature.
  • Allosaurus rule: Allosaurus Rider's alternative cost: exile 2 green cards from hand. Must be green card types, not just green mana.
  • Neoform rule: Neoform sacrifices a creature and finds one with exactly 1 more MV. Sacrifice a 1-drop: find a 2-drop (Clever Lumimancer).
  • Kill line rule: T2 kill: T1 dork, T2 Neoform sacrifice dork → Lumimancer, then pitch Allosaurus for free → Neoform Lumimancer → Ghalta.
  • Pact rule: Cast Summoner's Pact only when you win before your next upkeep OR you have {2}{G}{G} available.
  • Sideboard rule: Consign counters cascade triggers. Know your counter targets for each matchup before sitting down.

Testing Framework

Block 01
T2 Kill Line
Execute the T2 Ghalta line at tournament speed. Allosaurus pitch → Neoform chain → kill.
Block 02
Consign Timing
Drill holding Consign for opponent's disruption. Know which spell to counter.
Block 03
Pact Math
Practice knowing exactly when you can and cannot cast Summoner's Pact safely.
Block 04
Resilience Lines
Practice what you do after T1 interaction hits your setup. The deck has multiple lines.

Matchday Routine

  • Register with Consign confirmed. Counter the disruption, execute the combo.
  • Know your kill line before Round 1. T2 or T3? Which route based on your hand?
  • Between rounds: Pact timing review. Can you pay {2}{G}{G} next upkeep? If unsure, don't cast it.
  • Don't cast Pact as a desperation play. Only cast when you know the outcome.
  • The deck is fast. Trust the speed and don't slow down to play around disruption you haven't confirmed.
