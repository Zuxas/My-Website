Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"You strip their hand, remove their threats one-for-one, and deploy threats that generate more value than they can answer. Sheoldred drains them every draw step. Fable generates two bodies. You never run out of gas."

Rakdos Boomer Midrange is the most traditional fair deck in Pioneer. The plan never changes: disrupt with Thoughtseize and Fatal Push, generate value with Fable of the Mirror-Breaker and Bloodtithe Harvester, and close with Sheoldred, the Apocalypse and Graveyard Trespasser. The deck is called "Boomer" because it plays nothing exotic — no combo, no GY synergy beyond Trespasser — just efficient black-red interaction cards that have been proven format-defining threats for years.

The deck's strength is its density of individually powerful cards. Thoughtseize on T1 gives you perfect information and strips the most dangerous card from their hand. Fatal Push kills virtually any creature in the format for {B}. Fable of the Mirror-Breaker creates a 2/2 Goblin (that makes Treasure), loots away bad cards, then transforms into Reflection of Kiki-Jiki — copying your best creature every turn with haste. Sheoldred, the Apocalypse is the deck's premier closer: a 4/5 deathtouch that makes opponents lose 2 life each time they draw a card. Against a deck drawing 2-3 cards per turn, Sheoldred drains 4-6 life per turn and draws them into death.

Why Register Rakdos Boomer

Inevitability
Sheoldred Ends Every Long Game
Sheoldred, the Apocalypse deals 2 damage whenever any player draws a card (except the first each turn). In a format where control decks draw 3-4 cards per turn via Teferi and Consult the Star Charts, Sheoldred deals 6-8 damage per turn passively. Removing her costs a removal spell + 2 more life taken to the face first. Opponents who let Sheoldred live die to their own draw steps.
Disruption
Thoughtseize + Perfect Information
A T1 Thoughtseize reveals your opponent's entire hand, lets you strip the most dangerous card, and gives you perfect sequencing knowledge for the next 3-4 turns. Against combo (Greasefang, Lotus Field), strip the key piece. Against control, strip Teferi or their sweeper. Against aggro, strip Eidolon or their combat trick. The information gain is as valuable as the card stripped.
Value Engine
Fable Generates Two Bodies + Loot
Fable of the Mirror-Breaker: Chapter 1 creates a 2/2 Goblin that produces Treasure tokens. Chapter 2: loot (discard and draw). Chapter 3: transforms into Reflection of Kiki-Jiki — copies any nonlegendary creature you control with haste. Flipping Fable with Sheoldred in play creates a haste 4/5 deathtouch that immediately activates Sheoldred's drain. One Fable flip can swing the game entirely.

Key Card Notes

The Engine
Fable of the Mirror-Breaker
Chapter 1: 2/2 Goblin Shaman that makes a Treasure when it attacks. Chapter 2: discard and draw a card. Chapter 3: transform into Reflection of Kiki-Jiki — whenever a nonlegendary creature enters under your control, create a haste token copy exiled at EOT. Reflection copying Sheoldred = free 4/5 deathtouch haste that drains 2 per opponent draw. Reflection copying Bloodtithe Harvester = free removal + Blood token. Fable is the deck's most important card to protect.
The Closer
Sheoldred, the Apocalypse
4/5 deathtouch for {2BB}. You gain 2 life when YOU draw a card. Opponent loses 2 life when THEY draw a card. Against a control opponent drawing via Consult the Star Charts and Memory Deluge, Sheoldred drains 6-10 life per turn passively. Against any opponent, Sheoldred converts their draw steps into life loss. Deathtouch means any blocker dies to Sheoldred in combat. It wins long games by existing.
The GY Hate
Graveyard Trespasser
3/3 for {2B} with ward (opponent must discard a card to target it). On entry and on each attack: exile up to one card from a target player's GY, and if a creature card was exiled, drain 1 life. Shuts off Arclight Phoenix and Greasefang GY engines while draining life. Ward forces opponents to spend a card to remove it — making it both a value engine and a removal-resistant threat. The drain stacks quickly against GY-heavy decks.
The Removal
Fatal Push + Bloodtithe Harvester
Fatal Push: {B} instant, destroy target creature with MV 2 or less (MV 4 or less with revolt — triggered by a permanent leaving the field this turn). Bloodtithe Harvester: 3/2 for {BR} that makes a Blood token and can sacrifice itself + Blood tokens to deal 3 damage to a target creature (scales with more Blood). Two overlapping removal sources that handle creatures of different sizes. Harvester's sacrifice ability doesn't use the stack target selection — it resolves faster than most responses.
Chapter 03

GAME
FLOW

"T1 Thoughtseize. T2 Bloodtithe Harvester. T3 Fable. T4 Sheoldred. They die to their draw step."
Early (T1–T2)
Disrupt + Deploy
T1: Thoughtseize — see their hand, strip the best card. Perfect information shapes your T2-T4 sequencing. T2: Bloodtithe Harvester is the ideal T2 play: 3/2 body that makes a Blood token (rummage later) and serves as removal when you have Blood tokens. Alternatively, Fatal Push kills their T1 creature. Your goal: answer their T2-T3 threat while deploying your own.
Mid (T3–T4)
Fable + Value Accrual
T3: Fable of the Mirror-Breaker. The Goblin token attacks on T4 for a Treasure, Chapter 2 loots, Chapter 3 transforms T5. T4: Sheoldred or Graveyard Trespasser. Sheoldred on T4 with a Fable in play means T5: Reflection of Kiki-Jiki copies Sheoldred, creating a haste 4/5 deathtouch that attacks immediately. Two Sheoldreds on T5 drains 4 per their draw step. Most opponents can't survive this.
Late (T5+)
Sheoldred Drain or Creature Pressure
Sheoldred in play kills control opponents through draw steps (2 life per draw = 6 per turn if they draw 3). Against aggro, Sheoldred's 4/5 deathtouch blanks attackers and Kalitas exiles their creatures preventing recursion. The game's won through incremental advantage: every creature you play generates more value than their single-removal answer. Fable's loot Chapter 2 refills your hand when you're running low.
Mulligan
Need: 3 Lands + Disruption + Threat
Keep: 3 lands (including at least one black source), 1 Thoughtseize or Fatal Push, and a 2-3 drop threat. Mulligan: 1-2 land hands (you miss T2 plays which are critical), all threats with no interaction (you die to combo before impacting the board), 4+ lands with nothing to do. At 6: 3 lands + Thoughtseize + any threat. At 5: 2 lands + Thoughtseize + Harvester + any removal.
Chapter 04

CORE
ENGINES

"Thoughtseize gives information. Fable generates value. Sheoldred drains. Three engines, all running simultaneously."
Engine 1 — Information
Thoughtseize + Sequencing
Thoughtseize on T1 reveals their entire opening hand. This information tells you: what's their kill condition, what's their next 3 turns, what's dangerous vs. ignorable. Strip the most important card — usually their combo piece, their sweeper (vs. your threats), their Treasure Cruise (prevents them from refueling), or their best T2 play. The information from Thoughtseize shapes every subsequent decision in the game.
Engine 2 — Value
Fable → Reflection of Kiki-Jiki
Fable generates value across all three chapters. Chapter 1 (Goblin → Treasure): Treasure enables higher-mana plays earlier. Chapter 2 (loot): dig for Sheoldred, remove lands, put Kroxa in GY. Chapter 3 (Reflection): copy your best nonlegendary creature with haste. Reflection copying Graveyard Trespasser exiles a GY card and drains 1. Reflection copying Bloodtithe Harvester provides removal + Blood. Fable flippped with Sheoldred = game over.
Engine 3 — Drain
Sheoldred, the Apocalypse
Sheoldred is a passive win condition that grows more powerful as the game progresses. Early game: a 4/5 deathtouch that demands immediate answer. Mid game: 2 life per opponent draw step turns their card advantage into a clock. Late game: against decks that need to draw 3+ cards per turn to keep up (control, Phoenix), Sheoldred generates 6-8 damage per turn passively. Opponents must kill her immediately or die to their own draws.
Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Thoughtseize on T1 for information. Use Fatal Push revolted. Protect Fable — it's worth more than anything else."
Line 01 — Thoughtseize Timing: Information vs Disruption
T1 Thoughtseize gives information early but the hand they show on T1 is 7 cards that become increasingly stale as the game progresses. However, knowing their opening hand on T1 tells you which of their cards to answer T2-T4. Against combo: strip the key piece (Parhelion II, Greasefang, Lotus Field). Against control: strip their most powerful card (Teferi, Supreme Verdict) to buy turns. Against aggro: strip Eidolon of the Great Revel. If you hold Thoughtseize until T3-T4, they've already deployed their dangerous cards. Strip early.
Line 02 — Fatal Push Revolt: Create the Trigger
Fatal Push normally kills MV-2 or less creatures. With revolt (a permanent left your battlefield this turn), it kills MV-4 or less. Revolt triggers: a fetch land cracking, a Blood token being sacrificed, a Treasure being sacrificed, a creature dying in combat, Bloodtithe Harvester activating (sacrifices itself and Blood tokens). The standard sequence: sacrifice a Blood token from Harvester to "set up revolt," then Fatal Push a 3-4 MV creature. This sequence kills threats Fatal Push would normally miss entirely — including Bonecrusher Giant and most four-drops.
Line 03 — Protect Fable: It's Worth More Than Any Card It Could Replace
Fable of the Mirror-Breaker is the deck's most valuable card — worth protecting with a Thoughtseize to strip opponent's answer before deploying it. When you have Fable in hand and Thoughtseize available, always Thoughtseize first to check for removal. If they have Abrupt Decay or a counter, strip it first, then deploy Fable safely. A flipped Fable (Reflection of Kiki-Jiki) that survives a full turn generates more value per turn than almost any other card in Pioneer.
Line 04 — Graveyard Trespasser Ward: Force the Discard
Graveyard Trespasser has ward — opponent must discard a card to target it with a spell or ability. This is a resource tax, not prevention. Force the opponent to pay the ward cost at the worst time: deploy Trespasser when their hand is depleted after casting multiple spells. A Trespasser deployed when the opponent has 0-1 cards is essentially uncounterable because they can't afford the discard cost. Trespasser deployed into a full hand just gets answered. Sequence your T3 Trespasser after their T3 action has emptied useful cards from their hand.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"You're the fair deck in an unfair format. Your edge is individual card quality and perfect information from Thoughtseize."
vs Aggro
Answer Their Threats, Deploy Yours
Against RDW: Fatal Push kills Monastery Swiftspear and Soul-Scar Mage on T1. Bonecrusher Giant's Stomp deals 2 to kill their one-drops (and prevents damage prevention — ignoring protection spells). Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet gains life and exiles their creatures on death. Graveyard Trespasser's drain stabilizes. You can't race burn — answer everything and deploy Sheoldred at 6+ life.
vs Midrange
Out-Value Their Resources
Against Selesnya Company and similar: your 1-for-1 removal trades favorably with their 1/1 Elves. Sheoldred blanks their draw-step value. Graveyard Trespasser with ward forces extra resource spending. Fable's Reflection copying Sheoldred on T5 generates overwhelming advantage they can't answer profitably. You win midrange mirrors by generating more value per card than they do.
vs Combo/Control
Strip Key Pieces, Pressure Life
Against Greasefang, Lotus Field, and UW Control: Thoughtseize strips the kill piece (Parhelion II, Emergent Ultimatum, Teferi). Dreadbore handles planeswalkers (the only non-blue planeswalker removal in Pioneer). Sheoldred kills control through draw steps. Against combo: don't over-discard — Thoughtseize the ONE critical piece, then race with creatures. They only need one piece to win; you only need to answer that one piece.

Chapter 08

SIDEBOARD
GUIDE

"Your sideboard converts fair midrange into specialized hate for each matchup type."
P1 — vs Control/Combo
Duress ×3
A {B} discard that forces the opponent to reveal their hand and discard a noncreature, nonland card. Against UW Control: strips Teferi, Supreme Verdict, Dovin's Veto, No More Lies. Against Lotus Field: strips Bring to Light, Hidden Strings, or Emergent Ultimatum. Against Greasefang: strips Parhelion II or Monument to Endurance. Duress is Thoughtseize's cheaper sibling for matchups where you need to strip multiple spells — {B} vs {1B} means you can cast Duress + another spell on the same turn more easily.
P2 — vs GY Decks
Unlicensed Hearse ×2
2-mana artifact Vehicle. Tap two creatures to crew it into a 3/3. More importantly: {2}: exile up to two cards from any GY — Hearse gets a +1/+1 counter for each card exiled. Against Arclight Phoenix: exile two Phoenixes per activation, permanently removing them. Against Greasefang: exile Parhelion II from GY. Once Hearse has exiled 4-6 cards, it becomes a 5-8/5-8 vehicle that crews into a massive threat. Requires no payment beyond the activation — just removing their GY pieces.
P3 — vs Artifacts/Artifacts/Aggro
Abrade ×2 + Kolaghan's Command ×2
Abrade: {1R} instant — deal 3 damage to any creature OR destroy target artifact. Hits Cori-Steel Cutter (before it transforms), Unlicensed Hearse (their SB hate), and any relevant artifact. Kolaghan's Command ({1BR}): four modes including deal 2 damage, destroy artifact, make opponent discard, or return creature from GY. Kolaghan's Command returning Sheoldred from GY is the deck's most powerful late-game play — Sheoldred is a legend so you can only have one in play, but it can be recast via Command.
P4 — vs Combo/Aggro Boards
Extinction Event + Brotherhood's End
Extinction Event ({3B}): exile all creatures with even or odd MV. The choice matters — say "even" to exile MV-2 creatures (Bloodtithe Harvester, Thalia) or "odd" to exile MV-1 and 3 creatures (Swiftspear, Bonecrusher Giant). Brotherhood's End ({1RR}): deals 2 damage to each creature and player, OR 3 damage to each artifact. Brotherhood's End G2 vs RDW clears their entire board including Eidolon. Two sweepers in the sideboard give you answers to wide boards that single removal can't handle.
Chapter 09

HEURISTICS

"Thoughtseize T1. Revolt Fatal Push for 4-drops. Protect Fable. Never Thoughtseize when losing life matters."
  1. 01Thoughtseize T1 in most games — don't hold it. The value of information decays rapidly. By T3, they've drawn 2-3 new cards and your T1 information is partially stale. Strip the most dangerous card on T1. The exception: vs RDW, don't pay 2 life to Thoughtseize when you're already at 12 from their attacks — 2 life matters more than information when you're racing burn.
  2. 02Create revolt for Fatal Push on demand via Blood token sacrifice. Bloodtithe Harvester creates Blood tokens. When you need to kill a 3-4 MV creature, sacrifice a Blood token first (triggering revolt), THEN cast Fatal Push. This sequence kills any creature up to MV 4. The Blood token sacrifice must be done before declaring Fatal Push as the target — order of operations matters.
  3. 03Fable Chapter 2 — loot away lands, not spells. Chapter 2 lets you discard a card and draw a card. Use it to discard: excess lands (flood), Blood tokens (can't cast), dead removal (vs a deck with no creatures). Keep: Thoughtseize, Sheoldred, removal spells you'll need. The loot helps with flood but don't discard cards you'll actually need in the next 2 turns.
  4. 04Against control, cut Fatal Push and board in Duress. Control decks run minimal creatures — Fatal Push is a dead card in hand. Bring in Duress to strip their high-impact spells (Teferi, Verdict, Dovin's Veto) instead. Dreadbore handles any planeswalker that resolves. Sheoldred kills them through draw steps. Fatal Push stays in for matchups with relevant creatures only.
  5. 05Dreadbore is sorcery speed — plan your Teferi answers accordingly. Dreadbore destroys any creature OR planeswalker at sorcery speed. Against Teferi (Hero of Dominaria): Dreadbore on your main phase before Teferi can activate. Against Wandering Emperor: you can't Dreadbore it at instant speed during combat. Know that Dreadbore requires sorcery-speed window — use Fatal Push for instant-speed answers, Dreadbore for planned main-phase answers.
  6. 06Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet: exile-on-kill prevents all recursion including Phoenix, Parhelion, and Kroxa. Whenever any non-token creature dies and Kalitas is in play, exile it and create a 2/2 Zombie. This exiles Arclight Phoenixes (prevents recursion), Greasefang (prevents returning Parhelion), and any creature your removal spells kill. Kalitas is your built-in GY hate vs creature-based strategies. Deploy it when your removal is active.
  7. 07Reflection of Kiki-Jiki copies nonlegendary creatures — Sheoldred is legendary, so time the flip right. Reflection copies nonlegendary creatures. Sheoldred IS legendary — Reflection can't copy it directly. However: Reflection creates a TOKEN COPY — token copies of legendary permanents don't trigger the legend rule. So Reflection copying Sheoldred works and creates a legal haste token. Just don't cast a second real Sheoldred while the token is in play.
  8. 08Graveyard Trespasser ward timing: deploy into their depleted hand, not their full hand. Ward — discard a card — is only a cost, not prevention. Deploy Trespasser after they've used multiple spells in a turn, leaving them with 1-2 cards. If they have 5 cards, paying ward cost is easy. If they have 0-1 cards, paying ward effectively means they can't answer Trespasser this turn.
  9. 09Sheoldred in play: their draw step is a clock. Count turns to death. With Sheoldred in play and opponent at 14 life: if they draw 2 cards per turn, that's 4 life per turn, and they die in 3.5 turns. Calculate this before each combat — know whether you need to attack aggressively to close or whether Sheoldred alone wins in time. Often Sheoldred + passive drain wins without you needing to attack at all.
  10. 10Unlicensed Hearse grows permanently — crew it early to start exiling. Hearse gains +1/+1 counters for each card it exiles from GYs. Start activating {2} on T3-T4 to exile Phoenixes or Parhelions before they matter. A Hearse that's been activating for 3 turns is a 5-6/5-6 that can crew into a massive attacker. Don't save it for "when the GY gets big" — exile early, exile often, and it becomes a legitimate threat.

TUNING
NOTES

"4 Thoughtseize, 4 Fatal Push, 4 Fable are immovable. Everything else responds to meta threats."
Lock
4 Thoughtseize + 4 Fatal Push
Thoughtseize is the best T1 play in Pioneer across every matchup. Fatal Push kills virtually any relevant creature at {B}. These 8 cards define the deck's identity. Never cut below 4 of either.
Lock
4 Fable of the Mirror-Breaker
The deck's best card after Thoughtseize. Generates value across all three chapters and transforms into the deck's primary win engine. Always 4 copies — finding Fable consistently by T3 is non-negotiable.
Lock
3–4 Sheoldred
3 is the reference number; 4 in control-heavy metas where Sheoldred wins games alone. Sheoldred is legendary so multiples in hand are dead. 3 copies maximizes seeing it in most games without the redundancy problem.
Flex 2–4
Bloodtithe Harvester
4 is standard. Can trim to 3 in metas where the 3/2 body is less relevant (control-heavy). The Blood token provides valuable looting and the sacrifice ability scales with Blood count — more Harvesters = more Blood = better removal. 4 is correct in most metas.
Flex 0–3
Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet
2 in standard builds. The 3/4 lifelink body and exile-on-kill are valuable vs aggro and GY decks. 0 copies vs control (irrelevant). 3 copies in heavy aggro or GY-combo metas. Kalitas is the most meta-dependent slot in the deck.
Flex
Removal Suite
Bonecrusher Giant (Stomp deals 2, prevents damage prevention — runs 3). Dreadbore (kills planeswalkers at sorcery speed — runs 2). Power Word Kill (cheap unconditional creature removal — 1 copy). Adjust the ratio: more Dreadbore vs planeswalker-heavy metas, more Giant vs aggro metas, more Power Word Kill vs wide creature fields.

EVENT
PREP

"Know which card to Thoughtseize. Know when to Fatal Push with revolt. Know when Sheoldred wins without attacking."
Know Cold
Thoughtseize Priority by Matchup
vs UR Cutter: strip Stormchaser's Talent (their engine). vs RDW: strip Eidolon of the Great Revel (their tax card). vs Greasefang: strip Parhelion II (their kill piece). vs Lotus Field: strip Emergent Ultimatum or Bring to Light. vs UW Control: strip Teferi, Hero of Dominaria (their win condition and draw engine). vs Selesnya: strip Collected Company (their refuel) or Thalia (their tax). vs Phoenix: strip Artist's Talent (their loot engine) or Treasure Cruise.
Know Cold
Sheoldred Drain Math
With Sheoldred in play: each opponent draw costs them 2 life. Standard draws: 1/turn = 2 life per turn (dies in ~10 turns from 20 life at no draws before). With cantrips: 3 draws/turn = 6 life per turn (dies in ~3 turns from 18 life). Control with Teferi: draws 1+ per turn + untap lands. Know the turn count to death so you know whether to attack or wait. Often: Sheoldred + 2-3 turns is lethal without attacking at all.
Verify
SB Matches Expected Field
Heavy GY (Phoenix, Greasefang): max Unlicensed Hearse + Go Blank. Heavy control: max Duress + Chandra. Heavy aggro (RDW, Selesnya): Brotherhood's End + Extinction Event. Heavy combo (Lotus Field): Duress + Extinction Event for odd-MV creature exiling (Vizier of Tumbling Sands = MV 3, odd). Confirm 15 cards match the expected field before registration.
