Chapter 01

Deck
Identity

"Aether Vial at 2. Meddling Mage naming their win condition. Thalia's Lieutenant pumping the whole board. Coppercoat Vanguard giving everything ward. You're deploying threats AND hate pieces simultaneously — no other aggro deck in Modern does this."

5-Color Humans is Modern's original tribal aggro-disruption deck. It applies serious pressure through Champion of the Parish and Thalia's Lieutenant snowball counters while simultaneously deploying hate pieces that break the opponent's game plan. Meddling Mage names their win condition. Thalia, Guardian of Thraben taxes all their noncreature spells. Unsettled Mariner taxes all their targeted interaction. All of this at instant speed through Aether Vial, which cheats everything into play uncountably and without spending mana.

This build leans into the classic Humans shell — Noble Hierarch for acceleration and exalted, Coppercoat Vanguard for the lord effect + ward {1} blanket, Mantis Rider as the 3-mana hasty closer. It trades the newer tech cards (Aang, Mai, Safekeeper) for the proven, resilient core that has been putting up results for years. The deck is not tier 1 — it sits around 0.5% meta share — and that's not a criticism, it's a feature. It rewards deep knowledge of YOUR interactions over raw card power.

This is a pet deck built to perfection. Play it because it's yours.

What This Deck Does

Primary Plan
Snowball Aggro
T1 Noble Hierarch or Champion of the Parish or Aether Vial. T2 Thalia's Lieutenant pumping everything. T3 Mantis Rider or Adeline closing with haste/vigilance. Every Human entering grows your board. By T4 you're swinging with 12+ power across 4 creatures.
Secondary Plan
Disruption While Attacking
Meddling Mage names their key spell. Thalia taxes their interaction. Coppercoat Vanguard gives every Human ward {1}, meaning every removal spell costs 1 more. The opponent has to fight through a tax AND answer your clock simultaneously — and you're doing both with the same cards.
Win Condition
Critical Mass of Bodies
You win through combat damage. Champion with 5+ counters, Adeline creating tokens every attack, Guide of Souls converting energy into +1/+1 and flying on your biggest threat. Mockingbird copies their best creature. There's no combo finish — you out-board them and punch through.

Why Play This Deck

Play This If
You Want Proactive Disruption
You like applying pressure while deploying hate. You enjoy naming the right card with Meddling Mage and watching them squirm. You want Aether Vial instant-speed tricks on their turn. You like growing a board that gets harder to answer every turn. High skill ceiling with real racing power.
Skip This If
You Want Tier 1 Consistency
The manabase is fragile — 19 lands across 5 colors with no fetches. Blood Moon can end you on the spot without Witch Enchanter. You're slower than Boros Energy and less resilient than Jeskai Blink. If you want the highest win rate in an open field, this isn't it. But if you know the deck cold, it punches well above its weight class.

Meta Position — April 2026

Humans sits in the "rogue" tier at roughly 0.5% of the Modern metagame — below the radar of most sideboards. The current top of the format is dominated by Boros Energy (18.8%), Jeskai Blink (9.0%), Ruby Storm (7.6%), Izzet Affinity (5.8%), and Eldrazi Tron (5.3%). Humans' natural matchup spread is solid against combo (Meddling Mage + Thalia + Lavinia), reasonable against midrange (your clock + disruption outpaces their value), and tough against Boros Energy (their removal is too efficient and cheap).

Recent results: Zannty took 10th at a Modern Challenge (5-2) with a newer build featuring Aang and Safekeeper. The SCG CON Milwaukee $1K saw Humans at 16th place (5-2). Someone unironically registered Humans at the LDXP SEA26 Modern 10k (211 players). The deck keeps showing up because experienced pilots know how to exploit the metagame's blind spots.

Chapter 03

Gameplan

"T1 accelerant. T2 lord + disruption. T3 close the door. Every creature that enters grows the board AND does something to the opponent."

Turn-by-Turn Ideal Sequences

The God Draw
T1 Vial, T2 Double Deploy
T1: Aether Vial. T2: Tick Vial to 1, cast Thalia's Lieutenant from hand (0 Humans to pump, but she enters). Vial in Champion of the Parish at their end step — Champion gets a counter from Lieutenant entering, Lieutenant gets a counter from Champion entering. T3: Tick to 2. Cast Meddling Mage naming their removal, Vial in Coppercoat Vanguard. Now: Champion is 3/3, Lieutenant is 4/4, Mage has ward {1}, Vanguard lords everything +1/+0. Swing 12.
The Hierarch Draw
T1 Hierarch, T2 Three-Drop
T1: Noble Hierarch. T2: Tap Hierarch for mana, cast Mantis Rider (flying, vigilance, haste) — attack for 4 immediately (3 power + 1 exalted). Or T2: Adeline, Resplendent Cathar — she has vigilance, her power = number of creatures you control (2), and she creates a 1/1 Human token tapped and attacking when you swing. T3: deploy 2-drop + attack = Adeline is now a 4/4 making tokens. Snowball.
The Disruption Draw
T1 Champion, T2 Mage + Thalia
T1: Champion of the Parish (1/1). T2: Meddling Mage naming their best spell (Champion → 2/2). If you have a second land or Hierarch: also cast Thalia, Guardian of Thraben (Champion → 3/3). Now their noncreature spells cost {1} more AND one specific spell can't be cast at all. They're attacking through both a tax and a lock while a growing Champion threatens lethal by T4.
The Grindy Draw
T1 Guide, T2 Coppercoat
T1: Guide of Souls (1/2). T2: Coppercoat Vanguard — Guide triggers, gain 1 life + {E}. Now every Human entering generates energy + life. T3: Deploy 2 more Humans — Guide now has {E}{E}{E}. Attack, pay 3 energy: put two +1/+1 counters and flying on your best attacker. A 5/5 flying Champion closes games through any ground stall.

Mulligan Guide

The most important card to see in your opener is Aether Vial or Noble Hierarch. A hand without either needs two lands and a strong curve (1-drop into 2-drop). Mulligan any hand that can't deploy a creature on T1 or T2. The deck's power scales with board presence — a slow start against the current Modern meta means death.

Always Keep
Vial + 2 Humans + Land
Any hand with T1 Vial and at least 2 creatures to deploy is a keep regardless of what else is in the hand. Vial makes your creatures uncounterable and generates instant-speed tricks. The rest of the deck is all 1-2 drops — you'll draw into a curve.
Usually Keep
Hierarch + 3-Drop + Land
T1 Hierarch → T2 Mantis Rider (swing 4) or Adeline is a strong opening. Also keep Hierarch + Lieutenant + Coppercoat — the T2 double-deploy off Hierarch mana is excellent. Any Hierarch hand with a follow-up is keepable.
Mulligan
No Accelerant, No T1 Play
A hand with 3 lands, 2 Meddling Mages, Adeline, and Witch Enchanter is a mulligan — you can't deploy until T2 and your best cards arrive T3+. Against Boros Energy or Storm, that's too slow. Mulligan to 6 and look for Vial or Hierarch.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 Thalia or Meddling Mage
On the play your T1 matters more than any other decision. Thalia, Guardian of Thraben on T1 taxes every non-creature spell opponents cast for the rest of the game — every Counterspell, Lightning Bolt, and Violent Outburst costs 1 extra. Meddling Mage T1 (via Aether Vial) names their combo piece and locks them out immediately. On the play: always Vial on T1 if it's in hand.
On the Draw
Extra Card Finds the Lock Piece
On the draw you see 8 cards, dramatically improving odds of finding Thalia + Meddling Mage together. Keep slightly looser hands on the draw if they contain Aether Vial — Vial on T1 lets you deploy multiple creatures per turn without spending mana. Against aggro on the draw, Mantis Rider hands are keepable even without disruption because you need the flying clock more than the tax pieces.

When Disrupted

Thalia Removed
Re-tax With Reflector Mage
Thalia killed: the tax is gone but Reflector Mage bounces their key creature AND prevents it being recast until their next turn. This gives you one free attack step against the now-tapped opponent. Rebuild Thalia via Chord of Calling or Aether Vial. Humans is resilient because every creature is a threat — you don’t lose when individual pieces die; you lose when the board clears completely.
Board Swept
Vial Rebuild
Sweeper resolves: count the Aether Vial counters. At 2 counters: Meddling Mage + Thalia re-enter simultaneously next turn. At 3 counters: Mantis Rider closes immediately. The Vial rebuild is faster than topdecking: you can deploy 2 creatures the turn after a sweep. Prioritize rebuilding lock pieces (Thalia, Meddling Mage) over clock pieces (Mantis Rider) after a sweep.
Chapter 04

Core Engines

A
The Vial Advantage Engine
Deploy
T1 Aether Vial
Vial on T1 is the most important play in the deck. It ticks up each upkeep (1 → 2 → 3). At 2 counters it hits 18 of your 36 creatures. Every activation: creature enters from hand, bypasses counterspells, triggers Champion/Lieutenant/Guide, and you keep your mana open for a second creature from hand. Vial effectively doubles your mana every turn.
Flash
Instant-Speed Hate
End of their turn: Vial in Meddling Mage naming whatever they revealed or tutored. Main phase response: Vial in Thalia before they cast their critical noncreature spell. Block step: Vial in Jirina, then sacrifice her for hexproof+indestructible in response to their removal. Every Vial activation is a gotcha moment the opponent has to respect.
Snowball
Free +1/+1 Counters
Every Vial activation triggers Champion (+1/+1 counter) and Lieutenant (gets a counter, already pumped everything on ETB). Guide of Souls triggers: gain 1 life, gain {E}. At 3 energy: pay to put two +1/+1 and flying on an attacker. A single Vial activation at their end step can represent +3/+3 across the board and a flying threat.
B
The Coppercoat Ward Blanket
Lord
+1/+0 to Every Human
Coppercoat Vanguard gives each other Human you control +1/+0 and ward {1}. This is a lord effect that doesn't just pump — it taxes every piece of removal the opponent aims at your creatures. A 2/2 for {1}{W} that makes your entire board harder to interact with. Multiple Vanguards stack: 2 Coppercoats = +2/+0 and ward {2} on everything.
Tax Stack
Ward + Thalia + Mariner
Coppercoat's ward {1} stacks with Unsettled Mariner's "counter unless pay {1}" and Thalia's noncreature tax. With all three in play: their Lightning Bolt targeting your creature costs {R} + {1} (Thalia) + {1} (Coppercoat ward) + {1} (Mariner) = {3}{R} for a Bolt. Their Fatal Push costs {B} + {1} + {1} + {1} = {3}{B}. Their removal suite becomes effectively uncastable on curve.
Resilience
Survives What Others Don't
The ward blanket means opponents can't efficiently one-for-one your creatures. They need excess mana to answer each threat. This buys you time to deploy more creatures and grow your board. Against sweepers: Jirina sacrifices for hexproof+indestructible, protecting the entire board. The combination of ward + Jirina insurance gives Humans resilience that other aggro decks lack.
C
The Guide of Souls Energy Loop
Accumulate
Every ETB = Energy
Guide of Souls: whenever another creature you control enters, gain 1 life and get {E}. Every Vial activation, every creature cast, every Adeline token entering — all generate energy. In a typical game you accumulate 3-6 energy by T3-T4 without any special effort. The life gain is incidental but real against burn-based decks.
Convert
Pay 3 → Flying Threat
Whenever you attack: pay {E}{E}{E}, put two +1/+1 counters and a flying counter on target attacking creature — it becomes an Angel in addition to its other types. Target your biggest Champion or Lieutenant. A 5/5 flying Champion closes over any ground stall. The flying is permanent — the counter stays.
Repeat
Every Attack Step
If you have 6+ energy, you can pump two creatures in one attack step. Guide's energy generation continues every turn as long as creatures keep entering. With Vial active, you're generating 1-2 energy per turn cycle passively. The deck never runs out of energy sources because every creature is a Human that triggers Guide.

Engine Priority Order

Priority 01
Aether Vial + Lock Pieces
Aether Vial at 2 counters lets you flash in Meddling Mage or Thalia at instant speed for free. This is your highest-value engine because it produces both disruption and board presence while leaving mana open for other plays. Every turn Vial is in play at the right counter number, you threaten a free creature. Protect Vial over individual creatures.
Priority 02
Thalia + Meddling Mage Tax Lock
Thalia taxes all non-creature spells by {1}. Meddling Mage names a key spell and prevents it being cast. Together they create a tax + ban wall that forces opponents to either remove the creatures (while you refill from Vial) or try to play through the lock. Against combo, name their win condition. Against control, name Counterspell. Deploy both simultaneously when possible.
Priority 03
Champion of the Parish Growth
Champion of the Parish gets a +1/+1 counter whenever a Human enters under your control. With a full curve of Humans entering each turn, Champion becomes a 4/4+ by T3 and an 8/8+ by T4. This engine runs in the background while you’re deploying lock pieces — don’t focus on it, but account for it when calculating lethal. A 6/6 Champion + Mantis Rider connecting for 3 is 9 damage in one swing.

Chapter 05

Lines &
Tactics

"Every Human tribal interaction and every name Meddling Mage calls must be verified before you sit down."

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Line 01 — Critical
Meddling Mage Names on ETB
Meddling Mage’s name-a-card ability triggers on ETB. Name the card before any other ETB triggers resolve. You cannot change the named card later. If you Vial in Meddling Mage in response to their spell being cast, the naming happens before their spell resolves — it’s too late to name that spell (it’s already been cast, not yet resolved). Name the NEXT spell they’re going to cast, not the current one.
Line 02 — Critical
Thalia Taxes Aether Vial Activations: NO
Thalia, Guardian of Thraben taxes noncreature SPELLS — spells you cast. Aether Vial is an activated ability, not a casting. Vial’s activated ability is not taxed by Thalia. Your opponent’s Vial activations are also not taxed. The tax applies to Lightning Bolt, Violent Outburst, Counterspell, Thoughtseize — anything cast from hand. Activated abilities (Vial, planeswalker +/-) are always free of Thalia’s tax.
Line 03 — Critical
Reflector Mage’s Return Is Optional
Reflector Mage says “return target creature an opponent controls to its owner’s hand” — this is mandatory when it ETBs, not optional. However, the “can’t cast a spell with that name” clause applies until your NEXT turn. If Reflector Mage dies before your next turn, the casting restriction ends. Chain Reflector Mages (Vial deploys one each turn) to perpetually bounce their key creature while they can never legally recast it.

Meddling Mage Naming Guide

The single highest-skill-cap card in the deck. Naming correctly wins games; naming wrong wastes a 2/2 body. These are the default names by matchup — adjust based on information from their plays and your read of the game state.

vs Boros Energy
Name: Galvanic Discharge
Their most efficient removal spell. Stopping Discharge means your board survives longer. Secondary name: Ajani, Nacatl Pariah (stops their best 1-drop from flipping). If you've seen their hand via information: name whatever kills you fastest.
vs Ruby Storm
Name: Past in Flames
Without Past in Flames, their combo is far less reliable. They can still win through Rite of Flame chains, but Past in Flames is the card that lets them rebuild from a disrupted hand. Secondary: Grapeshot (stops the kill itself). Thalia taxing is your primary disruption — Mage is insurance.
vs Jeskai Blink
Name: Ephemerate
Ephemerate is their engine — rebuy Solitude, Stoneforge, value creatures. Without it their deck is fair Magic. Secondary: Solitude (stops their free removal). If you have Thalia in play, naming Ephemerate + Thalia tax = they can't blink efficiently at all.
vs Living End
Name: Living End
They literally cannot win without resolving Living End (or the very rare Grief beatdown). Name it G1 and they need to find Force of Negation or bounce your Mage. With Lavinia from SB: cascade into a free spell = Lavinia counters it. Double lock.
vs Izzet Affinity
Name: Thought Monitor
Their draw engine. Without Monitor their deck runs out of gas. Secondary: Sojourner's Companion (stops their biggest free body). Kataki from SB is your primary answer — Mage is supplementary disruption.
vs Eldrazi Tron
Name: All Is Dust
The sweeper that ignores your entire board. All Is Dust destroys all colored permanents — your Humans, your Vials, everything. Naming it forces them to beat you through creature combat where your disruption and lords shine. Secondary: Karn, the Great Creator.

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

Jirina + Aether Vial vs Living End — The Blowout Line
Living End is on the stack (from cascade). Response: Vial in Jirina at instant speed. Jirina ETB: "exile target player's graveyard" — target the OPPONENT. Their entire GY (all cycled creatures — 5/5s, 7/7s, 3/4s) is exiled permanently by Jirina, NOT by Living End. Now sacrifice Jirina — Humans gain hexproof + indestructible until EOT, and Jirina goes to YOUR GY. Living End resolves: Step 1: exile creature cards from GYs — Jirina is in your GY, gets exiled by Living End. Opponent's GY is already empty. Step 2: sacrifice all creatures (sacrifice bypasses indestructible — your Humans still die). Step 3: return cards exiled in Step 1 — Jirina comes back (she was exiled by Living End's Step 1). Opponent gets NOTHING (their creatures were exiled by Jirina's ETB, not Living End). Net result: opponent spent their whole turn cascading into a blank Living End, you have Jirina back as a 2/2. This is the single best interaction in the matchup.
Jirina ETB — "Exile Target Player's Graveyard" Targets a PLAYER
Jirina's ETB targets a PLAYER, not a graveyard. This means: Unsettled Mariner's "counter unless pay {1}" DOES apply if they target you with removal while Jirina's ETB targets them (Mariner triggers on targeting "you or a permanent you control" — Jirina targets the player, and the player might be you or the opponent). Also: Jirina can target YOUR OWN graveyard if you need to reset it (rare but relevant against Dauthi Voidwalker or GY hate that punishes you for having cards in GY).
Jirina's Sacrifice — Hexproof + Indestructible Until EOT, but NOT vs Sacrifice
"Sacrifice Jirina: Humans you control gain hexproof and indestructible until end of turn." Indestructible prevents DESTROY effects (Fatal Push, Wrath of God). Hexproof prevents TARGETING (Bolt, Unholy Heat). But SACRIFICE effects bypass both. All Is Dust (sacrifice all colored permanents), Liliana's -2 (sacrifice a creature), Living End (each player sacrifices all creatures) — all still work through Jirina's protection. Time your sacrifice for when they're using targeted removal or destroy-based sweepers, not sacrifice-based effects.
Aether Vial Creatures Don't Trigger Thalia's Tax
Vialed creatures aren't CAST — they enter the battlefield directly. This means: Thalia's tax ({1} extra on noncreature spells) doesn't apply to Vial activations. Cavern of Souls' "can't be countered" is irrelevant for Vialed creatures (they were never on the stack). But all ETB triggers still fire: Champion gets counters, Lieutenant pumps, Guide generates energy, Meddling Mage names a card. Vial bypasses the stack entirely — the only thing that matters is the ETB.
Meddling Mage — Name Is Chosen on Entry, Can't Be Responded To
Meddling Mage's "name a nonland card" happens as it enters the battlefield — it's part of the ETB, not a triggered ability on the stack. The opponent CANNOT respond to the naming. If you Vial in Mage at their end step, you name the card and they have no window to cast it in response to the naming. The name choice is locked in before they get priority. This is why Vial + Mage at their end step is the strongest disruption play in the deck.
Coppercoat Vanguard Does NOT Give Itself Ward
"Each OTHER Human you control gets +1/+0 and has ward {1}." Coppercoat does not protect itself. It's your most removal-vulnerable creature. With 2 Coppercoats: each gives the OTHER one ward {1} and +1/+0. They protect each other but not themselves alone. Deploy Coppercoat into a board that already has creatures — don't lead with it into open removal mana.

How Fast Can This Deck Kill?

The T3 Kill — Christmas Land
Vial + 2 Champions + 2 Lieutenants
T1: Aether Vial.
T2: Tick to 1. Cast Champion #1 ({W}) = 1/1. EOT Vial in Champion #2. Champion #1 grows to 2/2.

Vial in Lieutenant #2. Champion #1: 5/5 → Lt ETB +1/+1 = 6/6. Champion #2: 4/4 → Lt ETB = 5/5. Lt #1: 4/4 → Lt ETB = 5/5. Lt #2 sees 3 Humans = 4/4. Attack Champions (entered T2): 6 + 5 = 11 damage T3. T4: attack all = 6+5+5+4 = 20. Exactly lethal T4, 11 damage T3.
The T4 Kill — Consistent Goldfish
Any Vial/Hierarch + Curve
T1: Hierarch or Vial.
T2: Champion + Lieutenant (3 mana off Hierarch) or Champion + Vial in 1-drop. Champion is 3/3+ after Lieutenant ETB.
T3: Deploy 2 more Humans. Board is 4-5 creatures, Champion at 5/5+, Lieutenants at 4/4+. Attack for 11-15 damage. Guide energy at 2-3.
T4: Deploy last creatures. Pay 3 energy: +2/+2 flying on Champion. Attack everything. 20+ damage. Lethal.

The deck consistently kills on T4 and presents T3 damage that forces opponents to have an answer or die.

Best Plays & Decision Trees

When to Attack with 1 Creature (Exalted) vs Go Wide
T2-T3 with Noble Hierarch: Attack with ONE creature alone = gets +1/+1 exalted per Hierarch. A 3/3 Champion + exalted = 4/4 attack. This deals MORE damage than attacking with Champion 3/3 + Lieutenant 2/2 (5 total but they can double-block Champion and trade). Rule: attack with one creature in the early game when they have blockers. Go wide T4+ when your board is large enough that no blocks matter. Exalted only triggers when EXACTLY one creature attacks. Two attackers = zero exalted.
When to Tick Vial Past 2 — Almost Never
20 of 36 creatures are MV 2 (Lieutenant ×4, Coppercoat ×4, Meddling Mage ×4, Thalia ×2, Mariner ×2, Jirina ×2). Vial at 2 covers 56% of your creatures. Tick to 3 ONLY when: you have Mantis Rider, Adeline, or Reflector Mage in hand AND need instant-speed deployment (surprise haste attacker, EOT bounce). If you don't have a 3-drop in hand, leave Vial at 2. A Vial stuck at 3 with only 2-drops in hand is a dead artifact.
Guide of Souls Energy Math — Target the Biggest Creature
Each creature entering after Guide = 1 {E}. Need 3 for the pump. Typical timeline: Guide T1/T2 → two Humans enter T3 → 2 energy. Third Human → 3 energy. Attack, pay {E}{E}{E}: put +2/+2 and FLYING COUNTER on your biggest attacker. The flying is PERMANENT — the counter stays. Always target your biggest Champion (5/5 → 7/7 flying). A 7/7 flying Champion closes games over any ground stall. Don't waste energy on a 1/1 Guide — the +2/+2 matters most on your biggest threat.
Adeline's Power Updates in Real-Time During Combat
Adeline's power = creatures you control. She creates a 1/1 Human tapped and attacking when you attack. So: 4 creatures pre-attack → Adeline is 4/4. She attacks → creates token → now 5 creatures → Adeline is 5/4. Champion also triggers from the token entering (another Human → +1/+1). Lieutenant gets a counter too. Every attack with Adeline creates a chain reaction: new token → Champion grows → Lieutenant grows → Adeline's power increases (more creatures). She's your best grind engine against decks that trade 1-for-1.
Mockingbird — Best Targets by Situation
Copy your own Lieutenant: second ETB pumps everything AGAIN — a wave of +1/+1 counters across your board. This is usually the strongest play. Copy opponent's Murktide Regent: you get their 8/8 flying with YOUR flying keyword added. Copy Champion: the copy grows from future Humans (but starts as a 1/1 without existing counters). Copy at X=0: Mockingbird for {U} enters as a 1/1 flying Bird. Triggers Champion, Lieutenant, Guide. Sometimes the ETB triggers are worth more than the copy. Mockingbird IS a Bird Bard — if it copies a Human, it's a Human Bird Bard (triggers Champion).
Lieutenant Timing — Never Cast as Your First Creature
Lieutenant's ETB: +1/+1 on each other Human. With 0 Humans: Lieutenant is a 1/1 that pumped nothing. With 3 Humans: Lieutenant enters as a 4/4 that distributed 3 counters. Always deploy cheap Humans first, then Lieutenant. The ideal sequence: T1 Champion, T2 Lieutenant (Champion = 3/3, Lieutenant = 2/2). Never: T1 nothing, T2 Lieutenant alone (1/1 that pumped nobody). Exception: if your only play is Lieutenant, cast it — a 1/1 body still triggers Champion later.
Mantis Rider — Hold Until Lethal, Don't Deploy into Open Mana
3/3 flying vigilance HASTE. The haste means they can't play around it — it deals damage the turn it enters. Don't play Rider T3 into open removal. Hold it until the turn where 3 flying (+ potential exalted) represents lethal or near-lethal. A surprise Rider off the top on T5 when they've stabilized at 5 life wins games no other card can. The vigilance means it attacks AND blocks — relevant against aggro mirrors where racing matters.

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • 01
    Not naming the right card with Meddling Mage on the draw. Name the card they’re most likely to cast next — not the most powerful card in their deck. Lines & Tactics

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Humans wins on stacked triggers and precise sequencing — know exactly when each fires."
Exalted Triggers Only When Exactly ONE Creature Attacks
Noble Hierarch's exalted says "Whenever a creature you control attacks alone." If you attack with two creatures, zero exalted triggers fire. If you attack with one creature and have two Hierarchs in play, that creature gets +2/+2 (one exalted from each). Your early-game default: pick your best attacker and swing with it alone to maximize exalted. Late game when you need to go wide: swarm regardless of exalted. The decision point is usually turn 2-3 when you're choosing between one efficient attacker or pressuring with multiple smaller creatures.
Aether Vial — The Creature Is NOT Cast
Aether Vial says "Put a creature card with mana value equal to the number of charge counters on Aether Vial from your hand onto the battlefield." Vialing in a creature is NOT casting it — it bypasses counterspells entirely (nothing to counter), doesn't trigger prowess, doesn't pay the Thalia tax, and doesn't interact with Spell Snare. This also means: Cavern of Souls is redundant when using Vial (you don't need Cavern's anti-counter protection if you're not casting the creature). The critical interaction: Lavinia, Azorius Renegade says opponents can't cast noncreature spells without paying full mana — but she says nothing about putting creatures onto the battlefield via Vial.
Meddling Mage Name Happens on ETB — They Can't Respond to the Choice
Meddling Mage's ability is a replacement effect that triggers as it enters the battlefield — you choose the card name as part of the ETB. Opponents cannot respond to the name choice with an instant-speed spell because the name is locked in the moment Mage enters. This means: Vialing in Mage at their end step and naming Ephemerate is uncounterable and unresponsable. They can kill Mage after it enters, but the name is already chosen and active for as long as Mage lives.
Coppercoat Vanguard Does NOT Give Itself Ward
Coppercoat Vanguard says "Each other Human you control has ward {1}." The word "other" is critical — Vanguard itself has no ward. It's your most removal-vulnerable creature precisely because it protects everything else. Sequence accordingly: if you deploy Vanguard T2 after a T1 Human, your T1 creature immediately gets ward {1}, but Vanguard itself is open to any removal spell. If they remove Vanguard in response to your T2 deployment, your T1 creature's ward expires — ward is a continuous effect from Vanguard being in play.
Jirina, Dauntless General — Sacrifice Is a Cost, Not a Target
Jirina's protection ability reads: "Sacrifice Jirina: Humans you control gain hexproof and indestructible until end of turn." Sacrificing Jirina is the activation cost — it happens immediately when you activate the ability, before any player receives priority to respond. This means: (1) they cannot respond to the sacrifice with removal on Jirina (she's already sacrificed), (2) the hexproof and indestructible resolve even if they try to respond (it's already on the stack), (3) use this in response to sweepers, targeted removal, or combat tricks — not preemptively. Hexproof means they can't target your creatures; indestructible means damage and destroy effects are blanked.
Champion of the Parish and Thalia's Lieutenant Trigger Simultaneously
When a Human enters the battlefield (including Champion itself or Lieutenant itself), all "whenever a Human enters" triggers fire simultaneously. Champion of the Parish gets a +1/+1 counter for each Human ETB. Thalia's Lieutenant pumps all Humans including itself when it enters (counting itself as the triggering Human). With two Champions in play and one Human entering: both Champions get a counter simultaneously. With Lieutenant + Champion in play and a new Human entering: both Lieutenant's "gets a counter" trigger AND Champion's "I get a counter" trigger fire at the same time. You control both — put them on the stack in any order and resolve them sequentially.

Heuristics

  1. 01
    Vial stays at 2 unless you have a specific reason to tick to 3. 20 of your 36 creatures cost 2. Tick to 3 only when you have Mantis Rider, Adeline, or Reflector Mage in hand and need instant-speed deployment. Most games, Vial at 2 is your sweet spot for the entire game.
  2. 02
    Name their best card with Meddling Mage, not their most common card. Name the spell that beats you, not the spell they cast most. Against Storm: name Past in Flames (enables the whole combo), not Desperate Ritual (they have redundant rituals). Against Boros: name Galvanic Discharge (kills your board), not Lightning Bolt (they can play around it).
  3. 03
    Noble Hierarch's exalted triggers on EACH creature attacking alone. If you attack with only Champion and nothing else, Champion gets +1/+1 from Hierarch's exalted. If you have 2 Hierarchs and attack with one creature, it gets +2/+2. But exalted only triggers when exactly one creature attacks — if you attack with Champion AND Lieutenant, nobody gets exalted. Early game: attack with one creature + exalted for maximum damage. Late game: swing wide.
  4. 04
    Jirina's sacrifice is a one-shot — save it for the right moment. Don't sacrifice Jirina preemptively. Wait until they cast the sweeper or aim lethal removal at your key creature, THEN sacrifice in response. Hexproof + indestructible until EOT protects everything. If they don't have a sweeper, Jirina is a 2/2 that pumps Champion and exiles a graveyard on entry. She's never wasted.
  5. 05
    Coppercoat Vanguard does NOT give itself ward — only "each OTHER Human." Vanguard is your most removal-vulnerable creature precisely because it protects everything except itself. Sequence accordingly: deploy Vanguard second (after your T1 play) so they have to choose between removing your 1-drop or Vanguard. If they remove Vanguard, your 1-drop survived. If they leave Vanguard, your entire team now has ward.
  6. 06
    Mockingbird at X=2 copies their best creature — or your best creature. Mockingbird copies any creature with MV ≤ X. At X=2 off Cavern of Souls: copy your own Thalia's Lieutenant (enters, pumps everything again, gets a counter from each Human in play). Or copy their Stoneforge Mystic, Orcish Bowmasters, or any high-value 2-drop. The Bird type is ADDITIONAL — it's a Human Bird if copying a Human. Still triggers Champion.
  7. 07
    Witch Enchanter is a land on T1, a creature on T4. If your opener has Witch Enchanter + no other white source: play Meadow (the land back) as your T1 land, pay 3 life if you need it untapped. If you already have white mana from Cavern/Plains/Courtyard: keep Enchanter in hand for T4 as artifact/enchantment removal. A 2/2 Human that destroys Blood Moon on entry is your primary answer to the card that otherwise ends the game.
  8. 08
    Guide of Souls energy is best spent making a LARGE creature fly, not a small one. Pay {E}{E}{E}: two +1/+1 counters + flying on target attacker. Target your 5/5 Champion, not your 1/1 Guide. A 7/7 flying Champion is game-ending; a 3/3 flying Guide is a minor annoyance. Exception: if their only blocker is a ground creature and Guide is your only attacker, the flying matters more than the counters.
  9. 09
    Thalia and Gaddock Teeg tax YOUR noncreature spells too. Thalia makes your Dismember cost {2} instead of {1} (plus Phyrexian mana). Teeg prevents you from casting Witch Enchanter's front face (MV 4). Be aware of your own taxes before deploying — don't lock yourself out of your own sideboard cards. Sequence: cast Dismember BEFORE deploying Thalia if you need both in the same turn.
  10. 10
    Aether Vial at their end step, every time. Holding Vial activations for their end step is almost always correct. It gives you maximum information (you saw their whole turn), denies them the chance to respond during your main phase, and sets up your next turn's attack with fresh pump triggers. The only exception: Vial in a blocker during their attack step when you need to survive.
  11. 11
    Mantis Rider has haste — deploy it the turn you need to close. Don't play Mantis Rider into open removal mana. Hold it until the turn where 3 flying damage (+ exalted if attacking alone) represents lethal or near-lethal. The haste means they can't play around it — it deals damage the turn it enters. A surprise Mantis Rider off the top on T5 after they've stabilized at 4 life is the reason this card is in the deck.
  12. 12
    Against Blood Moon: fetch your basic Plains and Island IMMEDIATELY. If you suspect Blood Moon (Prowess, Storm, any red deck): lead with your basic lands T1-T2. Seachrome Coast on T1 gives you both colors untapped. Plains T2 ensures {W} for Thalia/Vanguard. Witch Enchanter's back face (Meadow) produces {W}. A resolved Blood Moon with only Ziggurat/Courtyard/Territory in play turns all your lands into Mountains that can't cast Humans.

Classic Shell vs Aang Shell

Two distinct builds are currently putting up results. Your Classic Shell prioritizes acceleration (Hierarch), resilience (Coppercoat ward), and proven disruption (Thalia, Mantis Rider). Zannty's Aang Shell trades that for flash threats (Aang, Swift Savior), hexproof protection (Sylvan Safekeeper), and new tech (Mai, Makdee and Itla). Here's when to run which:

Your Build — Classic Shell
When the Meta Is Spell-Heavy
Run this when: Storm, Living End, Jeskai Blink, and Dimir Control are prevalent. Noble Hierarch accelerates you past their setup turns. Thalia taxes their noncreature-heavy decks. Coppercoat's ward blanket makes their 1-for-1 removal inefficient. Mantis Rider punishes slow starts. The classic shell excels when the opponent's game plan revolves around noncreature spells you can tax and name with Mage.
Zannty's Build — Aang Shell
When the Meta Is Creature-Heavy
Run this when: Boros Energy, Prowess, and creature midrange dominate. Aang, Swift Savior ({1}{U/W} 2/3 flash flying, ETB airbend bounce a creature/spell) provides instant-speed interaction + evasion that Mantis Rider can't match. Sylvan Safekeeper ({G} 1/1, sacrifice a land: target creature gains shroud) protects your key threats at the cost of lands. Mai, Scornful Striker adds aggressive 2-drop pressure. This shell trades disruption for protection and flash tricks.

Manabase Tuning

The Blood Moon Question
2 Witch Enchanter Is Non-Negotiable
Without Witch Enchanter, a resolved Blood Moon turns your entire manabase into Mountains that can't cast Humans (Ziggurat, Courtyard, Territory, Cavern all lose their abilities under Moon). 2 copies of Enchanter means you see one roughly 25% of games. The back face being a Plains-type land means you can play it as mana T1 and still have the Enchanter option later — never cut below 2.
Basic Count
2 Plains + 1 Island Minimum
Your basics survive Blood Moon. 2 Plains covers your white-heavy creature base (Champion, Lieutenant, Vanguard, Guide, Thalia all have {W}). The single Island enables Meddling Mage ({W}{U}), Mariner ({W}{U}), Mockingbird ({X}{U}). Seachrome Coast adds a 4th "real" dual land. Don't go below 3 fetchable basics — Blood Moon and Path to Exile both punish greedy manabases.

Low Supreme Verdict density — Humans folds to sweepers more than targeted removal. You’ve practiced Meddling Mage naming for all expected matchups before the event.
Avoid When
Red Lights
Heavy Supreme Verdict field — sweepers that can’t be Thalia-taxed (Verdict is uncounterable) clear the whole board. Pyroclasm or Anger of the Gods meta — all your creatures die to 2-3 damage. You don’t know the Meddling Mage naming guide for the top 5 expected matchups cold — a wrong name is a 2-mana 2/2 with no text.

SB Slot Priority Order

  1. 01
    Dismember (2 copies) — Kills Ragavan, Phlage, Thought-Knot Seer, and anything with 4 toughness for {B/P}{B/P}. Pays life but doesn’t require colored mana. Goes in vs Boros Energy, Affinity, Eldrazi Tron. Essential creature removal for a creature deck.
  2. 02
    Reflector Mage (2 copies) — Bounces their key creature and prevents recasting for a turn. Goes in vs Eldrazi Tron (bounce TKS before ETB) and Jeskai Blink (bounce their Solitude). Can Vial in at instant speed for surprise tempo.
  3. 03
    Sanctifier en-Vec (2 copies) — Protection from black and red + can’t be targeted by black/red spells. Blanks Dimir’s Fatal Push + Thoughtseize, Boros’s Lightning Bolt + Galvanic Discharge. Goes in vs any BR/UB removal-heavy deck. Makes that creature effectively unkillable by their most common removal.
  4. 04
    Lock creatures (Gaddock Teeg, Lavinia, Drannith Magistrate) — Teeg prevents casting non-creatures with CMC 4+ (shuts off Supreme Verdict, Violent Outburst). Lavinia prevents casting spells without proper mana (shuts Living End cascade). Drannith prevents casting from anywhere except hand (shuts cascade). Bring in per matchup.
  5. 05
    Kataki, War’s Wage + Plague Engineer (flex) — Kataki makes opponents pay {1} per artifact each upkeep (wrecks Affinity). Plague Engineer names a creature type and gives all of that type -1/-1 (Prowess: name Wizard or Human; Affinity: name Construct). Slot based on expected field composition.

Prep Sheet

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Mull any hand without a T1 mana creature. No Hierarch, no Aether Vial = too slow.
  • Vial rule: Vial stays at 2 unless you specifically need 3. 20 of your 36 creatures cost 2.
  • Meddling rule: Name their WIN CONDITION, not their most common card. Past in Flames vs Storm, not Ritual.
  • Jirina rule: Hold Jirina's sacrifice for their sweeper or lethal removal. Don't sacrifice preemptively.
  • Coppercoat rule: Coppercoat does NOT give itself ward. It gives every OTHER Human ward. Plan removal accordingly.
  • Sideboard rule: Know your Kataki target before G2 vs any artifact deck. T2 Kataki off Hierarch is game over.

Testing Framework

Block 01
Vial Timing
Every decision about when to tick Vial and when to use it at end step. Drill it.
Block 02
Meddling Mage Names
Name 20 matchups, state the correct Mage name immediately. Then state the backup. No pausing.
Block 03
Jirina Sacrifice
Drill holding Jirina's sacrifice for the exact right moment. Preemptive sacrifice is the mistake.
Block 04
Tax Stack Math
Practice Thalia + Coppercoat + Mariner tax calculations. Know the exact mana cost of opponent's spells.

Matchday Routine

  1. 01
    Register your Vial count and Cavern naming in hand. Both matter in G1.
  2. 02
    Know your Meddling Mage names before every round. Write them on your deck box.
  3. 03
    Between rounds: did you save Jirina? Preemptive sacrifice is the mistake that costs games.
  4. 04
    Never bring in more than 7 cards sideboard. The main deck is the plan — support it, don't replace it.
  5. 05
    The deck rewards aggressive sequencing. Trust Vial timing and play fast.
