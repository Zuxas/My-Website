Chapter 01

Overview

"Aether Vial flashes in a Meddling Mage naming their win condition at the end of their turn. Then it flashes in Thalia's Lieutenant pumping your whole board. Then it flashes in Norin who blinks in and out all game triggering Champion again and again. This is the most interactive aggro deck in Modern."

5-Color Humans is Modern's premier tribal aggro-disruption deck. It does something no other aggro deck can claim: it applies serious pressure while simultaneously deploying hate pieces that break the opponent's game plan. Meddling Mage names their win condition. Lavinia, Azorius Renegade stops free spells. Unsettled Mariner taxes all their targeted interaction. All while Champion of the Parish and Thalia's Lieutenant grow larger with every Human that enters.

The deck is not tier 1 — and that's not a criticism, it's a feature. It doesn't demand you know every card in the format, but it heavily rewards deep knowledge of your own deck's interactions. The pilot who understands Norin's blink loop, who knows when to Vial at instant speed, and who identifies the right Meddling Mage name in each matchup outperforms the pilot who doesn't by a massive margin. High skill ceiling. Real racing power. Genuine game plan.

This is a pet deck. Play it because it's yours.

Deck Identity

The Engine
Aether Vial
Every other decision in this deck flows from Vial. It lets you hold mana open for interaction (that you don't have) while still deploying threats. It makes your creatures uncounterable. It lets you flash in hate pieces at instant speed on their turn. It's the glue that holds 5 colors together on 19 lands. Every game that doesn't start with a T1 Vial is a harder game.
The Payoffs
Champion + Lieutenant
Champion of the Parish grows with every Human entering. Thalia's Lieutenant enters and pumps everything, then grows with every subsequent Human. Together they create a snowball effect where every Vial activation at instant speed on their turn represents free +1/+1 counters. A mid-game board of five Humans with two Lieutenants in play is often presenting 15+ power.
The Secret Weapon
Norin the Wary
{R} 1/1 Legendary Human. Whenever any player casts a spell OR when any opponent attacks, Norin exiles himself. He returns at the beginning of the next end step. This triggers Champion of the Parish and Thalia's Lieutenant every single turn — both yours and theirs. A resolved Norin with Champion and Lieutenant in play is a +2/+2 pump per turn cycle. For free.
The Disruption Suite
Hate Bears
Meddling Mage names their win condition. Lavinia stops noncreature spells beyond their land count and counters free spells. Unsettled Mariner taxes everything targeting you or your permanents. Jirina sacrifices to give the whole team hexproof and indestructible. The opponent has to fight through creatures AND disruption simultaneously — this is the deck's core tension advantage.
New Additions
Satoru + Guide of Souls
Satoru draws a card whenever Vial cheats in a nontoken creature — since no mana was spent to cast them. Every Vial activation replaces itself. Guide of Souls generates energy and life whenever Humans enter, then converts that energy into +1/+1 counters and flying on attacking creatures. Both dramatically improve the deck's late game staying power.
The Trade-Off
Not Tier 1, Still Real
The deck's weakness is the manabase — 19 lands across 5 colors, no fetches, dependent on Cavern/Unclaimed/Courtyard/Ziggurat. Blood Moon shuts you down hard. It's slower than the fastest decks in the format. But it has a real clock, real disruption, and real resiliency. Expert pilots win tournaments with it. Know your deck. Play your deck.

Oracle Accuracy — Critical Interactions

Aether Vial
{1} · Artifact
At beginning of your upkeep: put a charge counter on it. {T}: put a creature card from your hand onto the battlefield if its mana value equals the number of charge counters. The key: this is NOT casting. The creature enters from the hand bypassing counterspells (except Cavern of Souls naming the type, but Vial doesn't need Cavern — it's already uncounterable). Creatures entering via Vial trigger Satoru (no mana spent to cast). Vial at 2 is the sweet spot — most Humans cost 2.
Norin the Wary
{R} · 1/1 Human Warrior
When you cast a spell or when an opponent attacks, exile Norin. Return him at the beginning of the next end step. Critical: he triggers on CAST, not resolve. If an opponent counters your spell, Norin still exiles and returns. During their turn: they cast a spell (Norin exiles, returns at their end step) → triggers Champion and Lieutenant at end step. Then during your turn: you cast a spell (Norin exiles again, returns at your end step) → more triggers. Two pump cycles per full round.
Satoru, the Infiltrator
{U}{B} · 2/3 Menace Human Ninja Rogue
Whenever Satoru and/or one or more other nontoken creatures enter, if none of them were cast OR no mana was spent to cast them, draw a card. The "no mana spent" clause is the key: creatures put onto the battlefield via Aether Vial cost no mana. Every Vial activation with Satoru in play draws a card. This turns every late-game Vial activation into card advantage, solving the deck's traditional gas problem. One Satoru + active Vial = the most efficient draw engine available in the 75.
Guide of Souls
{W} · 1/2 Human Cleric
Whenever another creature you control enters: gain 1 life and get {E}. Whenever you attack: pay {E}{E}{E}, put two +1/+1 counters and a flying counter on target attacking creature — it becomes an Angel. The life gain is real vs burn decks. The flying pump converts a Champion or Lieutenant that's grown large into an aerial threat that closes games. Energy accumulates fast with a wide board — each Norin return trigger, each Vial activation generates energy.
Jirina, Dauntless General
{W}{B} · 2/2 Human Soldier
When Jirina enters: exile target player's graveyard. Sacrifice Jirina: Humans you control gain hexproof and indestructible until end of turn. The ETB is free GY hate that triggers Champion/Lieutenant. The sacrifice ability is the real gem: in response to a Supreme Verdict or Wrathful Annex, sacrifice Jirina — your entire team becomes indestructible and hexproof. The sweeper does nothing. This single ability shifts the matchup against Control from unfavorable to even.
Lavinia, Azorius Renegade
{W}{U} · 2/2 Human Soldier
Each opponent can't cast noncreature spells with mana value greater than the number of lands they control. Each opponent can't cast spells with {X} in their mana costs unless they pay {X}. Additionally: whenever an opponent casts a spell, if no mana was spent to cast it, counter that spell. This blanket-counters Force of Negation, Evoke Solitude (evoke cost is 0 additional mana), Cascade spells into Living End, and any other free spell. Game-ending against specific matchups.
Witch Enchanter
{3}{W} · 2/2 Human Warlock // Land
Double-faced card: front face is a {3}{W} 2/2 Human Warlock. ETB: destroy target artifact or enchantment an opponent controls. Back face enters as a tapped land that pays 3 life if you want it untapped, producing {W}. The 4-mana front is your primary use — it's your maindeck answer to Blood Moon, Leyline of the Void, Sanctifier en-Vec, Stony Silence, and Damping Sphere. Two copies main ensures you find it in matchups where Blood Moon would otherwise end the game immediately. Can be vial-deployed at 4 counters.
Razorkin Needlehead
{R}{R} · 2/2 Human Assassin
First strike during your turn. Whenever an opponent draws a card, this creature deals 1 damage to them. The passive ping is surprisingly relevant: against Looting effects (Jeskai Blink, UW Control's Snapcaster, Izzet Affinity's draw engine), Razorkin deals 1 damage per draw. In card-draw-heavy matchups it can contribute 3-5 damage over the game for free. First strike during your turn makes it an excellent blocker against aggressive 2/2s — it deals damage before they can. The {R}{R} cost requires managing colors carefully.

Chapter 03

Game
Flow

"Vial on turn 1 changes every decision in the game. Suddenly every end step of theirs is a threat. Every counter they hold could be irrelevant. Every path of play — yours and theirs — runs through that one artifact."

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turn 1
Ideal: Aether Vial. Play it and pass. If you don't have Vial, deploy Champion of the Parish or Norin the Wary. If you have both Vial and a one-drop, consider which is more impactful: Vial on T1 unlocks every subsequent turn, but Champion pumped immediately also snowballs. Generally: Vial first unless your hand has multiple one-drops and Vial can go on T2 without losing a counter cycle.
Turn 2
Vial ticks to 1. Deploy a two-drop normally. Or hold the two-drop and deploy it at instant speed at their end step on turn 2 — this is the Vial move. With Vial at 1: flash in a one-drop (Champion, Norin) at their end step for free counter triggers. With Satoru already in play: every Vial deployment at instant speed now draws a card. Prioritize getting Satoru into play if Vial is active.
Turn 3
Vial at 2 — your engine is online. Two-drops via Vial at instant speed are the backbone of the deck. End of their turn: flash in Meddling Mage naming their key card, or Thalia's Lieutenant to pump your board before untapping and attacking. This is the turn where you want both Vial firing AND a normal land-based deployment happening simultaneously. Two threats deployed in one turn cycle.
Turn 4+
Vial at 3-4 for silver bullets. Vial at 3 hits Jirina — flash her in at their end step to exile their GY and trigger Champion/Lieutenant. Vial at 4 hits Witch Enchanter — instant-speed artifact/enchantment removal. The key mid-game skill: keep Vial at 2 for longer if your hand is full of two-drops. Tick it once more intentionally when you need to deploy a higher-cost silver bullet from hand.
Norin Loop
Once Norin resolves, every turn is free value. Their turn: they cast a spell → Norin exiles → Norin returns at their end step → Champion and Lieutenant trigger. Your turn: you cast any spell → Norin exiles → returns at your end step → another trigger. With Champion + Lieutenant + Norin in play: you're gaining 2 counters per turn cycle for zero mana. This is the deck's hidden engine and why the deck can win games that feel impossible to close.

Mulligan Framework

Keep
Vial + 2 Lands + Any 2-Drop
Any hand with Aether Vial + 2 lands is a keep. The rest doesn't matter much — you'll deploy Vial, hit land drops 2-3, and start Vial-cheating creatures from turn 2 onward. This hand doesn't need Champion or Lieutenant specifically; Vial is the engine that makes everything else work.
Keep
Champion + Norin + 2 Lands
Without Vial but with Champion + Norin + any white/red land source, you have the core engine. Cast Champion T1, cast Norin T2. Now Champion triggers every time any spell is cast or opponent attacks. Even without Vial this hand generates snowball counters and creates a real racing clock by turn 4.
Marginal Keep
2 Lands + Disruption Heavy
A hand heavy on Meddling Mage + Lavinia + Unsettled Mariner but light on Champions is borderline. Keep on the play when you know the opponent's game plan well enough to name the right cards. Mulligan on the draw unless the disruption is perfectly matched to the matchup — you need a clock to back up the disruption.
Mulligan
0-1 Lands or No Vial + All Expensive
Without Vial and with a hand of Adeline + Witch Enchanter + 1 land, you're looking at a turn 4-5 first play with zero mana. Mulligan aggressively. This deck is built around the first 3 turns — a hand that can't do anything before turn 3 is a loss in most matchups.

Key Decision Points

Vial Timing
End Step vs Main Phase
Default: flash in the threat at their end step, not your main phase. This forces them to react during their next upkeep, not yours. They can't do anything between your deploy and your attack. Exception: if the ETB trigger is time-sensitive (Jirina's GY exile, Meddling Mage naming before they draw), deploy immediately.
Meddling Mage
Name the Right Card
The most skill-expressive decision in the deck. Name their primary win condition in game 1, not their answer. Against Amulet Titan: name Primeval Titan. Against Ruby Storm: name Past in Flames. Against Living End: name Living End. Against Neoform: name Neoform. Against Control: name their sweeper (Supreme Verdict). Every matchup section below has the correct name listed.
Jirina Sacrifice
Hold for Sweepers
Don't sacrifice Jirina proactively. Hold her sacrifice ability until they cast a sweeper (Supreme Verdict, Wrath of the Skies, Anger of the Gods). Sacrifice Jirina in response — your entire board gains hexproof and indestructible until end of turn. The sweeper resolves and does nothing. This window only exists if you've kept Jirina alive, so protect her carefully.
Chapter 04

Core
Engines

1
Aether Vial + Satoru Draw Engine
Primary Advantage Engine
Setup
Vial T1, Satoru T3
Play Aether Vial on turn 1. Tick to 2. On turn 3 or via Vial (Satoru costs {U}{B} = 2), deploy Satoru, the Infiltrator. Now every subsequent Vial activation draws a card. Satoru triggers when nontoken creatures enter if no mana was spent to cast them — Vial cheats creatures in for free. One Satoru + Vial = card draw on demand.
Execution
Instant-Speed Draw
During their turn at end step: tap Vial to flash in any two-drop. With Satoru in play: draw a card. The creature enters, Champion/Lieutenant trigger if relevant, and you replace the card spent. Your hand never depletes. Against control decks with Counterspell up: you're spending no mana and replacing your card — there is nothing to counter. This is the late-game inevitability engine the deck previously lacked.
Result
Inexhaustible Threats
A Satoru + Vial mid-game means you draw into more threats every turn cycle. Each drawn card is likely another Human, which pumps Champion and Lieutenant further. Against fair decks that trade removal for your threats, this engine refuels faster than they can deplete your hand. Three Vial activations over three turns = three draws, three enters triggers, three Counter pumps.
2
Champion + Lieutenant Pump Engine
Primary Damage Engine
Setup
T1 Champion, T2 Lieutenant
Champion of the Parish enters as a 1/1 and gains +1/+1 every time a Human enters under your control. Thalia's Lieutenant enters, immediately pumps every other Human +1/+1, then gains counters whenever another Human enters. T1 Champion + T2 Lieutenant: on Lieutenant's entry, Champion gains +1/+1 (now 2/2). Every subsequent Human pumps both.
Execution
Stack the Triggers
When multiple Humans enter simultaneously (via Adeline's attack trigger creating tokens, or multiple Vial + cast in the same turn): each Human entering is a separate trigger for both Champion and Lieutenant. Order them correctly — each Human entering triggers Champion once and Lieutenant once. With 4 Humans entering on one turn: Champion grows by 4 and Lieutenant grows by 4.
Result
Unmanageable Threats
By turn 4-5, Champion and Lieutenant are often 5/5+ or 6/6+. They outgrow Galvanic Discharge (3 damage cap), they outgrow Lightning Bolt, and they outgrow most one-for-one removal the format offers. Guide of Souls can pump one of them further with energy for flying. A turn-5 8/8 Champion with flying via Guide of Souls closes games in two swings.
3
Norin the Wary Loop
Free Value Engine
Setup
Norin + Champion/Lieutenant
Norin the Wary costs {R}. He exiles himself whenever any player casts a spell OR when any opponent attacks, then returns at the beginning of the next end step. In a game where spells are being cast every turn and opponents are attacking, Norin blinks in and out constantly — triggering Champion and Lieutenant every time he returns.
Execution
Two Triggers Per Round
Their turn: opponent casts a spell → Norin exiles → Norin returns at their end step → Champion and Lieutenant trigger (+1/+1 each). Your turn: you cast any spell → Norin exiles → Norin returns at your end step → Champion and Lieutenant trigger again (+1/+1 each). Net: +2/+2 on Champion, +2/+2 on Lieutenant per complete round, for zero additional mana investment.
Result
Inevitable Snowball
A Norin + Champion + Lieutenant board that survives to turn 5 has often generated 6-8 free +1/+1 counters total. With Guide of Souls generating energy from each Norin return, you're also accumulating flying counters. The opponent must answer Norin because ignoring him costs them 2-4 damage per round from the passive pump — even though Norin himself is a 1/1 that never attacks.

Tricks & Interactions

  • Vial bypasses Counterspell entirely. Creatures deployed via Aether Vial are not cast — they are put directly onto the battlefield. Counterspell, Force of Negation, Remand, Mana Leak, and all other counterspells have zero interaction with Vial. Against blue decks: Vial every critical creature, never tap mana to cast them into open countermagic.
  • Cavern of Souls + Vial = true zero-counter insurance. Cavern names "Human" and makes Human spells uncounterable when cast. Vial makes creatures uncounterable by not casting at all. Together: they provide two completely different routes to deploy threats that cannot be countered. Use whichever route is appropriate based on what you need to deploy.
  • Norin triggers on the CAST, not the resolve. If they counter your spell, Norin still exiles (because you cast a spell) and still returns (triggering Champion). Their counter doesn't stop the Norin loop — it only stops the spell.
  • Jirina's sacrifice ability is an instant-speed sweeper counter. In response to Supreme Verdict (or any sweeper): sacrifice Jirina. All your Humans gain hexproof and indestructible until end of turn. The sweeper resolves and does absolutely nothing. You retain your entire board. This is the single most important combat/interaction trick in the deck.
  • Sylvan Safekeeper gives targeted shroud. Sacrifice a land: target creature gains shroud until end of turn. Against Path to Exile, Swords to Plowshares, Fatal Push, Abrupt Decay — sacrifice a land to protect your most important creature. The land is a real cost but protecting a 6/6 Champion from a removal spell is often worth it. Keep Safekeeper alive for the matchups where targeted removal is their primary answer.
  • Adeline's power = number of creatures you control. Adeline's power scales with board size and she creates attacking tokens equal to opponents attacked. With 5 other creatures and Adeline attacking: she's a 6/4, and she creates an additional 1/1 Human Soldier token also attacking. These tokens trigger Champion and Lieutenant. Adeline works best when your board is already developed — don't deploy her into an empty battlefield.
  • Ancient Ziggurat and Unclaimed Territory only cast creatures. These lands produce mana only for casting creature spells. You cannot use them for Aether Vial's {1} activation cost — Vial's activation is not casting a creature spell. Keep Plains or Island for non-creature spell costs (Vial activation, etc.). The basics are critical.
  • Witch Enchanter via Vial at 4 is instant-speed artifact/enchantment removal. If you've ticked Vial to 4 (takes 4 upkeeps), you can flash in Witch Enchanter during their turn to destroy their Blood Moon, Leyline of the Void, or Damping Sphere in response to it resolving or at their end step. Plan your Vial counter count accordingly in matchups where you expect these.

Chapter 08

Heuristics

  1. Aether Vial turn 1 takes priority over everything. If your opening hand has Vial and any other play, Vial goes down first. The compounding value of Vial — uncounterable deployments, instant-speed threats, Satoru draws — outweighs any single creature you could cast instead. The only exception: if the game is clearly a turn-3 race and you need to deploy an answer immediately.
  2. Know your Meddling Mage names before round 1. Every matchup has a correct name. The list: vs Amulet Titan → Primeval Titan. vs Ruby Storm → Past in Flames. vs Living End → Living End (or Violent Outburst). vs UW Control → Supreme Verdict. vs Boros Energy → Galvanic Discharge. vs Prowess → Cori-Steel Cutter. vs Yawgmoth → Yawgmoth, Thran Physician. Study these between rounds. Misnamed Mage is a wasted two-drop.
  3. Hold Jirina's sacrifice ability until they cast a sweeper. The temptation is to sacrifice Jirina early for the ETB GY exile value, but her sacrifice ability — giving all your Humans hexproof and indestructible — is one of the most powerful responses to sweepers in Modern. A single Jirina can make their Supreme Verdict a complete blank. Don't burn this proactively; wait for the sweeper.
  4. Lavinia counters Living End via Cascade — deploy her before their kill turn. When Violent Outburst cascades into Living End, Living End is cast without spending mana. Lavinia's trigger fires and counters it. This is only active if Lavinia is in play before they cascade. Flash her in via Vial at their end step before their turn 3 or whenever you expect cascade. One Lavinia in play makes this matchup dramatically swing in your favor.
  5. Vial at end of their turn, not your main phase. Default to flashing in threats at their end step. This maximizes the pressure window — the creature attacks the turn it enters (your turn is next), and they can't react between your deployment and your attack. Main phase deployment is correct when the ETB trigger is time-sensitive (Jirina GY exile, Reflector Mage bounce before combat).
  6. Norin the Wary triggers on cast — counters still trigger his loop. If they counter your spell, Norin already exiled when you cast it. He returns at end step regardless of whether the spell resolved. Their counterspell does not stop the Norin loop. Never hesitate to cast spells into countermagic when Norin is in play — you're getting free pump triggers either way.
  7. Ancient Ziggurat and Unclaimed Territory can't pay for Vial activations. These lands produce mana only for casting creature spells. Aether Vial's activated ability is not casting a creature — it's putting one onto the battlefield. Keep at least one Plains or Island available in your land base for non-creature costs. Missing a Vial activation because you tapped your basics for spells is a common mistake.
  8. Sylvan Safekeeper protects your key threats — use it proactively in removal-heavy matchups. Sacrifice a land: target creature you control gains shroud until end of turn. In matchups where Path to Exile or Fatal Push is the primary answer to your Champions, using Safekeeper to blank their removal on your most important creature is correct even at the cost of a land. A 6/6 Champion surviving costs one land — a killed Champion costs the whole game.
  9. Satoru draws on every Vial activation — deploy Satoru as early as possible in grindy matchups. Against decks with lots of removal, Satoru + Vial means you never run out of cards. Every Vial deployment replaces itself. The earlier Satoru enters, the more card draw you accumulate over the game. If you're in a matchup where the game goes beyond turn 5, Satoru is often more valuable than the creature you could deploy instead.
  10. Guide of Souls converts energy into flying on your biggest threat. Energy accumulates passively from every creature entering. With Norin's constant triggers and a wide board, you often have 6+ energy by turn 4 without spending any. At 3 energy during your attack step: put a +1/+1 counter and flying counter on your largest attacker — Champion or Lieutenant becomes a 7/7 flier. This closes games that were otherwise stalled behind ground blockers.
  11. Adeline's power = number of creatures you control — deploy her with a board, not into an empty field. Adeline as a 1/4 into an empty battlefield is near-useless. Adeline as a 5/4 into a board of four Humans creates five attacking tokens on her attack, each triggering Champion and Lieutenant. Build the board first, then deploy Adeline as a board-widening finisher. The tokens she creates are Humans — they feed the entire engine.
  12. Cavern of Souls + Vial = two independent routes to uncounterable deployment. Use both simultaneously in blue matchups. Cavern makes casting uncounterable. Vial makes entering the battlefield without casting. Against a player holding Force of Negation, Counterspell, and Mana Leak: neither Cavern-cast nor Vial-deployed creatures can be stopped. They will waste countermagic on nothing if you deploy via Vial while they expect a Cavern cast.
  13. Damping Sphere in the SB is not just for Storm — it also destroys Amulet Titan. Damping Sphere makes all lands produce only colorless mana. Amulet Titan's entire combo requires colored mana from bounce lands — Simic Growth Chamber produces {G}{U}, Gruul Turf produces {R}{G}. Under Damping Sphere these produce {C}. Amulet of Vigor's untap trigger still fires but the colored mana requirement means their combo produces nothing useful. Board in vs Titan and vs Storm.
  14. Reflector Mage from the SB is Vial'd at 3 — plan your Vial count when you know the matchup. Reflector Mage costs {1}{W}{U} = CMC 3. Vial needs to be at 3 counters to deploy it. That takes 3 upkeep triggers from when Vial entered. If you know you're bringing in Reflector Mage for game 2, consider ticking Vial to 3 instead of keeping it at 2. The decision point: will you need more 2-drops (keep at 2) or do you need Mage deployed this game (tick to 3)?
  15. Witch Enchanter is your maindeck answer to Blood Moon — protect her or deploy her immediately. Two Witch Enchanters main means you have access to instant-speed Blood Moon destruction via Vial at 4. Against decks that might have Blood Moon (Boros Energy, any red deck sideboarding it), tick Vial to 4 deliberately in the early turns. The moment they deploy Blood Moon: tap Vial to flash in Witch Enchanter, destroy it before your colored lands become dead. She's your insurance policy.
  16. Gaddock Teeg from the SB also stops YOUR Witch Enchanter — don't deploy both. Gaddock Teeg prevents casting spells with CMC 4+. Witch Enchanter costs CMC 4. If you deploy Teeg vs Control to stop Supreme Verdict, you also lose the ability to cast Witch Enchanter from hand. Vial can still deploy Witch Enchanter (Vial bypasses casting restrictions). But plan around this interaction — don't strand Witch Enchanters in hand with Teeg in play unless Vial is at 4.
