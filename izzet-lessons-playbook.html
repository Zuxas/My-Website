Chapter 01

Deck
Overview

"This deck won the 2025 Magic World Championship. It is Tier 1 at 8.54% of the meta. You are playing the most technically demanding deck in Standard — and when executed correctly, one of the most powerful."

Izzet Lessons is a Combo-Control deck built around a single mechanic: the Lessons system. Lesson cards (Accumulate Wisdom, Firebending Lesson, Abandon Attachments, Combustion Technique, Iroh's Demonstration, Boomerang Basics) are instants and sorceries that also carry the Lesson subtype. As they land in your graveyard, two things happen simultaneously: Gran-Gran (your only creature) makes all your noncreature spells cost {1} less, and Accumulate Wisdom upgrades from "look at top 3, pick 1" to "look at top 3, put ALL 3 in hand." Three Lessons in the graveyard is the threshold that activates everything. Get there by turn 3-4 and the deck takes over.

The win condition is Monument to Endurance. This 3-mana artifact reads: whenever you discard a card, choose one that hasn't been chosen this turn — draw a card, create a Treasure, or opponent loses 3 life. You discard every time Artist's Talent or Gran-Gran triggers. Each discard generates value. In the late game with Artist's Talent at level 3 (+2 to all noncombat damage sources you control), each Monument "opponent loses 3" activation deals 5 instead. With enough Treasure generation and card draw fueling your hand, you drain them out of the game while generating resources they can't keep up with.

The Lesson Threshold
3 Lessons in GY = Everything Changes
Gran-Gran's cost reduction activates. Accumulate Wisdom draws all 3 cards instead of 1. You've crossed the threshold that makes this deck play at a different level from everything else. Getting to 3 Lessons in GY by turn 3 is the game plan every game — the rest of the deck is built to enable and protect this threshold.
The Monument Engine
Each Discard = Draw / Treasure / 3 Drain
Monument to Endurance converts your discard outlets into a value engine. Artist's Talent forces a discard every noncreature spell. Gran-Gran taps to discard. Each time you discard, Monument triggers and you choose one of three modes — draw replaces the card, Treasure gives mana, drain depletes them. You cycle through modes each discard, so every spell you cast is dealing 3 damage and drawing a card and generating Treasure simultaneously across multiple Monument triggers.
The Backup Plan
Reactive Control Without the Engine
When the engine isn't assembled, you're a reactive control deck with efficient instant-speed removal: Firebending Lesson (2 or 5 damage to creature), Combustion Technique (2+ damage scales with Lessons, exiles on death), Iroh's Demonstration (board wipe or 4 damage). These buy time to assemble the engine. Never purely reactive — always working toward the Lesson threshold while managing their board.

Why Izzet Lessons Is Tier 1

At RC Turin (1,025 players), Izzet Lessons had 5 of the 16 Top 8 placements (Izzet Lesson ×5 in Top 16 per the bracket). At RC Cincinnati and DC, expect to face this deck in roughly 1 in 12 rounds — more at the top tables where skill-intensive decks concentrate. Three specific matchup insights: vs Izzet Prowess (your worst matchup) — Gran-Gran must die immediately or never connect, their aggressive clock beats your setup time. vs Mono-Green Landfall — you are favored, their ramp plan is slower than your card advantage engine. vs Dimir Midrange — highly favorable, your enchantments (Artist's Talent) and artifacts (Monument) survive their creature removal suite entirely.

GY Threshold

The 3-mana slot is where the deck's core engine lives: Monument to Endurance ({3}, reduced to {2} by Gran-Gran/Artist's Talent at threshold), Accumulate Wisdom ({3} drawing 3 at threshold). The deck is built to cross the Lesson threshold in turns 2-3 and then operate everything off 4-5 lands for the rest of the game. 25 lands is correct — you want to hit 3 lands by turn 3 but not flood.

Card Notes

The Only Creature
Gran-Gran
{1}{U} 1/2 Legendary Human Peasant Ally. Whenever Gran-Gran becomes tapped, draw a card then discard a card. Noncreature spells you cast cost {1} less as long as there are 3+ Lesson cards in your GY. Gran-Gran tapping draws a card — triggering Monument to Endurance. She's a tap ability, not an ETB — Torpor Orb doesn't affect her. She is a 1/2 with no keywords; she dies to everything. Kill her with Burst Lightning ({R}, 2 damage) exactly. She must be protected. Opponents will always attack Gran-Gran first. Plan around keeping her alive: never tap her unless Monument is in play to capture the discard value.
The Win Condition
Monument to Endurance
{3} Artifact. Whenever you discard a card, choose one that hasn't been chosen this turn: draw a card / create a Treasure token / each opponent loses 3 life. The three modes cycle — one draw, one Treasure, one drain per three discards in a turn. With Artist's Talent at level 2 (spells cost {1} less) and Gran-Gran's cost reduction, Monument often costs {1} to deploy once the engine is live. Artist's Talent level 3 adds +2 to all noncombat damage you deal — "opponent loses 3" becomes "opponent loses 5." With multiple discard triggers per turn, Monument drains 3-15 life per turn while generating cards and Treasure.
The Cost Reducer + Discard Outlet
Artist's Talent
{R} Enchantment — Class. L1 (base): whenever you cast a noncreature spell, you may discard a card, if you do draw a card. {2}{R}: Level 2 — noncreature spells cost {1} less. {2}{R}: Level 3 — noncombat damage sources you control deal +2. Level 1 creates a discard-draw loop: cast any spell → discard → draw (net zero but triggers Monument). Level 2 stacks with Gran-Gran's threshold reduction — two {1} less reductions means most spells cost {2} less in the late game. Level 3 is the Monument kill: every "lose 3 life" becomes "lose 5 life."
The Draw Engine
Accumulate Wisdom
{2}{U} Instant — Lesson. Look at top 3 cards; put 1 in hand, rest on bottom. OR with 3+ Lessons in GY: put ALL 3 in hand. This is the card that rewards reaching the Lesson threshold. At threshold, Accumulate Wisdom for {2}{U} (or {1}{U} with reductions) draws 3 cards — comparable to an Ancestral Recall. It's an instant — cast it at their end step after they tap out for full value while holding your mana for responses.
The Board Control
Combustion Technique
{2}{R} Instant — Lesson. Deal damage = 2 + (number of Lesson cards in your GY) to target creature. If that creature would die this turn, exile it instead. Early game: 2 damage (no Lessons in GY). With 3 Lessons: 5 damage. With 5 Lessons: 7 damage. Scales to handle any threat in the format. The exile clause prevents graveyard recursion and stops death triggers. This is your catch-all removal that kills everything from a 1/1 Otter to a 5/4 Leatherhead as your GY fills. Being a Lesson, it also contributes to your own threshold count.
The Flexible Draw
Abandon Attachments
{1}{U} Instant — Lesson. You may discard a card. If you do, draw 2 cards. Discard 1 draw 2 = net +1 card for {1}{U}. Triggers Monument to Endurance (discard = choose draw/Treasure/drain). With Monument in play: you cast Abandon Attachments for {0} or {U} (with reductions), discard to draw 2, Monument triggers for a mode, AND the Abandon Attachments itself goes to GY as a Lesson. Net result: one spell cast = one Monument trigger + one card drawn. The cheapest way to cycle through your deck while fueling Monument simultaneously.
The Removal
Firebending Lesson
{R} Instant — Lesson (kicker {4}). 2 damage to target creature. Or 5 damage kicked. Early game: {R} kills anything with 2 or less toughness (Llanowar Elves, Spyglass Siren, Slickshot Show-Off). With kicker {4}{R}: kills anything with 5 or less toughness. In the late game with cost reductions, the kicked version becomes {3}{R} — reasonable 5-damage premium removal. Being a Lesson, every Firebending Lesson cast contributes to your GY count and gets you closer to the threshold. Don't hold it — deploy it immediately on their key creatures.
The Board Wipe / Spot Removal
Iroh's Demonstration
{3}{R}{U} Sorcery — Lesson. Two modes: deal 1 damage to each creature opponents control (sweeper for 1/1s), OR deal 4 damage to target creature (removal). The sweeper mode clears Otter tokens from Stormchaser's Talent, Warrior tokens from Boros, and 1/1 lifelinkers from Mono-White. The 4-damage mode kills most relevant threats in the format. As a 5-mana spell, it hits the cost reduction from Gran-Gran (threshold active) and Artist's Talent level 2 — often casts for {3} in the mid-game. Being a Lesson, it also advances your GY threshold count.
The Counter / Bounce
It'll Quench Ya!
{U} Instant. Counter target spell unless its controller pays {2}. Soft counter at one mana — functions as a tax that opponents often can't pay on curve. On turn 2-3 when they're casting their key spells (Kaito, Stormchaser's Talent, Sapling Nursery) and using all their mana, {U} to make them pay {2} extra is effectively a hard counter. In the late game it loses value — experienced opponents will have spare mana. Use It'll Quench Ya! proactively on their curve, not reactively when they have mana to burn.
Chapter 03

Gameplan

"Your first three turns build the Lesson threshold. Your next three turns build the Monument engine. Your final two turns drain them out."

The Three Phases

Phase 1 — Turns 1–3: Threshold
Get 3 Lessons in GY
Deploy Gran-Gran on T2 (your only creature — she's the engine). Cast any Lesson spell that solves a board problem: Firebending Lesson kills their T1 creature, Abandon Attachments draws cards and fills GY. T3: you now have 2-3 Lessons in GY. If not, prioritize deploying Artist's Talent (also triggers on every noncreature spell). With 3 Lessons in GY, Gran-Gran gives {1} cost reduction and Accumulate Wisdom draws 3.
Phase 2 — Turns 4–6: Engine
Deploy Monument + Artist's Talent
T4: cast Monument to Endurance (reduced to {2} with Gran-Gran + Artist's Talent L2 active). Now every discard triggers three rotating modes. T5-6: Artist's Talent levels up — L2 makes spells cost {1} less (stacks with Gran-Gran), L3 makes "lose 3 life" into "lose 5 life." At this point you're drawing cards, generating Treasure, and dealing 3-5 life per trigger across multiple discards per turn.
Phase 3 — Turns 7+: Drain
Monument + Cast Spells = Win
With Monument + Artist's Talent L3 active: each noncreature spell you cast triggers Artist's Talent → discard → draw (net zero cards) → Monument triggers "opponent loses 5." Each Gran-Gran tap → discard → Monument triggers "lose 5." With 3-4 spells per turn, that's 15-20 damage per turn through noncombat drain. They can't race it. The Treasure generation from Monument keeps your mana growing to cast more spells.

The Ideal Turn Sequence

On the Play (Aggressive Start)
T1 Lesson → T2 Gran-Gran → T3 Artist's Talent → T4 Monument
T1: land + Firebending Lesson on their creature (1 Lesson in GY). T2: land + Gran-Gran. T3: land + Artist's Talent (L1 immediately active) + cast Abandon Attachments (2nd Lesson in GY, discard 1 draw 2). T4: 3 Lessons in GY, Gran-Gran cost reduction active. Cast Monument for {2}. Now every spell you cast triggers Artist's Talent → discard → Monument → choose draw/Treasure/drain. T5: Accumulate Wisdom draws 3 cards, refuels your engine.
On the Draw (Reactive Start)
Hold It'll Quench Ya! → Deploy Gran-Gran Safe
On the draw, they have one more turn of setup than you. Hold {U} for It'll Quench Ya! on their T2 key spell (Badgermole Cub, Stormchaser's Talent). T2: deploy Gran-Gran if they didn't deploy a threat, or hold if they have open attack mana targeting Gran-Gran. T3-4: prioritize Artist's Talent over Monument — the cost reduction from level 2 is more important than Monument's engine when you need mana efficiency to catch up. Deploy Monument T4-5 once Artist's Talent is leveled.

Disruption
Lines

"Your only creature is a 1/2 with no keywords. Protect Gran-Gran like your game depends on it — because it does."

Protecting Gran-Gran

Gran-Gran is a 1/2 with no evasion and no keywords. Every opponent knows she's your engine and will spend removal on her immediately. She dies to: Burst Lightning (2 damage), any 2/2 that blocks her, any combat targeting, Requiting Hex (MV 2). Your protection options:

Protection Tools
How to Keep Gran-Gran Alive
It'll Quench Ya! counters targeted removal for {U} — hold it specifically for Burst Lightning responses and Requiting Hex. Don't tap Gran-Gran until Monument is in play to capture the discard value. Against Izzet Prowess, their first priority is always killing Gran-Gran with Burst Lightning — hold It'll Quench Ya! on T2 when they have {R} open. If Gran-Gran dies, she can be recast for {1}{U} (or less with reductions) — you have 4 copies. Don't over-commit to protecting her at the cost of your own tempo.
Gran-Gran's Tap Timing
Only Tap When Monument Is In Play
Gran-Gran tapping draws a card then forces a discard. Without Monument in play, that discard is pure loss (you discard a real card). With Monument in play, that discard is: draw (from tap) + discard (to Monument) = draw 1 draw/Treasure/drain. The tap becomes a three-effect action. Never tap Gran-Gran before Monument is deployed unless you're desperate for a card — the forced discard without Monument is always losing a card you need.

Monument Mode Sequencing

Monument to Endurance's three modes (draw, Treasure, opponent loses 3) can only each be chosen once per turn. With multiple discards in one turn, you cycle through all three automatically. Here's how to maximize each discard:

First Discard
Opponent Loses 3 Life (or 5 with L3)
Always use "opponent loses 3" as your first mode in any turn where you have multiple discards. You want damage applied early in the turn so subsequent discards cycle to Treasure (mana) and draw (cards). If you only have one discard this turn, choose based on what you need: low on mana → Treasure, low on cards → draw, opponent at low life → drain.
Second Discard
Create a Treasure Token
Treasure on the second discard gives you mana to continue casting spells this turn — which then trigger Artist's Talent → more discards → more Monument modes. The Treasure is self-sustaining: it pays for the next spell which triggers the next discard which generates another mode. With 3+ spells in a turn, Monument can generate 1-2 Treasures that pay for additional spells in the same turn, creating a loop.
Third Discard
Draw a Card
The draw mode replaces the discarded card. In a turn where you're casting Artist's Talent-triggered discards, this means every three spells you cast = net zero card loss + dealt 3-5 drain damage + generated 1 Treasure. The deck sustains itself through these Monument cycles. With level 3 Artist's Talent active, "lose 3" becomes "lose 5" — cycle shifts to always prioritize drain first.

Accumulate Wisdom — Instant Speed Draw-3

Accumulate Wisdom is your most powerful spell when the Lesson threshold is reached. Always cast it at instant speed on their end step:

Why End Step
Draw 3, Hold Mana for Responses
If you cast Accumulate Wisdom during your main phase, you draw 3 cards and immediately have them — but you've already passed your window to use them this turn. Cast at their end step instead: draw 3 cards, then untap with a full hand AND full mana. The 3 cards you drew are now available to cast next turn or hold as removal/counters. You also add Accumulate Wisdom to your GY (advancing or maintaining your Lesson threshold).
Pre-Threshold vs Post-Threshold
Different Cards, Same Slot
Before threshold (0-2 Lessons in GY): Accumulate Wisdom for {2}{U} draws 1 card — not worth it unless desperate. After threshold (3+ Lessons): draws 3 for {2}{U} (or {1}{U} with reductions) — cast it every time it's in your hand. The threshold flips this from a weak cantrip to the best draw spell in Standard. Track your Lesson count every turn to know when the upgrade is active.

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

Learn — Discard-Draw or Fetch from Sideboard
"Learn" lets you choose: discard a card then draw, OR reveal a Lesson card from outside the game (sideboard) and put it into your hand. In competitive play, "outside the game" = your sideboard. This means your sideboard does double duty: 15 slots for traditional SB cards AND Lesson targets. Budget your SB Lesson slots carefully — every Lesson slot is one fewer traditional SB card.
Plot Spells Trigger Prowess When Cast from Exile
Plotted spells are cast from exile as sorceries on a later turn. Casting IS casting — it triggers prowess, magecraft, "whenever you cast a noncreature spell." Plotting itself (exiling the card from hand) is NOT casting — it doesn't trigger anything. The power play: plot a spell T2, cast it for free T3 alongside other spells for maximum prowess chain.
Burst Lightning Kicker — Plan for Late Game
"Kicker {4}: deals 4 damage instead of 2." The kicked version costs {4}{R} total (5 mana). In the early game: 2 damage for {R} is efficient. In the late game: 4 damage for {4}{R} kills most midrange threats and can close the game pointed at face. Kicker is an additional cost — if the spell is countered, you still paid {4}{R}. The full 4 damage only applies if it resolves.
Monastery Swiftspear — Prowess Is +1/+1, Not +2/+0
Swiftspear gets +1/+1 per noncreature spell (prowess). Slickshot gets +2/+0 per noncreature spell (NOT prowess). The distinction matters: Swiftspear becomes a 4/4 after 3 spells (survives 3 damage). Slickshot becomes a 9/1 after 3 spells (dies to ANY 1 damage). In combat: Swiftspear survives trades better. Slickshot threatens more lethal damage. Know which creature is which when doing combat math.
Kaito Shizuki — Phases Out at Your End Step If He Entered This Turn
"At the beginning of your end step, if Kaito entered this turn, he phases out." Phasing out = Kaito ceases to exist until your next untap step, then phases back in. He keeps all counters, attachments, etc. This makes Kaito near-impossible to remove the turn he enters — he dodges sorcery-speed removal and most sweepers. He phases back in on YOUR untap (before your upkeep), ready to activate a loyalty ability immediately.

Role Assignment

"Before T1, decide: are you the control player or the combo player? Izzet Lessons can be both — but never at the same time."
Combo
Default role. Spend turns 1-3 filling your GY with Lessons, cross the 3-Lesson threshold, then execute the Monument drain loop. You are racing to a specific board state.
vs Control · vs Midrange · vs any deck without a T3 clock
Control
Take this role when they threaten to kill you before you reach threshold. Spend early turns on removal — Firebending Lesson, Combustion Technique, Sear. Delay their clock, then resume combo once the board is manageable.
vs Izzet Prowess · vs Boros Aggro · vs any T3 clock deck
Tempo
Against decks you're faster than but can't fully ignore. Use Boomerang Basics to bounce their key permanent and buy the one turn needed to hit threshold. Play one disruptive spell, then back to combo.
vs Mono-Green Landfall · vs slow ramp · vs any deck with one key permanent

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • Staying Combo against Prowess. If they have a 5/5 attacking by T3, you can't afford to pass the turn deploying Gran-Gran without holding up removal. Switch to Control — deal with their board first, reach threshold second.
  • Over-committing to Control. Casting too many removal spells delays your Lesson threshold. Every Firebending Lesson used as removal is one GY Lesson — that's the silver lining. Every non-Lesson removal spell delays threshold with no GY benefit. Prioritize Lesson-type removal when possible.
  • Deploying Artist's Talent into open mana. Artist's Talent at sorcery speed leaves you tapped. If they have removal, Gran-Gran dies on the stack or immediately after. Never deploy Artist's Talent when they have removal mana open.
  • Ignoring Soul-Guide Lantern. Lantern resets your entire engine. Even when playing Combo mode, always keep a counterspell available when Lantern could hit your GY. The role doesn't change — but your threat assessment does.

Gran-Gran protection: Dimir specifically runs Requiting Hex which exiles MV ≤ 2 creatures — Gran-Gran is MV 2, she dies to Hex. Hold It'll Quench Ya! to counter their Hex on Gran-Gran. Their Deep-Cavern Bat ETB strips a card from your hand — if you have Monument in hand, deploy it BEFORE Bat can strip it. Their Kaito, Bane of Nightmares has hexproof on their turn, but Combustion Technique deals damage to creatures — it doesn't "target" in the same sense, check the oracle. Actually Combustion Technique says "deal damage to target creature" — Kaito's hexproof on your turn prevents you from targeting him. Use Boomerang Basics (return target creature/enchantment you own OR opponent's nonland permanent) — wait, Boomerang Basics returns nonland permanents you DON'T own too if you give the fish. Gift the fish to bounce Kaito during your turn (you control Boomerang Basics but Kaito has hexproof during their turn not yours).

Bant RhythmRace + CounterUnfavorableTheir Craterhoof Behemoth one-shots you through any board. Disdainful Stroke is your answer — counter Craterhoof before it enters.

Bant Rhythm is your hardest matchup outside of Izzet Prowess. Their plan: ramp via Llanowar Elves + Badgermole Cub + Nature's Rhythm into Ouroboroid (pumps all your creatures each combat) + Craterhoof Behemoth (all creatures get trample + +X/+X where X = creature count). With 8 creatures and Craterhoof, that's +8/+8 trample on everything — a single attack can deal 60+ damage. You have no board to block with (one 1/2 Gran-Gran) and no sweeper that handles their large bodies without the GY threshold built.

Your plan: Counter Craterhoof Behemoth with Disdainful Stroke ({1}{U}, counter MV 4+ spell — Craterhoof is MV 8). Counter Nature's Rhythm (MV variable but almost always X=4-6 meaning MV 4+, Disdainful Stroke handles it). Apply drain pressure fast — if they're at 5-8 life by the time they're ready to Craterhoof, you drain them out before the attack resolves. Combustion Technique with 3+ Lessons kills their early ramp creatures (Badgermole Cub, Gene Pollinator, Spider Manifestation).

Chapter 08

Heuristics

  1. 01
    Never tap Gran-Gran before Monument to Endurance is in play. Her tap ability draws then discards. Without Monument, that discard is pure card loss. With Monument, the discard triggers three rotating value modes. The difference is enormous — a tap before Monument costs you a card; a tap after Monument generates 3-5 drain + Treasure + draw. Deploy Monument first.
  2. 02
    Track your Lesson count every turn. 0-2 = normal deck. 3+ = upgraded deck. This is the most important number in the game. With 2 Lessons in GY: Accumulate Wisdom draws 1. With 3 Lessons: draws 3. Gran-Gran gives {1} cost reduction at 3+. Combustion Technique jumps from 4 damage to 5 at 3+. Every sequencing decision should be oriented around crossing and maintaining this threshold.
  3. 03
    Cast Accumulate Wisdom at their end step, not your main phase. Accumulate Wisdom at threshold draws 3 at instant speed. Casting it during your main phase means the 3 drawn cards sit unused until your next turn. Cast it on their end step — draw 3, untap with full hand AND full mana available to respond. Accumulate Wisdom goes to your GY (maintaining threshold) and you enter your turn with maximum options.
  4. 04
    Monument's three modes cycle globally per turn — "hasn't been chosen this turn" is the rule. You cannot choose "opponent loses 3" twice in one turn, even with two Monuments in play. The restriction is per-turn across all copies. Priority order: always use "lose 3" first (the one that kills them), then Treasure (sustains casting), then draw (replaces the discarded card). With Artist's Talent L3, "lose 3" becomes "lose 5" — upgrade the priority of that mode further.
  5. 05
    Combustion Technique exiles on death — use it specifically on Ojer Axonil and any recursion creature. Ojer Axonil's death trigger transforms him into Temple of Power. Combustion Technique's exile clause prevents this: Ojer is exiled, not killed, so the death trigger never fires. He's permanently gone. At 3 Lessons in GY, Combustion Technique deals 5 damage — killing Ojer (4/4 toughness) exactly. Don't use Firebending Lesson on Ojer — use Combustion Technique for the exile.
  6. 06
    Soul-Guide Lantern is the single most dangerous card opponents bring in against you. One activation — {T}, sacrifice: exile each opponent's GY — resets your Lesson count to 0. You fall below threshold instantly. Gran-Gran no longer reduces costs. Accumulate Wisdom draws 1 again. Combustion Technique deals 2 damage. Your opponent rebuilds their hand for free. Side in Annul specifically to counter it. Against known Dimir opponents who board in Lantern, hold Annul in hand specifically for the turn they deploy it.
  7. 07
    Artist's Talent leveling is sorcery speed — don't try to level during combat or in response to removal. Both {2}{R} level-up activations are sorcery speed (Class enchantments level up as sorceries). Don't try to race to Level 2 or 3 when they have open removal mana — they'll answer Artist's Talent before it levels. Wait for their end step or a turn where their mana is tapped to safely level up. Level 2 ({1} less cost) is more immediately impactful than Level 3 (+2 noncombat damage) — prioritize hitting Level 2 first.
  8. 08
    Stormchaser's Talent is your backup plan when Gran-Gran dies — deploy it early. Stormchaser's Talent Level 1 creates a 1/1 Otter token. Level 3 creates an Otter every time you cast an instant/sorcery. In a game where Gran-Gran died before Monument deployed, Stormchaser's Talent as an enchantment provides board presence, tokens to block, and a clock. Against opponents who can't answer enchantments (Mono-Green, Boros Aggro), Talent at Level 3 during your Monument drain loop creates an Otter every spell — the board floods with prowess tokens while you drain them out.

Tuning Notes

"This deck is rigid. The template used to win the World Championship has minimal flex slots. Changing the wrong card breaks the Lesson threshold consistency."
Stormchaser's Talent: 3 copies
Non-negotiable backup
Some builds run 2 Stormchaser's Talent and fit more removal. 3 is correct for resilience — Talent is your backup when Gran-Gran dies and your board pressure when you need something opponents have to answer. Never cut below 2. Some lists run 0 Stormchaser's Talent and more Agna Qel'a or counterspells — only viable in control-heavy metas.
It'll Quench Ya! vs Spell Pierce
The soft counter slot
It'll Quench Ya! ({U}, counter unless pay {2}) and Spell Pierce ({U}, counter noncreature spell unless pay {2}) serve similar roles. The meta at RC Cincinnati is creature-heavy — Spell Pierce misses creatures entirely, making It'll Quench Ya! the correct choice. Spell Pierce is better in control-heavy metas where you need to counter Revelation, Day of Judgment, Inevitable Defeat.
Boomerang Basics: 2 copies
The flexible Lesson
Some lists run 3-4 Boomerang Basics. 2 is correct for the Lesson threshold — you want the GY to fill with damage Lessons (Combustion Technique, Firebending Lesson) faster than bounce Lessons. Boomerang Basics is your most versatile card (bounce any nonland permanent OR your own for card draw) but doesn't deal damage while filling the GY.
Iroh's Demonstration: 1 main
Situational sweeper
1 main deck copy is correct. Iroh's Demonstration is the go-wide answer that the deck lacks otherwise — but at 5 mana (pre-reductions) it's too clunky at 2+ copies main. It contributes to the Lesson count and fills the role of sweeper when drawn. Bring more in from SB vs token-heavy matchups or run 2 main if the meta is flooded with Mono-White and Boros Aggro.
25 lands is locked
Do not change the mana base
The deck needs exactly 3 lands by turn 3 and can function on 4-5 for the rest of the game. 25 lands hits this probability. Going to 24 risks missing threshold setup. Going to 26 causes flooding that hurts your Monument-draw-Treasure loops (you want spells in hand, not lands). The World Championship winning list ran 25 — trust it.
The Agna Qel'a Question
Tech option for control metas
Some Izzet Lessons lists run 1-2 Agna Qel'a — a legendary creature that interacts with the Lesson system differently. Not in the current consensus list but worth noting as a metagame option if control (Jeskai, Azorius) becomes more prevalent. Agna Qel'a provides additional value without changing the Lesson count math.
