Chapter 01

Deck
Identity

"Amulet of Vigor turns every bounce land into a ritual. With two Amulets, a single land drop generates four mana. Primeval Titan finds two more lands on entry and two more every attack. The math gets out of hand very fast."

Amulet Titan is Modern's most technically demanding land combo deck. The deck wins by exploiting Amulet of Vigor's interaction with bounce lands (Simic Growth Chamber, Gruul Turf, Boros Garrison): every tapped permanent entering immediately untaps, so a bounce land that normally costs you a land drop instead produces a net mana surplus. With multiple Amulets this compounds rapidly — two Amulets plus one bounce land can generate four mana from a single land drop.

The primary win condition is Primeval Titan. When Titan enters or attacks, it searches for two lands from the library and puts them onto the battlefield tapped — which Amulet immediately untaps. The standard kill line: Titan enters, fetch Slayers' Stronghold + bounce land. Activate Stronghold (giving Titan haste + vigilance), attack, fetch Sunhome + Vesuva (copying another bounce land). Sunhome gives Titan double strike. Titan deals 12 trample damage and kills from full health.

The deck's secondary kill through Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle is particularly important against ground stalls. Dryad of the Ilysian Grove makes all lands every basic land type, so every land you play counts as a Mountain for Valakut triggers. A Titan attack fetching two Mountains with two Valakuts in play deals 12+ damage from nowhere.

Deck Identity

Primary Win
Titan Haste Kill
Cast Primeval Titan (turn 2–4). On entry, fetch Hanweir Battlements + bounce land. Tap Hanweir: give Titan haste. Titan attacks as 6/6 trample. Attack trigger: fetch utility lands — Mirrorpool to create a second Titan token (another ETB trigger, 2 more lands), Boseiju to answer a threat, or more bounce lands for Scapeshift setup. Usually takes 2 attacks to close.
Secondary Win
Scapeshift
Sacrifice any number of lands. Search your library for that many land cards, put them onto the battlefield tapped. With Amulet in play all those lands untap immediately — including bounce lands, which chain to produce massive surplus mana in a single resolution. Four copies ensures you can assemble the Scapeshift line when Titan is answered.
Tertiary Win
Cultivator Colossus
When it enters, put a land from hand onto the battlefield tapped, draw a card, repeat. With 6+ lands in hand and Amulet active: each tapped land untaps immediately, produces mana, and the chain draws the next. Creates a massive threat while flooding the battlefield with mana. The GSZ-toolbox line finds it when needed.
Mana Engine
Amulet + Bounce Lands
Each Amulet trigger is a separate untap. Two Amulets + bounce land = 4 mana from one land drop. Three Amulets + bounce land = 6 mana. The bounce land returning a land gives an additional land drop next turn (playing the returned land). This creates mana surpluses that dwarf any other Modern mana source.
Skill Ceiling
Highest in Modern
CrisMTG77: "The deck has nearly infinite lines of play." Stack triggers correctly (Amulet resolves before bounce return), choose correct lands to fetch, sequence Titan attacks to maximize damage. New pilots cost themselves 20–30% win rate on sequencing alone — this deck rewards hundreds of reps.
Meta Position

Low meta share because the deck requires deep knowledge to pilot. Expert pilots post 55–60% win rates; novices post 40–45%. Best choice for players who commit to learning one deck deeply. March 28, 2026 Challenge first place (8-1 record). Consistent top-table presence at major events.

Oracle Accuracy — Critical Interactions

Amulet of Vigor
{1} · Artifact
Whenever a permanent you control enters TAPPED, untap it. This is a triggered ability — each Amulet is a separate trigger. With two Amulets and a bounce land: play the bounce land (ETB tapped), two Amulet triggers go on stack. Both resolve, untapping the bounce land twice (redundant but both fire). The bounce land's "return a land" trigger is separate — put it on stack after the Amulet triggers, so you can tap the bounce land for mana between triggers resolving.
Primeval Titan
{4}{G}{G} · 6/6 Trample
Whenever this creature enters OR attacks, search library for up to 2 land cards, put them onto battlefield TAPPED, shuffle. With Amulet in play, the lands immediately untap. On entry: fetch Slayers' Stronghold + bounce land (untap both). On each attack: fetch two more lands. This means a Titan that survives to attack multiple times generates 4+ free lands over two turns. The "or attacks" trigger is why Titan wins games even without haste.
Scapeshift
{3}{G} · Sorcery
Sacrifice any number of lands. Search your library for that many land cards, put them onto the battlefield tapped, then shuffle. With Amulet in play: every fetched land immediately untaps. Fetch bounce lands → each untaps via Amulet, taps for mana, bounce trigger returns a land to hand. A Scapeshift sacrificing 6 lands into the right mix of bounce lands and utility lands can generate 12+ mana in a single resolution. Key rule: lands enter simultaneously from Scapeshift, so all Amulet triggers go on stack together — let them resolve before the bounce triggers to tap each land for mana.
Malevolent Rumble
{1}{G} · Sorcery
Look at the top four cards, put one permanent into your hand, put the rest into the graveyard. The key word is permanent — this finds Amulet of Vigor, Spelunking, Arboreal Grazer, Primeval Titan, or any land. Three copies makes the deck remarkably consistent at finding its engine pieces. Discarding the other three also fuels Aftermath Analyst's death trigger, which returns all lands from the GY to hand.
Green Sun's Zenith
{X}{G} · Sorcery
Search for a green creature with mana value X or less, put it onto the battlefield, shuffle GSZ into your library. At X=0: finds Dryad Arbor (a 1/1 Forest creature land) — an instant land drop that costs only {G}. At X=3: finds Arboreal Grazer, Aftermath Analyst. At X=6: finds Primeval Titan. Shuffles itself back — effectively a 4th copy of Titan and a 5th copy of Grazer over the course of a game. Three copies means you have reliable access to any green creature in the 75.
Simic Growth Chamber
Bounce Land
ETB tapped. When it enters, return a land you control to its owner's hand. The trigger order on stack: Amulet trigger (untap Chamber) is separate from the bounce trigger (return a land). Let Amulet resolve first → tap Chamber for {G}{U} → then the bounce trigger resolves, returning a land. You don't have to return Chamber itself — return a Forest for an extra land drop next turn. This is the fundamental Amulet sequencing lesson.
Spelunking
{2}{G} · Enchantment
ETB: draw a card, you may put a land from hand onto the battlefield. Lands you control enter untapped. The "enter untapped" clause applies to ALL your lands, including bounce lands. With Spelunking but no Amulet, your bounce lands still ETB untapped — they tap for mana immediately, and the bounce trigger fires normally. Two Spelunkings means a second effect, but the untap is redundant. Spelunking is essentially a second Amulet of Vigor for ETB purposes, plus it cantrips.
Summoner's Pact
{0} · Instant
Search library for a green creature, put in hand. At beginning of your next upkeep, pay {2}{G}{G} or lose the game. The Pact cost is owed at your NEXT upkeep — if you win this turn, the upkeep never comes. If you Pact for Titan on your turn 3, win via the Titan kill line, you never owe the {2}{G}{G}. DO NOT Pact if you can't pay next turn and don't have a guaranteed kill. Pacting for Dryad or Arboreal Grazer on a turn when you're not going to win sets up a later kill safely.

Chapter 03

Game
Flow

Every Amulet Titan game follows the same structure: set up the Amulet engine early, ramp through bounce lands, resolve Dryad or Titan, then attack for lethal. The skill is in sequencing the triggers correctly and knowing when to go for the kill vs. when to develop more.

The Standard Kill Line (Turn 3)

Turn 1
Play Amulet of Vigor. With a bounce land in hand: play the bounce land, Amulet untap trigger resolves, tap for {G}{U}, bounce trigger returns a Forest to hand. You've now seen 8 cards and have 1 mana available. Alternatively: play Arboreal Grazer, use its ETB to put a bounce land from hand onto the battlefield. Amulet untap fires. If no Amulet: just play a Forest or Saga. Surveil lands via Spelunking and Urza's Saga to find key pieces.
Turn 2
With Amulet from T1: replay the bounce land you returned → untap, tap, bounce again. With 2 bounce land plays and 1 Amulet you've generated 4+ mana across two turns. Cast Spelunking (cantrips + makes all your lands enter untapped) or Malevolent Rumble to dig for Titan. In best case: Summoner's Pact for Titan (no upkeep cost if you kill this turn), or Green Sun's Zenith for Dryad Arbor as a free extra land drop.
Turn 3 — Kill
Cast Primeval Titan (6 mana from Amulet ramp). ETB trigger: fetch Hanweir Battlements + a bounce land (Gruul Turf). With Amulet, both ETB untapped. Tap Hanweir Battlements: {R}{W}{T} — target creature (Titan) gains haste. Titan attacks as 6/6 trample. Attack trigger: fetch Mirrorpool + another bounce land. Tap Mirrorpool sacrificing it: copy Titan — the token ETB fires, fetching 2 more lands. Those fetch Boseiju (for protection) + another utility land. The Titan and its copy dealing 12 trample damage, plus 4 more fetched lands entering = often lethal on this turn or the next.
Key Rule
Vesuva can copy ANY land already on the battlefield — but cannot copy a land that entered simultaneously with it. When Titan fetches two lands at once, Vesuva can only copy lands already on the battlefield before those two entered. Use Vesuva to copy Gruul Turf or Simic Growth Chamber for a second bounce land providing another Amulet untap trigger and more mana. Mirrorpool is strictly better than Vesuva in most lines because the Titan token provides a second full ETB trigger (2 more land fetches).

Mulligan Framework

Keep
Amulet + Bounce + Land
Any hand with Amulet of Vigor + a bounce land + a second land source is a keep. The Amulet hand snowballs immediately — you're generating surplus mana by turn 2 and have lines to Titan by turn 3. The hand doesn't need Titan, just the mana infrastructure.
Keep
Grazer + Bounce + Land
Arboreal Grazer on turn 1 puts a bounce land from hand directly into play (tapped, triggering Amulet). This effectively gives you two land drops on turn 1. With any green source + Grazer + bounce land + any other card, you're ahead of curve by turn 2. Particularly strong if the bounce land is Simic Growth Chamber (taps for two colors).
Risky Keep
Summoner's Pact Hands
A hand with Summoner's Pact can find any green creature — Dryad, Titan, Grazer, or Colossus. But the {2}{G}{G} upkeep must be paid next turn. Keep only if you can either (a) kill this turn or (b) generate 4 green mana on turn 2 to pay upkeep. Never Pact speculatively on turn 1 without a concrete plan for the payment.
Mulligan
No Amulet + No Grazer
Hands with only regular green ramp (Dryad, Spelunking) but no Amulet and no Grazer are too slow without the mana engine. These hands develop slowly, hit land drops normally, and cast a turn-4 Titan at best. Mulligan aggressively to find Amulet — the deck is built around the {1} artifact, not around being a standard ramp deck.

Reading the Board — When to Go

Go Now
Turn 3 Kill Available
You have Titan + 6+ mana + Hanweir Battlements accessible. Opponents on fair decks haven't set up disruption yet. Execute the haste kill line without hesitation. Don't develop more land or draw cards when the kill is available.
Develop
Turn 4 Is Safer
Against counterspell decks with open mana, develop first: cast Spelunking to cantrip and make lands enter untapped, bounce a land to keep the loop going, and use Urza's Saga to build Constructs. Make them use interaction on non-threats, then jam Titan when they're tapped out.
Discard Line
vs Living End / Cascade
Against Living End: on the draw, play a bounce land on turn 2, end turn with 8 cards, discard Titan or Colossus at end step. This puts a high-power creature in the GY that Living End would reanimate for them — but more importantly, it extends the game past their turn-3 cascade kill, giving you time to stabilize.
Chapter 04

Core
Engines

1
Amulet + Bounce Land Mana Engine
Primary Mana Engine
Setup
Amulet T1, Bounce T2
Play Amulet of Vigor on turn 1. On turn 2, play any bounce land (Simic Growth Chamber, Gruul Turf, Boros Garrison, Golgari Rot Farm, Selesnya Sanctuary). Amulet's trigger fires: untap the bounce land. The bounce land's own ETB trigger then fires: return a land to hand. Tap the bounce land for 2 mana before the bounce resolves. Net: 2 mana produced, 1 land returned to hand (replay next turn).
Execution
Stack Triggers Correctly
When the bounce land enters: Amulet trigger goes on stack, then the bounce land's "return a land" trigger also goes on stack. Let AMULET resolve first (untap the bounce land). Now tap the bounce land for {G}{U} (or whatever its colors are). Then let the bounce trigger resolve — return a Forest. Next turn: replay the Forest as your land drop, having "used" the bounce land for 2 mana. With 2 Amulets, both untap triggers resolve separately, letting you tap twice for 4 mana.
Result
Mana Surplus by T2
Turn 1: Amulet, 0 floating. Turn 2: Play bounce land, tap for 2 (from Amulet), bounce Forest, play bounce land again (from where you bounced Forest to hand — wait, you bounced Forest, so replay Forest as land drop). By turn 3 you've produced 6+ mana. Primeval Titan costs {4}{G}{G} = 6. With even minimal Amulet support, turn-3 Titan is consistently achievable. The surplus only grows with additional Amulets and Spelunking.
2
Primeval Titan Kill Engine
Primary Win Engine
Setup
Titan ETB — Fetch Hanweir
When Titan enters, fetch Hanweir Battlements + any bounce land. With Amulet: both ETB untapped. Tap Hanweir Battlements: {R}{W}{T}: target creature (Titan) gains haste until end of turn. Titan can now attack the turn it enters. A 6/6 trample with haste attacking into most boards is already threatening — add the upcoming attack trigger and this snowballs fast.
Execution
Attack — Fetch Mirrorpool
Titan attacks. Attack trigger: fetch Mirrorpool + a second bounce land. Both ETB untapped via Amulet. Tap Mirrorpool: {2}, sacrifice Mirrorpool — copy target creature spell you control that's attacking OR copy Titan's triggered ability. The most common line: sacrifice Mirrorpool to create a 6/6 Titan token that also triggers ETB — that token fetches 2 more lands. Chains of land fetches accumulate mana, Constructs from Saga, and more triggers.
Result
Lethal Combat or Scapeshift
A real Titan + a Mirrorpool copy both attacking: 12 trample damage plus Boseiju or utility lands fetched as backup. Even without one-shotting, the land acceleration from multiple Titan triggers generates enough mana to Scapeshift for lethal next turn. Fetch Boseiju with an attack trigger to answer a problematic permanent mid-combat. The line doesn't require any specific land configuration beyond Hanweir Battlements existing in the library.
3
Scapeshift + Amulet Burst Engine
Secondary Win Engine
Setup
Lands in Play + Amulet Active
Scapeshift requires you to sacrifice your lands and fetch replacements from the library. With Amulet of Vigor active: every land you fetch ETBs tapped and immediately untaps. Fetch bounce lands — each one untaps, taps for 2 mana, then the bounce trigger fires returning a land to hand. More Amulets = more untap triggers per land. The setup is simply: have Amulet in play, have Scapeshift in hand, have enough lands on the battlefield to sacrifice.
Execution
Cast Scapeshift, Chain the Triggers
Cast Scapeshift. Sacrifice 6 lands. Fetch: 3 bounce lands + Hanweir Battlements + Mirrorpool + Forest. All 6 ETB tapped simultaneously. Amulet triggers: 6 untap triggers go on stack. Let them resolve — tap each bounce land for {G}{U} before the bounce triggers fire. Bounce triggers return 3 lands to hand. You've net gained ~12 mana from the Scapeshift resolution. Cast Primeval Titan with that floating mana if you had 6 already — the deck can chain Scapeshift into Titan into another ETB trigger.
Result
Mana Explosion or Direct Win
Scapeshift is both a mana burst AND an alternate closer when Titan has been countered or killed. Sacrificing 10 lands and fetching 4 bounce lands with 2 Amulets generates 16+ mana in a single resolution — cast Titan off that, attack twice, win. Against graveyard hate that shuts off creature lines: Scapeshift doesn't require creatures in play at all. Against Ensnaring Bridge: Scapeshift generates mana to cast Boseiju to destroy the Bridge, then Titan next turn. Four copies ensures you draw it in extended games.

Lines & Tricks

  • Stack Amulet before Bounce: Always let Amulet's untap trigger resolve before the bounce land's "return a land" trigger. This lets you tap the bounce land for mana between the two triggers resolving. The order: play bounce land → Amulet trigger on stack → bounce trigger on stack → let Amulet resolve (untap) → tap for mana → let bounce trigger resolve (return a land).
  • GSZ at X=0 for Dryad Arbor is a free land drop: Green Sun's Zenith at X=0 costs {G} and puts Dryad Arbor directly onto the battlefield. Dryad Arbor is a Forest creature land — it counts as a land drop AND adds a green creature to your board. With Amulet: Dryad Arbor ETBs tapped (it's a Forest, which normally ETBs untapped, but Arbor itself says it ETBs tapped when played as a land... actually check: "Dryad Arbor is a Forest creature land. If you play Dryad Arbor as a land, it enters tapped." But GSZ puts it onto the battlefield, not "plays" it as a land, so it might not ETB tapped. Either way, GSZ-0 into Arbor is a mana-efficient way to hit a land drop and generate a creature blocker or Amulet trigger target.
  • Malevolent Rumble mills for Aftermath Analyst: When Malevolent Rumble puts three cards into the graveyard, Aftermath Analyst's death trigger can fire if Analyst dies later — it returns all lands from the GY to hand. A Rumble that discards 2+ lands sets up an explosive Analyst turn. Also: Rumble puts Titan itself into the GY, enabling Green Sun's Zenith lines that find Aftermath Analyst to chain into finding Titan from the library.
  • Tolaria West transmutes for Summoner's Pact (MV 0): Tolaria West's Transmute ability ({1}{U}{U}, discard Tolaria West) searches for any card with mana value 0. Summoner's Pact has MV 0 — transmute finds it, which then finds Titan. Also finds Vexing Bauble (MV 0). Note: Amulet of Vigor has MV 1, not 0 — Tolaria West cannot find Amulet directly, only Pact or Bauble.
  • Vesuva copies a land already on the battlefield — not one fetched simultaneously: When Titan's ETB fetches two lands at once, both enter simultaneously. Vesuva cannot copy a land that entered "with it" in the same batch. Always copy a bounce land that was already on the battlefield before the Titan trigger resolved. The copy enters tapped (since it's copying a land that was tapped), which immediately triggers Amulet to untap it.
  • Boseiju timing against Agatha's Soul Cauldron: Destroy Cauldron IN RESPONSE to its "exile a creature" trigger — before the exile resolves. If you wait until the ability fully resolves and the creature is already exiled, Cauldron's granting of that creature's abilities persists even after Cauldron dies. Destroy Cauldron while the "exile" trigger is still on the stack to prevent the ability from ever setting up.
  • Discard Titan to survive Living End: If Living End's cascade window is coming and you can't stop it, hold 8 cards and discard to hand size at end of turn. Discarding Titan or Colossus fills your GY — Living End exiles all GYs when it resolves, so their cascade into Living End removes your Titan from the GY rather than reanimating it for them. This also extends the game past their turn-3 window, giving you time to find Trinisphere or develop your own kill.
  • Trinisphere is your best interactive piece vs Storm and Neoform: Trinisphere makes every spell cost at least {3}. Ruby Storm cannot chain rituals cheaply — Pyretic Ritual ({1}{R}) becomes {3}, Manamorphose becomes {3}. Neoform ({1}{G}) becomes {3}. Post-board, Trinisphere on turn 2 completely stops their kill turn. The catch: Titan costs {4}{G}{G} = 6, which is already above 3, so Trinisphere doesn't hurt you at all.
Lines & Tactics

Lines &
Tactics

"The highest decision density in Modern. Every land drop is a puzzle. Amulet + bounce land = extra mana. Dryad + Valakut = Lightning Bolts on land entry. Titan finds the right 2 lands to win."

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

Amulet of Vigor + Bounce Land = Extra Mana
Bounce land (e.g. Simic Growth Chamber) enters tapped → Amulet trigger: untap it → tap it for {G}{U} → bounce-land ETB trigger: return a land you control to hand. With 2 Amulets: Amulet triggers twice → untap twice → tap for {G}{U}, untap again, tap for {G}{U} again → 4 mana from one land drop. The bounce trigger returns ANY land — return the bounce land itself to replay next turn. This is the deck's mana engine.
Amulet + Bounce Land — Trigger Ordering Matters
When a bounce land enters: TWO triggers go on the stack — Amulet's untap trigger AND the bounce land's "return a land" trigger. YOU choose the order (you control both). Correct order: put bounce trigger on stack first, then Amulet untap on top. Amulet resolves first (untap, tap for mana), then bounce trigger resolves (return a land). If you stack it wrong (bounce first): you return the land before untapping it → no mana.
Primeval Titan — Searches on ETB AND Attack
"Whenever this creature enters OR attacks, search for up to two lands, put onto battlefield tapped." The lands enter tapped — with Amulet they untap immediately. Titan ETB: find 2 bounce lands. Each enters tapped → Amulet untaps each → tap for mana → bounce triggers return lands to hand → replay them. A single Titan ETB with Amulet can generate 8+ mana. On attack: search again for 2 more lands. Titan on T3 with Amulet active often wins immediately.
Dryad of the Ilysian Grove + Valakut = Lightning Bolts
Dryad: "Lands you control are every basic land type in addition to their other types." Every land is a Mountain. Valakut: "Whenever a Mountain enters, if you control at least 5 other Mountains, deal 3 damage." With Dryad + Valakut + 5 other lands: every future land drop deals 3 damage. Titan searching for 2 lands = 6 damage. This is the Valakut kill — play lands, deal damage, don't need to attack.
Dryad — Extra Land Drop + Every Basic Type
"You may play an additional land on each of your turns." + "Lands you control are every basic land type." Two effects in one creature. The extra land drop stacks with Azusa (Azusa = +2 lands, Dryad = +1, total = 4 land drops per turn). The "every basic type" enables Valakut kills AND makes fetch lands find anything (Windswept Heath finds a "Forest" — with Dryad, every land is a Forest).
Mycosynth Gardens Copies Amulet — Two Amulets for {0}
{X}, {T}: This land becomes a copy of target nontoken artifact with MV X. Amulet of Vigor is MV 1. Activate Gardens for {1}{T}: Gardens becomes a copy of Amulet. Now you have 2 Amulets — bounce lands untap twice, generating double mana. The copy is permanent (until Gardens copies something else). Gardens producing {C} or colored mana is lost once it becomes an Amulet (it's no longer a land — wait, it IS still a land that's also a copy of Amulet). It retains its land types but gains Amulet's abilities.
Tolaria West Transmute — Finds Pact, Amulet, EE, Walking Ballista
"Transmute {1}{U}{U}: search for a card with MV 0." MV 0 targets in the deck: Summoner's Pact (finds Titan or Dryad), Pact of Negation (free counter), Engineered Explosives (sweeper), Walking Ballista (removal or win-con). Transmute only as sorcery — can't surprise opponents. Transmute discards Tolaria West (it goes to GY, not exile). The versatility makes Tolaria West one of the most important lands in the deck.
Cultivator Colossus — Chain Lands Until You Miss
"When this enters, put a land from hand onto battlefield tapped. If you do, draw a card and repeat." This loops: land from hand → draw → if you drew a land, play it → draw → repeat. With 5-6 lands in hand: dump them all, draw 5-6 cards, end with a massive board + full hand. Colossus's P/T = lands you control — after chaining, it's often a 12/12+ trampler. The lands enter tapped — with Amulet they untap. Without Amulet they're tapped but you're still drawing cards and building the board.
Spelunking — Lands Enter Untapped (Second Amulet Effect)
"Lands you control enter untapped." This replaces the "enters tapped" clause on bounce lands and Titan-searched lands. Unlike Amulet (which is a triggered ability that untaps), Spelunking is a static replacement effect — the land never enters tapped in the first place. This means: no Amulet trigger (already untapped), but also no window for opponent to respond before you tap for mana. Spelunking also draws a card on ETB and puts a land from hand onto battlefield — it's ramp + card advantage + Amulet effect in one.
Summoner's Pact — Lose the Game if You Don't Pay
{0}: search for green creature → hand. Next upkeep: pay {2}{G}{G} or lose. With Titan + bounce lands + Amulet: generating {2}{G}{G} is trivial. Without Titan: you MUST have 4 mana next turn or you're dead. Don't Pact unless you can pay OR you're winning this turn. The upkeep trigger is mandatory — you can't "forget" it. It goes on the stack — opponent CAN respond to it (e.g., with Stifle to counter the payment and force the loss).

"The highest decision density in Modern. Every land drop is a puzzle. Amulet + bounce land = extra mana. Dryad + Valakut = Lightning Bolts on land entry. Titan finds the right 2 lands to win."

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

Amulet of Vigor + Bounce Land = Extra Mana
Bounce land (e.g. Simic Growth Chamber) enters tapped → Amulet trigger: untap it → tap it for {G}{U} → bounce-land ETB trigger: return a land you control to hand. With 2 Amulets: Amulet triggers twice → untap twice → tap for {G}{U}, untap again, tap for {G}{U} again → 4 mana from one land drop. The bounce trigger returns ANY land — return the bounce land itself to replay next turn. This is the deck's mana engine.
Amulet + Bounce Land — Trigger Ordering Matters
When a bounce land enters: TWO triggers go on the stack — Amulet's untap trigger AND the bounce land's "return a land" trigger. YOU choose the order (you control both). Correct order: put bounce trigger on stack first, then Amulet untap on top. Amulet resolves first (untap, tap for mana), then bounce trigger resolves (return a land). If you stack it wrong (bounce first): you return the land before untapping it → no mana.
Primeval Titan — Searches on ETB AND Attack
"Whenever this creature enters OR attacks, search for up to two lands, put onto battlefield tapped." The lands enter tapped — with Amulet they untap immediately. Titan ETB: find 2 bounce lands. Each enters tapped → Amulet untaps each → tap for mana → bounce triggers return lands to hand → replay them. A single Titan ETB with Amulet can generate 8+ mana. On attack: search again for 2 more lands. Titan on T3 with Amulet active often wins immediately.
Dryad of the Ilysian Grove + Valakut = Lightning Bolts
Dryad: "Lands you control are every basic land type in addition to their other types." Every land is a Mountain. Valakut: "Whenever a Mountain enters, if you control at least 5 other Mountains, deal 3 damage." With Dryad + Valakut + 5 other lands: every future land drop deals 3 damage. Titan searching for 2 lands = 6 damage. This is the Valakut kill — play lands, deal damage, don't need to attack.
Dryad — Extra Land Drop + Every Basic Type
"You may play an additional land on each of your turns." + "Lands you control are every basic land type." Two effects in one creature. The extra land drop stacks with Azusa (Azusa = +2 lands, Dryad = +1, total = 4 land drops per turn). The "every basic type" enables Valakut kills AND makes fetch lands find anything (Windswept Heath finds a "Forest" — with Dryad, every land is a Forest).
Mycosynth Gardens Copies Amulet — Two Amulets for {0}
{X}, {T}: This land becomes a copy of target nontoken artifact with MV X. Amulet of Vigor is MV 1. Activate Gardens for {1}{T}: Gardens becomes a copy of Amulet. Now you have 2 Amulets — bounce lands untap twice, generating double mana. The copy is permanent (until Gardens copies something else). Gardens producing {C} or colored mana is lost once it becomes an Amulet (it's no longer a land — wait, it IS still a land that's also a copy of Amulet). It retains its land types but gains Amulet's abilities.
Tolaria West Transmute — Finds Pact, Amulet, EE, Walking Ballista
"Transmute {1}{U}{U}: search for a card with MV 0." MV 0 targets in the deck: Summoner's Pact (finds Titan or Dryad), Pact of Negation (free counter), Engineered Explosives (sweeper), Walking Ballista (removal or win-con). Transmute only as sorcery — can't surprise opponents. Transmute discards Tolaria West (it goes to GY, not exile). The versatility makes Tolaria West one of the most important lands in the deck.
Cultivator Colossus — Chain Lands Until You Miss
"When this enters, put a land from hand onto battlefield tapped. If you do, draw a card and repeat." This loops: land from hand → draw → if you drew a land, play it → draw → repeat. With 5-6 lands in hand: dump them all, draw 5-6 cards, end with a massive board + full hand. Colossus's P/T = lands you control — after chaining, it's often a 12/12+ trampler. The lands enter tapped — with Amulet they untap. Without Amulet they're tapped but you're still drawing cards and building the board.
Spelunking — Lands Enter Untapped (Second Amulet Effect)
"Lands you control enter untapped." This replaces the "enters tapped" clause on bounce lands and Titan-searched lands. Unlike Amulet (which is a triggered ability that untaps), Spelunking is a static replacement effect — the land never enters tapped in the first place. This means: no Amulet trigger (already untapped), but also no window for opponent to respond before you tap for mana. Spelunking also draws a card on ETB and puts a land from hand onto battlefield — it's ramp + card advantage + Amulet effect in one.
Summoner's Pact — Lose the Game if You Don't Pay
{0}: search for green creature → hand. Next upkeep: pay {2}{G}{G} or lose. With Titan + bounce lands + Amulet: generating {2}{G}{G} is trivial. Without Titan: you MUST have 4 mana next turn or you're dead. Don't Pact unless you can pay OR you're winning this turn. The upkeep trigger is mandatory — you can't "forget" it. It goes on the stack — opponent CAN respond to it (e.g., with Stifle to counter the payment and force the loss).
Chapter 05

Role
Assignment

COMBO AGGRESSOR
Your default role in 70% of games. You're executing a combo that bypasses opponent's interaction by sheer speed. Kill on turn 3 before they can set up graveyard hate, Blood Moon, or counterspells. Don't slow down to "protect" your combo — just execute it as fast as possible. Speed beats interaction.
vs Most decks G1, vs unprepared opponents
VALUE GRINDER
Against heavy interaction. Use Urza's Saga to build Constructs, chain Malevolent Rumble to find key pieces, cycle through Spelunking to gain card advantage. When they spend counters on your early plays, your later Titans resolve cleanly. Trinisphere post-board on your combo turn taxes their counterspells heavily. The grind axis is surprisingly strong — you have more "free" cards than most decks.
vs UW Control, Jeskai Blink, heavy-counter decks
RACE + DISRUPT
Against opposing fast combos (Living End, Storm, Neoform). You can't interact on their axis most of the time. Your role: race to kill before they do, and use sideboard pieces (Trinisphere vs Storm/Neoform, Collector Ouphe vs artifact decks, Force of Vigor to destroy their Damping Sphere). Bojuka Bog from the SB fetched with Titan ETB is your GY hate in games 2/3.
vs Living End, Ruby Storm, Neoform

Sideboard
Guide

Fire Magic ×3
{R}{W} instant. Fire Magic deals 2 damage to each creature your opponents control. Board wipe for small creatures at instant speed for just 2 mana. Against Boros Energy: kills Ocelot Pride tokens, Ragavan, Guide of Souls in one shot. Against Yawgmoth: kills Young Wolf, Geralf's Messenger, Hapatra before the combo assembles. Against Affinity: kills Pinnacle Emissary (3/3 dies to 2+2? No — Fire Magic deals 2 to each creature, and Emissary has 3 toughness. Won't kill Emissary but kills 1/1 Drones). Key: this is an instant — cast at end of their turn before your combo turn to clear the board and then Titan through without blockers.
Force of Vigor ×3
{2}{G}{G} instant. If not your turn: exile a green card from your hand, cast for free instead. Destroy up to 2 target artifacts and/or enchantments. Three copies at instant speed, free on opponents' turns. Primary targets: Damping Sphere (completely disables your mana engine — this is the #1 priority target), Amulet of Vigor in the mirror, Collector Ouphe, Blood Moon, Stony Silence. The free activation means you can hold all your mana for Scapeshift or Titan while still having interaction. In the mirror: Force of Vigor on their Amulet during their upkeep removes their key piece while keeping yours intact.
Dismember ×2
{1}{B/P}{B/P}: −5/−5 until end of turn. Pay 4 life instead of colored mana. Kills Thought-Knot Seer (4/4 → −1/−1), Ragavan (1/1), opposing Titan tokens, Primeval Titan in the mirror (6/6 → 1/1 then blocks clean). The 4-life payment is real against aggressive decks — use Dismember defensively to remove key threats, not aggressively. Against Scion of Draco: Dismember can't target hexproof Scion unless you destroy their Leyline of the Guildpact with Boseiju first.
Trinisphere ×2
{3} artifact. Each spell costs at least {3} to cast. Your primary lock piece against Storm, Neoform, and any cheap-spell deck. Against Ruby Storm: Pyretic Ritual ({1}{R}) → costs {3}, Manamorphose ({1}{G/R}) → {3}, Ruby Medallion ({2}) → {3}. Storm cannot chain rituals to generate mana. Against Jeskai Blink: Consign to Memory ({2}{U}) → still {3}, Orim's Chant ({W}) → {3}, their evoke cards become more expensive. Against your own deck: Titan costs 6, Scapeshift costs 4, Spelunking costs 3 — Trinisphere doesn't affect any of your key spells. Deploy Trinisphere on your turn 2-3 before going for the kill.
Bojuka Bog ×1
Land. ETB: exile target player's graveyard. Fetchable via Primeval Titan's ETB or attack trigger — use one of Titan's two fetches on Bojuka Bog to immediately exile a graveyard while also developing your mana. Against Grixis Reanimator: fetch Bog with Titan ETB before they can activate Persist or Goryo's Vengeance. Against Living End: if Bog is in play when Living End resolves, you can respond to the cascade trigger by playing Bog as a land drop to exile their GY — but timing is tight. The key is that Titan can tutor it mid-game as a free response.
Collector Ouphe ×1
{1}{G} 2/2. Activated abilities of artifacts can't be activated. Fetched by Green Sun's Zenith at X=2. Against Izzet Affinity: shuts off Mox Opal (can't tap for mana), Arcbound Ravager (can't sacrifice artifacts), Claws of Gix (can't sacrifice permanents), Urza's Saga Chapter II and III (both are activated abilities of an enchantment land — wait, Saga chapters aren't activated abilities, they're triggered abilities on the Saga. Ouphe doesn't stop Saga). Ouphe specifically targets artifact activated abilities — Saga chapters are triggered, not activated, so Ouphe doesn't stop them. Still excellent against Mox Opal and Ravager.
Icetill Explorer ×1
Snow creature. ETB: you may put a snow land from your hand or library onto the battlefield tapped. A utility creature that's fetchable via Green Sun's Zenith and provides an additional land drop of any snow land. Particularly useful for finding Bojuka Bog (which is a snow swamp) or just accelerating mana development in longer games. The GSZ toolbox nature means you have access to it in any game where you draw or tutor GSZ.
Six ×1
{5}{G} 6/6. ETB: draw cards equal to the number of lands you control minus the number of cards in your hand. In a game where you've flooded lands (Amulet games often get to 8-12 lands) with a depleted hand: Six draws 6-10 cards off a single ETB. This refuels the entire hand in one shot. GSZ-fetchable at X=6. Against control where the game has gone long and you need card advantage, Six is the catch-up mechanism. Pair with Spelunking's cantrip to have even more mana before casting Six.
The Wandering Minstrel ×1
Creature — Elf Bard. ETB: reveal cards from the top of your library until you reveal a land card. Put that land card onto the battlefield tapped, then shuffle the rest back. A land-fetching creature fetchable by Green Sun's Zenith (X = its MV). Provides guaranteed land development in games where you need specific lands — particularly useful for finding Hanweir Battlements or Mirrorpool when you need the specific utility. Also functions as an Amulet trigger target (land entering tapped = Amulet untaps it).
Chapter 08

Heuristics

  1. Amulet trigger resolves before bounce trigger — tap the land between them. When a bounce land enters, both the Amulet untap trigger and the bounce land's "return a land" trigger go on the stack. Always let the Amulet trigger resolve first (untapping the bounce land). Then tap the now-untapped bounce land for mana. Then let the bounce trigger resolve. This is the foundational Amulet sequencing rule that new pilots consistently miss.
  2. Never Summoner's Pact speculatively. Summoner's Pact requires you to pay {2}{G}{G} at your next upkeep or lose. Only Pact when you can either (a) win this turn or (b) have 4 green mana reliably on the next turn. Pacting for Titan on turn 1 with no kill line and 2 mana available loses to Storm kill on turn 2 or your own Pact trigger.
  3. Karn, the Great Creator does NOT shut off Amulet of Vigor. Karn's static says activated abilities of artifacts opponents control can't be activated. Amulet of Vigor has a TRIGGERED ability (whenever a permanent enters tapped, untap it). Triggered abilities are not activated abilities. Karn does nothing to Amulet. Don't play around a non-existent interaction. Karn CAN fetch Damping Sphere from his SB — that's the real threat, not Karn's static.
  4. Vesuva copies a land already on the battlefield — not one fetched simultaneously. When Titan's ETB fetches two lands at once, both enter at the same time. Vesuva cannot copy a land that entered "with it" in the same batch. Copy a bounce land that was already on the battlefield before the Titan trigger resolved. The copy enters tapped, Amulet untaps it, and the bounce trigger fires giving you additional mana and a land return.
  5. Boseiju destroys Agatha's Soul Cauldron IN RESPONSE to the exile trigger. If you wait until the Cauldron's ability fully resolves and the creature is exiled, the granted abilities persist even after Cauldron dies. Destroy Cauldron while the "exile a creature" trigger is still on the stack — the ability fizzles without a target in exile, and Cauldron is permanently gone.
  6. Trinisphere is your best interactive piece — deploy it before your combo turn. Trinisphere makes every spell cost at least {3}. Against Storm: Pyretic Ritual ({1}{R}) becomes {3}, Manamorphose ({1}{G/R}) becomes {3}. Against blue decks: Counterspell ({U}{U}) becomes {3}, Force of Negation also becomes more expensive. Against Neoform: Neoform itself ({1}{G}) becomes {3}. Titan costs 6 — Trinisphere doesn't affect you at all. Deploy it on turn 2 before jamming Titan.
  7. Three Forests mainboard protects against Blood Moon. Blood Moon turns nonbasic lands into Mountains. Your basic Forests survive Blood Moon and continue producing green mana. Always have a path to find Forests via Arboreal Grazer (puts land from hand to battlefield) or normal land drops. Even under Moon with just 3 Forests, you can eventually hardcast Titan if you hit land drops.
  8. Bojuka Bog in the SB is fetchable with Titan ETB — it's still a mainboard-quality answer. Bojuka Bog is in the SB for games 2/3. When you cast Primeval Titan, one of the two land fetches can grab Bojuka Bog. It ETBs immediately, exiling the target GY before opponents can respond. Against Reanimator and Living End, this is a free GY hate that costs you nothing but one of Titan's two fetch slots. The SB distinction just means it's not in game 1.
  9. GSZ at X=0 is a free land drop via Dryad Arbor. Green Sun's Zenith at X=0 costs {G} and puts Dryad Arbor (a 1/1 Forest creature land) directly onto the battlefield. This counts as both a creature and a land entering, triggering Amulet of Vigor since Dryad Arbor enters tapped when put onto the battlefield this way. Three GSZ copies means you have three additional land drop "spells" built into the deck.
  10. Malevolent Rumble is your primary consistency engine — use it to find Amulet early. Rumble looks at 4 cards and keeps any permanent. In a deck of 35+ permanents, Rumble almost always finds either Amulet of Vigor, Spelunking, a bounce land, or Primeval Titan. The 3 cards it discards fuel Aftermath Analyst — if Analyst dies later, those discarded lands all return to hand. Never hold Rumble waiting for a "better" time; cast it as soon as you have 2 mana and aren't otherwise tapped out for interaction.
  11. Discard Titan against Living End — their cascade exiles all GYs, so your Titan is gone. Living End when it resolves: exiles all creature cards from all graveyards and puts them onto the battlefield. If your Titan is in the GY it enters under YOUR control — actually fine. But the cascade window is instant-speed and Violent Outburst fires on their turn. Discarding Titan to hold 8 cards extends the game past their turn-3 window and sets up Trinisphere or your own kill on the following turn.
  12. Spelunking provides the same untap effect as Amulet but doesn't stack exponentially. Spelunking is a static effect: "lands you control enter untapped." Multiple Spelunkings don't stack — "untapped" is binary. Amulet is a triggered ability — each copy fires separately, letting you tap between triggers for extra mana. One Spelunking = one Amulet for ETB purposes but without the mana-between-triggers upside. Four Amulets + one Spelunking = five sources of land untapping, with the Amulets providing extra tap windows.
  13. Tolaria West transmutes for Summoner's Pact (MV 0), not Amulet (MV 1). Tolaria West's Transmute ({1}{U}{U}, discard) finds any card with MV exactly 0. Summoner's Pact has MV 0 — transmute finds it, which then tutors any green creature including Titan. Vexing Bauble has MV 0. Amulet of Vigor has MV 1 — NOT 0. Common mistake: trying to transmute for Amulet. You cannot. Use Tolaria West specifically for emergency Pact access or Vexing Bauble.
  14. Otawara bounces problematic permanents through its channel ability — no mana required. Otawara, Soaring City can be channeled: discard it and pay {3}{U} (or just play it as a land). Channel: return target nonland permanent with MV ≤ X to its owner's hand, where X = number of Islands you control. Against Reanimator, channel Otawara to bounce Archon of Cruelty before it attacks. Against any deck with an untargetable threat: Otawara bounces rather than targets, so hexproof doesn't apply.
  15. Mirrorpool creates a Titan token that provides a second full ETB trigger — use it over Vesuva. When Titan's attack trigger fires, fetching Mirrorpool lets you sacrifice it to copy Titan as an attacking creature. That token's ETB trigger fires — fetching 2 more lands. Total: 4 lands fetched across the two Titan triggers (entry + attack token ETB). Vesuva only copies a land and gives another Amulet trigger. Mirrorpool nearly doubles Titan's land output in a single attack step.
  16. Fire Magic kills boards at instant speed for 2 mana — hold it for their attack step. Fire Magic deals 2 damage to each creature opponents control at instant speed for {R}{W}. Against Boros Energy: cast it at end of their attack step or on their end step when they've tapped out on offense. Against Yawgmoth: use it before their combo turn to clear Young Wolf and Geralf's Messenger. The instant speed matters enormously — this isn't a sorcery-speed sweep, it can interrupt their combat math.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand with Amulet of Vigor + a bounce land, or any hand with Spelunking. Ship any hand with no acceleration.
  • Kill line rule: Know which line you're on before T1: Haste Titan via Hanweir Battlements, or bounce-land burst. Don't improvise mid-game.
  • Pact rule: Cast Summoner's Pact only when you can pay {2}{G}{G} next upkeep OR you win before your next upkeep. No exceptions.
  • Bounce land rule: Never tap a bounce land for mana unless you have no choice. Every untap = more mana next turn.
  • Sideboard rule: Know your Needle target before G2. Slayers' Stronghold vs most, Valakut vs Scapeshift lines.
  • Consign rule: Counter Titan's ETB trigger, not the Titan itself. Let it enter, then Consign the land-fetch trigger.

Kill in 20 minutes or time out. Amulet draws can be long — be deliberate and fast.

Matchday Routine

  • Register your list carefully. Confirm bounce land count and Pact count. Both are critical.
  • Know your kill line before Round 1. Which line are you on today? Have an answer.
  • Between rounds: review Pact timing rules. One wrong Pact loses a match you controlled.
  • Don't let the complexity slow you down. If you stall, it's sequencing. Practice more reps.
  • Hydrate and eat. Titan draws are cognitively demanding. 12 rounds is an athletic event.

Standard Kill Line Reference

Primary Kill Line (With Amulet)
Titan Haste via Hanweir Battlements
ETB: fetch Hanweir Battlements + bounce land (both untap). Tap Hanweir: Titan gains haste. Attack. Attack trigger: fetch Mirrorpool + bounce land. Sacrifice Mirrorpool: copy Titan as attacking token. Token ETB fires, fetching 2 more lands. Two 6/6 tramplers = 12 damage + land acceleration.
Secondary Kill Line
Scapeshift + Amulet Burst
Sacrifice 6+ lands via Scapeshift. Fetch bounce lands — each ETB tapped, Amulet untaps immediately, tap for mana before bounce trigger resolves. Generates 12+ mana in a single resolution. Cast Titan off that mana, or finish with a second Scapeshift the following turn.
