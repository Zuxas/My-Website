Chapter 01

Overview

"Amulet of Vigor turns every bounce land into a ritual. With two Amulets, a single land drop generates four mana. Primeval Titan finds two more lands on entry and two more every attack. The math gets out of hand very fast."

Amulet Titan is Modern's most technically demanding land combo deck. The deck wins by exploiting Amulet of Vigor's interaction with bounce lands (Simic Growth Chamber, Gruul Turf, Boros Garrison): every tapped permanent entering immediately untaps, so a bounce land that normally costs you a land drop instead produces a net mana surplus. With multiple Amulets this compounds rapidly — two Amulets plus one bounce land can generate four mana from a single land drop.

The primary win condition is Primeval Titan. When Titan enters or attacks, it searches for two lands from the library and puts them onto the battlefield tapped — which Amulet immediately untaps. The standard kill line: Titan enters, fetch Slayers' Stronghold + bounce land. Activate Stronghold (giving Titan haste + vigilance), attack, fetch Sunhome + Vesuva (copying another bounce land). Sunhome gives Titan double strike. Titan deals 12 trample damage and kills from full health.

The deck's secondary kill through Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle is particularly important against ground stalls. Dryad of the Ilysian Grove makes all lands every basic land type, so every land you play counts as a Mountain for Valakut triggers. A Titan attack fetching two Mountains with two Valakuts in play deals 12+ damage from nowhere.

Deck Identity

Primary Win
Titan Haste Kill
Cast Primeval Titan (turn 2–4). On entry, fetch Slayers' Stronghold + any bounce land. Activate Stronghold for haste. Attack, fetch Sunhome + Vesuva copying a bounce land. Double-strike Titan with +2/+0 = 16 damage. Usually lethal from full life.
Secondary Win
Valakut Burn
Dryad makes all lands Mountains. With 2 Valakuts in play and each Titan attack fetching 2+ Mountains: 3 damage per Mountain per Valakut. A Titan attack with Dryad and 2 Valakuts deals 12+ direct damage to face without combat math.
Tertiary Win
Cultivator Colossus
When it enters, put a land from hand onto the battlefield tapped, draw a card, repeat. With 10+ lands in hand from a stalled board: creates a 20/20 trampler and floods 10 lands into play. Against decks with graveyard hate that slows Titan lines, Colossus is the alternate route.
Mana Engine
Amulet + Bounce Lands
Each Amulet trigger is a separate untap. Two Amulets + bounce land = 4 mana from one land drop. Three Amulets + bounce land = 6 mana. The bounce land returning a land gives an additional land drop next turn (playing the returned land). This creates mana surpluses that dwarf any other Modern mana source.
Skill Ceiling
Highest in Modern
CrisMTG77: "The deck has nearly infinite lines of play." Stack triggers correctly (Amulet resolves before bounce return), choose correct lands to fetch, sequence Titan attacks to maximize damage. New pilots cost themselves 20–30% win rate on sequencing alone — this deck rewards hundreds of reps.
Meta Position
3.1% · Specialist Pick
Low meta share because the deck requires deep knowledge to pilot. Expert pilots post 55–60% win rates; novices post 40–45%. Best choice for players who commit to learning one deck deeply. March 28, 2026 Challenge first place (8-1 record). Consistent top-table presence at major events.

Oracle Accuracy — Critical Interactions

Amulet of Vigor
{1} · Artifact
Whenever a permanent you control enters TAPPED, untap it. This is a triggered ability — each Amulet is a separate trigger. With two Amulets and a bounce land: play the bounce land (ETB tapped), two Amulet triggers go on stack. Both resolve, untapping the bounce land twice (redundant but both fire). The bounce land's "return a land" trigger is separate — put it on stack after the Amulet triggers, so you can tap the bounce land for mana between triggers resolving.
Primeval Titan
{4}{G}{G} · 6/6 Trample
Whenever this creature enters OR attacks, search library for up to 2 land cards, put them onto battlefield TAPPED, shuffle. With Amulet in play, the lands immediately untap. On entry: fetch Slayers' Stronghold + bounce land (untap both). On each attack: fetch two more lands. This means a Titan that survives to attack multiple times generates 4+ free lands over two turns. The "or attacks" trigger is why Titan wins games even without haste.
Dryad of the Ilysian Grove
{2}{G} · Enchantment Creature
You may play an additional land each turn. Lands you control are every basic land type. The "every basic land type" clause is what enables Valakut — all your lands count as Mountains, so every land entering while Dryad is in play is a potential Valakut trigger. Important: this affects lands you CONTROL, not just basics. Your bounce lands, Urza's Saga, Boseiju — all become Mountains/Forests/Plains/Islands/Swamps simultaneously. Blood Moon overrides Dryad — Blood Moon makes nonbasics into Mountains only, losing other types.
Simic Growth Chamber
Bounce Land
ETB tapped. When it enters, return a land you control to its owner's hand. The trigger order on stack: Amulet trigger (untap Chamber) is separate from the bounce trigger (return a land). Let Amulet resolve first → tap Chamber for {G}{U} → then the bounce trigger resolves, returning a land. You don't have to return Chamber itself — return a Forest for an extra land drop next turn. This is the fundamental Amulet sequencing lesson.
Spelunking
{2}{G} · Enchantment
ETB: draw a card, you may put a land from hand onto the battlefield. Lands you control enter untapped. The "enter untapped" clause applies to ALL your lands, including bounce lands. With Spelunking but no Amulet, your bounce lands still ETB untapped — they tap for mana immediately, and the bounce trigger fires normally. Two Spelunkings means a second effect, but the untap is redundant. Spelunking is essentially a second Amulet of Vigor for ETB purposes, plus it cantrips.
Summoner's Pact
{0} · Instant
Search library for a green creature, put in hand. At beginning of your next upkeep, pay {2}{G}{G} or lose the game. The Pact cost is owed at your NEXT upkeep — if you win this turn, the upkeep never comes. If you Pact for Titan on your turn 3, win via the Titan kill line, you never owe the {2}{G}{G}. DO NOT Pact if you can't pay next turn and don't have a guaranteed kill. Pacting for Dryad or Arboreal Grazer on a turn when you're not going to win sets up a later kill safely.

Chapter 03

Game
Flow

Every Amulet Titan game follows the same structure: set up the Amulet engine early, ramp through bounce lands, resolve Dryad or Titan, then attack for lethal. The skill is in sequencing the triggers correctly and knowing when to go for the kill vs. when to develop more.

The Standard Kill Line (Turn 3)

Turn 1
Play Amulet of Vigor. With a bounce land in hand: play the bounce land, Amulet untap trigger resolves, tap for {G}{U}, bounce trigger returns a Forest to hand. You've now seen 8 cards and have 1 mana available. Alternatively: play Arboreal Grazer, use its ETB to put a bounce land from hand onto the battlefield. Amulet untap fires. If no Amulet: just play a Forest or Saga. Surveil lands via Spelunking and Urza's Saga to find key pieces.
Turn 2
With Amulet from T1: replay the bounce land you returned → untap, tap, bounce again. With 2 bounce land plays and 1 Amulet, you've generated 4+ mana across two turns. Cast Dryad of the Ilysian Grove (3 mana). Dryad enables the extra land drop AND makes all your lands Mountains for Valakut. In best case: Summoner's Pact for Titan (no upkeep cost if you kill this turn).
Turn 3 — Kill
Cast Primeval Titan (6 mana or more from Amulet ramp). ETB trigger: fetch Slayers' Stronghold + a bounce land (Gruul Turf). With Amulet, both ETB untapped. Tap Stronghold: Titan gets +2/+0 and haste + vigilance. Attack. Attack trigger: fetch Sunhome + Vesuva (copying a bounce land, ETB untapped via Amulet). Tap Sunhome: Titan gains double strike. Titan swings for (6+2)×2 = 16 damage. Plus any Valakut triggers. Lethal.
Key Rule
Vesuva can copy ANY land already on the battlefield — but cannot copy the land it enters alongside (it enters and the copy happens as part of entering). Copy Boros Garrison, Gruul Turf, or Simic Growth Chamber to get a second bounce land (which provides another Amulet trigger and another Valakut trigger under Dryad). Don't copy Sunhome — one double strike is enough and double copies don't stack.

Mulligan Framework

Keep
Amulet + Bounce + Land
Any hand with Amulet of Vigor + a bounce land + a second land source is a keep. The Amulet hand snowballs immediately — you're generating surplus mana by turn 2 and have lines to Titan by turn 3. The hand doesn't need Titan, just the mana infrastructure.
Keep
Grazer + Bounce + Land
Arboreal Grazer on turn 1 puts a bounce land from hand directly into play (tapped, triggering Amulet). This effectively gives you two land drops on turn 1. With any green source + Grazer + bounce land + any other card, you're ahead of curve by turn 2. Particularly strong if the bounce land is Simic Growth Chamber (taps for two colors).
Risky Keep
Summoner's Pact Hands
A hand with Summoner's Pact can find any green creature — Dryad, Titan, Grazer, or Colossus. But the {2}{G}{G} upkeep must be paid next turn. Keep only if you can either (a) kill this turn or (b) generate 4 green mana on turn 2 to pay upkeep. Never Pact speculatively on turn 1 without a concrete plan for the payment.
Mulligan
No Amulet + No Grazer
Hands with only regular green ramp (Dryad, Spelunking) but no Amulet and no Grazer are too slow without the mana engine. These hands develop slowly, hit land drops normally, and cast a turn-4 Titan at best. Mulligan aggressively to find Amulet — the deck is built around the {1} artifact, not around being a standard ramp deck.

Reading the Board — When to Go

Go Now
Turn 3 Kill Available
You have Titan + 6+ mana + Slayers' Stronghold accessible. Opponents on fair decks haven't set up disruption yet. This is the cleanest win — execute the haste kill line without hesitation. Don't develop more land or draw cards when the kill is available.
Develop
Turn 4 Is Safer
Against counterspell decks with open mana, develop first: cast Dryad to set up the Valakut angle, bounce a land to keep the loop going, and use Urza's Saga to build Constructs. Make them use interaction on non-threats, then jam Titan when they're tapped out.
Discard Line
vs Living End / Cascade
Against Living End: on the draw, play a bounce land on turn 2, end turn with 8 cards, discard Titan or Colossus at end step. This puts a high-power creature in the GY that Living End would reanimate for them — but more importantly, it extends the game past their turn-3 cascade kill, giving you time to stabilize.
Chapter 04

Core
Engines

1
Amulet + Bounce Land Mana Engine
Primary Mana Engine
Setup
Amulet T1, Bounce T2
Play Amulet of Vigor on turn 1. On turn 2, play any bounce land (Simic Growth Chamber, Gruul Turf, Boros Garrison, Golgari Rot Farm, Selesnya Sanctuary). Amulet's trigger fires: untap the bounce land. The bounce land's own ETB trigger then fires: return a land to hand. Tap the bounce land for 2 mana before the bounce resolves. Net: 2 mana produced, 1 land returned to hand (replay next turn).
Execution
Stack Triggers Correctly
When the bounce land enters: Amulet trigger goes on stack, then the bounce land's "return a land" trigger also goes on stack. Let AMULET resolve first (untap the bounce land). Now tap the bounce land for {G}{U} (or whatever its colors are). Then let the bounce trigger resolve — return a Forest. Next turn: replay the Forest as your land drop, having "used" the bounce land for 2 mana. With 2 Amulets, both untap triggers resolve separately, letting you tap twice for 4 mana.
Result
Mana Surplus by T2
Turn 1: Amulet, 0 floating. Turn 2: Play bounce land, tap for 2 (from Amulet), bounce Forest, play bounce land again (from where you bounced Forest to hand — wait, you bounced Forest, so replay Forest as land drop). By turn 3 you've produced 6+ mana. Primeval Titan costs {4}{G}{G} = 6. With even minimal Amulet support, turn-3 Titan is consistently achievable. The surplus only grows with additional Amulets and Spelunking.
2
Primeval Titan Kill Engine
Primary Win Engine
Setup
Titan ETB — Fetch Stronghold
When Titan enters, fetch Slayers' Stronghold + Gruul Turf (or any bounce land). With Amulet: both land ETBs untapped. Tap Slayers' Stronghold for {R}{W} + {R}{W}{T}: target creature (Titan) gets +2/+0 and gains vigilance and haste until end of turn. Titan now has haste — it can attack this turn. Titan is now 8/6 with trample, vigilance, and haste.
Execution
Titan Attack — Fetch Sunhome
Titan attacks. Attack trigger: fetch Sunhome, Fortress of the Legion + Vesuva. Vesuva enters as a copy of any land on the battlefield — copy a bounce land (enters untapped via Amulet). Tap Sunhome: {2}{R}{W}{T}: Titan gains double strike until end of turn. Titan is now 8/6 with trample, double strike. Two Valakuts with Dryad? Each land entering triggers both — 6 damage per Mountain entering. Between Vesuva's bounce copy entering and other land plays, Valakut adds 12+ direct damage.
Result
Lethal Combat
8/6 double-strike trample Titan with +2/+0 = 10/6 double strike = 20 first-strike damage, then 20 regular damage. Kills from full life with no assistance. Plus Valakut triggers for 12+ if Dryad was in play. The kill line requires only the Titan and two specific lands in the library — no additional cards from hand needed. This is the most consistent turn-3 combo kill in Modern that doesn't require two separate cards to be in hand simultaneously.
3
Valakut + Dryad Burn Engine
Secondary Win Engine
Setup
Dryad + 2 Valakuts
Dryad of the Ilysian Grove makes all lands every basic land type — including Mountain. Valakut triggers when a Mountain enters if you control 5+ other Mountains. With Dryad in play, EVERY land you play is a Mountain. With 5+ lands already, each new land triggers Valakut for 3 damage per Valakut in play. Two Valakuts = 6 damage per land. With Titan fetching 2 lands per trigger: 12 damage per attack from Valakut alone.
Execution
Titan Attacks, Valakuts Stack
Titan attack trigger: fetch 2 lands (both become Mountains via Dryad). Each triggers both Valakuts: 2 lands × 2 Valakuts × 3 damage = 12 damage on the stack immediately (assuming 5+ other Mountains already in play). CrisMTG77 example: Dryad + Titan + 2 Valakuts on turn 3, attack triggers 12 Valakut damage + Titan combat = enough to kill from 20 life in a single attack.
Result
Non-Combat Lethal
The Valakut line wins through blockers, Ensnaring Bridge (if Titan has vigilance from Stronghold, it untaps to block), and flying creatures. The opponent can't gain enough life to survive if Valakut deals 12+ per Titan trigger. Against decks that answer Titan in combat, the Valakut angle provides a second completely independent win condition that requires no creatures at all after Dryad resolves.

Lines & Tricks

  • Stack Amulet before Bounce: Always let Amulet's untap trigger resolve before the bounce land's "return a land" trigger. This lets you tap the bounce land for mana between the two triggers resolving. The order: play bounce land → Amulet trigger on stack → bounce trigger on stack → let Amulet resolve (untap) → tap for mana → let bounce trigger resolve (return a land).
  • Tolaria West transmutes for Amulet: Tolaria West's Transmute ability ({1}{U}{U}, discard) searches for any card with mana value 0 — including Amulet of Vigor. In games where you're digging for the Amulet engine, Transmute finds it, plus it can find Summoner's Pact (MV 0), Engineered Explosives (MV 0), and any zero-mana utility card.
  • Mycosynth Gardens copies an Amulet: The Mycosynth Gardens can become a copy of Amulet of Vigor ({1}, tap: this land becomes a copy of target artifact you control with MV = X). One activation for {1}{T}: Gardens becomes another Amulet. Now you effectively have 5–6 Amulets available. Gardens can also copy Engineered Explosives post-board.
  • Vesuva can't copy the land it enters with: If Titan fetches Sunhome + Vesuva in the same trigger, Vesuva enters at the same time as Sunhome — it cannot copy Sunhome because Sunhome isn't yet on the battlefield as Vesuva enters. Instead, copy a bounce land already in play to get an extra Amulet trigger and Valakut trigger.
  • Boseiju timing against Yawgmoth: Use Boseiju to destroy Agatha's Soul Cauldron IN RESPONSE to the "exile a creature" ability trigger (as soon as it targets a creature). If you wait until the ability fully resolves and the creature is exiled, the Cauldron's granting of that creature's abilities persists even with Cauldron gone. Destroying the Cauldron before the exile prevents the ability from setting up.
  • Discard Titan to survive Living End: If you can't stop Living End and their cascade window is coming, discard Titan or Colossus at end of turn (by holding 8 cards and discarding to hand size). This fills your GY with threats they'd reanimate — creating a tension. More importantly it extends the game: they don't get a free Titan off Living End if it's already in your GY and their Living End exiles all GYs including Titan.
  • Defense Grid on your turn = free comboing: Cast Defense Grid on your turn. During your turn, Defense Grid doesn't affect your own spells (only spells cast except during controller's turn are affected — and you ARE the controller). Opponents' counters cost {3} more, Force of Negation costs {4}{U}{U} (10 mana) to cast on your turn. Grid essentially locks out reactive interaction while you combo.
  • Cavern of Souls names Giant or Titan for Primeval Titan: Primeval Titan is a Giant. Cavern of Souls naming "Giant" makes Titan uncounterable. Alternatively name "Nymph Dryad" for Dryad of the Ilysian Grove. Use Cavern when you identify the opponent as a counterspell deck — feed the uncounterable creature into the matchup where it matters most.
Chapter 05

Role
Assignment

COMBO AGGRESSOR
Your default role in 70% of games. You're executing a combo that bypasses opponent's interaction by sheer speed. Kill on turn 3 before they can set up graveyard hate, Blood Moon, or counterspells. Don't slow down to "protect" your combo — just execute it as fast as possible. Speed beats interaction.
vs Most decks G1, vs unprepared opponents
VALUE GRINDER
Against heavy interaction. Use Urza's Saga to build Constructs, deploy The One Ring for card advantage, cycle through Spelunking to find answers. When they spend counters on your early plays, your later Titans resolve cleanly. Defense Grid on your turn protects the eventual kill. The grind axis is surprisingly strong — you have more "free" cards than most decks.
vs UW Control, Jeskai Blink, heavy-counter decks
RACE + DISRUPT
Against opposing fast combos (Living End, Storm, Neoform). You can't interact on their axis. Your role: race to kill before they do, and use sideboard pieces (Endurance on Living End's GY, Boseiju on Amulet to prevent their Pact, Defense Grid to protect your own kill turn). Sometimes you win by resolving the combo first; sometimes you win by disrupting theirs.
vs Living End, Ruby Storm, Neoform

Heuristics

  1. Amulet trigger resolves before bounce trigger — tap the land between them. When a bounce land enters, both the Amulet untap trigger and the bounce land's "return a land" trigger go on the stack. Always let the Amulet trigger resolve first (untapping the bounce land). Then tap the now-untapped bounce land for mana. Then let the bounce trigger resolve. This is the foundational Amulet sequencing rule that new pilots consistently miss.
  2. Never Summoner's Pact speculatively. Summoner's Pact requires you to pay {2}{G}{G} at your next upkeep or lose. Only Pact when you can either (a) win this turn or (b) have 4 green mana reliably on the next turn. Pacting for Titan on turn 1 with no kill line and 2 mana available loses to Storm kill on turn 2 or your own Pact trigger.
  3. Karn, the Great Creator does NOT shut off Amulet of Vigor. Karn's static says activated abilities of artifacts OPPONENTS control can't be activated. Amulet of Vigor has a TRIGGERED ability (whenever a permanent enters tapped, untap it). Triggered abilities are not activated abilities. Karn does nothing to Amulet. Don't play around a non-existent interaction.
  4. Vesuva can copy a bounce land, not a newly-arrived land. When Titan fetches Sunhome + Vesuva, both enter simultaneously. Vesuva cannot copy a land that "entered with it" — it must copy a land already on the battlefield. Always copy a previously-played bounce land to get the Amulet trigger and the mana production from the copy.
  5. Boseiju destroys Agatha's Soul Cauldron IN RESPONSE to the exile ability trigger. If you wait until the Cauldron's ability fully resolves and the creature is exiled, the granted abilities persist even after Cauldron dies. Destroy Cauldron while the "exile a creature" trigger is on the stack — the ability fizzles without a target in exile, and Cauldron is gone.
  6. Defense Grid on your turn, not their turn. Defense Grid's clause reads "except during its controller's turn." If you cast it on your turn, your own spells this turn still cost normally. Opponents' counterspells cost {3} more when they try to interact. The correct timing is to deploy Grid, then immediately start your combo sequence.
  7. Four Forests mainboard protects against Blood Moon. Blood Moon turns nonbasic lands into Mountains. Your basic Forests survive Blood Moon and continue producing green mana. Always have a path to find Forests via Arboreal Grazer (puts land from hand to battlefield) or normal land drops. Even under Moon: basics + Dryad (if you cast her before Moon) can function.
  8. Bojuka Bog ETBs immediately when Titan fetches it. When Titan's ETB trigger resolves and you fetch Bojuka Bog, it enters the battlefield and its own ETB trigger fires immediately: exile target player's graveyard. Against Reanimator and Living End, fetching Bog in response to their GY setup simultaneously kills their combo and progresses your mana development. This is why Bog is a mainboard land, not a sideboard card.
  9. Dryad's "every basic land type" is the key to Valakut — track your Mountain count. With Dryad in play, every land you control is a Mountain. Valakut triggers when a Mountain enters if you control at least 5 OTHER Mountains. With 6+ lands already in play and Dryad, every new land entering triggers Valakut for 3 damage per Valakut. Two Valakuts = 6 damage per land. Keep a mental count of your land total relative to 5.
  10. Discard Titan or Colossus against Living End — don't let them cascade into it. Living End's ETB when it resolves: all creatures from all GYs enter the battlefield. If your Titan is in the GY, it enters under YOUR control — that's actually fine. But Colossus entering with your lands in play is dangerous because it triggers for each land from hand. Discard strategically to extend the game, not to benefit them.
  11. Spelunking does the same job as Amulet for all lands — but doesn't stack exponentially. Spelunking says "lands you control enter untapped." This applies to bounce lands, utility lands, all of them. But unlike Amulet (which untaps as a separate trigger per copy), Spelunking is a static effect and multiple copies don't multiply the effect. Two Spelunkings = still just "lands enter untapped." Two Amulets = two separate untap triggers, which matters for tapping between triggers.
  12. Mycosynth Gardens can copy Amulet for a 5th copy. {X}, {T}: Mycosynth Gardens becomes a copy of target nontoken artifact you control with MV X. At X=1, Gardens becomes a copy of Amulet of Vigor. Now you effectively have 5 Amulets. The copy-lands (Mycosynth, Vesuva) provide significant redundancy for the Amulet engine in long games or when Amulets get destroyed.
  13. Tolaria West transmutes for zero-MV cards including Summoner's Pact. Tolaria West's Transmute ability finds any card with MV 0. Summoner's Pact has MV 0. Engineered Explosives at X=0 has MV 0. Amulet of Vigor has MV 1 — not 0. Tolaria West cannot find Amulet. It CAN find Pact for an emergency Titan tutor, or EE for board interaction.
  14. Cavern of Souls — name Giant for Primeval Titan, Nymph Dryad for Dryad. Primeval Titan is a Giant creature type. Cavern naming Giant makes it uncounterable. Against blue decks, deploy Cavern before the key threat to force them to use Solitude (non-counterspell) rather than Force of Negation. Dryad of the Ilysian Grove is a Nymph Dryad — naming Nymph or Dryad also works.
  15. Otawara bounces back stolen permanents — use it proactively against Dauthi Voidwalker. CrisMTG77: Otawara can return Dauthi Voidwalker or Grief (in Reanimator) back to opponent's hand. Channel Otawara with {3}{U}: return target nonland permanent. This recovers Titans stolen by Dauthi and bounces back problematic creatures without needing mainboard interaction.
  16. With Dryad in play: every land you play is a Mountain AND every other basic type. Blood Moon OVERRIDES Dryad for nonbasic lands — under Blood Moon, nonbasics become Mountains only, losing all other types Dryad granted. But basic lands retain all their types (they're immune to Blood Moon). With a basic Forest under Blood Moon and Dryad, the Forest is still a Forest/Mountain/Island/Plains/Swamp — can produce any color via Dryad's continuous effect.
