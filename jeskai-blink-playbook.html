JESKAI BLINK
Chapter 01

Identity &
Philosophy

"The UW engine. The red angle. Better when they're not ready for the third color."— Core Mantra

Jeskai Blink is UW Blink with a red splash. The core engine is identical — Ephemerate, Phelia, White Orchid Phantom, Solitude, Guide of Souls, Ocelot Pride — but the red splash adds Lightning Bolt and Orim's Chant to the main deck. Teferi, Time Raveler (2 main, 1 SB across the 75) is the key card bridging the colors.

The Team Resolve working verdict: UW is better in open and aggro-heavy fields. Jeskai is correct when the expected field is combo-heavy (Belcher, Neoform, Titan). Orim's Chant is the card that separates the two shells — it's a full-turn Time Walk against combo decks that cannot be replicated in UW.

What Red Adds (1)
Orim's Chant
Full-turn Time Walk against combo. Cast at the start of their main phase — they get zero plays. Best against Belcher, Amulet (taps mana in response to Pact), and Neoform. This is the primary reason to choose Jeskai over UW.
What Red Adds (2)
Lightning Bolt
One-mana instant answer to Ragavan, Guide of Souls, and DRC specifically. Goes to the face in races. UW doesn't have this — Bolt closes matchup gaps that Prismatic Ending can't fill at one mana.
What Red Adds (3)
Teferi TRav (2 main)
2 main vs 1 in the UW build. Against combo this is a free win condition — it locks opponents into sorcery speed. Against Glockulous: they cannot Persist at instant speed under Teferi.
The Cost
Mana Consistency
Three-color mana means more shock damage and occasional openers that don't produce the right colors. Against Boros Energy and Prowess, this matters. Evaluate the expected field before choosing Jeskai.
Choose Jeskai When
Combo-Heavy Field
Orim's Chant main is a free win against Belcher, Neoform, Amulet Titan, and stack-based combo. If the RC field is more than 20% combo, Jeskai is the correct call.
Choose UW When
Open or Aggro Field
Cleaner mana, more consistent openers, no tempo hits from shock damage. Against Boros + Prowess + open field — take UW's mana consistency.

Chapter 03

The Game Plan

"Teferi first. Then execute. Never the other way around."— Key Principle

The UW Blink engine is identical to the UW Blink playbook — see that guide for full detail on Ephemerate sequencing, White Orchid Phantom lines, and Ocelot Pride + Guide inevitability. This section covers what changes with the red splash.

The Teferi Rule

Against any deck with instant-speed interaction, the sequencing is: Teferi, Time Raveler on turn 3 → execute the plan on turn 4+. Never the other way around. Teferi shuts off Goryo's Vengeance, Consign to Memory, Force of Negation, and Counterspell all at once. Ephemerate on Teferi is almost always wrong — his value is the static ability, not loyalty. Blink Solitude, Phantom, or Guide instead.

Orim's Chant Rules

Cast at the start of their main phase — before they have priority to declare any actions. Against Belcher: tap mana before they can activate Charbelcher. Against Amulet: tap mana in response to Summoner's Pact or Amulet trigger. Against Neoform: tap mana before they can cast it. You get a free turn — use it to set up the Ephemerate chain or assemble your engine.

Lightning Bolt Priority

Bolt goes to the face in most matchups. The exceptions where killing a creature is worth more than 3 face damage: Ragavan before its first connection, DRC before delirium is online, and Ocelot Pride on their side (kills the Pride and the token immediately).

Mana Reality

Accept 2–4 life of shock damage per game. Track life carefully — the Boros Energy matchup is often decided at 6–8 life. Don't fetch Sacred Foundry unless you specifically need red mana that turn.

Chapter 04

Role Assignment

Same as UW Blink — control against aggro, beatdown against control — with one addition: Teferi creates a third mode where you lock opponents into sorcery speed and play as the proactive deck even against interactive opponents.

Chapter 06

Sideboard Guide

See the UW Blink playbook for full notes on Consign to Memory, Sanctifier en-Vec, Stony Silence, Kataki, Wrath of the Skies, Disruptor Flute, and Lavinia — these are identical between both shells. Jeskai-specific notes below:

Orim's Chant — 1 main + 1 SB (2 total)
Two total across the 75. Main deck copy for any unknown field since combo is always possible. SB copy boards in explicitly against combo-heavy expected matchups. Never boards out in combo matchups — if you're in Jeskai it's because you expected to need Chant. Always cast at the start of their main phase, not reactively.
Teferi, Time Raveler — 2 main + 1 SB (3 total)
From the TR RC Houston guide: "You want to draw one, not multiples; protects engines and forces sorcery speed through their stack." Board the third copy in for combo/stack-heavy matchups. Never Ephemerate Teferi — blink other targets and let his static ability do work.
Disruptor Flute — 1 copy
From the TR guide: "Broad combo hedge for one-axis decks — Neoform, Summoner's Pact, Charbelcher, Crashing Footfalls, The One Ring." Name the key card that makes their combo work. Flute naming Summoner's Pact against Amulet is often backbreaking.
Lavinia, Azorius Renegade — 1 copy
Stops free spells (Living End, Cascade, Persist) and taxes non-land plays when they've cast more spells than mana paid. Niche card that's backbreaking in the right matchup — specifically Glockulous, Belcher, and any cascade deck.
Chapter 07

Heuristics &
Quick Rules

  1. Teferi resolves before you execute the combo plan against any deck with instant-speed interaction. Sequence: Teferi on T3 → Ephemerate chains on T4+.
  2. Orim's Chant is cast at the START of their main phase — before they have priority. Don't wait for them to start acting. Tap everything before they can.
  3. Lightning Bolt goes to the face except against Ragavan, DRC (before delirium), and Ocelot Pride on their side. These three specific cards are worth killing over dealing 3 to the dome.
  4. Track shock damage carefully. The Boros Energy matchup is often decided at 6–8 life. Every fetch of Sacred Foundry is a tax on those games.
  5. Never Ephemerate Teferi. His value is the static ability — sorcery speed lock. Blink Solitude, Phantom, or Guide instead and let Teferi do his job.
  6. Choose Jeskai when you expect >20% combo in the RC field. Against open fields with heavy aggro, UW's cleaner mana is the better call. Don't pay the mana tax if you won't use Chant enough to justify it.
  7. Disruptor Flute naming the key combo card is often backbreaking. Know what to name before the tournament: Summoner's Pact (Amulet), Charbelcher (Belcher), Neoform (Neoform).
  8. Lavinia, Azorius Renegade stops free spells and cascade. It's a niche card that's backbreaking in the right matchup — specifically Glockulous (stops Persist under certain conditions) and Belcher.
  9. White Orchid Phantom + Flagstones interaction is identical to UW — deploy Phantom before they can fetch to deny their key land. The mana denial core of both shells works the same way.
  10. If the field ends up Boros Energy + Prowess heavy and you chose Jeskai — sideboard heavily into Wrath + Sanctifier and accept you're playing a slightly worse UW in those matchups.
