Chapter 01

Overview &
Philosophy

"Answer everything. Win with the last card in hand." — Core Mantra

Azorius Control is a meta-dependent pick. It is not the best deck in every room — it is the best deck in the right room. That room is heavy on midrange and combo, light on fast aggro. When Snapcaster Mage is clearly the best card in the format and the RC field skews toward Jeskai Blink, Glockulous, Amulet Titan, and other fair/combo decks, UW Control is the highest equity register.

The philosophy is simple: every opponent card that resolves is a mistake. Counterspell answers spells. Solitude answers creatures. Prismatic Ending answers permanents. Supreme Verdict answers boards. Nothing sticks. When they run out of threats, you win with the last card in hand and a 3-loyalty Teferi, Hero of Dominaria that untaps two lands every turn.

The deck's skill expression is in resource management, sequencing, and threat assessment — knowing which spell to counter, which to let through, and when to tap down to deploy your own threats. Playing too passively wastes mana. Playing too aggressively breaks the counter backup. The tension is real and mastering it separates 55% UW pilots from 70% pilots.

Core Philosophy

Primary Win Condition
Teferi + Counter Backup
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria resolves with Counterspell or Force of Negation in hand. +1 every turn draws a card and untaps two lands — maintaining counter backup while accruing card advantage. The game ends when they run out of resources and Teferi's emblem exiles the final permanents.
Secondary Win Condition
Snapcaster Mage Loops
When Teferi is answered, Snapcaster Mage + interaction generates two-for-ones indefinitely. A graveyard full of Counterspells and Prismatic Endings means every Snapcaster is worth more than one card. Multiple Snapcasters in a game represent a larger card advantage than any single threat they can deploy.
Advantage Type
Card Quality + Volume
Every spell is an answer or a card draw engine. No dead cards. No situational threats. Pure card quality — every card in the 60 either stops their plan or draws more cards. Snapcaster means your graveyard is also a resource pool.
Preferred Role
Control — Always
This deck never willingly becomes the beatdown. The exception: when Teferi, Time Raveler resolves, you briefly switch to pressuring them at sorcery speed while they can't respond. Otherwise: hold mana open, answer spells, generate cards, close with Teferi Hero.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths
Best-in-Class At
Best deck against combo — hard counters answer any key spell.

Snapcaster Mage loops generate more cards than any midrange deck can sustain.

Supreme Verdict is the cleanest sweeper in the format — cannot be countered.

Deep format knowledge produces meaningful edges in every interaction decision.
Weaknesses

When to Register This Deck

Registration Criteria Register UW Control when: the expected RC meta is heavy midrange and combo (Blink, Glockulous, Titan, Creativity variants), and aggro represents less than 25% of the expected field. When the format has stabilized around Snapcaster Mage being the consensus best card. When you have logged at least 40 hours on this specific 75 — UW Control punishes unfamiliarity with its own sequencing harder than any other Team Resolve archetype. Do not register this if you expect Boros Energy to make up more than 30% of the field.

Card Choices & Key Card Roles

The Engine
Snapcaster Mage
{1}{U} 2/1 Flash. ETB: target instant or sorcery in your GY gains flashback until EOT. The flashback cost equals the original mana cost — not free, not reduced. The critical rule: Snapcaster targets a card in your GY when it enters. You cannot Snap and then wait to see the board — the target is chosen immediately on ETB. Snap + Counterspell = 4-mana hard counter that replaces itself. Protect Snapcaster above almost everything else in the 60.
The Closer
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria
{3}{W}{U} Loyalty 4. +1: Draw a card. At the beginning of the next end step, untap up to two lands (delayed — not now). -3: Put target nonland permanent into owner's library third from top — not exiled, not on top. -8: Emblem — whenever you draw a card, exile target permanent an opponent controls. The +1 untap is a triggered ability that fires at the beginning of the next end step. Use this to untap lands and hold countermagic on their turn.
The Lockpiece
Teferi, Time Raveler
{1}{W}{U} Loyalty 4. Static ability: each opponent can cast spells only when they could cast a sorcery — this is always active, not from the +1. The +1 gives you sorcery flash until your next turn. -3: Return up to one target artifact, creature, or enchantment to owner's hand. Draw a card. Critical: TRav's sorcery lock is a static ability — it takes effect immediately when Teferi enters and does not require activating. They cannot hold up Counterspell with TRav in play.
The Free Answer
Solitude
{3}{W}{W} Flash. ETB: exile up to one target creature. Evoke — exile a white card from your hand rather than pay {3}{W}{W}. When you cast Solitude via evoke, you exile a white card as the alternative cost, Solitude enters, the ETB trigger fires exiling their creature, then Solitude is sacrificed. Force of Negation cannot counter Solitude cast via evoke — Solitude is still a colored spell. Consign to Memory CAN counter the ETB exile trigger.
The Uncounterable Sweeper
Supreme Verdict
{1}{W}{W}{U} Sorcery. Cannot be countered. Destroy all creatures. This includes your own creatures — Snapcaster Mage dies to Verdict. This is not a limitation, it's a feature: Snap into Verdict on the same turn by flashing Snap before Verdict. The uncounterable clause means Glockulous cannot Consign it, other control decks cannot counter it, and no protection effect stops it (protection prevents targeting, not destruction from non-targeted effects).
Flex Slot
Dress Down / Spell Snare
Dress Down: {1}{U} instant, creatures lose all abilities and have base 2/2 until EOT. Draw a card. Key targets: Archon of Cruelty ETB ability disabled, Emperor of Bones Adapt ability disabled, Guide of Souls' energy generation disabled. Spell Snare: {U} counter target spell with MV 2. Hits Counterspell, Cori-Steel Cutter, White Orchid Phantom, Teferi TRav. These flex slots shift based on the meta — Dress Down vs combo-heavy fields, Snare vs fair-blue fields.
Chapter 03

Game Flow

"Never tap out without a reason. Every tapped turn is their free turn." — Sequencing Principle

The Four Phases

01
Survive
Turns 1–3. Do not tap out. Prismatic Ending on their one-drop. Counterspell their two-drop. Hold up interaction for anything they try to resolve. The only exception: Teferi, Time Raveler on turn 3 to immediately lock them out of instant-speed play.
02
Establish
Turns 4–6. Supreme Verdict clears what you couldn't answer one-for-one. Snapcaster Mage generates card advantage while keeping mana open. Memory Deluge at end of their turn finds Teferi Hero or additional answers for the following turn.
03
Deploy
Turn 5–7. Teferi, Hero of Dominaria with Counterspell or Force of Negation backup. The Teferi +1 untaps two lands at the beginning of the next end step — meaning on their turn you have counter mana available. This is the defining asymmetry.
04
Close
Turn 8+. Their hand is empty. Teferi +1 draws a card every turn while they draw threats into your counters. Teferi -3 removes their last permanent. Emblem closes out the last resources. You close with a Shark token or Celestial Colonnade — almost any threat works from this position.

Offensive Gameplan

This deck does not apply pressure early — it generates card advantage until the opponent is out of threats. The "offensive" plan is Teferi, Hero of Dominaria accruing loyalty while you hold counter mana open. Pressure comes from the opponent being forced to overextend into Supreme Verdict or walk their key spells into Counterspell.

A "winning position" is: Teferi Hero on the battlefield at 5+ loyalty, one or more blue mana untapped, and at least one counter or Force of Negation in hand. From this position the opponent cannot win through fair Magic. Any threat they deploy is countered; any threat that resolves gets Teferi -3'd to third from top.

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turn 1
Never Tap Out
Fetchland into Island. Spell Snare open if applicable. Pass. The exception: if they are on a deck with known T1 mana dorks or cantrips that need to be answered, Prismatic Ending for {W} on a one-drop is correct. Otherwise, observe and identify their deck.
Turn 2
Answer or Develop
Counterspell their key two-drop. Or Prismatic Ending (converge 2 = hits MV 2 or less). Or Spell Snare their Counterspell if in a blue mirror. If their board is empty: Teferi, Time Raveler applies the sorcery lock at minimal cost. Never deploy Snapcaster without a target worth flashing back.
Turn 3
Assert Control
Teferi, Time Raveler if the coast is clear — the static sorcery lock immediately prevents their instant-speed responses. Or Supreme Verdict into a wide board. Or Snapcaster + Counterspell to counter their most important turn-3 play while developing a 2/1 body. This is the critical turn where you establish who controls the game.
Turn 4+
Teferi Hero
Deploy Teferi, Hero of Dominaria when you can hold up countermagic. +1 immediately: draw a card. At the beginning of the next end step, untap up to two lands — use those untapped lands to hold counters on their turn. Each turn Teferi survives is a +1 card advantage and threat to their entire board.

Sample Sequences

Sequence 1 — "Teferi Lock" (Ideal Line vs Midrange)
T1Flooded Strand, crack for Island. Pass with Spell Snare open.
T2They cast a threat. Counterspell it. Their board is empty on turn 2.
T3Cast Teferi, Time Raveler. Static lock is immediately active — they can no longer hold up counterspells or responses at instant speed.
T4Snapcaster Mage targeting Counterspell in the GY. Develop 2/1 body, hold 2 mana for the flashed-back Counterspell on their turn.
T5Cast Teferi, Hero of Dominaria. +1: draw. At next end step: untap two lands. On their turn: hold up Counterspell with untapped mana.
Result: Teferi Hero at 5 loyalty, counter backup on their turn every turn, opponent has no hand. Game is effectively over.
Sequence 2 — "Verdict Reset" (vs Aggro / Wide Board)
T1Fetchland, Island. Prismatic Ending their one-drop (converge W).
T2They develop two more creatures. Solitude (evoke a white card) exiles their biggest threat. Pass at 2 life.
T3They go wide. You have three lands. Pass — hold Supreme Verdict mana.
T4Supreme Verdict. Cannot be countered. Destroy all creatures. Board wiped. They have zero permanents.
Result: Empty board, you have 4+ lands and a full hand of interaction. From this point Teferi Hero closes.
Sequence 3 — "Snap Loop" (Grindy Game vs Control / Blink)
T3Cast Snapcaster Mage. Target Counterspell in GY — it gains flashback {U}{U}.
T3On their turn: they cast their key threat. Flash back Counterspell for {U}{U}. Counter it. Snap survives as a 2/1 blocker.
T5Second Snapcaster Mage. Target the original Counterspell again (now back in GY from Snap flashback). Another {U}{U} counter available.
Result: Two Snapcasters, two graveyard interactions recycled, opponent has seen 4 answers from 2 cards. Card advantage is insurmountable.

Mulligan Philosophy

Snap keep: 3–4 lands (including at least one blue source), one Counterspell or Solitude, and either Memory Deluge/Snapcaster to refuel or a second interaction piece. This hand functions from turn 1 and doesn't need to draw perfectly.

Snap mulligan: Zero interaction (all lands and card draw), five or more lands, one-land hands regardless of other cards, hands with no blue source by turn 2.

On the draw: The extra card means you can keep hands slightly weaker on interaction because you have one more card to find it. Snap/Memory Deluge hands become more keepable on the draw.

Teferi TRav in opener: A hand with Teferi TRav + 3 lands + 2 answers is almost always a snap keep. TRav on turn 3 into counter backup is the fastest and safest path to establishing control.

Step 1: Cast Teferi TRav. Static ability immediately active — opponents can only cast spells at sorcery speed.

Step 2: +1 activation gives you flash on sorceries. You can now Prismatic Ending and Supreme Verdict at instant speed while they cannot respond at instant speed.

Step 3: Deploy threats or counter freely — their interaction is halved in effectiveness.

Result
Interaction Asymmetry
Their Counterspell can only be cast on their own turn (when they're not casting the spell they want to protect). Force of Negation loses its free alternate cost (cannot use on their turn, which is now a sorcery window). Persist must be cast at sorcery speed — cannot be used to respond to graveyard hate. This alone wins entire games against blue interactive decks.

Lines & Tricks

  • StackTeferi TRav -3 bounces an artifact, creature, or enchantment AND draws a card: Even without a valid target, -3 resolves and you draw a card. Always activate -3 when you need a draw even without a bounce target. You do not need to choose a target — if you don't choose one, you still draw.
  • StackTeferi Hero +1 untap is a triggered ability at the next end step, not immediate: Cast Teferi Hero on turn 5, +1, draw a card. The untap trigger fires at the beginning of your next end step (after your main phase, in your own end step). Those lands untap while it's still your turn — meaning you can tap for the untapped mana in your OWN end step. This is useful for Memory Deluge at instant speed in your own end step.
  • SequenceSnap before Verdict on the same turn: Flash in Snapcaster Mage targeting Prismatic Ending or Counterspell. Then cast Supreme Verdict. Verdict destroys Snapcaster, but you got the Snap ETB trigger and the flashback spell — all before Verdict resolved. This is the correct sequencing when you need both a Snap target and the sweeper in the same turn.
  • CombatDress Down + ETB threat: Cast Dress Down in response to Archon of Cruelty's ETB trigger going on the stack. Dress Down resolves, Archon loses all abilities including its ETB text box — the ETB trigger already on the stack still resolves. Dress Down must be cast before Archon's ETB trigger is placed, not after. Time: respond to Archon entering before it generates ETB triggers on the stack.
  • AvoidNever counter Persist: Counterspell cannot counter Persist because Persist is a black sorcery — Counterspell counters any spell. It absolutely CAN counter Persist. The error is assuming Consign to Memory counters Persist — it doesn't (Persist is a colored sorcery, not a triggered ability or colorless spell). Use Counterspell or Force of Negation for Persist, not Consign.
  • AdvancedArchmage's Charm third mode: {U}{U}{U} counter target spell unless controller pays 3 — OR draw two cards — OR gain control of target nonland permanent with MV 1 or less. Steal their Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer at instant speed. The steal is permanent and removes one of the strongest threats in the format for the cost of a counter.
Chapter 05

Role Assignment

UW Control has one role in almost every matchup: the controller. The deck never applies pressure. The rare exception is when Teferi TRav locks them into sorcery speed and you briefly attack with Snapcaster Mage and Solitude. The distinction between the three modes is how you allocate your interactive resources — not whether you play offense or defense.

Survive + Stabilize
vs aggro. You are behind on board from turn 1. Every answer buys a turn. Supreme Verdict on turn 4 is your reset button. Don't tap out without Verdict mana. Solitude via evoke is free removal — use it aggressively to buy turns. Path to Exile postboard as extra removal.
vs: Boros Energy · Izzet Prowess · Any aggro deck
Counter + Outvalue
vs combo and graveyard. Hold Counterspell and Force of Negation for the key piece. Teferi TRav locks Persist to sorcery speed. Rest in Peace from the board removes the GY permanently. The goal is a hard stop on the combo before it assembles.
vs: Glockulous · Amulet Titan · Any combo
Answer + Teferi Hero
vs midrange and control mirrors. Answer their threats efficiently, generate card advantage with Snap, close with Teferi Hero at 5+ loyalty. Dovin's Veto counters their Teferi uncounterably — hold it specifically for the mirror Teferi wars. Whoever resolves Teferi Hero with backup wins.
vs: Jeskai Blink · UW Control Mirror · Dimir Midrange

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Tapping out on your turn without a reason. Every turn you tap out is a free development turn for them. The mana must accomplish something worth the risk — Teferi Hero, Verdict into a wide board, or a Snap that generates immediate value. Tapping for Memory Deluge on your own turn when you could cast it at their end step is one of the most common errors.
  2. Countering the wrong spell. When they cast Faithless Looting into Persist in the same turn, Counterspell Persist, not Faithless Looting. The Looting filters their hand — the Persist is the game-losing play. Always identify which spell wins them the game if it resolves and protect your answer for that spell.
  3. Deploying Teferi Hero into open mana without counter backup. A Teferi Hero that gets immediately Spell Pierced or Force of Negated with no backup is a 5-mana do-nothing. Only deploy Teferi Hero when you can protect him — hold Force of Negation or Counterspell in hand when you cast him.
  4. Misusing Dress Down timing vs ETB threats. Dress Down must be cast before the ETB trigger is placed on the stack to prevent the trigger. If Archon of Cruelty is already on the stack resolving, its ETB will trigger after it enters regardless of Dress Down. React in the casting window, not the resolving window.

Boros Energy — Survive Until Verdict

Boros kills faster than your reactive setup. Their Galvanic Discharge hits creatures and planeswalkers — your Teferi TRav dies to it (Discharge targets planeswalkers). Goblin Bombardment converts tokens into reach damage that bypasses your life total plan. Your path: survive on Prismatic Ending + Solitude through turns 1-3, resolve Supreme Verdict on turn 4 to reset their board, then close with Teferi Hero from an empty board.

The key pressure point: do not deploy Teferi, Time Raveler into a wide board. TRav has 4 loyalty — Goblin Bombardment can sacrifice 4 tokens to ping Teferi to death at instant speed. Only deploy TRav when their token count is at 2 or fewer.

Jeskai Blink — The Teferi Wars

Both decks run Teferi, Hero of Dominaria as their primary win condition. Whoever resolves it with counter backup first has a major advantage. They also apply early pressure through Guide of Souls energy generation, Ocelot Pride tokens, and Phelia bounce loops. You need to deal with their board while holding the Teferi wars. Dovin's Veto — {W}{U} counter target noncreature spell, cannot be countered — is the uncounterable answer to their Teferi Hero and any other key noncreature spell.

Izzet Prowess — Race to Verdict

Prowess kills on turn 3-4. You need Supreme Verdict on turn 4 to stabilize. The problem is they can chain spells in a single turn dealing 12+ damage while you're setting up Verdict. Prismatic Ending kills Dragon's Rage Channeler before delirium (MV 1), Solitude exiles Slickshot Show-Off (MV 2), and Verdict resets everything. The matchup is difficult because their kill is faster than your setup.

Key note: Dress Down makes every creature a 2/2 with no abilities — Dragon's Rage Channeler loses delirium, Cori-Steel Cutter equipped creatures lose all abilities including trample and haste. Dress Down at instant speed on their attack step is extremely powerful here.

Priority of your Dovin's Veto targets: (1) Their Teferi, Hero of Dominaria — never let this resolve if you can help it. (2) Any key noncreature spell that wins them the game. Priority of your Mystical Dispute targets: (1) Their Counterspell when your Teferi is on the stack. (2) Their Force of Negation when protecting your key spell. (3) Any blue spell for 1 mana when you're mana-ahead.

Heuristics,
Mistakes & Edge Cases

Pilot Principles

  1. Never tap out without a reason. Every turn you tap out is a free development turn for them. The mana must accomplish something that justifies the risk — Teferi TRav, Verdict into a wide board, or Snapcaster generating immediate card advantage. "Developing" without an answer in hand is a trap.
  2. Keep (minimum hand): 3–4 lands, one answer, one way to refuel or a second answer. One-land hands are never keepable. Hands with only lands and card draw and no interaction are risky on the play. Any hand that cannot answer a turn-2 threat should be mulliganed.
  3. Teferi, Time Raveler's sorcery lock is a static ability — it is always active. It does not come from the +1. When Teferi TRav enters the battlefield, opponents can immediately only cast spells at sorcery speed. The +1 gives YOU sorcery flash. Know the difference — it matters every turn he's in play.
  4. Teferi Hero +1 untaps two lands at the BEGINNING OF THE NEXT END STEP, not immediately. Cast Teferi Hero. +1 draws a card. Pass turn. At the beginning of YOUR end step (before you actually move to your opponent's turn), the untap trigger fires. Use those untapped lands to hold counters on their turn.
  5. Teferi Hero -3 puts a nonland permanent THIRD from top — not exiled, not on top. They can draw into it within three turns. Don't -3 a permanent if they have Memory Deluge or other topdeck manipulation — they'll find it. Use -3 to answer an immediate threat, knowing it's a temporary answer.
  6. Snapcaster targets a GY card immediately on ETB — you cannot wait. When Snapcaster Mage enters, the ETB trigger goes on the stack and you choose the target immediately. You cannot cast Snapcaster and then decide what to target after seeing how they respond. Decide before casting.
  7. Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities and colorless spells only. It cannot counter: Counterspell, Force of Negation, Persist, Faithless Looting, or any colored spell. Its targets: Archon's ETB trigger, Emperor's "counters placed" trigger, Summoner's Pact upkeep trigger, Snapcaster's ETB trigger. Know this list cold before deploying Consign.
  8. Supreme Verdict cannot be countered and destroys ALL creatures including yours. Snap before Verdict. Sequence Snapcaster Mage first to get the ETB value, then Verdict to destroy the board including Snapcaster. Don't Verdict into your own Snapcaster stack — flash it in before Verdict resolves.
  9. Force of Negation only counters noncreature spells and is only free on the opponent's turn. On your own turn, Force costs {1}{U}{U}. On their turn, you may pitch a blue card instead. If you have to Force on your own turn for some reason, you need the mana. Force also exiles the countered spell (not GY) — meaningful vs Snapcaster and Flashback cards.
  10. Dress Down must be cast before an ETB trigger is placed to prevent it. If you cast Dress Down as a creature is entering the battlefield (in the casting trigger window), Dress Down resolves before the creature's ETB triggers fire. If Archon has already triggered (ETB trigger is on the stack), Dress Down removing its abilities doesn't counter the trigger already on the stack.
  11. Dovin's Veto cannot be countered — hold it for their Teferi Hero in mirrors. Do not waste Veto on a minor noncreature spell. In blue mirrors, Veto is the most important card in your 75 postboard. Its targets in priority order: (1) Teferi Hero, (2) Persist (vs Glockulous — though Counterspell is better), (3) Any other key noncreature spell.
  12. Tournament clock management: UW games run long. Play briskly. RC rounds are 50 minutes. A typical UW game takes 25-30 minutes. You have time for two games if both are efficient. Never slow-play your own decision trees — know your lines before the card hits the table. Deliberation happens before the game, not during it.

Common Mistakes

  • MistakeCountering the wrong spell when they have two threats in hand: When they cast Faithless Looting into Persist in the same turn, counter Persist (the winning play) not Faithless Looting (the setup play). Identify which spell wins them the game if it resolves. Always counter the game-winning spell, not the setup spell. Looting is a sorcery — if TRav is in play, they can only cast it on their main phase and you see it in sequence. Counterspell the Persist on the chain.
  • MistakeDeploying Teferi Hero into open counter mana without backup: A Teferi Hero that gets immediately Spell Pierced or Force of Negated with no response is a 5-mana do-nothing. The golden rule: you need to hold at least one Counterspell, Force of Negation, or Dovin's Veto in hand when you deploy Teferi Hero. If you don't have backup, wait. Drawing for two turns with Snap is fine.
  • MistakeUsing Consign to Memory on Persist: Consign cannot counter Persist. Persist is a black sorcery. Consign only counters triggered abilities and colorless spells. The error usually happens when players panic-fire Consign at whatever is on the stack. Before you cast Consign, verbally confirm: "Is this a triggered ability or a colorless spell?" If neither, Consign does nothing.
  • MistakeMissing Dress Down timing vs ETB creatures: Dress Down must be cast before the creature enters and triggers. If you cast Dress Down after Archon of Cruelty's ETB trigger is already on the stack, the trigger still resolves. React when the creature is cast (in response to the spell on the stack), not when the ETB trigger fires. The window is narrower than most players expect.

Advanced Play & Edge Cases

  • NicheMemory Deluge at end of your own end step: Teferi Hero +1 draws, then untaps two lands at the beginning of your end step. Those untapped lands are available for spells during your end step. Cast Memory Deluge at your own end step: X=3 or more looks at many cards, then put two in your hand. This sets up your next turn with exactly the cards you need while spending mana in what would otherwise be a dead window.
  • Thread NeedleArchmage's Charm third mode — steal Ragavan: {U}{U}{U}: Gain control of target nonland permanent with MV 1 or less. Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer is MV 1. Stealing Ragavan at instant speed is a 3-for-1 — they lose their best threat, you gain a 2/1 haste that generates mana and cards. This is the correct use of Archmage's Charm in the Boros/Prowess matchup when Ragavan is the only 1-drop in play.
  • StackSolitude evoke vs Force of Negation: Solitude cast via evoke is still a spell — Force of Negation can counter it (it's not a noncreature spell pitch, it's a creature spell with an alternate cast cost). If they have Force of Negation mana available and Solitude is your answer to their threat, consider whether TRav's sorcery lock prevents them from using Force (Force is only free on their turn — under TRav's static, they can only cast at sorcery speed, but Force of Negation free pitch is an alternate cost, not an instant-speed play — this is contested rules territory; the safer line is to resolve Solitude when they're tapped).
  • Break RuleWhen to tap out for Memory Deluge on your turn: The standard rule is never tap out. Break this rule when: you're at 5+ lands, have already answered every threat on the board, and tapping for Memory Deluge on your turn finds Teferi Hero which resolves next turn with counter backup. The rule exists to prevent you from being empty when they develop — if their board is empty and they have no hand, tapping out is safe.
