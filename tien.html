The Philosophy

  • GreedyNot reckless — greedy. There's a difference. Reckless players take bad lines and lose to variance. Greedy players push margins that other pilots leave on the table, and know exactly which margins those are.
  • CalibratedThe "just cut a land" mentality isn't sloppiness. It's a calibrated read on what the deck actually needs versus what conventional wisdom says it needs. With Mono-U Belcher, he's right.
  • Pre-SolvedHe knows his decklist cold. He can reconstruct the majority of his opponent's list from memory before seeing a card. The game is already partially solved before he sits down.

The Framework

"What do I die to. How do I win. What does a winning game look like. How do I thread the needle to steal the match." — In order, every match, every game

Four questions before anything else. The opening hand evaluation, the mulligan decision, the sequencing call on turn three — all of it gets routed through that framework. Most players don't have a clear model for when the situation forces them to go for it versus when they have margin to sculpt a better position. Tien does. He knows which turns are free and which aren't — and that creates decisions his opponents never see coming.
