Player Profile

Urbae

"I have to know how to approach every possible matchup. That takes reps, not reading."

Garrison — Urbae — found control the moment Return to Ravnica hit the table. Supreme Verdict resetting the board, Sphinx's Revelation refilling his hand at end-of-turn — the whole archetype clicked, and it never let go. He grew up in southern Oregon, in a local scene flooded with other control players, and the shop turned into a sub-game of who could build and pilot the cleanest blue-white pile. Iteration on iteration. Mirror after mirror. The reps stacked up at a rate most players never log.

The polish came from study, too. Garrison learned the deeper layers of the archetype watching Wafo-Tapa grind MTGO challenges on YouTube — the patron saint of measured, technically perfect control play. The lessons stuck: don't lose to your own sequencing, don't tap out unless you're closing the door, every land drop matters in the end-game. For years it was reps without results. Then the 2024/2025 RCQ season hit, the work compounded, and Garrison qualified for his first Regional Championship — and made Day 2.

He's a control player at heart, but his strongest results have all come from combo-control hybrids — decks that fuse the inevitability of a control shell with a closer that ends games on his terms. Underworld Breach, Stock Up, Consult the Star Charts, Wandering Emperor, Jace's mill ultimate — these are his weapons. The deck doesn't need to win the long game when it can win the right one. His philosophy on archetype selection is open and ruthless: the best deck of the format is always the right call. If that means learning the mirror cold, he learns the mirror cold. There's no pet-deck loyalty in his approach — only edge.

Playstyle

Identity
Reps Over Theory
Garrison's approach to preparation is uncommon among analytical players: he prioritizes time at the table over time in spreadsheets. Theory has a ceiling. Reps don't. To enter an event with confidence, he has to feel how a matchup plays — what hands they keep, what mulligans they take, where the pivot points are, when the trap turns appear. That kind of knowledge can't be downloaded; it has to be grown.
Format Lean
Lower Power Wins
Garrison has gravitated toward lower-power formats in recent years. When the cards aren't doing the heavy lifting, sequencing and strategy do — and that's where his edge lives. He doesn't want to win because his deck is two power levels above his opponent's. He wants to win because he saw the line two turns earlier than they did.
Profile
Seven Modes
Psychological in reading opponent decisions. Analytical in evaluating board states. Adaptive as the game plan shifts mid-match. Reflexive in trigger and timing windows. Reactive by archetype and temperament. Patient when the right play is to do nothing. Pensive when the right play is to think longer.
