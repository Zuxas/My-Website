Chapter 01

Overview

"The deck doesn't care what you're playing. On turn three, it wins."

Ruby Storm is Modern's premier critical-mass combo deck. It doesn't set up a two-card lock or assemble a specific combo — it overwhelms the game by chaining cheap spells until the Storm count reaches 20, then wins with a single Grapeshot. The engine has three phases: deploy a reducer, chain rituals and draw spells, then recast everything via Past in Flames. When it works, it's essentially unstoppable. When it's interrupted early, it dies in the water.

What separates Ruby Storm from older Izzet Storm builds is the quality of the cost reducers. Ruby Medallion is an artifact that dodges creature removal. Ral, Monsoon Mage is a creature that can survive a removal spell and keep his static ability active on the back side. With either reducer in play, Desperate Ritual and Pyretic Ritual each produce two net mana instead of one — turning every ritual into a Dark Ritual. That doubled efficiency is what makes the storm count tractable.

The deck sits at 6.2% of the Modern meta (March 2026). It was the second-most-played deck at Pro Tour Edge of Eternities but posted poor win rates there due to a field loaded with targeted hate like Drannith Magistrate. In a field less prepared for Storm, it is dramatically better. Its game 1 is among the strongest in Modern — pure speed with no dead cards against an unprepared opponent.

Deck Identity

Primary Axis
Critical Mass Combo
Not "A+B" combo. Wins by casting ~20 spells in one turn, generating enough storm count that a single Grapeshot deals lethal. No deterministic loop required — just enough mana and draws to keep chaining.
Fastest Kill
Turn 2
Turn 1 Strike It Rich → Turn 2: Ritual → Reducer → Ritual → chain draws → Past in Flames → Wish for Grapeshot. The realistic consistent kill is turn 3. Turn 2 requires a very specific hand but is achievable.
Game 1 Posture
Heavily Favored
Almost every deck in Modern is unprepared for Storm game 1. No main-deck hate, no answers that line up cleanly. The impulse draw spells are 2-for-1s that can't be countered efficiently — opponents run out of answers first.
Post-Board Posture
Known Weakness
Grafdigger's Cage, Damping Sphere, Deafening Silence, Drannith Magistrate — all hard stops. Opponents bring multiple hate pieces. Ruby Storm pivots via Orim's Chant, Prismatic Ending, and speed before hate can be deployed.
Skill Distribution
Mana Counting
The primary skill is precise mana counting before committing to a combo turn. Wasted rituals on aborted turns lose games. The deck rewards players who count to a winning line before spending resources, not those who "feel" their way through combos.
Meta Position
Glass Cannon
Excellent at unfair tables, poor against players who prepared. RCQ meta is consistently under-prepared for Storm. The deck rewards knowing your metagame: is your room running Cages and Spheres, or hoping their creatures kill you first?

Not blue, not colorless. Does NOT reduce Ral, Monsoon Mage (Ral is red but also {1}{R}=2 mana, Ruby makes it {R}). However, Ral DOES reduce Ruby's cast cost from {2} to {1}. These directions are asymmetric — critical for sequencing.
Ral, Monsoon Mage
{1}{R} · Creature // Planeswalker

Back (Ral, Leyline Prodigy): +1 makes I/S cost {1} less until YOUR NEXT TURN — this persists through opponent's turn even if Ral dies. −8 exiles top 8, cast for free. Desperate Ritual
{1}{R} · Instant — Arcane

Critically: Pyretic Ritual is NOT Arcane, so splice only triggers off Arcane spells. Always cast Pyretic Ritual first — Pyretic generates mana then the next Arcane spell can splice DR onto it. Incorrect order wastes the splice.
Glimpse the Impossible
{2}{R} · Sorcery
Exile top 3, play THIS TURN only. At the beginning of the NEXT end step, cards remaining in exile from this effect go to the GY and create 0/1 Eldrazi Spawn tokens. Critical: cards can't be played next turn like Reckless Impulse — they're gone. Use Glimpse mid-combo only; using it early on a non-combo turn wastes cards unless you can immediately play everything revealed.
Past in Flames
{3}{R} · Sorcery

Critical: Grafdigger's Cage says "players can't cast spells from graveyards" — this shuts down ALL Past in Flames flashback. If Cage resolves, hold priority after casting Flames while it's still on the stack to cast instant-speed spells from hand before the Cage resolves. Leyline of Sanctity doesn't stop PIF — it only blocks targeting.
Orim's Chant
{W} · Instant
Target player can't cast spells this turn. Kicker {W}: creatures can't attack. Requires WHITE mana — that's why Elegant Parlor (ETB tapped, {T}: W) and Sacred Foundry exist in the mana base. On your combo turn, chanting yourself lets you go off without counterplay. Chanting opponent during their upkeep buys one full turn to combo. The mana cost is real: you spend {W} from your non-red mana source.

Chapter 03

Game
Flow

Ruby Storm plays three distinct phases in every game: Setup, Threshold, and Execution. The skill of the deck is compressing Setup into as few turns as possible and recognizing the exact moment Threshold is reached — then committing without hesitation.

Three Phases

Phase 1 — Turns 1–2
Setup
Goal: resolve a cost reducer (Ruby Medallion or Ral, Monsoon Mage) and reach at least 3 mana. Without a reducer, winning the same turn requires a near-perfect hand. Use surveil lands and Reckless Impulse effects to find the missing piece. Don't waste rituals trying to "half-combo" — each wasted ritual costs you a full extra mana on the real turn.
Phase 2 — Turn 3+
Threshold
Count before you commit: "Do I have enough mana to cast 8+ spells AND find Past in Flames OR flip Ral?" The Threshold check is mathematical. If the answer is yes with a reasonable margin, go. If no, durdle one more turn. The mistake is going "on feel" and running out of steam at 14 storm count with no Grapeshot.
Phase 3 — Combo Turn
Execution
Once off the ground, the deck basically can't be stopped. The draw spells find more mana, the mana finds more draws. Use Wish for the sideboard Past in Flames if needed. Prioritize flipping Ral once Ruby is in play — the -8 casting 8 spells for free essentially wins on the spot. End with Wish → Grapeshot.

Turn-by-Turn Example — Turn 3 Kill

Turn 1
Play Mountain. If on the draw: Elegant Parlor ETBs tapped, look at top card (surveil). If holding Strike It Rich, cast it — create a Treasure. Don't crack the Treasure yet. If holding Gemstone Caverns on the draw, open with it in play (exile a card), giving you free colorless mana.
Turn 2
Play second land. Cast Ruby Medallion for {2} (or use Strike It Rich Treasure to reach {2}). Pass. Ral costs {1}{R} — also castable if you have RR. With Ruby in play, every subsequent red spell costs {1} less.
Turn 3 — Combo
Untap with Ruby and 3 mana. With Ruby: Pyretic Ritual costs {R} → adds RRR = net +2R. Cast Pyretic (count: 1 spell, 5 mana). Cast Desperate Ritual (count: 2 spells, 7 mana). Play Wrenn's Resolve (3 spells, 5 mana) — exile 2 cards. Play Reckless Impulse (4 spells, 3 mana) — exile 2 more. Find a Manamorphose among the 4 cards: cast it (5 spells, 3 mana, draw a card). Find another ritual: +2 mana. Reach 8-10 spells, find Past in Flames. Flashback all spells from GY. Now you're at 16-20 storm. Wish → Grapeshot for 20.

Mulligan Framework

You need to keep hands that can either (a) resolve a reducer by turn 2 or (b) cast enough impulse draws to find one by turn 3. Raw ritual hands with no draws are traps — they generate mana and go nowhere. Raw draw hands with no mana are stranded. The balance is 2 lands, 1 reducer, and a mix of rituals and draws.

Snap Keep
The Ideal 7
2 lands + 1 reducer + 2 rituals + 2 draw spells. This hand wins on turn 3 almost regardless of opponent's game plan. The mana curves perfectly — T1 land, T2 reducer, T3 ritual → ritual → draw → draw → Past in Flames → Grapeshot. Even if opponents hold up interaction, you can wait or chant through it.
Risky Keep
The Glass 6
1 land + fetch + reducer + 2 rituals + 1 draw. On the draw, the fetch + surveil land gives 3 looks at a second land (~72% to find it). On the play this drops to 60% — borderline. Keep on the draw, mulligan on the play unless the spells are excellent. The key: with a reducer and rituals, a single topdeck land wins the game.
Mulligan
Pure Ritual Hands
Hands with 4+ rituals and no draw spells. You make 10 mana and can't find Grapeshot. These hands feel powerful but are fundamentally broken — Storm needs to draw into more spells, not just float mana. Always ship these back.
Mulligan
No Reducer
A hand with zero reducers and no strong path to finding one by turn 2. The deck can technically win without a reducer but requires ~30+ spells in a turn — essentially impossible. Always mulligan any hand that can't realistically resolve Medallion or Ral by turn 2.

Speed Plays by Turn

Turn 2 Kill
Hand Required
Turn 1 Strike It Rich → Turn 2: use Treasure + Mountain for RR. Ritual → reducer → ritual → chain. Needs 2 rituals + Manamorphose + Past in Flames or multiple draws to finish. Realistic but requires perfect opening. Primarily relevant to beat removal on the reducer deployment turn.
Turn 3 Kill
Consistent
The bread and butter. T1 land, T2 reducer, T3 go off. At this speed, most decks haven't assembled enough interaction to stop you. Boros Energy might have 4–6 damage in, but Grapeshot at 20 doesn't care. Against control, T3 catches them before they've set up full counter coverage.
Turn 4+ Kill
High Risk
If you haven't killed by turn 4, the opponent has set up. Assume: (a) they've found their hate pieces, (b) creature decks have 8+ damage in, (c) combo opponents are also ready to win. Use Orim's Chant on their upkeep if needed to steal the extra turn. Valakut Awakening on turn 3 to cycle bad cards can enable a turn 4 kill instead.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 Ruby Medallion Is Everything
On the play, T1 Ruby Medallion dramatically accelerates your T2-3 kill. Every subsequent red spell costs 1 less. Grapeshot with Medallion in play costs {R} instead of {1}{R}. Past in Flames costs {2}{R} instead of {3}{R}. Mulligan aggressively for T1 Medallion hands on the play — a hand without Medallion or a cantrip to find it is playing a slower version of the deck.
On the Draw
Extra Card Finds Missing Piece
On the draw you see 8 cards, improving Medallion + kill spell odds. Keep looser hands on the draw — a cantrip-heavy hand without Medallion can keep on draw since you’ll see more cards. Against aggro on the draw: T1 Medallion is still correct over any other play. Against combo mirrors on the draw: Orim’s Chant hands (stop their combo turn) are more valuable since they’re a turn ahead.

When Disrupted

Medallion Destroyed
Second Medallion or Rebuild
Medallion removed: assess your mana. Can you still reach kill mana without the discount? If you have a second Medallion in hand, deploy it immediately. If not, your kill turn requires 1 additional mana per spell — count whether you can still chain enough spells at full cost. If not, develop another cantrip turn to find the second Medallion or enough rituals.
Grapeshot Countered
Empty the Warrens Backup
Grapeshot countered: if your storm count was 8+, Empty the Warrens makes 16+ Goblin tokens. Attack on their empty board for lethal next turn. This backup plan requires no additional cards — just a second win condition in the SB. Post-board vs Leyline of Sanctity (stops Grapeshot targeting face), Empty the Warrens becomes the PRIMARY plan since tokens attack, not target.
Chapter 04

Core
Engines

1
The Medallion Ritual Chain
Primary Win Engine
Setup
Deploy a Reducer
Land Ruby Medallion or Ral, Monsoon Mage by turn 2. Both reduce red spells — Medallion reduces all red; Ral reduces instants and sorceries. With either on board, Desperate Ritual ({1}{R}→{R}) now adds RRR for R spent = net +2R each. Every ritual becomes a Dark Ritual.
Execution
Chain Until Storm = 20
From 3 mana: Pyretic Ritual (→5), Desperate Ritual (→7), Wrenn's Resolve exile 2 (→5), Reckless Impulse exile 2 (→3), find Manamorphose (→3, draw), find another ritual (→5), continue. Cast Past in Flames, flashback everything. At 18+ storm, Wish for Grapeshot for lethal.
Result
Storm 20, Lethal
Grapeshot copies equal to spells cast before it. Each copy deals 1 damage to any target. 20 copies = 20 damage to opponent's face. Note: Leyline of Sanctity gives hexproof — Grapeshot can't target the player. Against Leyline, Empty the Warrens for 20+ Goblins and attack. Always track whether they can win through tokens.
2
Ral Flip — Free Spell Cascade
Secondary Win Engine
Setup
Cast Ral, Chain 6 Spells
Cast Ral, Monsoon Mage with extra mana floating. Each instant or sorcery cast during your turn triggers a coin flip. Track flip count. After 6 flip triggers resolve with a win, Ral transforms to Ral, Leyline Prodigy. Key trick: hold priority and cast instant-speed spells in response to flip triggers — both trigger "count" for the next trigger before either resolves.
Execution
Immediate −8 Ultimate
Ral enters with loyalty = instants/sorceries cast this turn. If you've cast 8+ spells before flipping, Ral enters with 8+ loyalty — immediately use the −8 to exile the top 8 cards and cast them for free. This is usually enough to close the game on the spot. Ral's +1 also grants cost reduction through the opponent's turn — useful if you're setting up for next turn.
Result
8 Free Spells → Lethal
8 free spells from Ral's −8 dramatically raise storm count. Combined with prior spells, reaching 20 storm is trivial. If life total is low, avoid flipping Ral early in the combo — losing coin flips at 4 life while chaining spells can kill you before you win. Prioritize speed over Ral engine at low life.
3
Past in Flames Rebuy
GY Engine
Setup
Fill GY, Resolve PIF
By turn 3, 8–12 instants and sorceries have passed through your hand into the GY. Past in Flames costs {3}{R} → {2}{R} with reducer → {R} with two reducers. Flashback costs the same as the original. This is the second "deck" — you've effectively drawn all the cards in your GY again.
Execution
Flashback Everything
After resolving PIF, flashback rituals (each costs {1}{R}→{R}), flashback draws (exile more cards), flashback a second PIF from SB via Wish if needed. Priority rule: if opponent responds to PIF on the stack with Grafdigger's Cage, hold priority and cast all instant-speed spells from hand first — Cage only matters when PIF actually resolves.
Result
Storm Doubles
Going from 10 storm to 20+ via flashback is typical. The sideboard PIF tutored via Wish means you functionally have 4 copies of this card. The deterministic Wish loop: Wish (SB) → get PIF → flashback entire GY → Wish again → get Grapeshot → win. No randomness once PIF resolves.

Lines & Tricks

  • Pyretic before Desperate: Always cast Pyretic Ritual first. Then cast an Arcane spell (like another Ritual-carrying spell or Manamorphose) and splice Desperate Ritual onto it via Arcane. Splice costs {1}{R} and adds the effect — generates extra mana without spending a card from hand.
  • Hold priority vs Cage: If opponent casts Grafdigger's Cage in response to Past in Flames, hold priority while Flames is on the stack. Cast any instant-speed spells you want to flashback from GY before Cage resolves. Once Cage resolves, PIF resolves but flashback is now locked. You still get to use spells cast while holding priority.
  • Ral flip stacking: Cast Ral with 2 mana floating. Cast an instant, both triggers (flip check + new flip check) go on stack. Before either resolves, cast another instant. Now all three triggers are on stack. Ral checks cumulative count when transforms — all pre-transform spells count toward loyalty on entry.
  • Don't count Manamorphose as +1R: Count it as +1 storm and "+1 card." The mana is incidental. Starting a combo turn with Manamorphose lets you see what you drew before committing all your rituals — abort if needed and you've only spent 2 mana, not your whole ritual package.
  • Wish timing: Wish finds a card from outside the game — you choose the card on Wish resolution, but you don't have to cast it immediately. You can Wish mid-combo, then choose PIF or Grapeshot at the last safe moment. If you Wish and the game state changes, you can choose differently than planned.
  • Shatterskull Smashing vs hate bears: Shatterskull Smashing (MDFC) kills Drannith Magistrate, Ethersworn Canonist, Thalia, and Deafening Silence creatures as a sorcery. Cast it before going off — this is an exception where spending a card pre-combo is correct because the hate piece costs you the game otherwise.
  • Valakut Awakening cycle: Cast Valakut Awakening, put all your excess lands or redundant spells on the bottom, draw that many +1. This is the Brainstorm of Ruby Storm — converts a bad hand into a good one mid-setup, and is especially powerful in post-board games where opponents have stripped your hand with discard.

Engine Priority Order

Priority 01
Ruby Medallion + Rituals
Ruby Medallion reducing costs + Desperate Ritual/Pyretic Ritual generating mana = the storm engine. Every ritual at full cost generates {R}{R} from {R}. With Medallion, Pyretic costs {R} and generates {R}{R}{R} = net +2 mana. Chain rituals to build storm count. This engine is the foundation: Medallion first, then chain.
Priority 02
Past in Flames Flashback Chain
Past in Flames gives everything in your GY flashback until end of turn. After PiF resolves, flash back every ritual in your GY for massive mana generation and storm count building. Flash back cantrips for card selection. Flash back a second PiF if available. This engine takes a 3-5 storm count to a 15-20 storm count in one turn.
Priority 03
Grapeshot or Empty the Warrens
Grapeshot closes when storm count = enough damage. Empty the Warrens closes when facing Leyline of Sanctity or when they’re at 20 life and you can’t reach lethal storm. Calculate before casting: Grapeshot storm count × 1 damage per copy. If that’s not lethal, switch to Empty the Warrens and win on the attack next turn with 16+ tokens.

Lines & Tactics

Lines &
Tactics

"Medallion math and Past in Flames chains are calculated before you cast the first spell."

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Critical
Ruby Medallion Reduces Generic Mana Only
Ruby Medallion reduces the cost of red spells you cast by {1}. It reduces GENERIC mana only — not colored pip requirements. Lightning Bolt costs {R}: Medallion doesn’t reduce it (no generic). Grapeshot costs {1}{R}: Medallion reduces it to {R}. Past in Flames costs {3}{R}: Medallion reduces to {2}{R}. With 2 Medallions, Past in Flames costs {1}{R}. Calculate mana requirements before starting your chain or you’ll strand mid-combo.
Critical
Past in Flames Gives Itself Flashback
Past in Flames gives all instants and sorceries in your graveyard flashback until end of turn — including itself. After PiF resolves, it goes to your GY. You can then flash it back again from the GY (paying {3}{R} or {2}{R} with Medallion) to give everything in GY flashback again. This means a single PiF can generate multiple copies of your storm pieces if you have the mana. The key: PiF’s flashback is granted by the RESOLVED PiF — PiF in GY before casting gets flashback from a newly resolved PiF.
Critical
Storm Counts Spells Cast This Turn, Not Copies
Grapeshot’s storm copies one copy for each spell cast before it this turn. The storm copies themselves are NOT spells cast — they’re copies put directly on the stack. Storm count: every ritual, every Medallion discount trigger, every cantrip, every PiF cast. Grapeshot itself counts as 1 spell. So storm of 10 = 9 previous spells + Grapeshot = 10 copies total. Count carefully before casting Grapeshot to know if it’s lethal.

Timing Windows

When to Hold Mana vs Spend It
The core timing decision for this deck: every land drop and every spell cast is a commitment. Know before your upkeep which lines require open mana this turn and which don’t. Holding mana = passing priority; spending it = committing to a plan. Make this decision in your upkeep, not in response to their action.
When to Cast at Their End Step vs Your Main Phase
End-step casts maximize information: you’ve seen their full turn, they’re often tapped out. Main-phase casts maximize mana availability for follow-up. Know which of your key spells gain most from end-step timing and which need main-phase mana backup.
Chapter 05

Role
Assignment

Ruby Storm has a fixed role in almost every matchup: the proactive combo player trying to win before the opponent stabilizes. The nuance is how urgently you need to go — and whether you should go in the face of open mana or wait for a safe window.

KILLER
Your natural role in 80% of games. The opponent is trying to set up their own plan. You win by killing them before they do. Don't waste turns playing around interaction they might not have — go when your math says you can win, not when the board state feels "safe."
vs Aggro, Midrange, Combo
PATIENT KILLER
Against heavy-interaction decks (UW Control, Jeskai Blink), slow down. Use impulse draw every turn, accumulate resources, and identify the exact turn they tap out or exhaust counters. Then kill them in a single uninterrupted turn. Never fight over counters — wait for the free window.
vs Control, Blink
HATED OUT
When a hate piece resolves (Cage, Sphere, Silence), you're in "pivot mode." Prismatic Ending or Shatterskull removes the piece. Orim's Chant buys a turn. Sometimes Empty the Warrens is the backup win — don't tilt, adapt. Removal in the SB exists specifically for this role.
Post-board vs prepared opponents

When to Go vs When to Wait

Go Now
Opponent tapped out for a permanent. Opponent has fewer than 2 cards in hand. You have a clean kill line (counted the mana). You're on turn 3 and opponent has no visible hate. You're racing a combo deck that wins on turn 4. These are your unambiguous green lights.
Wait One Turn
Opponent has 2+ mana open and a full hand. You're missing one key piece (second ritual, Manamorphose to bridge mana). You have Reckless Impulse and can use it now to see 2 more cards that might complete the line. Valakut Awakening can cycle away dead cards to find the missing piece.
Never Go
When you can't count a line to winning. Generating 12 mana and having no Grapeshot or Wish doesn't win games. The discipline of aborting a combo turn mid-way (by casting Reckless Impulse rather than your last ritual) is what separates experienced Storm players from pilots who go out on 15 storm.

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • 01
    Not counting storm before casting Grapeshot. Count every spell cast this turn before casting Grapeshot. Chain: Medallion, Ritual × 3, Cantrip × 2, PiF, flashback Ritual × 3, flashback Cantrip = 11 spells + Grapeshot = 12 copies = 12 damage. Do the math in advance — not after Grapeshot resolves.
  • 02
    Casting PiF before building enough storm in the GY. PiF only flashes back spells already in your GY. If you cast PiF immediately with 2 spells in GY, you get 2 flashbacks. Chain more rituals/cantrips before casting PiF to maximize the flashback pool. Optimal: 5+ spells in GY before PiF.
  • 03
    Using Orim’s Chant on your turn when it needs to be their upkeep. Orim’s Chant is most powerful at the BEGINNING OF THEIR UPKEEP — they can’t cast spells for the whole turn. Casting on their end step only stops their end-step instant play. Casting during your own turn does nothing for the combo protection you need.
  • 04
    Forgetting Empty the Warrens vs Leyline of Sanctity. Leyline prevents opponents from being targeted. Grapeshot targets them — it’s blocked. Empty the Warrens doesn’t target — it makes tokens. The tokens attack. Post-board vs Leyline: always pivot to Empty as the primary win condition. Never jam Grapeshot into a Leyline.

Role-Switching Signals

Force T2-3 Kill When
No Blue Mana Visible
Their opening land is not blue. They’ve spent their mana on T1-2. They’re on a fair deck with no stack interaction. Execute the combo as fast as possible. Every turn you wait gives them a draw step to find Force of Negation or Orim’s Chant.
Orim’s Chant First When
Blue Mana Visible
Any open blue mana = potential Force of Negation. Cast Orim’s Chant at the beginning of their upkeep — they can’t cast spells this turn. Then combo freely on your turn. Chant is the strongest protection piece in the deck and costs 0 tempo when used optimally.
Switch to Empty the Warrens When
Leyline or Low Storm Count
Leyline of Sanctity in play: Grapeshot can’t target them. Switch to Empty. Storm count will be 14+ anyway — 28 Goblin tokens is a 2-swing kill at 20 life. Also switch when your storm count is high but Grapeshot math doesn’t reach lethal.
Never Commit Without Counting
Pre-Chain Calculation
Before casting the first ritual in the chain, map your full line: available mana, available GY flashbacks, expected storm count, kill calculation. Committing to the chain without a kill line is a game loss. Calculate — then execute.

Lines & Tactics

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Storm wins when your opponent wastes counters on the wrong spell. Know exactly what to protect."
Storm Copies — Counter the Original Before Copies Exist
Storm says "when you cast this spell, copy it for each other spell cast before it this turn." The copies are created by a triggered ability — but the copies only exist AFTER the storm trigger resolves. The window to counter the storm spell is BEFORE the trigger fires: counter Grapeshot when it first hits the stack and no copies exist yet. If you wait until after storm resolves, countering one copy does nothing to the others — each copy is an independent object on the stack. Against opponents holding interaction: always cast the storm spell first and force them to decide before copies exist.
Ruby Medallion — Cost Reduction, Not Mana Generation
Ruby Medallion says "red spells you cast cost {1} less to cast." This is a static ability that modifies the cost of your spells — it's not a triggered or activated ability, so Consign to Memory cannot counter it. The reduction is continuous and automatic. Removing Medallion (Prismatic Ending, March of Otherworldly Light) is the only way to stop the cost reduction. With Medallion in play: Desperate Ritual costs {R} (effectively free), Manamorphose costs {1} (net mana positive), the entire chain becomes dramatically more efficient. Opponents should prioritize destroying Medallion on sight — it's the difference between a turn-4 kill and a turn-6 kill.
Past in Flames Flashback — Teferi TRav Makes It Sorcery Speed
Past in Flames gives all instant and sorcery cards in your graveyard flashback equal to their mana cost until end of turn. Normally, instants with flashback can be cast at instant speed. Under Teferi, Time Raveler's static ability ("each opponent can only cast spells during their own turn"), your opponents are restricted to their own main phases — but YOU are not. Under opponent's Teferi, your flashback instants (Manamorphose, Desperate Ritual) are cast at sorcery speed. Plan your chain accordingly: with Teferi on board, sequence flashback instants before sorceries to avoid sorcery-speed conflicts. If you control Teferi: this doesn't affect you since the restriction is on opponents.
Empty the Warrens — Tokens Enter Without Haste
Empty the Warrens creates 2 goblin tokens per copy but does NOT give them haste. This means: (1) the tokens cannot attack the turn they enter, (2) your opponent has at minimum one full turn to set up a block or answer. Against opponents with sweepers (Supreme Verdict, Anger of the Gods) this is the window where they get a free turn. Against opponents with nothing: you still win on your next attack. Empty the Warrens is your backup plan when Grapeshot is countered — but time your Empty correctly to avoid giving opponents a "use Verdict for free" turn when you're at low storm count.
Birgi, God of Storytelling — Adds Mana on Cast, Not on Resolution
Birgi says "whenever you cast a spell, add {R}." This trigger fires when you CAST the spell — before it resolves. This means: if your Desperate Ritual is countered by a Counterspell, Birgi's trigger already fired and you still got the {R} from Birgi (plus the {R} from Ritual before it was countered). The counter is "too late" to stop Birgi's trigger. Sequence: Birgi in play → cast any red spell → Birgi trigger fires (add {R}) → then the spell resolves or gets countered independently. Opponents must counter Birgi herself or accept that every spell they counter still generates {R}.
Manamorphose — Counts Toward Storm and Replaces Itself
Manamorphose costs {1}{R} or {1}{G}, adds 2 mana of any color combination, and draws a card. Net cost: 0 mana (costs 2, generates 2) and replaces itself in hand. Crucially: casting Manamorphose counts as a spell for storm purposes — it increments your storm count while costing nothing. With Ruby Medallion: Manamorphose costs {R} and generates 2 mana, netting {1} positive. Lavinia, Azorius Renegade counters Manamorphose if it's cast for no mana (alternative cost {0} net effect). Vexing Bauble counters any "spell cast without mana spent" — Manamorphose spent mana so Bauble doesn't trigger on it.
Chapter 07

Sideboard
Guide

The SB has one primary goal: beat Damping Sphere, Grafdigger's Cage, and Drannith Magistrate. Every SB card either removes hate or gives you a backup win plan around it. The white mana cards (Orim's Chant, Prismatic Ending) are the core; everything else is meta-call flex.

Sideboard Card Roles

Orim's Chant ×4
{W} instant. Target player can't cast spells this turn. This is Storm's best SB card, period. Cast on opponent's upkeep during their turn 3–4 kill attempt: they pass the turn unable to combo or interact. Cast on your own combo turn: protect your chain from Force of Negation or Consign. The kicker ({W}: creatures can't attack) is irrelevant in most matchups. You need one white mana source — Elegant Parlor or Sacred Foundry.
Prismatic Ending ×3
Exile target permanent with MV equal to colors of mana spent. Spend W = exiles MV 1 (Drannith Magistrate, Ethersworn Canonist). Spend WR = exiles MV 2 (Damping Sphere, Deafening Silence, Grafdigger's Cage). Spend WRG = exiles MV 3, etc. It's always sorcery speed — deploy Shatterskull Smashing instead if you need instant-speed or MV-flexible removal. Prismatic never hits activated ability artifacts (like Mox Opal under Stony).
Grapeshot ×1
The main deck win condition. Why in SB? Because drawing it mid-combo is a dead card that slows your storm count. Wish retrieves it at the exact moment you need it. Always the target of your first Wish in a combo turn. Storm deals 1 damage to any target — copies resolve separately, so 20 storm = 20 triggers, each can target the opponent for 1 damage each. Can't target players with Leyline of Sanctity in play.
Empty the Warrens ×1
Storm — create 2 Goblin tokens per copy. 20 storm = 42 Goblins. The backup win against Leyline of Sanctity (hexproof doesn't protect against being attacked). Also the backup when you can't reach 20 storm but can reach 12 and create 26 tokens. Against UW Control with Teferi that bounces things — 26 Goblins can't all be bounced before you attack for lethal. Warrens requires a clear attacking path — if opponent has Ensnaring Bridge (Eldrazi Tron), Goblins don't attack.
Past in Flames ×1
The SB Flames. Retrieved via Wish to double your GY flashback power or to enable the deterministic loop: Wish → PIF → flashback GY → Wish → Grapeshot. Having this in the SB functionally gives you 4 copies of PIF between main deck and Wish. Bring in via Wish, never actually SB-in to the main deck.
Galvanic Relay ×1
Storm sorcery — exile top card, play next turn, per storm copy. At storm 6, you exile 7 cards that persist to next turn. Not counterable (the copies are tokens on the stack). The hedge against counterspell-heavy opponents — jam Galvanic Relay with 6 storm, pass the turn with 7 exiled cards, win next turn easily. Also excellent when you've been disrupted and need to rebuild over two turns.
Brotherhood's End ×1
{1}{R}{R} sorcery. Deals 3 damage to each creature with power 2 or less. OR destroy each artifact with MV 3 or less. Mode 1 clears Boros Energy boards (Guide of Souls, Voice of Victory, Ocelot tokens are all ≤2 power). Mode 2 destroys Damping Sphere, Grafdigger's Cage, Chalice of the Void. Versatile removal that works against both hate pieces and board states.
Abrade ×1
{1}{R} instant. 3 damage to creature OR destroy target artifact. The instant-speed artifact answer. Use before going off to remove Grafdigger's Cage or Damping Sphere at instant speed. Also kills Thalia, Ethersworn Canonist, and Drannith Magistrate. Strictly inferior to Brotherhood's End on board but instant speed matters for removing mid-combo hates.
Into the Flood Maw ×1
{4}{U} Instant. Counter target spell OR exile target nonland permanent. The catch-all counter that requires blue mana. Available via Manamorphose (which can produce UU). Primarily for countering opponent's hate piece as it resolves before it enters play — removes Damping Sphere or Force of Negation before they land. The blue cost is real — you need Manamorphose producing blue.
Return the Favor ×1
Counter target spell or activated ability. If countered this way, you may copy it and may choose new targets. The combo answer for Eldrazi Tron: counter their Karn, then cast a copy of Karn for free to fetch their own hate artifact against them. Also counters Thought-Knot Seer's ETB trigger (it's a triggered ability — check if Return the Favor hits triggered abilities; oracle says "counter target spell or activated ability" — so triggered abilities are NOT hit). Primarily for Karn and Ugin lines.

Universal Rules for Sideboarding

Always cut the same cards in: Strike It Rich (slowest card, weakest mid-combo), Glimpse the Impossible (can't play cards next turn, awkward proactively), and Valakut Awakening (pure utility, not needed in combo-speed matchups). The core 44 spells (8 reducers, 8 rituals, 4 Manamorphose, 4+4 impulse draws, 3 PIF, 3 Wish) are never cut. The flex 8 (Strike It Rich ×2, Glimpse ×3, Valakut ×2, Shatterskull ×1) are the pool you draw SB cuts from.

Chapter 08

Heuristics

16 decision rules distilled from competitive play. Internalize these before an event — they replace slow in-game calculation with automatic pattern recognition.

  1. Count before you commit. Before spending any ritual, trace a line to 20 storm and Grapeshot. If the line doesn't exist, don't spend the ritual. Aborted combo turns cost you the game more often than slow turns do.
  2. Pyretic before Desperate, always. Cast Pyretic Ritual first. Then cast the next Arcane spell and splice Desperate Ritual onto it. Splice costs {1}{R} and adds RRR without spending a card. Wrong order wastes the splice entirely.
  3. A reducer is not optional. You cannot win without Ruby Medallion or Ral, Monsoon Mage in play (or a near-infinite number of spells). Every mulligan decision starts with "can I find a reducer by turn 2?" If no, ship it back.
  4. Ral's +1 persists through your opponent's turn. After flipping Ral to Ral, Leyline Prodigy, use the +1 ability immediately. Even if Ral dies to removal on opponent's turn, the cost reduction persists until your next turn — letting you combo off next turn at reduced costs.
  5. Don't Glimpse proactively. Glimpse the Impossible exiles cards you can only play THIS turn. On setup turns, those 3 cards are gone if you don't play them immediately. Save Glimpse for the combo turn — it's a mid-combo card, not a setup card.
  6. Deploy the reducer the turn you go off. Against removal-heavy decks, casting Medallion on turn 2 and passing telegraphs everything. Deploy the reducer on the turn you plan to win, ideally with mana up to attempt a flip or cast the first ritual before they can respond.
  7. Manamorphose first, always. Start every combo attempt by casting Manamorphose first. It draws a card and tells you what you have before you commit all your rituals. If the drawn card completes the line, continue. If not, you've only spent 2 mana and saw one extra card — not a wasted ritual.
  8. Elegant Parlor is your life total investment. Elegant Parlor ETBs tapped — it costs you tempo on turn 1. Play it only if (a) you have no better turn-1 land, or (b) the surveil is worth more than the tempo. Never play Parlor as your first land on turn 2.
  9. Grafdigger's Cage stops Past in Flames. If they cast Cage in response to PIF on the stack, hold priority and cast all instant-speed spells from hand before Cage resolves. PIF still resolves but can't flashback under Cage. Having instant-speed spells to cast is why you don't dump your hand before resolving PIF.
  10. Leyline of Sanctity = Empty the Warrens. If opponent starts with Leyline in play, you can't target them with Grapeshot. Automatically pivot to Empty the Warrens for 20+ Goblins. Keep this in mind during G2 mulligans — against white decks, consider whether your hand beats Leyline.
  11. Orim's Chant on their upkeep buys a full turn. Chanting during their draw step or upkeep means they can't cast spells for their entire turn — including their combo, interactive spells, or cascades. This is the correct timing against Living End, Reanimator, and any deck that needs to cast spells to kill you on turn 3.
  12. Ral at low life = danger. Each coin flip while Ral is on the front side deals 1 damage to you on a loss. At 4 life, three bad flips kill you mid-combo before you can win. Below 8 life, consider going off via Medallion line only and skipping Ral triggers entirely.
  13. Valakut Awakening on setup turns, not combo turns. Cast Valakut Awakening to shuffle away extra lands or dead cards and draw replacements on setup turns. During the combo, it's a tempo-negative spell unless you're desperate. The "put on bottom and draw+" effect is a Brainstorm — use it to fix bad hands, not to chain deeper on the kill turn.
  14. Don't filter Manamorphose into RR reflexively. Sometimes you need WW for Orim's Chant, or GG for Veil of Summer. Manamorphose can produce any two mana — plan one turn ahead for what colors you'll need. Against blue decks with Chants in hand: filter into WW with Manamorphose to enable your protection piece.
  15. Strike It Rich generates two spells for Ral. Strike It Rich creates a Treasure (spell 1) and then you can sacrifice that Treasure (not a spell — activated ability). But the Flashback also costs {2}{R}: Strike It Rich flashback casts it again (spell 2). Two spell triggers for Ral for the price of one card in a later turn. This matters when counting Ral's flip triggers.
  16. The impulse draws can't be countered efficiently. Opponents know this and often let Reckless Impulse and Wrenn's Resolve resolve rather than waste a counter. This is correct play on their part — but it means your setup phase goes uninterrupted. The counter threat is for your reducer and your Past in Flames. Probe with impulse first.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand with Ruby Medallion + 2 rituals, or a hand that cycles to find them by T3.
  • Storm rule: Count your storm count BEFORE casting Grapeshot. You need N copies to kill for N damage.
  • Medallion rule: Ruby Medallion reduces red spell costs by {1}. It does NOT generate mana — it reduces cost.
  • Past in Flames rule: PiF flashes back your whole GY. Cast it when you have 5+ mana AND 3+ spells to flash back.
  • TRav rule: Under Teferi, Time Raveler, PiF is sorcery speed. It already was sorcery speed. No change.
  • Sideboard rule: Damping Sphere prevents more than 1 spell per turn. Find your Wear // Tear before going off.

    Register with Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens counts confirmed. Both need to be there.
  2. 02
    Count storm BEFORE casting Grapeshot. Count your spells this turn. Lethal storm = N damage needed.
  3. 03
    Between rounds: did you wait for the right moment to cast PiF? Early PiF loses to disruption.
  4. 04
    Medallion vs no-Medallion lines are different. Know both paths before sitting down.
  5. 05
    The deck wins through speed and sequencing. Practice until the line is automatic.

Pre-Game Checklist

Always Ask Before Round 1
White Mana Ready?
Confirm you have Elegant Parlor + Sacred Foundry in your SB build for Orim's Chant. If you don't have white mana sources, Chants are dead cards. Check your land count: you need 20 lands total. Confirm fetch targets exist (Sacred Foundry for W/R).
Always Ask Before Round 1
Know Your SB Targets
Prismatic Ending hits MV 1 (Drannith Magistrate) with {W} or MV 2 (Damping Sphere, Cage) with {W}+another color. Brotherhood's End mode 2 destroys artifacts MV ≤ 3. Abrade is instant. These are not interchangeable — know which removal piece to bring for which hate piece before each round.
