Team Resolve · Modern · 2026 RC Season

decks we're testing

These are the Modern archetypes Team Resolve is currently testing for the 2026 RC season. Each deck has been gauntlet-tested against the expected field. Reference lists sourced from LDXP SEA26 results (Mar 27–29 2026).

Boros Energy (Mardu potential?)

Status: Known quantity — full playbook complete. Mardu shell under evaluation.

The established baseline. Ragavan generates resources on attack, Guide of Souls and Ocelot Pride create inevitability, and Ajani closes the game. The deck punishes stumbles immediately and rewards reps harder than anything else in the format. I qualified on this deck last season — 17 RCQ Top 8s.

The open question is whether adding black (Mardu) is worth it. Black provides Thoughtseize for disruption-first openers and potentially Overlord of the Balemurk as an ETB engine. The cost is mana consistency and losing some of the pure speed that Boros offers. Under review.

What it does well

  • Closes games extremely fast when the opponent stumbles
  • Generates advantage while applying pressure — not a linear aggro deck
  • Sideboard is deep and well-understood after a full season of reps
  • Rewards familiarity — every percentage point of efficiency matters at an RC

What we're watching

  • Sanctifier en-Vec is a hard lock the field is prepared to deploy
  • Opponents have refined their Wrath sequencing against the deck
  • Whether Mardu splash is net positive vs. mana base cost

Grixis Reanimator "Glockulous" (put a thing in the gy and look at your opponent — i have a gun)

Status: Full playbook complete.

Archon of Cruelty reanimated through Persist or Emperor of Bones — one trigger (sacrifice, discard, lose 3) is often enough to end the game. The deck loads the graveyard with Faithless Looting and Thought Scour, then deploys Archon as early as turn 3. Psychic Frog provides a resilient pressure element that the opponent must answer or die to. It's called "Glockulous" internally because once Archon resolves you're pointing a gun — they can't come back from the ETB.

What it does well

  • Archon of Cruelty on turn 3 is a near-unbeatable threat against fair decks
  • Multiple reanimation vectors — Persist, Emperor of Bones, Goryo's Vengeance
  • Psychic Frog is a legitimate threat even when the combo is disrupted
  • Teferi TRav locks Persist to sorcery speed — a meaningful meta interaction to understand

What we're watching

  • Rest in Peace and Leyline of the Void are hard stops
  • Consign to Memory counters the ETB trigger at instant speed
  • The deck needs reps on the Persist timing — multiple interaction windows

UW Control

Status: Full playbook complete — updated to Evariel's 8-0-2 Scepter-Chant list (LDXP SEA26 PTQ, Mar 27 2026).

The current iteration has shifted dramatically from the Snapcaster Mage shell. Evariel's 8-0-2 PTQ run features an Isochron Scepter + Orim's Chant full lock, Narset + Day's Undoing for a +6 net card draw, and Tune the Narrative banking energy for scalable Wrath of the Skies. This is control with multiple overlapping lock engines rather than a pure interaction-and-Teferi plan.

What it does well

  • Scepter + Chant lock completely prevents the opponent from casting spells
  • Narset + Day's Undoing generates +6 card advantage in one action
  • Wrath of the Skies hits artifacts and enchantments — broader than Supreme Verdict
  • Still meta-dependent — correct to register against midrange + combo fields

What we're watching

  • Artifact removal (March of Otherworldly Light, Kozilek's Command) breaks the Scepter lock
  • Fast aggro can close before the lock stabilizes
  • Tournament clock — UW games run long across a 9-round RC day

Jeskai Blink (aggressive — consider new Casey Jones card)

Status: Testing. Reference: Connor Mackenzie 9-0-4, LDXP SEA26 10k, Mar 28 2026. Decklist →

The aggressive Blink variant. Ragavan, Phlage, Phelia, Quantum Riddler, Solitude — all four-ofs — backed by Casey Jones, Vigilante (2x) as a new flexible threat. Casey Jones is notable: a 3/2 vigilance ninja that turns your blue spells into free cantrips when she connects. This version applies earlier, more sustained pressure than the Esper build but sacrifices some of Esper's card advantage top-end.

SB plan leans on Obsidian Charmaw (3x), High Noon (2x), Wrath of the Skies (2x), Mystical Dispute (2x). The Charmaw package is the anti-big-mana angle.

What it does well

  • Ragavan + Phelia on turns 1–2 generates enormous resource advantage vs. fair decks
  • Casey Jones punishes opponents for holding up blue mana — free value when she connects
  • Phlage is both early interaction and a recurring late-game threat
  • Consign to Memory provides counterplay at instant speed without disrupting the aggro clock

What we're watching

  • Lower top-end than Esper — runs out of gas against controlling decks that stabilize
  • Mana base (three colors) is less consistent than UW or Esper under Blood Moon
  • Casey Jones is an evaluation-in-progress; unclear if she's better than a 4th Solitude

Esper Blink (controlling and card advantage)

Status: Testing. Reference: robert seder 11-2-2 (rank 3), LDXP SEA26 10k, Mar 28 2026. Decklist →

The controlling Blink build — no red at all. Overlord of the Balemurk (4x) and Witch Enchanter (4x) generate card advantage rather than damage. Thoughtseize (4x) + Fatal Push (4x) form the disruption backbone. Flickerwisp (3x) reblinks permanents and acts as a blink payoff. Emperor of Bones (3x) provides graveyard recursion as value engine. Fewer threats but significantly more staying power.

SB leans on Damping Sphere (3x) and High Noon (3x) as a hard-stop anti-fast-mana package.

What it does well

  • Thoughtseize + Overlord ETB chains generate unrecoverable card advantage
  • No red means clean mana — Godless Shrine and Hallowed Fountain base is consistent
  • Flickerwisp at instant speed through Ephemerate resets opponent permanents at will
  • Higher top-end than Jeskai — can grind against any fair strategy

What we're watching

  • Slower clock — needs time to establish, punished by turn-3 combo kills
  • Losing Ragavan and Phlage means less pressure on the opponent's resources early
  • Damping Sphere is a two-way hate piece — can slow our own Ephemerate synergy

UW Blink (if Blood Moon is good this deck RIPS — better aggro line, lower top end)

Status: Theoretical — not yet in active testing. Depends on meta read.

The UW build is the cleanest mana — Island + Plains base means Blood Moon doesn't touch you while punishing every greedy three-color deck in the room. If Domain Zoo, Neoform, and multi-color goodstuff are over 25% of the field, registering pure UW is correct. The trade is losing Phlage (red), Ragavan (red), and Thoughtseize (black) for mana consistency and Blood Moon resilience.

Better aggro line than Esper (still has Solitude + Phelia + Ephemerate). Lower top-end ceiling than Jeskai because no Ragavan snowball or Phlage reach. Not yet on a specific list — this is the configuration to build toward if the meta calls for it.

What it does well

  • Clean UW mana base is immune to Blood Moon
  • Opponents boarding Blood Moon for the field get a dead card against you
  • Simpler sequencing — fewer decisions under pressure, important across a long event

What we're watching

  • No Ragavan means less free pressure and fewer stolen resources
  • No Phlage means less reach and fewer sweeper effects
  • Correct only in a specific meta — wrong register in Boros-heavy rooms

Dimir Midrange

Status: Testing. Reference: Roy Yang 10-1-3 (rank 5), LDXP SEA26 10k, Mar 28 2026. Decklist →

Counterspell-backed Dimir midrange built around Psychic Frog, Orcish Bowmasters, and Subtlety. Kaito, Bane of Nightmares (2x) acts as the planeswalker value engine — drawing cards and generating damage in the same body. Quantum Riddler (3x) and Tamiyo, Inquisitive Student (3x) are the flip threats. The deck plays 4x Counterspell + 3x Force of Negation + 2x Spell Snare as the interaction suite, making it the most counter-dense midrange shell in the format.

SB has Harbinger of the Seas (2x) for land-dependent strategies and Pest Control (2x) for tokens, plus Elesh Norn as the top-end silver bullet.

What it does well

  • Counterspell + FoN + Spell Snare means the opponent can never safely tap out on a key spell
  • Psychic Frog is a must-answer threat that generates card advantage while blocking
  • Orcish Bowmasters punishes card-draw decks severely — every opposing Cantrip = a 1/1 and a ping
  • Kaito generates steady card advantage while pressuring the life total

What we're watching

  • No sweeper in the main — wide aggro boards (Boros tokens) can overwhelm
  • Fatal Push is the primary removal; sorcery speed is a liability against flash threats
  • Harbinger of the Seas is a two-way hate piece — make sure basic lands are counted

No dedicated playbook yet — testing in progress.

Amulet Titan (two options)

Status: Full playbook complete. Two reference lists under evaluation.

Option A — Devon Straub (PTQ, Mar 27 2026): Decklist →
Malevolent Rumble (4x) package + 3x Scapeshift + Aftermath Analyst (2x). Vexing Bauble as a main deck hand-trap. Runs a broader toolbox via Green Sun's Zenith. SB includes Insidious Fungus and Dosan the Falling Leaf as lock pieces against interaction-heavy fields.

Option B — aljce (10k Winner, 13-1-2, Mar 28 2026): Decklist →
Winner of the LDXP SEA26 10k. 4x Scapeshift + 3x Green Sun's Zenith + 3x Malevolent Rumble. Dryad Arbor (1x) as a GSZ-0 target. SB leans on Fire Magic (3x) as board control and Force of Vigor (3x) for artifact/enchantment hate. Trinisphere (2x) as the lock piece against interaction.

Key differences

  • Option B (aljce) went 13-1-2 and won the 10k — this is the validated version
  • Fire Magic in Option B is a sweeper that doesn't need the combo to win games
  • Trinisphere SB in Option B shuts down low-cost interactive decks entirely
  • Option A runs Aftermath Analyst for graveyard-based value recursion

What we're watching

  • Extremely high decision density — suboptimal sequencing with bounce lands loses games outright
  • Requires the deepest reps of any deck in the gauntlet
  • Counter packages (Force of Negation) punish the single-threat game plan

Ruby Storm

Status: Full playbook complete. Reference: Chance McDougal 9-0-4, LDXP SEA26 10k, Mar 28 2026. Decklist →

Ruby Medallion reduces every red spell's cost by one, enabling turn-3 Grapeshot or Empty the Warrens through ritual chains. Current list runs Ral, Monsoon Mage (4x) as the second ritual-enabler — Ral's flip side generates mana on each spell cast, extending chains further than Medallion alone. Artist's Talent (2x) is a notable addition: it sets up the top of the library and generates cantrips, giving the storm count a permanent ramp-up tool across multiple turns.

Wish (2x) provides SB access to flex bullets mid-combo. SB includes Orim's Chant (2x) and Untimely Malfunction (3x) as protection, plus Silence (1x) as the classic answer to answer-heavy fields.

What it does well

  • Consistent turn-3 kill with minimal setup — no graveyard dependency
  • Ral + Medallion double-dipping makes chains reach lethal faster than older versions
  • Artist's Talent is a persistent top-deck manipulator that compounds over multiple turns
  • Empty the Warrens via Wish is a second kill condition that dodges counterspells

What we're watching

  • Counterspells and Chalice of the Void at 1 are hard stops
  • Mulligans are punishing — needs specific pieces to function
  • Requires knowing when to go for it vs. set up a larger kill the following turn

Mono-Blue Belcher (playing Mountain in the SB over Island)

Status: Evaluating. Reference: Tiendav1s 4-0-3, LDXP SEA26 $5k RCQ, Mar 29 2026. Decklist →

Goblin Charbelcher as a combo deck hidden inside a Mono-Blue control shell. The deck plays exclusively MDFC lands (Sea Gate Restoration, Jwari Disruption, Suppression Ray) and zero basic Islands in the main — meaning Charbelcher reveals the entire library dealing half the library's size as damage. Whir of Invention tutors Charbelcher or Lotus Bloom at instant speed. The deck also functions as a legitimate draw-go shell when Charbelcher isn't needed: Tameshi, Thundertrap Trainer, Disrupting Shoal, Force of Negation.

The Mountain SB note: The SB includes 1 Mountain instead of 1 Island. Because Charbelcher stops at the first basic land it hits — playing Mountain means when you bring it in, opponents don't know your "basic" is red. The psychological angle matters, but more practically: Mountain in the SB means Charbelcher can potentially still deal damage if a fetch or Endurance shuffles a basic in. This is a corner-case line, not a default — understand the rules interaction before registering.

What it does well

  • The combo can kill from any board state once Charbelcher resolves and activates
  • MDFC lands mean the deck never floods — every "land" card is also a spell
  • Waterlogged Teachings provides card advantage in the control mode
  • Opponents unfamiliar with the combo may tap out at the wrong moment

What we're watching

  • Artifact removal (Kozilek's Command, March of Otherworldly Light) destroys the combo
  • Chalice of the Void at 0 hits Lotus Bloom tokens; at 1 hits most of the spell suite
  • Requires complete familiarity with the MDFC sequencing — most complex mana in the gauntlet
  • The Mountain line is cute but don't overvalue it — the real question is whether the shell is competitive

No dedicated playbook yet — in evaluation.

Izzet Prowess

Status: Full playbook complete — monitoring.

Highest ceiling of any aggro deck when it's on. Swiftspear, DRC, and cheap instants create explosive turns that other decks can't race. Lava Dart flashback pumps all prowess creatures for zero mana — ends games from nowhere.

What it does well

  • Highest damage ceiling in the format — wins from nowhere
  • Light Up the Stage provides card velocity through disruption
  • Lava Dart creates free pump effects that end games on turn 3–4

What we're watching

  • Sanctifier en-Vec is a hard stop — needs a dedicated answer
  • Inconsistent hands lose fast with no recovery path
  • Requires the most tournament clock awareness of any deck we're testing

Izzet Affinity

Status: Full playbook complete.

Thought Monitor, Sojourner's Companion, and Urza's Saga combine to create an artifact-based engine that generates card advantage and board presence simultaneously. The deck attacks on the artifact axis while most hate is oriented toward creature-based strategies.

What it does well

  • Resilient to spot removal — threats come from multiple angles
  • Urza's Saga constructs are free beaters that win races on their own
  • Thought Monitor refuels on an empty board better than most blue draw spells

What we're watching

  • Artifact hate (Shatterstorm, Hurkyl's Recall) requires dedicated SB answers
  • Linear game plan is easier to hate than flexible midrange

Eldrazi Tron

Status: Full playbook complete.

Tron lands plus Eldrazi threats — Thought-Knot Seer and Reality Smasher on turn three backed by the disruption package. Slower than traditional Tron but more resilient to Blood Moon and artifact hate. Map and Expedition Map find Tron quickly and consistently.

What it does well

  • Ahead of the curve from turn 3 — 4/4 that strips a card is a massive tempo swing
  • More resilient to artifact removal than traditional Tron shells
  • Chalice of the Void locks out most of the format for essentially free

What we're watching

  • Blood Moon doesn't stop us but slows the setup significantly
  • Land destruction targeting Map and Tron pieces disrupts the plan

Living End

Status: Full playbook complete.

Cascade spells (Shardless Agent, Violent Outburst, Demonic Dread) unconditionally hit Living End — sweeping the board and reanimating your graveyard of cycled threats. Street Wraith and Architects of Will cycle for one mana and load the graveyard in a single turn. The combo requires no setup beyond having cyclers and a cascade spell.

What it does well

  • One cascade spell clears the board and puts 15+ power into play simultaneously
  • Violent Outburst wins at instant speed through most defenses
  • Cycling tutors and fixes hands in a way most combo decks can't

What we're watching

  • Force of Negation and Subtlety can counter Living End without mana
  • Graveyard hate is backbreaking — the plan needs a counter package as insurance

Golgari Yawgmoth

Status: Full playbook complete.

Yawgmoth, Thran Physician generates infinite value loops with two undying creatures — sacrifice one to draw, Yawgmoth's ability makes the other undying, repeat. Young Wolf and Strangleroot Geist are the enablers. The deck also functions as a solid fair midrange plan through Chord of Calling and Eldritch Evolution when the infinite loop isn't available.

What it does well

  • Generates infinite damage and draws with two undying creatures in play
  • Chord of Calling and Eldritch Evolution provide redundancy for finding Yawgmoth
  • The fair backup plan beats fair midrange — the deck isn't fragile

What we're watching

  • Graveyard hate disrupts the undying engine
  • Instant-speed interaction on Yawgmoth in response to a tap can stop the loop cold

Domain Zoo

Status: Full playbook complete.

Territorial Kavu and Scion of Draco require all five basic land types — fetchlands and shocklands fulfill this requirement immediately. Tribal Flames deals five damage for two mana with domain online. Arid Mesa fetching Stomping Ground and Sacred Foundry enables domain by turn one.

What it does well

  • Territorial Kavu is a 5/5 or better for two mana in nearly every opener
  • Tribal Flames at full domain is better burn efficiency than Lightning Bolt
  • Scion of Draco provides flying evasion and multicolor keyword suite

What we're watching

  • Greedy mana base is punished by Blood Moon — needs SB answers
  • Loses to itself when fetches and shocks drain too much life in aggro mirrors

Neoform Combo

Status: Full playbook complete.

Neoform sacrifices Eldritch Evolution targets to tutor Omnath, Locus of Creation, generating cascading landfall triggers and eventually winning through Uro or a looped Omnath bounce. The combo goldfishes well but requires specific sequencing to avoid fizzling on the stack.

What it does well

  • Tutors are redundant — Neoform and Eldritch Evolution both find the same targets
  • Omnath generates so much value that even a disrupted version can stabilize
  • Wins through creature removal — counterspells are the main obstacle

What we're watching

  • Counterspell-heavy matchups require Force of Negation backup or are very hard
  • Precise sequencing on the combo turn — easy to fizzle without reps
