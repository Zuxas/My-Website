Team Resolve · Modern · 2026 RC Season

decks we're testing

These are the Modern archetypes Team Resolve has full playbooks on for the 2026 RC season. Each one has been run through our gauntlet — win rates against the expected field, sideboard plans, and play-tested lines. Nothing here is just theory.

Boros Energy

Status: Known quantity — full playbook complete.

I qualified on this deck last season — 17 RCQ Top 8s. The question isn't whether it's good; it is. Ragavan generates resources on attack, Guide of Souls and Ocelot Pride create inevitability, and Ajani closes the game with a single activation. The deck punishes stumbles immediately and rewards reps harder than anything else in the format.

What it does well

  • Closes games extremely fast when the opponent stumbles
  • Generates advantage while applying pressure — not a linear aggro deck
  • Sideboard is deep and well-understood after a full season of reps
  • Rewards familiarity — every percentage point of efficiency matters at an RC

What we're watching

  • Sanctifier en-Vec is a hard lock that the field is prepared to deploy
  • Opponents have refined their Wrath sequencing against the deck
  • The mirror is decided heavily by who has the better sideboard plan

UW Blink

Status: Primary focus — most testing hours this season.

Ephemerate is one of the best cards in Modern. At its floor it's a cantrip that resets an ETB; at its ceiling it's a counterspell, Time Walk, and 4-for-1 in the same card. White Orchid Phantom is underrated against greedy three-color mana bases. Solitude + Ephemerate is three removals and nine life for two mana. The deck shifts roles cleanly — control against aggro, beatdown against control.

What it does well

  • Extremely high skill ceiling — reps compound into a meaningful edge
  • Difficult to hate out — pivots into fair midrange when hate sticks
  • White Orchid Phantom creates free wins against greedy mana bases
  • Ocelot Pride generates inevitability in any long game

What we're watching

  • Very fast aggro can close before engines come online
  • Decision density is high — mental stamina matters across a long event
  • Round clock is a real concern; brisk play is required

Jeskai Blink

Status: Full playbook complete.

Jeskai adds Teferi, Time Raveler and Lightning Bolt to the UW blink shell. Teferi locks opponents into sorcery speed — massive in combo matchups. The cost is mana consistency in aggro matchups where clean openers matter most.

Vs. UW Blink — the tradeoffs

  • Jeskai gains: Teferi main, Lightning Bolt, better combo matchups, Orim's Chant access
  • Jeskai loses: Mana consistency, cleaner openers against fast aggro, simpler sideboard
  • Working verdict: UW is better in open fields. Jeskai is better if the expected field skews combo-heavy.

Grixis Reanimator ("Glockulous")

Status: Full playbook complete — high ceiling, high variance.

Fill the graveyard with Atraxa or Griselbrand through Faithful Mending, Persist, or Goryo's Vengeance, reanimate it, and close before the opponent answers. The field is largely built to interact with creatures on the board — not to stop a turn-two combo kill from the graveyard.

What it does well

  • Fastest potential kill in the format — can win on turn two on the play
  • Punishes creature-interaction-heavy decks that can't answer the combo
  • Atraxa's ETB refuels even through disruption — one answer isn't enough
  • Griselbrand draws seven cards immediately into more threats or protection

What we're watching

  • Hard stops: Sanctifier en-Vec, Nihil Spellbomb, Rest in Peace
  • Inconsistent openers lose immediately with no path back — lowest floor we're testing
  • Post-board games are significantly harder — opponents board heavily against graveyard

UW Control

Status: Full playbook complete — dependent on meta read.

Best in fields skewed toward midrange and combo. Against Blink mirrors, Amulet Titan, and Reanimator variants, UW Control's card advantage engine and hard counterspells are better positioned than the blink shell's tempo approach.

When we'd choose this

  • Expected field is skewed toward midrange and combo with less aggro
  • RC field where slower strategies show up more than at RCQs
  • When Snapcaster Mage is clearly the best card in the format

Izzet Prowess

Status: Full playbook complete — monitoring.

Highest ceiling of any aggro deck when it's on. Swiftspear, DRC, and cheap instants create explosive turns that other decks can't race. Lava Dart flashback pumps all prowess creatures for zero mana — ends games from nowhere.

What it does well

  • Highest damage ceiling in the format — wins from nowhere
  • Light Up the Stage provides card velocity through disruption
  • Lava Dart creates free pump effects that end games on turn 3–4

What we're watching

  • Sanctifier en-Vec is a hard stop — needs a dedicated answer
  • Inconsistent hands lose fast with no recovery path
  • Requires the most tournament clock awareness of any deck we're testing

Izzet Affinity

Status: Full playbook complete.

Thought Monitor, Sojourner's Companion, and Urza's Saga combine to create an artifact-based engine that generates card advantage and board presence simultaneously. The deck attacks on the artifact axis while most hate is oriented toward creature-based strategies.

What it does well

  • Resilient to spot removal — threats come from multiple angles
  • Urza's Saga constructs are free beaters that win races on their own
  • Thought Monitor refuels on an empty board better than most blue draw spells

What we're watching

  • Artifact hate (Shatterstorm, Hurkyl's Recall) requires dedicated SB answers
  • Linear game plan is easier to hate than flexible midrange

Ruby Storm

Status: Full playbook complete.

Ruby Medallion reduces the cost of every red spell, enabling chains of cantrips and rituals that culminate in a lethal Grapeshot or Empty the Warrens. One of the few pure combo decks in Modern with a consistent turn-3 kill that doesn't need the graveyard.

What it does well

  • Consistent turn-3 kill with minimal setup — no graveyard dependency
  • Ruby Medallion's cost reduction cascades through an entire hand
  • Empty the Warrens provides a second kill condition that dodges counterspells

What we're watching

  • Counterspells and Chalice of the Void are hard stops
  • Mulligans are punishing — the deck needs specific pieces

Eldrazi Tron

Status: Full playbook complete.

Tron lands plus Eldrazi threats — Thought-Knot Seer and Reality Smasher on turn three backed by the disruption package. Slower than traditional Tron but more resilient to Blood Moon and artifact hate. Map and Expedition Map find Tron quickly and consistently.

What it does well

  • Ahead of the curve from turn 3 — 4/4 that strips a card is a massive tempo swing
  • More resilient to artifact removal than traditional Tron shells
  • Chalice of the Void locks out most of the format for essentially free

What we're watching

  • Blood Moon doesn't stop us but slows the setup significantly
  • Land destruction targeting Map and Tron pieces disrupts the plan

Amulet Titan

Status: Full playbook complete — highest skill ceiling in the gauntlet.

Amulet of Vigor and bounce lands generate enormous mana quickly, enabling Primeval Titan on turn two or three. When Titan resolves it immediately fetches Tolaria West and Boros Garrison — looping for Slayers' Stronghold, Sunhome, and enough haste activations to end the game on the spot.

What it does well

  • Turn-2 Titan is achievable in a significant percentage of games
  • The deck is extremely difficult to interact with profitably once Titan resolves
  • Bounce land synergy generates advantages that snowball past removal

What we're watching

  • Extremely high decision density — suboptimal sequencing loses games outright
  • Blood Moon is not a hard stop but requires sideboard management
  • Requires the deepest reps of any deck in the gauntlet to pilot correctly

Living End

Status: Full playbook complete.

Cascade spells (Shardless Agent, Violent Outburst, Demonic Dread) unconditionally hit Living End — sweeping the board and reanimating your graveyard of cycled threats. Street Wraith and Architects of Will cycle for one mana and load the graveyard in a single turn. The combo requires no setup beyond having cyclers and a cascade spell.

What it does well

  • One cascade spell clears the board and puts 15+ power into play simultaneously
  • Violent Outburst wins at instant speed through most defenses
  • Cycling tutors and fixes hands in a way most combo decks can't

What we're watching

  • Force of Negation and Subtlety can counter Living End without mana
  • Graveyard hate is backbreaking — the plan needs a counter package as insurance

Golgari Yawgmoth

Status: Full playbook complete.

Yawgmoth, Thran Physician generates infinite value loops with two undying creatures — sacrifice one to draw, Yawgmoth's ability makes the other undying, repeat. Young Wolf and Strangleroot Geist are the enablers. The deck also functions as a solid fair midrange plan through Chord of Calling and Eldritch Evolution when the infinite loop isn't available.

What it does well

  • Generates infinite damage and draws with two undying creatures in play
  • Chord of Calling and Eldritch Evolution provide redundancy for finding Yawgmoth
  • The fair backup plan beats fair midrange — the deck isn't fragile

What we're watching

  • Graveyard hate disrupts the undying engine
  • Instant-speed interaction on Yawgmoth in response to a tap can stop the loop cold

Domain Zoo

Status: Full playbook complete.

Territorial Kavu and Scion of Draco require all five basic land types — fetchlands and shocklands fulfill this requirement immediately. Tribal Flames deals five damage for two mana with domain online. Arid Mesa fetching Stomping Ground and Sacred Foundry enables domain by turn one.

What it does well

  • Territorial Kavu is a 5/5 or better for two mana in nearly every opener
  • Tribal Flames at full domain is better burn efficiency than Lightning Bolt
  • Scion of Draco provides flying evasion and multicolor keyword suite

What we're watching

  • Greedy mana base is punished by Blood Moon — needs SB answers
  • Loses to itself when fetches and shocks drain too much life in aggro mirrors

Neoform Combo

Status: Full playbook complete.

Neoform sacrifices Eldritch Evolution targets to tutor Omnath, Locus of Creation, generating cascading landfall triggers and eventually winning through Uro or a looped Omnath bounce. The combo goldfishes well but requires specific sequencing to avoid fizzling on the stack.

What it does well

  • Tutors are redundant — Neoform and Eldritch Evolution both find the same targets
  • Omnath generates so much value that even a disrupted version can stabilize
  • Wins through creature removal — counterspells are the main obstacle

What we're watching

  • Counterspell-heavy matchups require Force of Negation backup or are very hard
  • Precise sequencing on the combo turn — easy to fizzle without reps
