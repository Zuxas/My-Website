Chapter 01

Deck
Identity

"The deck has two modes: fair creature midrange that grinds with Collected Company and Chord of Calling, and an infinite combo engine that kills on the spot. You're always one Chord of Calling away from either finding the missing combo piece or closing the game."

Golgari Yawgmoth is a creature combo deck built around Yawgmoth, Thran Physician and the undying mechanic. The infinite loop requires Yawgmoth, two creatures with undying (like Young Wolf + Geralf's Messenger), and Melira, Sylvok Outcast: Melira prevents -1/-1 counters from being placed on your creatures. Undying creatures return with a +1/+1 counter — but Melira prevents -1/-1 counters, not +1/+1 counters, so they still return. However, Yawgmoth's ability places a -1/-1 counter and Melira prevents that counter from being placed — so the undying creature's +1/+1 counter is never removed, meaning it can keep dying and returning infinitely.

The practical loop: Sacrifice undying creature A to Yawgmoth → A gets -1/-1 counter (Melira prevents it — A dies with no counters) → A returns with +1/+1 counter (undying, since it had no counters when it died) → sacrifice undying creature B → B returns similarly → repeat. Each loop draws a card and puts a -1/-1 counter on a target creature (usually opponent's creatures or opponent directly). With Blood Artist or Zulaport Cutthroat on board: each death triggers life drain. The loop kills the opponent from any life total.

Deck Identity

The Combo
Yawgmoth + Melira + 2 Undying
Yawgmoth + Melira + Young Wolf + Geralf's Messenger + Blood Artist. Sacrifice Wolf: -1/-1 counter prevented (Melira), Wolf dies with no counters, returns (undying). Draw a card. Target opponent with -1/-1. Sacrifice Messenger: same. Repeat infinitely. Blood Artist drains 1 per death = infinite life loss.
Why Just One Undying Works
Melira + 1 Undying = Infinite
With Melira in play, even ONE undying creature is enough for the infinite loop: sacrifice it, -1/-1 counter is prevented, it dies with no counters, returns with +1/+1 (undying), sacrifice again. Each loop: draw a card, place -1/-1 on a target. You need a drain outlet (Blood Artist) to convert this into a kill. Without a drain outlet, you can draw your whole deck.
The Tutor Package
Chord + Evolution + CoCo
Chord of Calling (instant, convoke) finds any creature at sorcery speed. Eldritch Evolution (sorcery) sacrifices a creature to find one with MV ≤ sacrifice MV + 2. Collected Company hits up to two creatures with MV ≤ 3 at instant speed. The density of creature tutors makes the combo reliably findable by turn 3–4 even without opening the pieces.
Fair Midrange Mode
Beats Without Combo
When the combo is disrupted, the deck plays fair: Young Wolf and Geralf's Messenger are resilient threats (undying makes them permanent value). Hapatra, Vizier of Poisons creates Snake tokens when -1/-1 counters are placed. Yawgmoth's protection from Humans makes him hard to answer with the most common removal. The midrange plan is often enough to win against unprepared opponents.
Oracle Precision
Protection From Humans
Yawgmoth has protection from Humans — not from creatures in general, not from black. Many players incorrectly try to target Yawgmoth with Solitude (exiles a creature) or Swords to Plowshares (exile a creature). Protection means: can't be blocked, targeted, dealt damage, or enchanted by Humans. Solitude and StP are Human spells/effects from non-Human permanents — they CAN target Yawgmoth.
Meta Position
2.0% · Resilient Specialist
Golgari Yawgmoth has been a Modern staple since Yawgmoth's printing. Low meta share reflects the narrow meta slot but consistent performance. Strong against creature-based midrange, weak against Clarion Conqueror (shuts activated abilities) and fast combo that kills before the loop assembles. A skilled pilot wins matchups others lose.

Yawgmoth, Thran Physician
{2}{B}{B} · 2/4 Legendary
Protection from Humans. Pay 1 life, sacrifice another creature: put a -1/-1 counter on up to one target creature and draw a card. {B}{B}, discard a card: proliferate. The sacrifice ability is an ACTIVATED ability (cost: pay life + sacrifice). Clarion Conqueror shuts this off completely. Yawgmoth can activate the turn he enters — no summoning sickness on activated abilities. Protection from Humans: can't be targeted by Humans (creature type), not by Human spells.
Melira, Sylvok Outcast
{1}{G} · 2/2 Legendary
You can't get poison counters. Creatures you control can't have -1/-1 counters placed on them. The "can't have counters placed" clause is what enables the loop — when Yawgmoth tries to place a -1/-1 counter on the undying creature (as part of his activation cost — wait, the counter IS placed on a target creature, not on the creature being sacrificed). Key: Yawgmoth places -1/-1 on "up to one target creature." When targeting your undying creature, Melira prevents that counter. Without a counter, the undying trigger fires when it dies with no counters.
Undying (Young Wolf / Geralf's Messenger)
Returns With +1/+1 Counter
When this creature dies, if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner's control with a +1/+1 counter on it. In the loop: the undying creature is sacrificed to Yawgmoth (not targeted, just sacrificed). Yawgmoth places a -1/-1 counter on a target (which can be the opposing creature, or another target). The undying creature died with no counters (Melira blocks -1/-1, and it had no +1/+1). Undying fires: returns with +1/+1 counter. Next sacrifice: now has +1/+1 counter. Sacrifice again: Yawgmoth places -1/-1 on the +1/+1 creature (they cancel out) → it now has 0 counters → undying fires if it dies again.
Blood Artist
{1}{B} · 0/1 Vampire
Whenever this creature OR another creature dies, target player loses 1 life and you gain 1 life. Blood Artist triggers on ANY creature dying — including your own undying creatures. Each infinite loop iteration: one creature death = Blood Artist trigger = 1 life loss for opponent. Infinite loops = infinite drain. With two Blood Artists: 2 life per loop. Compare Zulaport Cutthroat: triggers only on YOUR creatures, hits EACH opponent (relevant in multiplayer). Blood Artist is better in 1v1 for the loop.
Eldritch Evolution
{1}{G}{G} · Sorcery
Sacrifice a creature. Search for a creature with MV ≤ sacrifice MV + 2. Sacrifice Young Wolf (MV 1): find MV ≤ 3. Can find Melira (MV 2), Hapatra (MV 2), Grist (MV 3), Bloodline Cultivator (MV 3). CANNOT find Yawgmoth (MV 4). Sacrifice Geralf's Messenger (MV 3): find MV ≤ 5. Can find Yawgmoth (MV 4), Nissa (MV 4), or Endurance (MV 3). The tutor chain: Evolution on Wolf → Melira. Evolution on Messenger → Yawgmoth.
Grist, the Hunger Tide
{1}{B}{G} · Legendary Planeswalker
Grist is a creature in all zones except the battlefield. This means Chord of Calling and Collected Company can find him (both look for "creature cards"). Grist's +1: each opponent mills 1, you create a 1/1 Insect if a creature was milled. −2: destroy target creature with MV ≤ number of Insects you control. −5: each opponent loses life equal to the number of creature cards in their GY. A planeswalker that acts as a creature tutor target is uniquely powerful for this deck's search package.

Chapter 03

Game
Flow

Yawgmoth plays fair until it doesn't. Turns 1–3 build a creature base: Young Wolf on turn 1, Bloodline Cultivator on turn 2, Eldritch Evolution or Chord on turn 3 to assemble combo pieces. The ideal kill is turn 4, but strong hands can goldfish turn 3 with the Evolution chain.

The Primary Combo Kill

Requirements
Yawgmoth + Melira in play + at least 1 undying creature in play + Blood Artist (or Zulaport) in play. This is the minimum: 4 pieces. The tutor package allows assembling this from a single Chord of Calling or Collected Company hit, provided 2–3 pieces are already in play.
The Loop
1. Sacrifice undying creature to Yawgmoth: pay 1 life, place -1/-1 on target (use opponent or their creature as target). Draw a card.
2. Undying creature died with no counters (Melira prevented -1/-1 from being placed). Undying triggers: return with +1/+1 counter.
3. Sacrifice again: Yawgmoth places -1/-1 on the undying creature's +1/+1 (they cancel — creature now has 0 counters). Draw another card.
4. Undying creature dies with no +1/+1 counters → undying triggers again. Repeat.
Each loop: Blood Artist triggers (creature died) = opponent loses 1 life.
With 2 Undying
With 2 undying creatures (Wolf + Messenger), alternate sacrificing them. Sacrifice Wolf → Wolf returns → sacrifice Messenger → Messenger returns → repeat. Wolf starts clean (no counters), loops cleanly. Messenger has "ETB: target opponent loses 2 life" — each time it enters, opponent loses 2 more life. The Messenger line can kill extremely fast even with a slow Blood Artist.
Killing Without Loop
Yawgmoth's -1/-1 ability is also a legitimate tool without the infinite. Against a creature deck: sacrifice a creature to put -1/-1 on their best creature. With Hapatra on board: each -1/-1 counter placed creates a 1/1 deathtouch Snake. Multiple Snakes + Yawgmoth can overrun opponents without needing Melira at all. This is the fair midrange mode.

Mulligan Framework

Keep
Tutor + 2 Lands + Creatures
Any hand with a Chord of Calling, Eldritch Evolution, or Collected Company + 2 lands + early creatures is a keep. The tutors find any missing combo piece by turn 3–4. You don't need to open with all 4 combo pieces — you just need the infrastructure to find them.
Keep
Wolf + Messenger + Yawgmoth Line
Wolf T1 + Messenger T3 + Yawgmoth (from Evolution on Messenger) T4 is the baseline gold standard. If your hand has the first two pieces and 4 mana available (Eldritch Evolution on Messenger costs {1}{G}{G} + 3 lands to cast Messenger), this is a clean line.
Mulligan
No Tutors + No Undying
Hands with Yawgmoth + Melira but no undying creatures and no tutors cannot win — they need the undying part of the combo. Hands with all undying but no Yawgmoth and no tutors are just attacking with small creatures. Both are mulligans.
On the Draw
Prioritize Disruption Hands
On the draw against known fast combo (Ruby Storm, Living End, Amulet Titan): keep hands with Thoughtseize (SB) or disruption. Post-board, disruption hands take priority over pure combo speed — the opponent is likely faster than you in game 1 if you're drawing second.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 Wolf, T4 Combo
T1 Young Wolf establishes the blocker and combo piece simultaneously. T2 Bloodline Cultivator (mana filter). T3 Evolution into Geralf’s Messenger (3/2 zombie = more undying bodies). T4 Chord for Yawgmoth. This is your cleanest line. On the play: never skip T1 Young Wolf if it’s in hand — it’s too important for blocking AND combo.
On the Draw
T1 Wolf Still Correct
The extra draw can find Melira, Blood Artist, or a missing combo piece. T1 Wolf is still correct. On the draw: you can keep slightly more speculative hands (6-land hands with CoCo) since you see more cards. T2 CoCo is a strong line on draw — you’ve seen 8 cards and CoCo hits roughly 60% of the time with the right deck configuration.

When Disrupted

Yawgmoth Answered
Rebuild via Chord
They Solitude or Path your Yawgmoth: don’t immediately recast. First assess: do you have 2 undying creatures and Melira still in play? If yes, Chord for Yawgmoth again next turn. If no, spend the turn rebuilding the creature base with CoCo or Evolution before tutoring Yawgmoth again.

Pivot to Beatdown
Rest in Peace or Grafdigger’s Cage shuts off undying. Pivot to CoCo fair value plan: undying creatures still attack and block profitably. Yawgmoth is still a 2/4 that draws cards. Melira + Blood Artist can race in this mode. Find Haywire Mite from SB to clear the hate piece before going back to combo.

Lines & Tricks

  • Sacrifice is a cost — it can't be responded to: Yawgmoth's activation cost is "pay 1 life, sacrifice another creature." Sacrifice happens as part of paying the activation cost — before the ability goes on the stack. Opponents can't respond to the sacrifice itself (it already happened). They CAN respond to the ability on the stack by killing Yawgmoth before the -1/-1 counter is placed and the card is drawn.
  • Yawgmoth can activate the turn he enters — no summoning sickness: Summoning sickness prevents creatures from attacking or using {T} abilities. Yawgmoth's activated ability doesn't use the tap symbol. He can activate the same turn he enters the battlefield (via Chord or Eldritch Evolution). This makes instant-speed Chord of Calling for Yawgmoth an instant kill during opponent's end step if the other 3 pieces are in play.
  • Geralf's Messenger ETB loss triggers each time it returns: Messenger's ETB says "target opponent loses 2 life." This fires each time Messenger enters — including each undying return. In the infinite loop, Messenger gains you 2 life per loop iteration (from Blood Artist) + drains opponent 2 from ETB = net 3 life swing per loop (opponent loses 2 from ETB + loses 1 from Blood Artist). Extremely fast kill with Messenger.
  • Chord of Calling finds Grist — he's a creature in zones other than battlefield: Grist, the Hunger Tide is a legendary Planeswalker on the battlefield but a creature card everywhere else (hand, library, GY). Chord of Calling looks for "a creature card." Grist in the library qualifies — Chord can find him even at CMC X=3. This is unique to Grist among planeswalkers.
  • Eldritch Evolution exiles itself: Eldritch Evolution says "then shuffle. Exile Eldritch Evolution." It removes itself from the game after resolving. You can't find another copy in the same library search turn, and you can't retrieve it from GY. Run 3–4 copies since each one is a one-shot tutor.
  • Agatha's Soul Cauldron as backup combo with Yawgmoth in GY: If Yawgmoth dies, Agatha's Soul Cauldron can exile him from GY and grant his activated ability to any tapped creature you control. Other creatures with counters now have Yawgmoth's ability. This is the backup combo line when Yawgmoth is removed. Watch for Boseiju from opponents targeting the Cauldron — destroy it before it exiles Yawgmoth if you want to prevent this.
  • Clarion Conqueror shuts Yawgmoth's activated ability — and yours: Clarion Conqueror from the opponent's board means Yawgmoth's sacrifice ability can't be activated. Force of Vigor (SB) destroys Clarion (it's a creature, not artifact/enchantment — wait, Clarion Conqueror is a Dragon creature. Force of Vigor destroys artifacts and enchantments, not creatures. To answer Clarion: use your own removal (Doom Blade effects) or return it via Karakas effects. Chord for a creature that destroys creatures: Reclamation Sage only hits artifacts/enchantments. Use Grist's -2 (destroy creature with MV ≤ Insect count).
  • Hapatra's Snakes get deathtouch — use them aggressively: Each Snake Hapatra creates has deathtouch. Against an opponent with large creatures (Primeval Titan, Kappa Cannoneer), block with Snakes. They trade with anything regardless of toughness. This means your token generation doubles as removal. Don't hold Snakes back on defense — attack to pressure life totals and let deathtouchers discourage blocks.

Lines &
Tactics

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Line 01 — Critical
Exiled Creatures Don’t Trigger Undying
Undying triggers on death — meaning the creature goes to the graveyard. Solitude, Path to Exile, and Prismatic Ending EXILE the creature. Exiled creatures skip the graveyard, so undying doesn’t trigger. Your Young Wolf stays dead permanently when Pathed. Galvanic Discharge deals damage (they die normally, undying works). Know the difference.
Line 02 — Critical
Yawgmoth Can Target ANY Creature
Yawgmoth says “target creature.” That’s any creature — yours OR theirs. You can put -1/-1 counters on their creatures by sacrificing one of your undying creatures. Kill their 1/1 tokens. Run a 3/3 down to a 2/2 before blocks. Draw a card doing it. This is not a rare line — use Yawgmoth actively to answer their threats while assembling your combo.
Line 03 — Critical
Protect Melira — She’s the Loop
Melira prevents -1/-1 counters on creatures YOU control. If Melira is removed between loop activations, the next undying creature returns WITH a -1/-1 counter and immediately dies as a 0/0, breaking the loop. Protect Melira above Yawgmoth. A Melira + 2 undying board without Yawgmoth still wins with one Chord. A Yawgmoth board without Melira is just a draw engine.

The Core Combo — Step by Step

Setup Required
Yawgmoth + 2 Undying Creatures
Yawgmoth, Thran Physician in play. Two undying creatures — typically Young Wolf (1/1) and Strangleroot Geist (2/1 haste). One has a +1/+1 counter from undying, one doesn't. Sacrifice the creature WITHOUT the counter. Put the -1/-1 counter on the creature WITH the +1/+1 counter. Counters cancel via CR 122.3. The sacrificed creature returns via undying with a fresh +1/+1. Repeat. Each cycle: draw 1 card, pay 1 life.
The Kill
Blood Artist / Zulaport / Geralf's Messenger
With Blood Artist: each sacrifice = opponent loses 1, you gain 1. Life gain offsets Yawgmoth's payment. Draw your entire deck, opponent loses life = your starting total. With Zulaport Cutthroat: same as Artist but "each opponent" — can't be redirected, hits through hexproof.

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

Yawgmoth Has Protection from Humans
Often forgotten. Can't be targeted, damaged, enchanted, or blocked by Humans. Thalia can't block him. Reflector Mage can't bounce him. Meddling Mage can still name him (naming doesn't target). In the mirror: YOUR Yawgmoth can't target THEIR Yawgmoth with -1/-1 counters (both Humans). Only answers: Grist's -2 (sacrifice, not target), Cauldron-granted ability on a non-Human creature (CAN target the real Yawgmoth), or post-board removal.
Yawgmoth's -1/-1 Is "Up to One Target" — Can Choose Zero
You can activate and choose ZERO targets for the -1/-1 counter. You still sacrifice + draw. Per Scryfall ruling: "You may activate Yawgmoth's first activated ability without choosing a target creature. You'll just draw a card." Use when: no enemy creatures, don't want to shrink your own board, just need to dig for combo pieces.
+1/+1 and -1/-1 Counters Cancel as State-Based Actions (CR 122.3)
Cancellation happens BETWEEN ability resolutions, not during. If multiple Yawgmoth activations are stacked: each resolves, state-based actions check (counters cancel), then next resolves. This means you can safely stack multiple activations targeting the same creature — the counters cancel correctly between each resolution.
Cauldron + Yawgmoth Exiled — Bypasses Protection from Humans
Cauldron grants Yawgmoth's abilities to non-Human creatures with +1/+1 counters. A Young Wolf with Yawgmoth's ability does NOT have protection from Humans. It CAN target the opponent's Yawgmoth with -1/-1. Critical mirror tech — the only way to directly kill an opposing Yawgmoth without Grist or sideboard cards.
Cauldron + Grist — One Loyalty Ability Per Turn Per Creature (Rule 606.3)
If 3 Grists are exiled under Cauldron, a creature with a +1/+1 counter still only gets ONE Grist activation per turn. However, DIFFERENT creatures each get their own activation. Board of 5 creatures with +1/+1 counters = 5 Grist activations per turn across different creatures. This is NOT the same as Wall of Roots (which uses a per-card "once each turn" restriction, not the loyalty rule).
Cauldron + Walking Ballista — Infinite Damage Loop
Exile Ballista under Cauldron. Undying creature with +1/+1 counter gains "Remove a +1/+1 counter: deal 1 damage to any target." Remove the counter → creature has no +1/+1 counters → sacrifice to Yawgmoth or it dies from 0 toughness → undying returns it with fresh +1/+1 → repeat. With Blood Artist: infinite damage + life gain. The creature shoots itself, dies, returns, shoots again. Each loop = 1 damage from Ballista + 1 drain from Artist.
Wall of Roots Under Cauldron — Multiple Activations Per Creature
"Put a -0/-1 counter: Add {G}. Activate only once each turn." The restriction applies PER COPY of Wall exiled. 3 Walls under Cauldron = a single creature can activate 3 times per turn, adding {G}{G}{G} and getting 3 -0/-1 counters. This is different from Grist (Rule 606.3 for loyalty). A creature with Yawgmoth's ability + 3 Walls can generate {G}{G}{G} AND use Yawgmoth's proliferate (costs {B}{B} but Cauldron allows any color) in the same turn.

Heuristics

  1. Yawgmoth has protection from Humans, not from creatures generally. Many players assume Yawgmoth can't be targeted by removal. He can be targeted by Solitude (the card itself is cast by a non-Human), Prismatic Ending, and most removal spells — they're cast by Humans but the spell itself is the targeting source, not the Human casting it. Protection from Humans means: can't be blocked, targeted, damaged, or enchanted by Human CREATURE CARDS (and their effects). Non-Human sources target him freely.
  2. Sacrifice is a cost — happens before the ability goes on stack. When activating Yawgmoth's ability, the cost is "pay 1 life, sacrifice another creature." This cost is paid before the ability goes on the stack. Opponents cannot respond to the sacrifice — the creature is already gone. They can respond to the ability itself (before -1/-1 placement and draw) by killing Yawgmoth. But the sacrificed creature is gone regardless.
  3. Yawgmoth can activate the turn he enters — use Chord EOT for the combo. Yawgmoth has no summoning sickness restriction on his activated ability. Chord for Yawgmoth at end of opponent's turn: he enters with haste (in terms of activated abilities), immediately enabling the loop if Melira, undying creature, and Blood Artist are already in play. This is the ideal line for instant-speed combo assembly.
  4. Eldritch Evolution finds MV ≤ sacrifice MV + 2, not exactly MV X. You can find any creature with MV UP TO sacrifice MV + 2. Sacrificing Young Wolf (MV 1) finds MV ≤ 3 — that's any creature with MV 1, 2, or 3. You're not locked into finding exactly MV 3. This flexibility lets you find the most situationally appropriate piece.
  5. Sacrificing Young Wolf to Evolution to find Melira (MV 2) is the core chain. Wolf (MV 1) → Evolution → find Melira (MV 2, ≤ 3). Then find Yawgmoth via Chord or a second Evolution sacrificing a MV 3 creature. The Wolf-to-Melira chain is the critical step because Melira is the hardest piece to draw naturally — only 2 copies in the deck.
  6. Grafdigger's Cage prevents undying — Force of Vigor destroys it at instant speed for free. Grafdigger's Cage says "creature cards can't enter the battlefield from graveyards or libraries." When an undying creature dies and tries to return from GY with undying, Cage prevents this. Your entire combo engine breaks down. Force of Vigor on their turn: exile a green card, destroy Cage for free. Haywire Mite (Chord-tutorable MV 1) also exiles Cage at instant speed.
  7. Clarion Conqueror shuts YOUR activated abilities too — never Chord for it in mirror. Clarion Conqueror shuts activated abilities of artifacts, creatures, AND planeswalkers — for ALL players. If you Chord for Clarion Conqueror to stop Yawgmoth in a mirror, you also shut your own Yawgmoth. Only board Clarion into matchups where the benefit (shutting their abilities) outweighs the cost (shutting yours). Against Living End: safe to use since LE has no key activated abilities.
  8. Grist is a creature in all zones except the battlefield — Chord and CoCo find him. This unique property (Grist is a planeswalker creature) means your 12-tutor package can find him. Chord X=3 at instant speed can find Grist. CoCo (reveal top 6, put up to 2 MV≤3 creatures) can hit Grist. Grist's +1 mills and makes Insects, −2 destroys creatures with MV ≤ Insects, −5 drains based on GY. The −2 on Clarion Conqueror (MV 3) requires 3+ Insects — achievable after a couple turns.
  9. Blood Artist triggers on ANY creature dying — including your opponent's. Blood Artist: "whenever this creature OR another creature dies." Not just your creatures. In combat, when their creatures die, Blood Artist also triggers. Against creature-heavy boards, combat becomes incremental life drain. Zulaport Cutthroat only triggers on YOUR creatures — Blood Artist is better for the infinite loop (triggers on your own undying dying each iteration).
  10. The infinite loop with 2 undying: alternate sacrificing Wolf and Messenger. With Wolf (no counters) + Messenger (no counters) + Melira + Yawgmoth: sacrifice Wolf → Yawgmoth places -1/-1 on Messenger's +1/+1 counter (they cancel, Messenger at 0) → Wolf returns (undying, was clean). Sacrifice Messenger → Yawgmoth targets Wolf's +1/+1 (cancel) → Messenger returns. Alternate. Both creatures cycle through undying, drawing cards and triggering Blood Artist each iteration.
  11. Veil of Summer on your combo turn blanks targeted removal AND counterspells. Veil of Summer: spells can't target you or your permanents, your spells can't be countered by blue or black. The turn you cast Veil, Solitude can't target Yawgmoth, Force of Negation can't counter Evolution, Thoughtseize can't target you. This creates a "free turn" window for the combo. The single green mana cost makes it easily castable alongside your combo pieces.
  12. Agatha's Soul Cauldron — destroy it before it exiles Yawgmoth, not after. Agatha's Soul Cauldron's ability to exile a creature from GY and grant its abilities to other creatures is powerful. Against you: if Yawgmoth is in GY and opponent uses Cauldron to exile him, their creatures gain his ability. Destroy Cauldron proactively — before they activate it. Once Yawgmoth is exiled, the ability has already been granted and destroying Cauldron doesn't remove the granted abilities.
  13. Collected Company at instant speed hits up to 2 MV≤3 creatures — use it to surprise combo pieces. CoCo reveals top 6, puts up to 2 MV≤3 creatures onto battlefield. Cast at end of opponent's turn when they're tapped out. Hitting both Melira and Blood Artist (or Blood Artist and Young Wolf) can suddenly present the entire combo with only Yawgmoth missing — Chord for Yawgmoth in response to their next spell. The instant speed means you dodge sorcery-speed answers.
  14. Young Wolf's job G1 is blocking Ragavan — don't sacrifice it early in aggro matchups. Against Boros Energy, Young Wolf is your critical early blocker. Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer attacking on turn 1 is devastating — Young Wolf blocks and trades (Ragavan dies, Wolf returns from undying). Don't sacrifice Young Wolf to Eldritch Evolution on turn 2 against Boros unless you're immediately going for the combo — you need the blocker.
  15. Fulminator Mage via Chord destroys their nonbasic land at instant speed. Chord for Fulminator Mage (MV 3, X=3 with convoke from creatures) at end of opponent's turn destroys a nonbasic land. Against Amulet Titan: destroy their Simic Growth Chamber or Slayers' Stronghold to prevent the haste kill. Against Eldrazi Tron: destroy a Tron piece. This is the deck's toolbox land destruction, instantly available via Chord at any point in the game.
  16. Protect against exile effects — they circumvent undying. Undying returns creatures from the graveyard. If a creature is EXILED instead of going to the GY (Solitude, Prismatic Ending, Leyline of the Void), undying never triggers. Against exile-heavy decks, the combo can sputter out without the loop regenerating. Prioritize Thoughtseize or Veil of Summer to protect key pieces. Haywire Mite and Force of Vigor handle Leyline before it becomes active.

SB Slot Priority Order

  1. 01
    Thoughtseize (3 copies minimum) — Your primary interaction. Strip Solitude, Force of Negation, or Grafdigger’s Cage before your combo turn. The most important SB card. Never cut below 3. Goes in vs every interactive deck.
  2. 02
    Veil of Summer (2 copies) — Counter targeted removal and draw a card for {G}. Mostly relevant vs UW Control and Jeskai Blink which run Solitude + Path. Also blanks Thoughtseize mirror situations. Never cut if blue/white control is above 10% of the expected field.
  3. 03
    Endurance (2 copies) — 3/4 flash body + exile target GY. Critical vs Living End (exile their creatures before cascade resolves), Glockulous (counter the reanimate), and Goryo’s Vengeance (exile the target). Also a reasonable combat creature vs aggro. Bring in wherever GY hate matters.
  4. 04
    Haywire Mite / Force of Vigor (2 copies) — Answer Grafdigger’s Cage, Leyline of the Void, Rest in Peace, and Cursed Totem. Without these, GY hate completely shuts off undying. Always include at least 2 artifact/enchantment answers. Haywire Mite is preferable (also survives removal to come back with undying).
  5. 05
    Flex (3–4 slots) — Fulminator Mage (vs Eldrazi Tron, destroy land), Grist (removal + value), additional Hapatra copies (vs creature-heavy fields). Shift these based on your expected field composition. Never cut the top 4 priorities to fit flex slots.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand with Young Wolf or Strangleroot Geist + a black source. Undying is your engine.
  • Yawgmoth rule: {1}{B}: sacrifice a creature, target gets -1/-1 counter, draw a card. This loops infinitely with two undying creatures.
  • Combo rule: Two undying creatures + Yawgmoth + Blood Artist or Zulaport Cutthroat = kill. Sacrifice one, it comes back, sacrifice the other, it comes back. Loop.
  • Protect rule: Chord of Calling and Collected Company find your missing pieces. Chord for Yawgmoth at instant speed during their EOT.
  • Viscera Seer rule: Viscera Seer scrys when you sacrifice a creature. In the combo, scry before drawing to set up the next card.
  • Sideboard rule: Endurance and Collected Company are your post-board resilience. Know which matchups want which.

Testing Framework

CoCo Timing

Practice through Grafdigger's Cage (stops Persist/CoCo), Solitude, and Endurance.

Matchday Routine

  1. 01
    Register and confirm Chord of Calling count. It's your primary tutor.
  2. 02
    Know the exact loop sequence before Round 1. Sacrifice, -1/-1, draw, undying, repeat. Mechanical.
  3. 03
    Between rounds: did you protect Yawgmoth? He's the one piece that matters. Everything else is support.
  4. 04
    Chord at EOT is almost always correct. Instant speed Yawgmoth is the line.
  5. 05
    The combo is clean. If you're taking a long time executing it, practice more reps.

The Combo Requirements — Never Forget

4 Pieces Needed
Yawgmoth + Melira + Undying + Drain
Yawgmoth (activated ability: pay life, sacrifice, -1/-1, draw) + Melira (prevents -1/-1 on your creatures) + 1 undying creature (Young Wolf, Geralf's Messenger) + Blood Artist or Zulaport Cutthroat. Missing any one = no infinite loop. But missing just Melira = still functional midrange.
Key Evolution Chain
Wolf → Melira → Chord → Yawgmoth
T1 Young Wolf. T3 Eldritch Evolution (sacrifice Wolf MV1 → find Melira MV≤3). T4+ Chord of Calling X=4 with convoke from tapping creatures → find Yawgmoth. Blood Artist in play = combo active. Instant-speed Chord at EOT is the clean kill line.
