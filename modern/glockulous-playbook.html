Chapter 01

Identity &
Philosophy

"Discard the Archon. Return the Archon. They lose." — Core Mantra

Glockulous is a graveyard midrange deck built around one axis: get Archon of Cruelty into the graveyard as fast as possible, then return it repeatedly for 2 mana. The Archon ETB + attack trigger is a 6-point life swing per occurrence — opponent sacrifices a creature or planeswalker, discards a card, loses 3 life; you draw a card and gain 3 life. Against most decks, two Archon triggers is a functional win.

What makes Glockulous better than pure Grixis Reanimator is its dual-axis structure. Psychic Frog and Emperor of Bones are legitimate standalone threats that win games independently of the Archon plan. Psychic Frog draws on combat damage and grows as a discard outlet — but its role as a "setup piece" undersells it. A Frog that has discarded 4-5 cards is a 5/6+ flying creature that draws a card every time it connects. It is the correct primary win condition when the GY plan is disrupted: pivot to Frog immediately when you see Rest in Peace, Surgical Extraction, or repeated Counterspells on your Persist. Protect Frog with Mystical Dispute and Thoughtseize the same way you would protect Archon. Emperor of Bones reanimates any creature from any graveyard as a combat ability — not a spell, not counterable by Consign to Memory. This resilience means graveyard hate alone doesn't shut you down — you play through it on the fair axis.

The third axis is Harbinger of the Seas as a land-hate piece. Against Boros Energy, Amulet Titan, and Jeskai Blink, a Harbinger entering the battlefield means all their nonbasic lands immediately become Islands. No red mana. No white mana. A midrange 2/2 that collapses three-color manabases is the cleanest form of disruption available at 3 mana.

Core Engines

The Target
Archon of Cruelty
{6}{B}{B} 6/6 flying. Not legendary — multiple can coexist. Triggers on enter OR attack each time: opponent sacrifices creature or planeswalker, discards a card, loses 3 life; you draw, gain 3. This is a 6-point life swing per trigger, not 7. A Persisted Archon fires the ETB on entry, then again on attack — two triggers from one return, 12 life swings total.
The Return — 2 Mana
Persist
{1}{B} sorcery. Return target nonlegendary creature card from graveyard to battlefield with a -1/-1 counter. Archon enters as a 5/5 flier (6/6 base minus -1/-1). Still triggers the full ETB swing at 5/5. Persist is a colored sorcery — Consign to Memory cannot counter it. Only Counterspell, Force of Negation, and similar spell counters can answer it. This matters enormously when planning your protection.
The Enabler
Faithless Looting
{R} sorcery: draw 2, discard 2. Flashback {2}{R}. Primary graveyard filler. The draw and discard are simultaneous in a sense — you draw 2 first, then discard 2. You cannot draw Archon then discard it in the same Looting. Ideal discard: Archon + Troll of Khazad-dûm. Flashback {2}{R} later for additional filtering. One Looting on turn 1 can have Archon in the yard ready for Persist on turn 3.
The Midrange Threat
Psychic Frog
{U}{B} 1/2. Combat damage to player or planeswalker → draw a card. Discard a card: put a +1/+1 counter on it (activated ability). Exile 3 cards from GY: flying until EOT. The Frog simultaneously draws cards, discards Archon into the graveyard, and grows as a threat. A Frog that connects twice in a game represents both a 3/3+ attacker and two free Archon discards. Its flying ability requires 3 graveyard exiles — plan that resource carefully.
The Combat Reanimator
Emperor of Bones
{1}{B} 2/2. At beginning of combat: exile up to one target card from any graveyard. {1}{B}: Adapt 2 (activated ability). When +1/+1 counters are placed on it, put the exiled creature onto the battlefield under your control with a finality counter and haste. Sacrifice it at beginning of next end step. The Adapt is an activated ability — Consign to Memory cannot counter it. The creature enters with a finality counter: when it dies it is permanently exiled.
The Hate Piece
Harbinger of the Seas
{1}{U}{U} 2/2 Merfolk Wizard. Nonbasic lands are Islands — continuous static effect, not a trigger. Doesn't destroy or return lands. It changes their subtype. When Harbinger enters, every nonbasic land in play immediately becomes an Island. Sanctifier en-Vec on a board with Harbinger in play cannot generate W from converted nonbasics. Boros Energy loses access to red and white mana from their shocks and fetches. Amulet Titan's bounce lands become Islands.

The Cycling Package — Troll of Khazad-dûm

Troll of Khazad-dûm is {5}{B} 6/5 — can't be blocked except by 3 or more creatures. Its role in this deck is not as a castable card but as a two-function utility: in hand with no Looting, swampcycle it for {1} to find a black source (Watery Grave, Blood Crypt, basic Swamp). With Looting in hand, discard Troll and Archon together — don't swampcycle when Looting is available, since Looting is far superior. A Troll in the yard is a large threat that can be Persisted (nonlegendary, qualifies) as a 5/4 that can't be blocked except by 3+ creatures.

Chapter 03

The Game Plan

"Fill the yard on 1. Threaten on 2. Persist on 3. They concede." — Execution Principle

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turn 1
Fill or Disrupt
Option A: Faithless Looting — draw 2, discard Archon + Troll. Archon is now in the yard with 1 mana spent. Option B: Thought Scour target player — mill 2 (yourself), draw 1. If you mill Archon, even better. Option C: Thoughtseize their removal spell or Solitude to protect your turn 3. Option D: Swampcycle Troll if you need a black source. In order of priority: Looting > Thoughtseize > Thought Scour > Swampcycle.
Turn 2
Deploy a Threat
Psychic Frog ({U}{B}) or Emperor of Bones ({1}{B}). Emperor on turn 2 means on turn 3: exile Archon at beginning of combat, Adapt for {1}{B}, Archon enters with finality counter and haste, attacks immediately — two Archon triggers total on turn 3. Psychic Frog turn 2 means turn 3 you attack, deal damage, draw a card, and then Persist Archon — a full midrange turn plus combo payoff.
Turn 3
Persist or Emperor Trigger
Ideal: cast Persist ({1}{B}), Archon enters as a 5/5 flier, ETB fires. Opponent sacrifices a permanent, discards a card, loses 3. You draw and gain 3. At 17 life with one fewer permanent and one fewer card — the game is nearly won. Attack with Archon on turn 4 for the second trigger. Alternatively: Emperor of Bones Adapt resolves, Archon enters with haste, attacks — both triggers on the same turn.
Turn 4+
Close or Rebuild
Attack with Archon (second trigger if Persisted on 3). If Archon was answered, Psychic Frog continues drawing into the second copy. Faithless Looting flashback ({2}{R}) refills. Unearth returns Harbinger of the Seas (MV 3) at instant speed if needed. Second Persist or second Emperor reanimates again. You have 4 Archons, 4 Persists, 4 Emperors — the redundancy outlasts most hate.

The Faithless Looting Sequence in Detail

Draw two, then discard two — this is the correct order. You cannot draw Archon and then discard it in the same Looting. You draw your two new cards, then make your discard decisions with full information. Optimal discard: Archon of Cruelty + Troll of Khazad-dûm. Archon goes in the yard ready for Persist. Troll goes in the yard as a threat (Persist-able for a 5/4 that can't be blocked by fewer than 3 creatures). You keep your actionable spells and lands. Flashback Looting later for {2}{R} to filter again — the double-dip is what makes Looting better than any other enabler.

When not to discard Troll: If you have 2 lands and no other black source, swampcycle Troll for {1} to find a Watery Grave or Swamp. You cannot cast Persist without black mana — finding the black source is more important than the yard synergy in this edge case.

The Emperor of Bones Full Sequence

Turn 2: Cast Emperor of Bones ({1}{B}). 2/2 enters.

Turn 3 — Beginning of Combat Step: Emperor's triggered ability fires — exile up to one target card from a graveyard. Target Archon of Cruelty in your yard.

Turn 3 — Priority in Beginning of Combat: Activate Adapt ({1}{B}: Adapt 2). This is an activated ability — it goes on the stack. Opponent can respond, but Consign to Memory cannot counter it (it's not a triggered ability or colorless spell). If Adapt resolves, two +1/+1 counters are placed on Emperor.

Turn 3 — Trigger from Adapt: "Whenever +1/+1 counters are placed on Emperor of Bones, put the exiled creature card onto the battlefield under your control with a finality counter and haste." Archon enters as a 6/6 flier with a finality counter and haste. ETB trigger fires immediately — opponent sacrifices, discards, loses 3. You draw, gain 3.

Turn 3 — Declare Attackers: Archon attacks (it has haste). Attack trigger fires — a second sacrifice, discard, lose 3 / draw, gain 3 trigger. Two full Archon triggers on turn 3 from one Emperor of Bones.

Critical: The Archon reanimated via Emperor has a finality counter. If it dies in combat or to removal, it goes to exile permanently — Persist cannot return it. Only attack into situations where Archon will not die unless the game is already won.

Mulligan Philosophy

Snap keep: Any 7 with Faithless Looting (or Thought Scour) + a black source + Persist or Emperor. This hand wins on turns 3-4 regardless of the other 4 cards.

Snap mulligan: Any hand where Archon can't get to the graveyard by turn 2, or any hand with 0 return mechanisms and 0 ways to draw into them.

The Frog keep: Psychic Frog + Thoughtseize + 2 lands + any spell is a legitimate keep. You play the fair midrange game: Thoughtseize their answer on turn 1, Frog on turn 2, attack for cards. You'll draw into the combo line naturally.

Troll in opening hand rule: With Looting → keep and discard via Looting. Without Looting and 2 lands → swampcycle for black source. Without Looting and only 1 land → keep and swampcycle on turn 1 to find a second land. A castable Troll (6 mana) is never the plan.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 Faithless Looting Sets Everything
On the play, T1 Faithless Looting mills 2 cards and discards 2 — filling your GY with Griselbrand and the fatties you want to reanimate while finding your reanimation spell. The sooner Griselbrand is in the GY, the sooner you can reanimate it. On the play: Looting > any other T1. Never spend T1 on a land-pass if Looting is in hand.
On the Draw
Grief First on Draw
On the draw, opponents have had a T1. They may have deployed a threat or GY hate. Consider T1 Grief (evoke for {B}) to strip their Endurance, Relic, or key spell from hand before you try to reanimate T2. The extra card on draw makes Grief+reanimate lines more consistent. On draw vs blue decks: always consider Grief first to clear the counter before attempting reanimation.

When Disrupted

GY Exiled
Fair Mode — Grief + Drown
Nihil Spellbomb or Endurance exiles your GY. Pivot: Grief (3/1 menace body), Drown in the Loch (counter or destroy at instant speed), and Unearth targets that aren’t in the GY yet (small creatures). Surveil aggressively to fill GY again for a second reanimation window. Don’t scoop to GY exile — the fair plan is real.
Griselbrand Answered
Second Target from Hand
Griselbrand removed: look for your second reanimation target in hand or GY. Troll of Khazad-dûm is a backup large threat. Persist creatures (Kitchen Finks) generate ETB value. Grief is a 3/1 body. Chain reanimation attempts — you have multiple fatties and multiple reanimation spells. The second reanimation is almost always available.

Core
Engines

Three engines win games with Glockulous. Engine A is the primary plan. Engine B is the advanced line that turns Engine A into a permanent threat. Engine C is the backup plan when the GY plan is shut off.

A
Faithless Looting → Persist → Archon ETB
The primary combo. Draw, pitch Archon, reanimate next turn. One trigger often wins.
Setup
Archon in the Graveyard
Faithless Looting draws two cards then discards two. Cast on turn 1 — discard Archon of Cruelty (MV 8, impossible to hardcast) and one other card. Archon is now in the GY as a Persist target. Thought Scour mills 2 cards — if Archon is in those 2, it goes directly to the GY without spending a card from hand. Both cards set up turn 2 Persist.
Execution
Persist on Turn 2
Persist (MV 2, black sorcery): return target nonlegendary creature card from your GY to the battlefield with a finality counter. Archon enters — ETB triggers: target opponent sacrifices a permanent or planeswalker, discards a card, loses 3 life. You draw a card and gain 3 life. This single trigger swings the game state by 6 life and removes their best card and permanent simultaneously. Archon is also an 8/8 flying lifelink attacking next turn.
Result
One Trigger = Winning Position
A single Archon ETB from Persist puts you so far ahead that most decks cannot recover. You gained 3 life, drew a card, they lost 3 life, discarded their best card, and sacrificed a permanent — all for 2 mana. If Archon attacks next turn (8/8 flying lifelink, gains you 8 more life), the game is effectively over. The finality counter means if Archon dies it goes to exile — not back to the GY for a second Persist.
B
Emperor Adapt → Consign the Sacrifice Trigger → Permanent Archon
The advanced line. Archon enters with haste, ETB fires, attacks — then Consign counters the sacrifice trigger in your end step. Archon stays permanently.
Setup
Emperor + Archon in Exile
Emperor of Bones has two relevant abilities: (1) At beginning of combat, exile up to one target card from any GY. (2) {1}{B}: Adapt 2 — put two +1/+1 counters on Emperor if it has none. Whenever counters are placed on Emperor, put the exiled creature card onto the battlefield with a finality counter — it gains haste and is sacrificed at beginning of next end step. The sacrifice clause is a delayed triggered ability that fires in your end step.
Execution
Consign the End Step Sacrifice Trigger
Emperor exiles Archon at combat. You Adapt — counters placed, trigger fires, Archon enters with haste + finality counter. Archon ETB fires (they sacrifice, discard, lose 3 — you draw, gain 3). Archon attacks for 8 flying lifelink. In your end step, the delayed sacrifice trigger fires: "sacrifice Archon at the beginning of the next end step." This sacrifice trigger is a triggered ability. Fire Consign to Memory at your own sacrifice trigger. It resolves. Archon is never sacrificed.
Result
Permanent 8/8 Flying Lifelink for {1}{B}
Total cost: {1}{B} for Adapt plus {U} for Consign = 3 mana. Result: a permanent 8/8 flying lifelink with a finality counter (if it dies to removal later, it goes to exile — cannot be Persisted back against you). This is the primary reason Consign to Memory is in the main deck. You are not just protecting — you are creating an unstoppable threat for 3 mana total.
C
Psychic Frog Backup Finisher
When the GY plan is shut off, Frog is not a consolation prize — it is a legitimate primary win condition. Recognize the signal and pivot immediately.
Setup
Recognize When to Pivot
When you see Rest in Peace, Leyline of the Void, or Surgical Extraction on all copies of Archon — stop trying to fight through graveyard hate with a graveyard deck. The pivot signal is immediate. Stop treating your hand as Archon fuel. Start treating every card in hand as Frog growth. Every discard goes toward growing Frog's power and enabling its flying ability.
Execution
Discard to Grow, Exile to Fly
Psychic Frog: {1}{B}, 2/2. Whenever it deals combat damage, draw a card. Discard a card: put a +1/+1 counter on it. Exile three cards from your GY: gains flying until end of turn. Discard your lands, redundant Thoughtseize copies, excess removal — anything that grows Frog. Exile your own GY cards (now useless under RiP anyway) to enable flying. By turn 4-5, Frog is commonly a 5/5 or 6/6 flying creature.
Result
Self-Sustaining Card Engine
A 6/6 flying Frog that connects for a card every attack is a better threat than most decks' primary win conditions. The drawn card is often discarded back to grow Frog further — the engine is self-sustaining. Protect Frog with Mystical Dispute and Thoughtseize exactly as you would protect Archon. Frog backed by disruption wins games that Archon cannot win once the GY is locked.

Lines & Tricks

  • StackConsign to Memory — the two offensive lines: (1) Emperor sacrifice trigger in your end step: fire Consign at the "sacrifice Archon" delayed triggered ability to keep it permanently. (2) Future testing — Quantum Riddler Warp exile trigger: Warp creates a delayed triggered ability (exile at next end step). Consign can counter it, keeping the permanent. The Warp bait line (cast Persist with Riddler, Consign your own Warp copy trigger to force their counter) may enter the 75 once testing is complete.
  • StackThoughtseize before Persist, always: If they're holding up UU and you cannot see their hand, assume Counterspell. Thoughtseize the turn before Persist. If you can't Thoughtseize first, use Mystical Dispute to fight through their counter on the chain. One Persist countered with no follow-up is close to a game loss.
  • SequenceFaithless Looting draws THEN discards — full information discard: You draw two new cards before making your discard decision. You see your entire new hand before choosing what to pitch. This means you always have perfect information on the discard — never pitch a card you just drew unless it's worse than what you're keeping. Common mistake: treating Looting like a blind discard and pitching incorrectly.
  • AvoidDon't exile GY cards for Frog flying when you still need those cards for Persist: Frog's flying cost is three GY cards — those might be the Archon, a Persist target, and your Emperor. Evaluate: is connecting with Frog this turn (drawing one card) worth permanently removing three key GY resources? Only grant flying when the combat result is more valuable than the three cards you're exiling.

Lines & Tactics

Lines &
Tactics

"Reanimation timing and Griselbrand’s activations win or lose games in narrow windows."

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Critical
GY Exile in Response = Fizzle
Reanimation spells target a creature card in a graveyard. If the GY is exiled in response — Endurance flash, Nihil Spellbomb, Relic of Progenitus — the target is illegal and the reanimation spell fizzles. Griselbrand does not enter. The spell is countered by rules (illegal target). This is why your Grief must strip Endurance before you attempt reanimation vs blue decks.
Critical
Griselbrand Draws 7 Can Be Repeated
Griselbrand’s ability costs 7 life and has no mana cost. You can activate it multiple times per turn as long as you have 7+ life each time. At 21 life: activate 3 times = draw 21 cards. Use this to find your combo pieces, protection, or second reanimation target. After activation 1, you have 14 life — activate again. At 14: activate again, 7 life left. Optimal: stop at 1 activation if you already have what you need.
Critical
Persist/Undying Counters Cancel Each Other
If a creature has both Persist (-1/-1 counter on return) and a +1/+1 counter already on it, the -1/-1 and +1/+1 cancel and the creature returns clean without either counter. Kitchen Finks with a +1/+1 counter on it that dies: Persist triggers, returns, the counters cancel = clean Finks. This enables infinite ETB loops with sacrifice outlets: sac Finks (+1/+1 counter), Persist returns it clean, sac again.

Timing Windows

When to Hold Mana vs Spend It
The core timing decision for this deck: every land drop and every spell cast is a commitment. Know before your upkeep which lines require open mana this turn and which don’t. Holding mana = passing priority; spending it = committing to a plan. Make this decision in your upkeep, not in response to their action.
When to Cast at Their End Step vs Your Main Phase
End-step casts maximize information: you’ve seen their full turn, they’re often tapped out. Main-phase casts maximize mana availability for follow-up. Know which of your key spells gain most from end-step timing and which need main-phase mana backup.
Chapter 05

Role Assignment

Glockulous has three modes. The biggest mistake is staying in Reanimate mode when the game has shifted to requiring Midrange or Disrupt. Read the board state before committing every turn.

Reanimate
Default mode. Fill the yard on turn 1, return Archon on turn 3. Every decision evaluated by "does this get Archon into play faster?" Thoughtseize clears the way. Faithless Looting fills the yard.
vs: Boros Energy (race) · Amulet Titan (race) · Any slow or midrange deck
Fair Midrange
When graveyard hate is active or the Archon plan is disrupted. Psychic Frog as a legitimate card advantage engine — it grows via Frog discard outlet and draws every combat. Harbinger of the Seas shuts off mana. Emperor reanimates from their graveyard too.
vs: Any deck sideboarding Rest in Peace · Mirrors · Grindy control games
Disrupt + Kill
When they have specific answers to your plan. Thoughtseize their counter before Persist. Mystical Dispute protects Persist at instant speed. Spell Pierce counters their removal turn 2 before Emperor Adapts.
vs: Jeskai Blink · Azorius Control · Any counterspell deck

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Swampcycling Troll when you have Faithless Looting. Looting is categorically better than swampcycling. Looting puts Troll in the yard (where it's a Persist target), draws two cards, and discards your second unwanted card. Swampcycling only puts a land in your hand. If you have Looting, always use it to pitch Troll — never swampcycle first.
  2. Persisting Archon into open blue mana. If they're holding up UU with no information, you're walking into Counterspell. Cast Thoughtseize the turn before Persist. If you didn't clear their hand, wait or use Mystical Dispute to fight through. One Persist countered with no follow-up is close to a loss.
  3. Attacking with Emperor-reanimated Archon recklessly. That Archon has a finality counter. If it dies in combat or to removal, it goes to exile permanently. Only attack into clear boards or situations where death is impossible. The ETB trigger already fired — the attack trigger is a bonus, not a requirement.
  4. Exiling three graveyard cards for Frog flying carelessly. Those three cards might be the Archon, a Persist target, and an Emperor target you need later. Only grant Frog flying when the combat result (connecting for a card) is more valuable than those three graveyard resources.
  5. Psychic Frog is not just a setup piece — it is a legitimate primary win condition. A fully powered Frog is a 5/5, 6/6, or larger flying creature that deals combat damage to draw cards, growing faster than opponents can answer it. When the Persist plan is disrupted by Rest in Peace, Surgical Extraction, or Counterspell — pivot immediately to the Frog plan. Discard aggressively into the GY to fuel its power, grant flying once combat kills are guaranteed, and close the game. Frog backed by Thoughtseize and Mystical Dispute protection wins games that Archon cannot.
  6. Consign to Memory is an offensive tool — the primary reason it is in the main deck. Two proactive lines that win games:

    Line 1 — Counter the Emperor of Bones sacrifice trigger in your own end step to keep Archon on the field permanently. Emperor's trigger reads: Archon enters the battlefield with a finality counter, gains haste, and is sacrificed at the beginning of the next end step. That sacrifice clause is a delayed triggered ability — it fires in your end step after Archon has already attacked. When that sacrifice trigger goes on the stack in your end step, fire Consign to Memory at it. Consign counters triggered abilities — the sacrifice trigger is countered, Archon is never sacrificed, and it remains on the battlefield permanently. The result: a 6/6 flying lifelink with haste that attacked this turn and now stays forever. The finality counter means if it does die to removal later, it goes to exile rather than their graveyard — it cannot be Persisted back against you. This is the flagship offensive Consign line and the core reason the card is in the main deck at all.

    Line 2 — Counter your own Quantum Riddler Warp trigger to bait out their countermagic. Riddler's Warp keyword copies the spell you cast as a triggered ability on the stack. Concrete line: you cast Persist with Riddler in play and two Consigns in hand. The Warp trigger goes on the stack, creating a copy of Persist. Fire Consign at your own Warp copy trigger. Opponent now faces a decision: counter your Consign (Warp copy resolves, Archon enters off the copy — the original Persist is still on the stack and also resolves) or let Consign resolve (Warp copy is countered, but the original Persist is untouched and resolves). Either way Archon enters. They cannot counter both the original and the copy in the same window with one counterspell. This forces their interaction at the worst possible time for them.
  7. Forgetting that Faithless Looting draws before discarding. You can't "loot away" Archon to then draw it back. You draw two, keeping the best two, then discard. The discard decision is made with full information on your new hand — use this correctly and you'll always pitch the right two cards.

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • 01
    Not Griefing before reanimating vs blue decks. Open blue mana = Endurance or Force possible. T1 Grief strips the hate piece. T3 reanimate safely after stripping their answer.
  • 02
    Paying too many Griselbrand activations at low life. 3 activations = 21 life. At 20 life, 3 activations puts you at -1 — you die. Stop at 2 activations (14 life spent) and hold 6 life as a combat buffer against their remaining threats.
  • 03
    Discarding the reanimation spell instead of the fatty. Faithless Looting: discard Griselbrand, keep Animate Dead. Never discard the spell that returns your target. Double-check your discard choices under time pressure.
  • 04
    Reanimating into active Grafdigger’s Cage. Cage prevents creatures entering from GY. Answer Cage with Mystical Dispute (counter it) or remove it before the reanimate resolves. Never tap out for reanimate into visible Cage.

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Glockulous wins on trigger sequencing. Know exactly when each fires and what counters it."
The Consign Line — Counter Your Own Emperor Sacrifice Trigger
When Emperor of Bones reanimates Archon, the Archon enters with haste and a finality counter, and a delayed triggered ability fires at the beginning of the NEXT end step: "sacrifice Archon." This sacrifice is a triggered ability on the stack. Fire Consign to Memory at it — Consign counters triggered abilities. The sacrifice trigger is countered, Archon is never sacrificed, and it remains permanently. Result: a 6/6 flying lifelink Archon that attacked this turn and never leaves. The finality counter means if Archon ever dies later, it goes to exile (can't be Persisted) — but that's a minor cost for keeping it permanently in play.
Persist and the Finality Counter — Where Archon Goes When It Dies
Persist says "Return target creature card from your graveyard to the battlefield. It gains haste until end of turn. Put a finality counter on it." A finality counter reads: "If a permanent has a finality counter on it, it's put into exile instead of any other zone when it leaves the battlefield." This means: Archon with a finality counter that dies goes to EXILE, not your graveyard. You cannot Persist it again. Emperor of Bones cannot exile it from the graveyard to reanimate (it's not in the graveyard). Track your finality counter Archons — they are one-time use. Your main-deck Archons without finality counters can be Persisted repeatedly.
Emperor of Bones Adapt Trigger — Counters Placed, Not Adapt Resolution
Emperor of Bones's reanimate ability fires from "Whenever one or more counters are placed on Emperor of Bones." The Adapt ability places N +1/+1 counters — and the moment those counters are placed, the "counters placed" trigger fires. This is a triggered ability, and it can be countered by Consign to Memory (which counters triggered abilities). If your opponent has Consign up when you Adapt, they CAN counter the reanimate trigger, preventing Archon from entering. Play around this: Thoughtseize their Consign the turn before Adapting. The Adapt action itself (paying the Adapt cost) cannot be countered — only the resulting trigger.
Consign to Memory Cannot Counter Persist or Activated Abilities
Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities or colorless spells. Persist is a black sorcery — it is NOT a triggered ability, it IS a colored spell. Consign cannot counter Persist. Your Emperor of Bones Adapt (paying {2} plus counters) is an activated ability — Consign cannot counter activated abilities either. What they CAN counter with Consign: your Emperor's "counters placed" reanimate trigger, Archon's ETB trigger (sacrifice, discard, lose 3). The most important opponent protection: if they have Consign up when your Archon ETB fires, they can counter the ETB trigger and you get nothing. Play around it by stripping Consign with Thoughtseize before Persisting.
Psychic Frog Flying — Exile FROM Your Graveyard Only
Psychic Frog's flying ability: "Pay {0}: Exile three cards from your graveyard. Psychic Frog gains flying until end of turn." The exile is from YOUR graveyard — not any graveyard, not a library, not hand. This means: (1) you need 3 cards in your own GY to activate it, (2) activating it removes your own Archon, Emperor, or Persist targets from the GY (the cost is real), (3) under Rest in Peace, there is no graveyard and this ability cannot be activated. Calculate GY resources before granting flying — losing the 3 exiled cards may cost you future Persist or Emperor targets.
Reality Smasher Discard — Only When Targeted, Not When Swept
Reality Smasher says "Whenever Reality Smasher becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, counter it unless its controller discards a card." This ONLY triggers when it is specifically TARGETED. Board sweepers (Supreme Verdict, Wrath of God, Archon ETB's "sacrifice a permanent") do NOT target — they affect all or use an instruction without a target. Against Smasher: use Archon's ETB sacrifice trigger (which forces them to sacrifice Smasher without targeting it), use Persist to get Archon into play and let the ETB do the work, or use untargeted sweepers. Targeted removal like Fatal Push triggers the discard. One way to "blank" the discard: use Fatal Push when their hand is already empty — they have nothing to discard so the discard is irrelevant, and the counter clause only applies if they CAN discard.

Heuristics &
Quick Rules

Automatic decisions drilled before the event. Glockulous has many layers of sequencing — these rules prevent the most common errors.

  1. Looting pitches Troll to the yard; swampcycling puts a land in hand. These are different outcomes. With Looting in hand, always use it to discard Troll — the yard Troll is a Persist target. Swampcycle Troll only when you need a land immediately and have no Looting.
  2. Thoughtseize before Persist — always. If their hand is unknown and they have UU available, you're walking into Counterspell. Cast Thoughtseize the turn before Persist to strip the counter. One Persist countered and unrecovered is close to a loss in most matchups.
  3. Archon is not legendary — you can have multiple. Don't hesitate to Persist a second Archon when the first is already in play. Two Archons swinging means 4 Archon triggers per turn. This wins any game that reaches this state.
  4. The Archon ETB trigger fires on enter AND on attack. A Persisted Archon fires ETB when it enters (they sacrifice, discard, lose 3, you draw, gain 3). Then it fires again when it attacks next turn. Two triggers from one return = 12-point life swing before they take combat damage.
  5. Emperor of Bones Adapt is an activated ability — Consign to Memory cannot counter it. Consign counters triggered abilities and colorless spells. Adapt is a colored activated ability ({1}{B}). Safe from Consign. Only answers are: stop Emperor from entering the battlefield, prevent the exile during beginning of combat, or respond to the counter placement trigger by exiling Emperor (Solitude removes Emperor before counters are placed).
  6. A creature with a finality counter goes to exile when it dies. Emperor-reanimated Archon cannot be Persisted again after dying. Only swing with finality-counter Archon into boards where it will not die. The ETB already triggered — don't risk the permanent exile for a marginal attack trigger.
  7. Nihil Spellbomb timing: pop it before they cast Persist or Adapt, not after. If they tap 1B and cast Persist, the Archon is already in the yard being targeted. Pop Spellbomb immediately when they pass the turn with Persist mana and an Archon in yard. The moment to deny the Persist is before it's cast, not in response.
  8. Unearth returns MV 3 or less only — it does not return Archon or Troll. Unearth targets: Psychic Frog (MV 2), Emperor of Bones (MV 2), Harbinger of the Seas (MV 3). Persist targets: Archon (MV 8), Troll (MV 6), and all of the above. Don't confuse these — Unearth is for rebuilding your fair game, Persist is for the combo.
  9. Psychic Frog's flying ability requires exiling 3 graveyard cards. Those 3 cards are a finite resource that might also be Persist/Emperor targets. Only grant Frog flying when the combat damage drawn card and positional value is worth more than those 3 graveyard cards. Don't grant flying reflexively.
  10. Thought Scour mills any target player — use it on yourself to fill the yard. In nearly all cases, mill yourself. Milling Archon into your own yard sets up turn 3 Persist. The draw (blue) + mill self = free setup for the combo. Milling the opponent is situationally useful in the mirror to deny their resources, but defaulting to self-mill is almost always correct.
  11. Faithless Looting draws first, then discards. You cannot see your new cards and then decide to discard one of them from a "pre-Looting" perspective. You draw your two new cards, then make the discard decision with full updated information. Use this information advantage — if you drew into a second Persist, you might discard differently than if you drew two lands.
  12. Mystical Dispute costs {U} against blue spells — use it to protect Persist, not to deny cantrips. A {U} Dispute is your cheapest Persist protection. Don't waste it countering a Preordain or Thought Scour. Hold it for when your Persist is on the stack and they respond with Counterspell. That's the exchange that matters: your {U} defeats their {U}{U}.
  13. Harbinger of the Seas is a static effect — it doesn't destroy lands, it changes their type. When Harbinger enters, nonbasic lands immediately become Islands. They don't go to the graveyard. They don't get bounced. They just lose their nonbasic mana-producing abilities and become Islands. Harbinger dying removes the effect — their lands return to normal. Protect your Harbinger.
  14. Persist is a black sorcery — not a triggered ability, not a colorless spell. This means Consign to Memory cannot counter it. The only cards that stop Persist are: Counterspell, Force of Negation, Flare of Denial, Spell Pierce (if they can pay 1), and similar spell counters. Do not play around Consign when resolving Persist — it's irrelevant.
  15. Blood Moon requires {2}{R} — have a red source before deploying it. You play Blood Crypt and Steam Vents as your red sources. Fetch Blood Crypt before casting Blood Moon on turn 3. A Blood Moon you can't cast is a dead card. Check your mana carefully and ensure RR or equivalent before boarding it in.
  16. Emperor of Bones exiles from any graveyard — use it on their best threat too. In the mirror or against Prowess, Emperor can exile Archon or Murktide Regent from their graveyard and reanimate it on your side. Their best threats becoming your threats is the most efficient use of Emperor in those matchups.

Non-Negotiable Rules

Rule 01
Thoughtseize Before Persist
If their hand is unknown and they have UU available, Thoughtseize first. Strip the counter. Then resolve Persist into a clear hand. One Persist countered without a follow-up is close to a game loss.
Rule 02
Finality Counter = Permanent Exile
Emperor-reanimated creatures go to exile when they die. Never swing Emperor-reanimated Archon into lethal blocks unless the game is won regardless. The ETB already fired — the attack trigger is bonus, not mandatory.
Rule 03
Persist Counters Consign — Not Stoppped By It
Persist is a black sorcery. Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities and colorless spells. Persist is neither. Play Persist into Consign mana with full confidence. Only Counterspell, Force of Negation, and similar stop it.
Rule 04
Nihil Spellbomb — Pop Before Persist
Pop the Spellbomb on their upkeep or end step when Archon is in their yard. Don't wait for them to tap into Persist. The window to deny their return is before they cast it, not in response.
