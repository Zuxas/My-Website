Chapter 01

Identity &
Philosophy

"Don't just play more cards — play more turns." — Core Mantra

UW Blink is a tempo-control hybrid that weaponizes time. It is not all-in combo, and it is not pure control. It plays like disruptive midrange — stall, interact, then snowball with repeatable ETB value until inevitability takes over.

The deck's strength lies not in any one card, but in the synergy between cheap interaction and recursive ETBs. Every Ephemerate rebound, every Phelia trigger, every Starfield Shepherd warp opens multiple lines of play.

Core Axes

Axis 01
Tempo Denial
Phelia time-walks by blinking threats. Phantom destroys nonbasics. Consign buys entire turns. You are always reaching into the opponent's next turn and stealing it.
Axis 02
Flexible Interaction
Solitude, Prismatic Ending, and Wrath of the Skies answer almost everything in the format. The deck is never locked into a single answer type.
Axis 03
Resilient Engines
Guide + Ocelot generates inevitability. Riddler and Shepherd refill your hand. Once engines are live, the deck is very hard to exhaust.
Axis 04
Role Flexibility
Acts as control vs Aggro, tempo vs Combo, and proactive pressure vs Control. No other archetype in the format shifts roles this cleanly.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths
What You Do Well
High skill ceiling — reps compound.

Resilient to hate — pivots into fair midrange.

Powerful bullets that are tutorable.

Inevitability in every long game.
Weaknesses
What Beats You
Decision density punishes mistakes.

Very fast aggro (Burn, Prowess) on stumbles.

Torpor Orb / Void Mirror require full pivots.

Tournament clock — brisk play is required.

Who Should Play This

UW Blink rewards players who enjoy interactive, decision-rich games, who prefer tempo-control hybrids over linear decks, and who are willing to grind reps until sequencing is automatic. If you want every match to feel like a puzzle you're solving in real time — this is your deck.

Offensive Priorities

Your clock is 4–6 turns postboard. If you cannot present lethal in that window, you are playing into the opponent's plan. Never durdle when the kill is available.

Core Offensive Lines

  • Guide + Ocelot: T1 Guide → T2 Ocelot → lifegain triggers tokens → Ascend doubles every end step. Fastest go-wide kill.
  • Phantom + Ephemerate: T2 Phantom destroys land → Ephemerate → rebound → 3 lands by T3 vs greedy mana.
  • Phelia Attack Engine: Each attack is either removal, ETB value, or mana denial. Always ask "what turn am I stealing?"
  • Solitude Clock: A 3/2 lifelinker closes games on its own. Don't forget it's also a body.
  • Riddler Inevitability: Once hellbent, draws +1 every turn. With Ephemerate = Ancestral Recall with wings.

Defensive Priorities

Defense in UW Blink isn't about building a wall — it's about stealing enough time to transition into inevitability. Every defensive action must aim at reaching the point where Ocelot, Riddler, or Shepherd can carry.

"The goal of defense is not just to survive — it's to survive while building a path to pivot."

Defensive Turn Examples

  • Solitude + Ephemerate: Pitch Solitude → exile → blink → exile again → rebound exiles third threat. One card → 3-for-1 + 6–9 life.
  • Wrath of the Skies: Reset button. Don't cast for value — cast when behind to buy 2 rebuild turns.
  • Phelia as Time Walk: Attack → blink their Atraxa/Titan/walker. They lose that permanent's effect for the full turn cycle.
  • Guide + Ocelot Stabilizer: Cats chump + lifegain buys turns. Ascend online = 2+ blockers per turn.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T2 Solitude on Their Threat
On the play, T2 evoke Solitude to exile their key threat or T3 Restoration Angel sets up the blink loop. On the play you dictate tempo — evoke Solitude + Ephemerate on T2 converts your 2-card investment into a 5/5 body and 2 exile effects before they’ve developed. This tempo advantage compounds with Teferi’s sorcery lock on T3.
On the Draw
Hold Ephemerate Open
On the draw, your extra card gives you more Ephemerate / Solitude coverage. Hold Ephemerate mana open on their T2 and T3 to respond to their threats in real time. Against aggro on the draw: Solitude T1 evoke (pay white card) to stop their T1 deployment is the highest-priority play.

When Disrupted

Teferi Removed
Reactive Ephemerate Mode
Teferi removed: opponents interact at instant speed again. Switch to reactive: hold Ephemerate + Solitude mana open every turn. Deploy threats during their end step or in response to their attacks. Without Teferi’s lock, you play draw-go but your flash pieces still generate value without the sorcery-speed protection.
Solitude Answered
Angel + Riddler Rebuild
Solitude countered or removed: rebuild with Restoration Angel (blinks any non-Angel creature you control) and Quantum Riddler (draw on each blink). Chain Angel + creature ETBs to refill hand and rebuild board. Teferi’s +1 continues generating card draw. You can win entirely through Teferi + Angel value without ever resolving another Solitude.

Core Engines

"Every creature in this deck does something when it enters. Your job is to make it enter as many times as possible."
E1
Ephemerate Blink Loop
Primary engine — fires every ETB in the deck twice, then rebounds
Trigger 1
Cast Ephemerate
Target any creature you control. That creature is exiled and immediately returns — ETB fires. Cost: {W}. Every creature in the deck has an ETB worth firing.
Trigger 2
Rebound
Ephemerate exiles itself as it resolves if cast from hand. At your next upkeep, it casts itself for free — firing the ETB a second time. One {W} spell = two blink triggers across two turns.
Best Targets
Solitude · Phantom · Shepherd
Solitude: exile two threats for free. White Orchid Phantom: destroy two nonbasic lands. Starfield Shepherd: two Warp tutors. Every target doubles its effect.
E2
Phelia Attack Loop
Tempo engine — resets permanents on attack, returns at end step
On Attack
Exile Any Nonland Permanent
Phelia's trigger fires when she attacks — exile a nonland permanent you or your opponent controls. No mana required. This fires before damage, so no connection needed.
End of Turn
Permanent Returns
The exiled permanent returns at end of turn. Your creatures re-enter triggering ETBs again. Opponent's permanents "reset" — Planeswalkers lose activations made this turn.
Best Uses
Bounce Chalice · Reset Phantom · Loop Shepherd
Exile Chalice of the Void = restore your instant-speed interaction for a full turn. Exile your own Phantom = second land destruction trigger on re-entry. Exile own Shepherd = second Warp tutor.
E3
Guide of Souls Energy Loop
Resource engine — generates energy + lifegain, fuels Ocelot Pride cats
On Creature ETB
You Gain Life
Guide of Souls: when a creature enters the battlefield under your control, you gain 1 life. In a blink deck, this fires multiple times per turn cycle. Every Ephemerate target + every Phelia loop generates life.
Life Threshold
Ocelot Pride Triggers
Ocelot Pride creates a 1/1 Cat token whenever you gain life above your starting total. In a single Ephemerate turn (2 ETBs = 2 life), Pride generates 2 Cat tokens. A looping engine becomes a token engine.
Energy Sink
+2/+2 Flying Grant
Pay {E}{E}{E}: target attacking creature gets +2/+2 and flying until end of turn. Use on your largest attacker on the turn you need to close. Guide of Souls also gains {E} when it attacks — start banking energy by turn 2.
E4
Starfield Shepherd Warp Chain
Tutor engine — ETB searches any enchantment, fueled by Ephemerate rebound
On ETB
Warp Searches Any Enchantment
Starfield Shepherd's Warp keyword lets you cast it for {1}{W} and search your library for an enchantment, putting it into your hand. The ETB tutors for the enchantment you need most: Witch Enchanter vs artifacts, Rest in Peace vs GY, Ocelot Pride vs control.
With Ephemerate
Two Tutors for One Card
Ephemerate Shepherd on cast = tutor #1. Rebound next upkeep = Shepherd ETB #2 = tutor #2. For {W} + {1}{W} you get two enchantment searches across two turns. This makes your sideboard functionally redundant — you find what you need.
Priority Targets
Context-Dependent
G1: Witch Enchanter (answers Blood Moon), Ocelot Pride (clock). G2 vs combo: Rest in Peace. G2 vs creatures: Sanctifier en-Vec. G2 vs control: Quantum Riddler (draw engine). The tutor makes every SB enchantment accessible on turn 3.

Engine Priority Order

Priority 01
Evoke + Ephemerate ETB Loop
Evoke Solitude (exile white card) + Ephemerate (in response to sacrifice trigger) = 2 exile ETBs + a permanent 5/5. Ephemerate rebounds: second blink next upkeep = another exile. This loop is the highest-value engine and should be executed every time you have Solitude + Ephemerate + a relevant target.
Priority 02
Teferi + Sorcery Flash
Teferi, Time Raveler locks opponents to sorcery speed while +1 draws cards. His +1 also enables sorcery-speed flash for Restoration Angel and other creatures (not directly useful since Angel is already instant-speed, but applies to other sorceries). Each Teferi +1 is +1 card and the sorcery-speed lock continues. Protect him once resolved.
Priority 03
Quantum Riddler Blink Draw
Quantum Riddler draws a card each time you blink a permanent. With Ephemerate + Restoration Angel chains, Riddler generates 2-4 cards per turn. This card advantage engine makes your late game inevitably better than any fair opponent. Deploy Riddler once Teferi is in play to protect the blink chain.

Lines & Tactics

Lines &
Tactics

"Ephemerate’s rebound timing and Solitude evoke + blink windows define this deck’s power."

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Critical
Ephemerate Rebounds at Your Next Upkeep
Ephemerate exiles the creature, which returns at the beginning of the next end step. On your turn: exile + return at YOUR end step, then rebound copy at YOUR NEXT upkeep = exile + return again at your next end step. Total: 2 ETB blinks from one Ephemerate. On opponent’s turn (cast during their attack): exile + return at THEIR end step, rebound at YOUR upkeep = exile + return at your end step. Same 2 blinks, different timing windows.
Critical
Evoke + Ephemerate: Keep the Creature
Evoke Solitude: ETB trigger (exile target creature) and evoke sacrifice trigger both go on the stack. Ephemerate Solitude in response to the sacrifice trigger: Solitude exiles before being sacrificed. Sacrifice trigger resolves with no target. Solitude returns at end of step as a full 5/5. Ephemerate’s rebound blinks it again next upkeep — another exile ETB. Two free exile effects from Solitude + one Ephemerate. Always have Ephemerate ready when evoking.
Critical
Restoration Angel Cannot Blink Angels
Restoration Angel says target non-Angel creature you control. This restriction means: you cannot blink another Angel with it, and you cannot blink itself. In practice this rarely matters for UW Blink but know it so you don’t target an Angel creature incorrectly. All your value creatures (Eternal Witness, Wall of Omens, Recruiters) are non-Angel and are valid targets.

Timing Windows

When to Hold Mana vs Spend It
The core timing decision for this deck: every land drop and every spell cast is a commitment. Know before your upkeep which lines require open mana this turn and which don’t. Holding mana = passing priority; spending it = committing to a plan. Make this decision in your upkeep, not in response to their action.
When to Cast at Their End Step vs Your Main Phase
End-step casts maximize information: you’ve seen their full turn, they’re often tapped out. Main-phase casts maximize mana availability for follow-up. Know which of your key spells gain most from end-step timing and which need main-phase mana backup.

Role
Assignment

Before turn 1, decide your role. This is the single most important pre-game decision with UW Blink. Stick to your role — but reevaluate every two turns.

"By turn 2, you should know your role. If you wait until turn 4, it is often too late."
Control
You are the stabilizer. The opponent is faster and your job is to reach the late game where your inevitability takes over.
Aggro: Prowess · Zoo · Burn · Boros Energy
Tempo
Buy 1–2 turns with disruption, just enough to force a stumble, then race with your clock before they reset.
Combo: Reanimator · Belcher · Neoform · Titan
Beatdown
You are the aggressor. Opponent is slower but more powerful long-term. Get under them and close before they stabilize.
Control: Azorius · Esper Blink Mirror · Tron

How to Identify Your Role

Question 01
Are they faster?
If yes → you are Control. Stabilize first, then pivot. Every life point matters until you have an engine online.
Question 02
Are they more powerful long-term?
If yes → you are Beatdown. Jam Ocelot/Phantom aggressively. Use Ephemerate offensively to push damage.
Question 03
Neither?
You are Tempo. Buy one critical turn with Consign, Phelia, or Phantom — then race with your modest clock before they rebuild.

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • Misassigning the role. Treating yourself as Control vs Azorius Control — you'll lose. You have inevitability only if you pressure them early.
  • Refusing to pivot. Against Zoo, staying Control after you've stabilized. They'll burn you out. Flip into Beatdown once you have footing.
  • Splitting the difference. Playing Phantom T2 against Belcher and holding Consign, leaving you mana-pinched. Neither pressured nor disrupted.
  • Over-durdling. Looping Shepherd without developing pressure vs combo. You are not winning by accruing value — you're losing by not clocking them.

Role-Switching Signals

Reevaluate your role every two turns. Look for these triggers:

Shift to Beatdown
You're now the aggressor when:
You've stabilized vs aggro at 10+ life. You've disrupted combo once and have 4 turns of clock available. They're hellbent and rebuilding slowly.
Stay Control
Hold the line when:
Life total is still in danger. They have payoffs in hand (Titan, Atraxa) you haven't answered. You don't have a clean clock established yet.

Lines & Tactics

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Blink wins on triggered ability sequencing. Every ETB is a decision point."
Ephemerate Rebound — Only From Hand, Not From Other Zones
Ephemerate's Rebound says "if you cast this spell from your hand, exile it as it resolves. At the beginning of your next upkeep, you may cast this card from exile without paying its mana cost." The critical qualifier: cast FROM HAND. If Ephemerate is cast from any other zone — Snapcaster Mage flashback, Eternal Witness recursion, any other zone — it does NOT rebound. Each copy of Ephemerate you cast from hand will rebound once. Never waste a hand Ephemerate on a low-value target when you have a Snapcaster copy available for that purpose.
Solitude Evoke — Ward Triggers After Cast, Before Resolution
When you evoke Solitude targeting a creature with Ward, the Ward trigger fires as Solitude is being cast (Ward says "whenever this becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls"). The Ward trigger goes on the stack above Solitude. If the Ward cost isn't paid, Solitude is countered — it never resolves, and since Solitude was cast via evoke, the creature is sacrificed anyway (evoke sacrifice is a state-based action on resolution, but if Solitude is countered it doesn't resolve or sacrifice). Ward protection against evoke Solitude: they must pay the Ward cost or Solitude whiffs and they've lost nothing but the pitch card.
Stoneforge Mystic — Equip at Instant Speed During Opponent's Turn
Stoneforge Mystic's second ability: "{1}{W}, {T}: You may put an Equipment card from your hand onto the battlefield. If you do, attach it to a creature you control." This is an activated ability that can be used at instant speed at ANY time — including during your opponent's combat. Putting a Batterskull or Kaldra Compleat into play with a Germ token attached during their declare attackers step forces them to recalculate blocks and attack math with no warning. The key: SFM's ability doesn't have "sorcery speed only" or "main phase" restrictions. Use it as a combat trick, not just as a development step.
Consign to Memory — Counters ETB Triggers, Not the Creature
Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities (look for "when," "whenever," "at the beginning of") and colorless spells. When a creature with an ETB trigger enters the battlefield, its ETB trigger is a triggered ability — Consign counters it. But the CREATURE itself still entered and is on the battlefield. Consign on Archon of Cruelty's ETB: Archon is still a 8/8 flying lifelink on the board, you just prevented the sacrifice/discard/3 life trigger. Consign on Thought-Knot Seer's ETB: TKS is still a 4/4 on their board, but you kept your hand. The creature always stays — Consign only handles the trigger.
Force of Negation — Only on the Opponent's Turn, Noncreature Only
Force of Negation has two restrictions: (1) its alternative cost ("exile a blue card from your hand rather than pay this spell's mana cost") only works "during an opponent's turn" — on your own turn you must pay {1}{U}{U} normally, (2) Force of Negation can only counter noncreature spells. It cannot counter a creature being cast. This means: FoN cannot protect your creatures on your own turn for free, cannot stop an opponent's creature spell at any time, and cannot be used as free interaction during your own combat tricks. FoN is strictly for countering noncreature spells (instants, sorceries, planeswalkers, artifacts, enchantments) during the opponent's turn only.
Batterskull Germ Token — Bouncing Skull Returns Toughness, Not the Germ
Batterskull creates a 0/0 Germ token that gets equipped with Batterskull (becoming a 4/4 lifelink vigilance). If the Germ dies, Batterskull remains on the battlefield. Batterskull's built-in ability: "{3}: Return Batterskull to its owner's hand" returns the equipment card only — the Germ token is already dead, not returned. When Batterskull is re-put onto the battlefield via Stoneforge Mystic, a NEW Germ token is created and immediately gets the equipment. This bounce-and-redeploy is the correct line when opponents are trying to exile the Germ: return Skull to hand before it dies, re-enter with a fresh Germ.

Chapter 08

Master
Heuristics

"Blink isn't just a deck. It's a way of playing Magic."

Ephemerate Mastery

  • Jam when it flips the tempo of the turn cycle. Hold it when it only provides incremental value. Never fire Ephemerate first in a stack war.
  • Rebound is delayed inevitability. Opponents must always respect the second trigger. Even representing Ephemerate reshapes their sequencing.
  • Ephemerate as counterbait. Blinking Guide into open mana draws their Counterspell — then Riddler resolves cleanly next turn.
  • Solitude + Ephemerate = 3-for-1 minimum. Pitch → blink → rebound. Three removals, 6–9 life, and a 3/2 lifelinker.
  • Don't Ephemerate first in stack wars. Whoever blinks last usually wins the entire exchange.

Phelia & Phantom Lines

  • Phelia is Time Walk before blink engine. Use on their permanents first. Ask: "What turn am I stealing by blinking this?"
  • Phantom is land denial before damage. A 2/2 flying first striker that destroys nonbasics — the damage is secondary.
  • Together they create hard locks. Phelia attack → blink Phantom → destroy 2 lands in one combat step. With Ephemerate: 4 lands in 2 turns.
  • Phantom is weaker on the draw. On the play it's game-winning vs greedy mana. On the draw, it's often too slow to matter vs fast combo.
  • Denial > value every time. Don't blink your own Shepherd when you could blink their Atraxa.

Sequencing Principles

  • Identify your role before casting your first spell. Control, Tempo, or Beatdown — this decision shapes every sequence.
  • By turn 2, your plan should be set. Phantom on 2 = you're tempo. Ocelot = you're beatdown. Holding Consign = you're disruption-control.
  • Commit to one axis. Don't half-develop. If it's an Ocelot game, protect Guide and Ocelot. Don't scatter plays into Shepherd and Riddler.
  • Count real turns to lethal. If it's 7+, find a way to shorten it. If it's 4–6, stop durdling and close.
  • Stagger threats vs control. Never give Verdict free 3-for-1s. One or two threats at a time forces them to react awkwardly.

Playing Through Hate

  • Pivot to fair midrange when hate sticks. Ocelot tokens, Shepherd fliers, and Solitude beats win through Torpor Orb, Void Mirror, and Blood Moon.
  • Exploit overboarding. More hate = fewer win conditions. When they jam Chalice + Void + Orb, their clock disappears. Punish with Ocelot pressure.
  • Fetch basics early against Moon decks. Phantom can snipe red sources to prevent Blood Moon entirely.
  • Diversify disruption. Mistcaller + Consign overwhelms their one-for-one answers. Don't mulligan to oblivion for one perfect card.

Closing Games

  • Stop durdling when lethal is 2–3 turns away. The Durdle Trap kills games. Recognize the window and step through it.
  • After disrupting once, go immediately to kill mode. Don't give Reanimator a second Atraxa. Don't give Titan a rebuild turn.
  • Respect the round clock. 1–0–1 is worse than 2–1. Time management is a skill as real as sequencing.
  • Solitude + Ocelot = your fastest kills. Don't over-engineer. These two cards close games more often than any Shepherd loop.

Tuning

"Every flex slot in UW Blink trades off one axis for another. Know what you're cutting before you cut it."
Quantum Riddler: 2 vs 3 vs 4
The Draw Engine Count
Most lists run 3. At 4: more consistent draw engine, but Riddler at Warp is slow against fast aggro and clogs blink lines. At 2: leaner, more room for interaction or Mockingbird, but you see it less in grind games. Run 4 if your meta is heavy on control or mirror. Cut to 2 if Prowess or Burn is above 15% of the room — their clock is faster than Riddler can generate value.
Mockingbird: 0 vs 1 vs 2
The Mirror / Flex Creature
The 1-copy is standard. Mockingbird at X copies any creature with MV ≤ X — at 2 mana it's your best mirror tool (copies their Phelia or Riddler for immediate ETB value). Cut to 0 against aggro-heavy metas where you need more interaction. Go to 2 in mirror-heavy tournament environments. Never go above 2 — dilutes your main engine density.
Consign to Memory: 0 vs 1 main
The Main Deck Counter
The current shell runs 1 main + 3 SB. The 1 main copy is primarily for triggered ability counters you need in G1 — Archon ETB in Glockulous, Pact upkeep trigger vs Titan. Cut the main copy if your expected field has no combo. Keep it if you expect Glockulous, Neoform, or Amulet Titan above 10% combined. The 3 SB copies are non-negotiable regardless.
Gemstone Caverns: SB vs Main
The Speed Caverns
Currently 1 SB copy. Caverns enters with a luck counter if it's in your opening hand (exile any other card) — you gain a free mana on turn 1 on the draw. Board in vs any combo matchup where being "on the play" for the first spell matters — Neoform, Glockulous, Storm. Consider main decking if your local meta is more than 30% combo. Never go above 1.
Sanctifier en-Vec: 1 main vs 0 main
Flex Hate Bear Slot
Currently 1 main. Sanctifier has protection from black and red (can't be targeted/blocked/damaged by either). In a meta with Boros Energy and Prowess it's a free threat that turns off their removal suite. Cut it to 0 main in control-heavy metas where it does nothing G1. Never cut the 2 SB copies — they're mandatory for aggro matchups.
Wrath of the Skies: 0 vs 2 SB
The Board Wipe Count
Currently 2 SB. X-cost sweeper that hits creatures with power ≤ X. At X=3 it clears most boards without hitting your own Kappa or large Constructs (but does hit your Phelia/Guide). Consider going 1 SB if your matchup spread has little aggro. Go 3 SB if token-wide decks like Boros Energy or Jeskai Blink are prevalent — the extra copy improves your ability to find it in G2 without Shepherd.

Prep

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: If the hand doesn't clock by T4 or disrupt by T3, it's a mull. Six with Solitude + Ephemerate beats seven without.
  • Role rule: Before T1, decide: Control, Tempo, or Beatdown. Reevaluate every two turns.
  • Combo rule: Mulligan for disruption + clock. Don't keep value hands vs Reanimator or Belcher.
  • Ephemerate rule: Never fire first in a stack war. Hold for Solitude vs payoffs. Rebound is the real half.
  • Phelia rule: Blink their permanents before yours. She's a Time Walk, not a value engine.
  • Clock rule: Count turns to lethal. If it's 7+, accelerate. Stop durdling when it's 3.
  • Sideboard rule: Never bring in more than 7 cards. Mockingbird out first. Double-check Needle targets.

  • Register decklist carefully. Double-check SB counts and spellings. Needle targets must match what you've playtested.

  • Between rounds: breathe. Don't tunnel on mistakes. The next round starts at 0–0.
  • End of Day 1: hydrate, review your notes, reset mentally for Day 2.
  • Treat it like athletics. Body and mind both need to be ready for 12+ rounds of complex sequencing.
