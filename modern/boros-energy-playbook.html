Chapter 01

Identity &
Philosophy

"Pressure compounds. Every turn they don't die is a turn you hand them for free." — Core Mantra

Boros Energy is an aggressive midrange deck that wins by front-loading pressure and converting early tempo into board-state inevitability. It is not a pure aggro deck — it has real card advantage engines in Guide of Souls, Ocelot Pride, and Ragavan. It is not a midrange deck in the traditional sense — it cannot afford to durdle. It occupies the exact space between those two poles: the deck that is always the beatdown but has the tools to grind when the game goes long.

The deck has a deceptive skill ceiling. A new pilot can go 6-3 with it. Zuxas goes 17 Top 8s on it across RC season. That gap lives in Galvanic Discharge energy math, Goblin Bombardment reach lines, Ajani sequencing, and — most importantly — the rare moments when you correctly identify that Boros is not the beatdown and adjust accordingly.

Joe Leo's rule: If you ever think you need to splash a third color, that is a signal to play a different deck — not a reason to add a color. The power of this deck is its consistent mana base and its ability to keep all of its pieces live in every game. Diluting that is how you lose to yourself.

Core Engines

Engine 01
The Aggression Package
Ragavan generates mana and cards from the first swing. Ocelot Pride builds a scaling cat army that dwarfs the opponent's board by turn 4. Guide of Souls converts that army into energy and life. These three operate as a system: Ragavan opens the door, Pride fills it with threats, Guide converts those threats into resources that fund removal.
Engine 02
Galvanic Discharge System
Discharge scales with energy: 2 energy kills a 2-toughness creature, 4+ kills almost anything in the format. Guide of Souls and Ragavan fund energy passively. The key skill is knowing your full energy pool before spending: Discharge gives you 3 energy on cast, then you deal damage equal to however much you spend. Floor is always 3. With 3 stored you deal 6. Count stored energy + 3 before casting.
Engine 03
Goblin Bombardment Reach
Bombardment converts your board into burn. With 4 cats in play, that's 4 direct damage you can deploy at instant speed before combat is even declared. Opponents constantly miscalculate their safe life total. The highest skill expression in this deck is calculating Bombardment lethal before every combat step — every single turn.
Engine 04
Phlage as the Pivot
Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury is the late-game inevitability engine. Cast it early for tempo — 6/6 flash — or escape it for the full engine. Once escaped, Phlage recurs every turn, generates energy, and provides reach. Getting to 6 mana to escape Phlage is a priority that overrides other sequencing decisions in long games.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths
What You Do Well
Consistent — rarely loses to mana. The 21-land base with two-drops means you're never flooded or screwed.

Multi-axis pressure — Bombardment reach + combat damage + Phlage escape means opponents can never "stabilize" in the traditional sense.

Punishes greedy manabases hard — Blood Moon out of the board shuts down Blink, Titan, Tron, Domain.

17 Top 8s on this build means the lines are documented and proven.
Weaknesses
What Beats You
Lava Dart kills your best one-drops — Prowess is a bad matchup because of this.

Supreme Verdict and Wrath of the Skies can reset a developed board.

Sanctifier en-Vec shuts off Galvanic Discharge — must answer immediately.

Very fast combo (Neoform, Belcher) can kill before you establish pressure.

Who Should Play This

Boros Energy rewards pilots who enjoy proactive, pressure-forward game plans, who want a deck where every turn has a clear correct line, and who are willing to do meticulous combat and reach math every game. If you want to goldfish consistently and make your opponents react to you — this is your deck.

Key Card Notes

Why 4 Ajani
Ajani, Nacatl Pariah
Now a full 4-of. The legend rule means you can only have one in play but running 4 copies maximizes seeing it by turn 2 — the earliest and most impactful deployment. The flip is what you're playing for: Ajani Scattered to the Winds is a game-ender with 3+ cats in play. At 4 cats, a single activation can represent 16+ power on board. Seasoned Pyromancer fills the role of discarding redundant Ajanis profitably.
Why 3 Goblin Bombardment
Goblin Bombardment
You want to draw exactly one per game. Three is the right number — seeing it by turn 3 reliably but not flooding on it. Once Bombardment is down, every creature you deploy has implicit direct damage value. Changes how opponents block, trade, and sequence their answers.
The Pivot Card
Phlage
The most underutilized card by newer pilots. Phlage cast as a 6/6 flash on turn 4 is already a strong play. Phlage escaped on turn 6 is a game-ending engine. Know both modes. The escape cost of 3 cards + 5RW sounds expensive but the energy generation and recurring pressure it provides is worth building toward.
The Card Advantage Engine
Seasoned Pyromancer
When Seasoned Pyromancer enters, discard up to two cards then draw that many. Each discarded card creates a 1/1 Human token. This is the deck's primary refueling engine — pitch an unneeded land or a Phlage you can't escape yet, draw live cards, and get blockers/attackers simultaneously. When it dies, exile it to create two more 1/1 tokens. Four copies means this deck can outgrind midrange and control that expect a pure aggro plan.
Chapter 03

The Game Plan

"Every attack you skip is a turn you hand them. Every Bombardment line you miss is a game you don't close." — Execution Principle

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turn 1
Lead Pressure
Ragavan on turn 1 is the ideal start — attack every subsequent turn. If no Ragavan, Ocelot Pride or Guide of Souls. Do not hold a one-drop "for protection." Deploy it. The value compounds. Even a Pride with no immediate trigger is applying pressure and threatening to snowball.
Turn 2–3
Layer Threats
Add Guide of Souls or a second Ocelot Pride. Keep attacking with everything. Use Galvanic Discharge to clear blockers — check energy before spending it. Never let them stabilize behind a single blocker for free. If Goblin Bombardment is in hand, resolve it now — it changes the math on every future combat.
Turn 3–4
Generate Inevitability
Ocelot Pride should be generating 2+ tokens by now. Guide of Souls is gaining 1 life and 1 energy per creature ETB — and can spend 3 energy when attacking to put two +1/+1 counters and a flying counter on any attacking creature, making it an Angel. That pump changes combat math dramatically mid-combat. Ajani at 3 mana flips cleanly on turn 4 if you attacked on 2 and 3. This is when the Bombardment reach calculation becomes critical — count your damage every turn from here out.
Turn 4–5
Ajani Closes
A single Ajani flip with 3 cats = each cat gets +3/+3 and creates another cat. From 3 cats you suddenly have 6 attacking for 18+. Do not cast Ajani into open Wrath mana — wait one turn. Either they tap out first, or you attack twice more and have Bombardment reach anyway. Never lose a game because you rushed Ajani.

Critical Decision Points

Galvanic Discharge Timing

Discharge scales with energy. At 2 energy it kills a 2-toughness creature. At 4 it kills a 4-toughness creature. At 6+ it becomes a flexible answer to almost everything in the format. The correct question before spending Discharge is always: what is the highest-value target I might need this for in the next two turns? Do not spend it on a 1/1 token when a Solitude or Guide of Souls is coming. Do not sandbag it forever — a card in hand that isn't spent is a card that didn't win you the game.

Goblin Bombardment Math

Before every combat step, count: how many creatures do you have × 1 damage each via Bombardment. Then ask: is your opponent at or near that number? If yes — attack first, let them block, then sac everything that didn't matter to the combat. Converting your last three cats into 3 face damage through a wall of blockers wins games opponents thought were safe. The math takes 3 seconds. Do it every turn, not just when you're desperate.

Ragavan Attacks

Ragavan attacks every turn it's alive unless your life total in a race demands blocking. The gold generation compounds — even connecting once is often a one-mana advantage for the rest of the game. The stolen spells range from irrelevant to format-warping. Do not voluntarily "protect" Ragavan by holding it back. If they kill it, you develop the next threat. The attack is always worth it.

Phlage Escape Priority

Once you have 6 mana available, escaping Phlage should be your primary consideration. Escaped Phlage creates an energy-generating recurring threat that most decks cannot effectively answer. In games that go long — against Control, Blink, or any attrition-based opponent — the difference between winning and losing often comes down to whether you sequenced toward Phlage escape or not.

Mulligan Philosophy

Snap keep: Any hand with Ragavan or Ocelot Pride + 2 lands + at least one removal or interaction spell. You have a one-drop, you have mana, you have a plan.

Snap mulligan: No one-drop, no meaningful pressure on turns 1–2, or more than 4 lands. Also mulligan any hand that is purely defensive with no threat — you are never the control player in game 1.

Borderline: Two-drop heavy hands without a one-drop are keepable on the play, questionable on the draw. Apply one additional card of scrutiny for each additional card of information the opponent has on you.

On the draw: Be one turn more defensive than on the play. Do not commit your full hand to the board on turn 2 if they could Wrath of the Skies at X=2 on turn 3. Apply pressure but stagger slightly.

Sequencing Priority Order

  • Priority 1Ragavan on turn 1 into a clear board. This is always correct. The mana, card, and pressure advantage it generates cascades into every subsequent turn.
  • Priority 2Goblin Bombardment on turn 2 if you're already applying pressure. Getting this down early means every subsequent creature has implicit damage value.
  • Priority 3Answer their trump before deploying your own. Sanctifier en-Vec shuts off Discharge. Consign to Memory prevents Persist reanimates. Clear the blocker before running your engine into it.
  • Priority 4Stagger threats into open Wrath mana. Against Blink and Control, lead with one threat at a time if they're holding up 3+ mana. Don't give them a 2-for-1 with a sweeper.
  • Priority 5Escape Phlage when 6 mana is available and you're in a grind. This overrides "keep developing the board" if the game has gone long enough that inevitability matters more than tempo.
Chapter 04

Core
Engines

Three engines drive every Boros Energy win. Know the setup, the execution, and the result cold — great pilots run all three simultaneously.

A
Ocelot Pride Token Engine
Every noncreature spell is a free 1/1. Ajani turns them into an army.
Setup
Ocelot Pride on T1
Cast Ocelot Pride on turn 1. Every noncreature spell you cast while Pride is in play creates a 1/1 white Cat token. Ideal curve: Pride T1, two noncreature spells T2, one more T3 = three free 1/1s before your opponent has stabilized. Each cat is also a free attacker that triggers Guide of Souls energy generation on the attack step.
Execution
Tokens + Anthem
Cats accumulate while you play your normal game plan. Ajani, Nacatl Pariah's second chapter gives all your creatures +1/+1, converting a board of 5 cats plus your other creatures into a lethal swing. Token count also doubles as sacrifice fodder for Goblin Bombardment when the board stalls behind blockers.
Result
Overwhelming Width
Against decks without sweepers, Pride on T1 generates more creatures than they can answer one-for-one. Against sweepers: Bombardment before the wipe resolves converts the entire board into direct damage. A Pride + Bombardment hand beats virtually every creature-based strategy in the format.
B
Energy Accumulation + Galvanic Discharge
Patience scales Discharge into a removal spell and a reach weapon simultaneously.
Setup
Passive Energy Gain
Guide of Souls generates energy when creatures with different power values attack together — this happens automatically every attack step with a diverse board. Thraben Charm generates energy when you flash it in at instant speed and holds up interaction. Energy accumulates across turns and does not reset — it is a patience resource.
Execution
Fire at the Right Threshold
Galvanic Discharge deals X damage equal to energy paid. At 3 energy: kills Teferi TRav (4 loyalty — wait, Discharge targets planeswalkers for loyalty damage, which works differently — it deals damage equal to energy spent). At 5+ energy: kills large threats and hits face meaningfully. Hold Discharge for their key threat rather than spending early on irrelevant targets. The ceiling scales with patience.
Result
Flexible Removal + Reach
Unlike fixed-damage burn, Discharge scales. A late-game Discharge with 8+ energy kills any creature in the format and represents meaningful face damage from nowhere. This reach is what closes games against opponents who think they've survived your creature pressure — they stabilize the board and die to Discharge accumulation.
C
Goblin Bombardment Reach Engine
Sacrifice converts a blocked board into unstoppable damage. Hexproof, protection, and indestructible are irrelevant.
Setup
Bombardment + Any Creature
Goblin Bombardment: sacrifice a creature, deal 1 damage to any target. No mana cost to activate — just a sacrifice. With 10 tokens and Bombardment, that is 10 damage to any combination of targets at instant speed for zero mana. The only requirement is having the enchantment in play and creatures to sacrifice.
Execution
Close Through Any Defense
Sacrifice is not targeting. Protection from red and white, hexproof, and shroud do not prevent Bombardment damage. Scion of Draco has hexproof — Bombardment pings it to death anyway. Teferi, Hero of Dominaria at 10 loyalty — sacrifice 10 cats. Dead. Their blocker wall makes combat impossible — sacrifice 15 tokens for 15 face damage. None of their defenses apply.
Result
Wins Stalled Games
Games that appear lost — opponent at 20 life, full blocker wall, large planeswalker — convert into wins when Bombardment is active. Calculate total creature count times 1 damage. At 15+ creatures, Bombardment represents lethal from a completely blocked board. This is why opponents make suboptimal plays trying to answer Bombardment even when it is not yet in play.

Lines & Tricks

  • StackSacrifice to Bombardment in response to removal targeting your creature: They cast Fatal Push targeting your Ocelot Pride. In response, sacrifice Pride to Goblin Bombardment — deal 1 damage to their face. Pride was being destroyed anyway. You convert their removal spell into your advantage and they spent a card for nothing.
  • SequencePing planeswalkers to zero with Bombardment before combat: Teferi, Hero of Dominaria resolves at 4 loyalty, they activate to 5. In your main phase before attacking, sacrifice 5 tokens to Bombardment targeting Teferi. Teferi dies. No combat math required, no targeting effects relevant, no way to respond without holding instant-speed interaction specifically for the sacrifices.
  • SequenceSacrifice the board before Supreme Verdict resolves: They cast Supreme Verdict (cannot be countered). In response, sacrifice your entire board to Goblin Bombardment. Verdict resolves and destroys nothing. Your creatures were going to die anyway — you convert the wipe into 10-15 direct damage. This is often the correct play even if you survive the Verdict.
  • CombatTrack Ajani's flip condition: Ajani, Nacatl Pariah transforms into Ajani, Nacatl Avenger when a nontoken cat you control dies. When a cat dies in combat or to removal, Ajani flips at end of turn — you get a free +2/+2 counter on a creature and a new cat token. Plan combat around triggering this flip on the turn you need the counter most.
  • AvoidDon't sacrifice your last blocker to Bombardment if they have lethal on board: If they have creatures that deal lethal combat damage next turn and you sacrifice your last blocker to ping for 1, you die to combat and deal 1 for nothing. Calculate: does the 1 damage from the sacrifice reduce their life total to 0 or less? If not, keep the blocker.
Chapter 05

Role Assignment

Before the first spell is cast, know whether you are the beatdown or the control. Getting this wrong costs games. Boros Energy is almost always the beatdown — but there are specific matchups and game states where you must pivot, and failing to recognize those moments is how you lose winnable games.

Beatdown
Default role. Apply relentless pressure. Every attack matters. Do not slow down to play around potential answers — force them to have it. Time is always on the opponent's side if you stop pressing. Any hesitation is a gift.
vs: Control · Midrange · Combo · UW Blink · Grixis Reanimator
Tempo Control
Rare, but critical to recognize. In matchups where the opponent is faster or your life total is under pressure, use removal to buy time and don't race their burn. Stabilize first, then pivot to closing once you have the board.
vs: Izzet Prowess · Burn · Very fast aggro racing your life total
Read the Mirror
The mirror is about Goblin Bombardment and combat math, not raw aggression. Whoever resolves Bombardment and converts tokens into reach most efficiently wins the close games. Don't just attack blindly.
vs: Boros Energy Mirror · Energy-based creature mirrors

How to Identify Your Role

Question 01
Are They Faster?
If yes → you are Tempo Control. Stabilize first. Use Discharge and Wrath of the Skies to buy time. Do not race when you cannot win the race. Every life point matters until you have a stable board.
Question 02
Are They More Powerful Long-Term?
If yes → you are Beatdown. Press aggressively. Use Bombardment reach math every turn. Don't give them the turns they need to develop their engine. Close before they get to full power.
Question 03
Is the Board Symmetrical?
Mirror situations. Identify the asymmetries — who has Bombardment, who has more pump, whose tokens are bigger. Then make the role decision based on those asymmetries, not on raw position.

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • Treating yourself as control vs. Control. Against UW Control, you are the beatdown. They have inevitability — Teferi, Snapcaster, Verdict loops. You must close before that comes online. Aggressive hands beat passive hands in this matchup.
  • Racing Prowess without the life buffer. When they're on Izzet Prowess and your life total is at 8, switch to control mode. Use Discharge proactively. Do not race their burn — you cannot win that race without Phlage online.
  • Over-committing into Wrath mana. Against Blink, if they have 4+ mana open and you have 3 threats in hand, deploy one. Force them to react. Don't give them a 3-for-1 by flooding the board into a Wrath of the Skies at X=3.
  • Under-committing when they're hellbent. The mirror mistake in reverse. If your opponent has spent their hand and you have three creatures and a Bombardment, you are the beatdown now. Stop holding cards — finish the game.

Role-Switching Signals

Shift to Beatdown
You're the aggressor when:
You've stabilized against aggro at 10+ life. They've used their key disruption pieces. They're on a slow draw. Your board is bigger than theirs and they need to find specific answers to beat you.
Stay Tempo Control
Hold the line when:
Life total is below 8. They have multiple creatures threatening your life. They have untapped mana for burn. You haven't stabilized the board — a dead Ragavan and two tokens is not a stable board.
Close Mode
Transition to pure close when:
They're hellbent. Bombardment + tokens = lethal in 1–2 turns. Phlage has escaped. Any further development is slower than just attacking and sacrificing everything. Count the lethal, then execute.

Grixis Reanimator — Why You Win

Their interaction doesn't touch your board. Persist at instant speed doesn't block Ragavan. Thoughtseize takes one card but doesn't kill a creature. Archon of Cruelty entering is the only real threat — a 7-point swing — but if you've established a Bombardment and 4 cats, you can still close through it. The game plan is to apply pressure early, force them to tap out answering threats, then either close before Archon lands or maintain enough reach to win through the ETB.

G1 is very strong for you. They have no dedicated hate for Boros and you're faster than their combo clock. The key is not giving them a clean Persist window — apply pressure so they have to tap out attacking instead of holding up mana for instant-speed reanimates.

UW / Jeskai Blink — The Dynamic

Blink's core disruption is Solitude + Ephemerate — they can answer two threats for the cost of one card. Your answer is to apply pressure before Phantom lands and denies you land drops, and to use Goblin Bombardment so your creatures have reach value even when they're answered. The matchup is decided by who has the better turn 3: you developing Ajani or them deploying White Orchid Phantom.

Post-Phantom, the matchup shifts. If they land Phantom and you lose a land, you may need to slow your development. But Bombardment tokens are indestructible to Wrath of the Skies — use this. Build a token army, get Bombardment online, and convert those tokens into reach damage that their interaction can't touch.

Key Threats to Respect

Threat 01
White Orchid Phantom
Denies a land drop on entry and they blink it repeatedly. If Phantom costs you a land, adjust by playing around their mana rather than developing into it. Don't panic — you still have your existing lands, just count on one fewer.
Threat 02
Solitude + Ephemerate
Answers two creatures for one card. Stagger your threats — don't deploy two creatures in the same turn if they have 2+ mana up. Force them to choose which threat to answer, then deploy the other one next turn.
Threat 03
Ocelot Pride (Theirs)
Their Ocelot Pride generates tokens that outpace your reach. Answer it immediately with Celestial Purge (postboard) or Galvanic Discharge. An unchecked Pride on their side is a faster clock than yours and generates lifegain from Blink triggers.
Threat 04
Wrath of the Skies
Their sweeper. It kills your whole board at X=2-3. Never overcommit — deploy one threat at a time when they have 4+ mana open. Post-board Wrath of the Skies from your own side boards in to clear their token army.

They may have Solitude T2 via pitch. Keep hands with 2+ threats so a pitch Solitude doesn't blank your whole turn. On the draw you're slightly behind on tempo — lean on Bombardment reach over combat damage to close.

Izzet Prowess — The Problem

Prowess is the hardest matchup in the format for Boros. They are faster on damage, Lava Dart kills your best one-drops at zero mana, and Cori-Steel Cutter generates a 1/1 Monk with prowess on their second spell each turn — they can attach Cutter to it immediately, giving it +1/+1, trample, and haste, so it attacks the same turn it enters. Your Ocelot Pride tokens die to Dart for free. Your Ragavan dies to Dart. The matchup demands you respect their burn clock, use Discharge proactively rather than defensively, and bring in the spell-chain hate.

The correct adjustment is to treat this like a Burn matchup. Every life point matters. You need Galvanic Discharge to kill their threats early rather than holding it for value. Wrath of the Skies at X=1 is a tempo play — it clears Monks and Swiftspears cleanly and stops the damage clock. Deafening Silence and High Noon postboard are backbreaking — they literally cannot function without chaining spells.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
Go Under Dart Range
Deploy threats that dodge Lava Dart: Guide of Souls is 2 toughness and survives. Phlage is out of Dart range. Focus on guide + Phlage and use discharge early to kill their prowess threats before they connect.
On the Draw
Mulligan Harder
Prioritize hands with Guide of Souls, Phlage, and Discharge. Ragavan is a liability here — they will Dart it freely and you fall behind. On the draw, hands without answers to their T1 are mulligans.

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Boros wins on incremental edges. Every rules interaction you know precisely is a turn of damage they don't expect."
Galvanic Discharge — Energy Generated on Cast, Not Resolution
Galvanic Discharge says "this spell deals X damage, where X is 1 plus the number of energy counters you pay." When you cast it, you immediately generate 3 energy ({E}{E}{E}) as a triggered ability — then you decide how much energy to pay to scale the damage. This means you can pay 0 energy and still deal 1 damage. Even with 0 energy banked when you cast it, the 3 generated on cast let you immediately pay for a 4-damage Discharge in the same spell window. Important: the energy generation is a triggered ability on cast — under Stony Silence or Consign to Memory, that trigger could be countered, but Stony Silence doesn't affect instant/sorcery spells.
Goblin Bombardment — Sacrifice Doesn't Target
Goblin Bombardment's ability reads "sacrifice a creature: deal 1 damage to any target." The sacrifice is a COST, not a targeted effect — it happens immediately when you activate the ability, before any responses. The "any target" is what you're dealing damage to. This means: hexproof doesn't protect against Bombardment (you're targeting the player or creature for 1 damage, which does use targeting — so hexproof from opponent's spells DOES protect the creature). But the sacrifice itself can't be responded to with removal — by the time anyone can respond, the creature is already sacrificed.
Voice of Victory — Protection from Multicolored Is Not Hexproof
Voice of Victory has "protection from multicolored." Protection means: can't be damaged, blocked, enchanted, equipped, or targeted by anything multicolored. A multicolored spell like Prismatic Ending (white) — wait, Prismatic Ending is only white, so it IS a valid target. Counterspell is only blue — can target Voice. Fatal Push is only black — can target. Scion of Draco (multicolored) can't block Voice and Scion's triggered multicolored abilities can't target it. Key: protection only covers the exact color identity, not all interaction.
Blood Moon — Nonbasics Become Mountains, Mana Abilities Apply Immediately
Blood Moon says "nonbasic lands are Mountains." When it resolves, all nonbasic lands immediately lose their other types and abilities, becoming basic Mountains (they produce {R}). Your own Inspiring Vantage, Sacred Foundry, and Arena of Glory all become Mountains under Blood Moon — except your basic Plains and Islands. Plan your mana sequence: fetch your basic lands before Blood Moon lands. Note: Flagstones of Trokair is nonbasic — it becomes a Mountain under Blood Moon and loses its trigger. Arena of Glory becomes a Mountain but keeps its land type "Mountain" — not relevant since Mountains already produce {R}.
Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury — Escape Enters with Haste
Phlage's escape clause gives it haste when it escapes from the graveyard. Escape costs {2}{R}{W} plus exile 5 other cards from your graveyard. The 5 cards exiled must be other cards (not Phlage itself). Phlage's ETB trigger fires on escape just as on cast: deal 3 damage to each opponent and each creature they control, you gain 3 life. The haste from escape means you can attack immediately. Important: when Phlage escapes, Nihil Spellbomb timing matters — if they crack Spellbomb before you escape, they exile your graveyard and Phlage can't escape without 5 other GY cards.
Ocelot Pride — Cat Triggers on Life Gained ABOVE Starting Total
Ocelot Pride creates a 1/1 Cat token "whenever you gain life for the first time each turn if your life total is greater than it was at the start of your most recent turn." This means: if you start your turn at 20 life and gain 2 life, you create a cat (you're now above 20). If you then gain more life, no additional cats that turn (only the FIRST time counts per turn). On opponent's turns, you can also trigger Ocelot if you gain life above your turn-start total. Guide of Souls on your opponent's creature ETBs can generate a cat on their turn if the life gain crosses your threshold.

Heuristics &
Quick Rules

These are the rules that apply automatically — decisions made before the game starts so you don't have to think about them in the heat of a match. Internalize these until they're reflexes.

  1. Ragavan attacks every turn it's alive unless blocking wins a race you'd otherwise lose. The gold and card generation compounds turn over turn. Even one connection is often the difference. Do not hesitate — deploy and attack.
  2. Check the Galvanic Discharge energy breakpoint before spending it. At 2 energy it hits a 2-toughness creature. At 4 it kills most threats in the format. At 6+ it answers almost anything. Know the breakpoint. Never spend it without knowing the value you're getting and the value you might need.
  3. Calculate Goblin Bombardment damage before every combat step — every single turn. Not just when you're desperate. Opponents miscalculate their safe life total all the time. You want to find hidden lethal before the declare attackers phase, not after.
  4. Never cast Ajani into open Wrath mana. Wait one turn. Either they tap out first and Ajani is safe, or you attack for two more turns with your existing board and have Bombardment reach anyway. An Ajani walked into a Wrath is two cards and 4 mana wasted.
  5. Phlage escape is the game-state pivot. Once you have 6 mana and a graveyard, escaping Phlage should be your primary consideration. This is especially true against Control and Blink — recurring threats win attrition games. Prioritize sequencing toward 6 mana when the game goes long.
  6. Joe Leo's rule is non-negotiable: if you want to splash, play a different deck. The Boros mana base is the deck's foundation. Adding a third color makes your draws inconsistent, makes Blood Moon asymmetric against you, and removes the reliability that produced 17 RC Top 8s. Run straight Boros.
  7. On the draw, be one turn more defensive. Do not commit your full hand to the board if they can Wrath it profitably. Stagger threats. Apply pressure but don't give them a 3-for-1 sweeper before you've established Bombardment reach.
  8. Against combo, your pressure is their clock — never slow down to interact unless the interaction stops their combo directly. Galvanic Discharge to the face beats Discharge to a creature against Grixis Reanimator. Attacking matters more than killing their setup.
  9. Ocelot Pride tokens are expendable. Trade them into blockers freely if it keeps Ragavan or Guide of Souls alive — Pride will rebuild the token army in 2-3 turns. The Bombardment reach the cats represent is their primary value, not the cats themselves as combat threats.
  10. Know your Ajani flip math before casting it. With 3 cats: each cat gets +3/+3 and generates another cat. That's 6+ attacking power in a single activation. With 4 cats it's often 20+ power on board. Calculate the attack step before flipping — know if it's lethal before spending the mana.
  11. Discharge Guide of Souls before it generates too much energy and life. Their Guide of Souls is their resource engine. A Guide that connects twice has given them significant energy and lifegain that changes the math. Kill it early, even if you only have 2 energy — the denial of their engine is worth the below-breakpoint Discharge.
  12. Sanctifier en-Vec is your highest-priority answer in the mirror and vs Blink. It shuts off Galvanic Discharge (red card) and their graveyard-based synergies. Every turn Sanctifier is on board is a turn your interaction package is broken. Answer it the turn it resolves — hold removal specifically for this.
  13. Never let Cori-Steel Cutter connect without an immediate answer. Every second spell they cast creates a 1/1 Monk with prowess — they can attach Cutter instantly giving it haste, so it attacks immediately. That token snowball compounds rapidly. Celestial Purge (red permanent) is the fastest answer. If you can't Purge it, Wrath of the Skies at X=1 clears the token army.
  14. Stagger threats into Supreme Verdict mana. Against Control and Blink with 4+ mana up, deploy one threat per turn. Bait their sweeper. The threat they spend Verdict on was one threat — deploy the follow-up the next turn when they've tapped out. Never give them a 3-for-1 Verdict.
  15. Blood Moon timing is T2 on the play before they can fetch basics. The postboard plan vs Titan and greedy manabases is to resolve Blood Moon before they can fetch out enough basics to function under it. T2 Blood Moon on the play often wins the game immediately against Titan. Don't sandbag it waiting for a "better" moment.
  16. Goblin Bombardment wins the mirror — the pilot who resolves it first and uses it most aggressively wins close mirror games. In the mirror specifically: as soon as Bombardment is on board, every combat becomes a Bombardment reach calculation rather than a creature-vs-creature exchange. Resolve it early, protect it, and use it every turn.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any 7 with Ragavan + removal OR Phlage + two lands. Never keep a hand that doesn't produce energy or apply immediate pressure.
  • Energy rule: Amped Raptor and Ocelot Pride generate energy. Spend energy on tokens or on Phlage triggers. Track your energy count every turn — running out mid-combo is the most common mistake.
  • Ragavan rule: Ragavan Nimble Pilferer must connect in combat to generate treasure. Protect Ragavan with removal on their blocker — don't attack into a brick wall.
  • Phlage rule: Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury escapes from GY by exiling other cards. Track your GY for Phlage fuel. Cast Phlage when you have 5+ cards in GY for the escape cost.
  • Board rule: Obsidian Charmaw destroys a nonbasic land and costs less for each nonbasic your opponent controls. Against greedy manabases this is a free Stone Rain by T3.
  • Sanctifier rule: Sanctifier en-Vec (pro black AND red) is your trump post-board. It's immune to their black/red removal — once in play they need a board wipe or sacrifice effect.

Testing Framework

Block 01
Ragavan Connection Lines
Practice protecting Ragavan in combat. Use removal on their blocker before attacking with Ragavan. Know when Ragavan is worth protecting versus when it can be sacrificed for a free treasure.
Block 02
Phlage Escape Timing
Practice evaluating when your GY has 5 cards for Phlage escape. Don't escape Phlage prematurely — the GY cards have value for future escapes. Track your GY count from T1.
Block 03
Energy Tracking
Practice counting energy throughout a game. Know your exact energy count at each decision point. Running out of energy mid-combo is the most common mistake with Boros Energy.
Block 04
G2 Sanctifier Lines
Practice the Sanctifier en-Vec game state. They cannot target it with black or red removal. Know what CAN remove it: sacrifice effects, exile board wipes, white/blue/green removal only.

Matchday Routine

  1. 01
    Ragavan must connect to generate value. Protect it with removal on their blocker before attacking.
  2. 02
    Phlage escape cost: 5 cards in GY. Track your GY count from T1 so you know exactly when Phlage is escapable.
  3. 03
    Energy count tracking is non-negotiable. Never estimate your energy — count it precisely every turn.
  4. 04
    Between rounds: identify the format's GY hate level. Leyline of the Void answers Phlage — have Obsidian Charmaw to kill it.
  5. 05
    Sanctifier en-Vec: know what can and can't answer it. Sacrifice effects, exile board wipes, non-B/R removal only.
