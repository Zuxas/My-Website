Chapter 01

Overview &
Philosophy

"Answer everything. Win with the last card in hand." — Core Mantra

Azorius Control is a meta-dependent pick. It is not the best deck in every room — it is the best deck in the right room. That room is heavy on midrange and combo, light on fast aggro. When the RC field skews toward Jeskai Blink, Glockulous, Amulet Titan, and other fair/combo decks, UW Control is the highest equity register — specifically the Scepter-Chant lock variant that punishes interactive fields hardest.

The philosophy is simple: every opponent card that resolves is a mistake. Counterspell answers spells. Solitude answers creatures. Prismatic Ending answers permanents. Supreme Verdict answers boards. Nothing sticks. When they run out of threats, you win with the last card in hand and a 3-loyalty Teferi, Hero of Dominaria that untaps two lands every turn.

The deck's skill expression is in resource management, sequencing, and threat assessment — knowing which spell to counter, which to let through, and when to tap down to deploy your own threats. Playing too passively wastes mana. Playing too aggressively breaks the counter backup. The tension is real and mastering it separates 55% UW pilots from 70% pilots.

Core Philosophy

Primary Win Condition
Teferi + Counter Backup
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria resolves with Counterspell or Force of Negation in hand. +1 every turn draws a card and untaps two lands — maintaining counter backup while accruing card advantage. The game ends when they run out of resources and Teferi's emblem exiles the final permanents.
Secondary Win Condition
Narset + Day's Undoing Lock
When the Scepter lock is disrupted, Narset, Parter of Veils + Day's Undoing generates +6 net card advantage in a single action — you draw 7, they draw 1. Narset's static cannot be responded to. The game typically ends on the spot from the resulting hand disparity.
Advantage Type
Card Quality + Volume
Every spell is an answer or a card draw engine. No dead cards. No situational threats. Pure card quality — every card in the 60 either stops their plan or draws more cards. Tune the Narrative banks energy passively while cantripping.
Preferred Role
Control — Always
This deck never willingly becomes the beatdown. The exception: when Teferi, Time Raveler resolves, you briefly switch to pressuring them at sorcery speed while they can't respond. Otherwise: hold mana open, answer spells, generate cards, close with Teferi Hero.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths

Best deck against combo — hard counters answer any key spell.

Do not register this if you expect Boros Energy to make up more than 30% of the field.

Card Choices & Key Card Roles

The Lock Engine
Orim's Chant + Isochron Scepter
Orim's Chant: {W} instant. Kicker {W}. Target player can't cast spells this turn. If kicked, creatures can't attack this turn. Isochron Scepter: {2} artifact. Imprint — exile an instant with MV ≤ 2 from your hand on ETB. {2}{T}: copy the imprinted card, may cast it without paying mana cost. Scepter imprinted with Chant = {2}{T} every turn to lock them off all spells. With kicker available, also stops all attacks. The lock is not counterable — Scepter's activated ability goes on the stack, Chant copy is created when ability resolves.
The Draw Lock
Narset, Parter of Veils + Day's Undoing
Narset: {1}{U}{U} planeswalker. Static: each opponent can't draw more than one card each turn. -2: look at top 4, may reveal noncreature nonland, rest to bottom. Day's Undoing: {2}{U} sorcery. Each player shuffles hand + GY into library, then draws 7. If it's your turn, end the turn. Narset + Day's Undoing: you draw 7, they draw 1 (Narset's static), then your turn ends. Net: you see 7 new cards; they see 1. The game typically ends here.
The Flexible Sweeper
Wrath of the Skies
{X}{W}{W} sorcery. You get X {E} (energy counters), then you may pay any amount of {E}. Destroy each artifact, creature, and enchantment with MV ≤ the amount of energy paid. Critical rule: hits artifacts AND enchantments AND creatures — Supreme Verdict only hits creatures. X=1 kills all MV-1 permanents; X=3 kills most of the format. Also hits Ocelot Pride, Guide of Souls, and energy creatures without exempting them. Tune the Narrative banks energy to fuel larger Wrath activations.
The Closer
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria
{3}{W}{U} Loyalty 4. +1: Draw a card. At the beginning of the next end step, untap up to two lands. -3: Put target nonland permanent into owner's library third from top. -8: Emblem — whenever you draw a card, exile target permanent an opponent controls. Now played as a 1-of closer rather than a 2-of engine. When it resolves the game should already be heavily in your favor. The -3 answers permanents that Wrath can't hit (planeswalkers) or that March misses.
Searchable Threat
Wan Shi Tong, Librarian
{X}{U}{U} flash legendary bird spirit. Flying, vigilance. ETB: put X +1/+1 counters on him, then draw half X cards (rounded down). Whenever an opponent searches their library: +1/+1 counter and draw a card. Cast X=4 (6 mana): enter as a 4/4 and draw 2. Each opponent fetchland or tutor after that = free card and larger blocker. Stock Up and Monumental Henge can find him. Against fetchland-heavy decks, he snowballs rapidly into a 10/10+ draw engine.
Card Selection
Stock Up + Tune the Narrative
Stock Up: {2}{U} sorcery. Look at top 5, put 2 into hand, rest on bottom in any order. Better than Opt in density; better than Preordain in ceiling. Finds Scepter, Narset, Chant, or whatever piece is missing. Tune the Narrative: {U} instant. Draw a card. You get {E}{E}. Cantrip that also banks 2 energy toward a Wrath of the Skies. Playing 4 Tunes in a game means Wrath can hit MV-8+ for free. The energy synergy between Tune and Wrath is the reason this build runs the Wrath package over Prismatic Ending.
Chapter 03

Game Flow

"Never tap out without a reason. Every tapped turn is their free turn." — Sequencing Principle

The Four Phases

01
Survive
Turns 1–3. Do not tap out. Prismatic Ending on their one-drop. Counterspell their two-drop. Hold up interaction for anything they try to resolve. The only exception: Teferi, Time Raveler on turn 3 to immediately lock them out of instant-speed play.
02
Establish
Turns 4–6. Supreme Verdict clears what you couldn't answer one-for-one. Stock Up and Tune the Narrative filter for key pieces. Narset generates card advantage passively. Isochron Scepter with Orim's Chant imprinted creates the full lock on turn 3.
03
Deploy
Turn 5–7. Teferi, Hero of Dominaria with Counterspell or Force of Negation backup. The Teferi +1 untaps two lands at the beginning of the next end step — meaning on their turn you have counter mana available. This is the defining asymmetry.
04
Close
Turn 8+. Their hand is empty. Teferi +1 draws a card every turn while they draw threats into your counters. Teferi -3 removes their last permanent. Emblem closes out the last resources. You close with a Shark token or Celestial Colonnade — almost any threat works from this position.

Offensive Gameplan

This deck does not apply pressure early — it generates card advantage until the opponent is out of threats. The "offensive" plan is Teferi, Hero of Dominaria accruing loyalty while you hold counter mana open. Pressure comes from the opponent being forced to overextend into Supreme Verdict or walk their key spells into Counterspell.

A "winning position" is: Teferi Hero on the battlefield at 5+ loyalty, one or more blue mana untapped, and at least one counter or Force of Negation in hand. From this position the opponent cannot win through fair Magic. Any threat they deploy is countered; any threat that resolves gets Teferi -3'd to third from top.

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turn 1
Never Tap Out
Fetchland into Island. Spell Snare open if applicable. Pass. The exception: if they are on a deck with known T1 mana dorks or cantrips that need to be answered, Prismatic Ending for {W} on a one-drop is correct. Otherwise, observe and identify their deck.
Turn 2
Answer or Develop
Counterspell their key two-drop. Or Prismatic Ending (converge 2 = hits MV 2 or less). Or Spell Snare their Counterspell if in a blue mirror. If their board is empty: Teferi, Time Raveler applies the sorcery lock at minimal cost. Never tap out below 3 mana unless the answer is worth the risk.
Turn 3
Assert Control
Teferi, Time Raveler if the coast is clear — the static sorcery lock immediately prevents their instant-speed responses. Or Supreme Verdict into a wide board. Or deploy Isochron Scepter to set up the Chant lock beginning turn 3. This is the critical turn where you establish who controls the game.
Turn 4+
Teferi Hero
Deploy Teferi, Hero of Dominaria when you can hold up countermagic. +1 immediately: draw a card. At the beginning of the next end step, untap up to two lands — use those untapped lands to hold counters on their turn. Each turn Teferi survives is a +1 card advantage and threat to their entire board.

Sample Sequences

Sequence 1 — "Teferi Lock" (Ideal Line vs Midrange)
T1Flooded Strand, crack for Island. Pass with Spell Snare open.
T2They cast a threat. Counterspell it. Their board is empty on turn 2.
T3Cast Teferi, Time Raveler. Static lock is immediately active — they can no longer hold up counterspells or responses at instant speed.
T4Cast Isochron Scepter, imprint Orim's Chant. Activate next turn — they cannot cast spells or attack from here on.
T5Cast Teferi, Hero of Dominaria. +1: draw. At next end step: untap two lands. On their turn: hold up Counterspell with untapped mana.
Result: Teferi Hero at 5 loyalty, counter backup on their turn every turn, opponent has no hand. Game is effectively over.
Sequence 2 — "Verdict Reset" (vs Aggro / Wide Board)
T1Fetchland, Island. Prismatic Ending their one-drop (converge W).
T2They develop two more creatures. Solitude (evoke a white card) exiles their biggest threat. Pass at 2 life.
T3They go wide. You have three lands. Pass — hold Supreme Verdict mana.
T4Supreme Verdict. Cannot be countered. Destroy all creatures. Board wiped. They have zero permanents.
Result: Empty board, you have 4+ lands and a full hand of interaction. From this point Teferi Hero closes.
Sequence 3 — "Full Lock" (Scepter + Chant vs Any Interactive Deck)
T2Cast Isochron Scepter. ETB imprint — exile Orim's Chant from hand.
T3Pay {2}{T}: Scepter ability resolves, copy Orim's Chant, cast it targeting them — they cannot cast spells this turn. Pay the {W} kicker: creatures can't attack this turn.
T4+Repeat every turn. Deploy Narset, Parter of Veils freely. She's finding pieces while they sit locked. Cast Teferi, Hero of Dominaria when ready — +1 draws a card, untap two lands.
Result: They cannot cast spells or attack. You develop freely. Game ends when Teferi emblem fires or library is exhausted.

Mulligan Philosophy

Snap keep: 3–4 lands (including at least one blue source), one Counterspell or Solitude, and either Stock Up/Tune the Narrative to refuel or a second interaction piece. Best openers have Isochron Scepter + Orim's Chant ready by turn 2.

Snap mulligan: Zero interaction (all lands and card draw), five or more lands, one-land hands regardless of other cards, hands with no blue source by turn 2.

On the draw: The extra card means you can keep hands slightly weaker on interaction because you have one more card to find it. Stock Up hands and Scepter-less hands both become more keepable on the draw.

Teferi TRav in opener: A hand with Teferi TRav + 3 lands + 2 answers is almost always a snap keep. TRav on turn 3 into counter backup is the fastest and safest path to establishing control.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 Interaction, T4 Teferi
On the play, your T1 is interaction: Spell Snare up, Brainstorm to fix hand, or simply pass and hold mana. On the play you’re dictating tempo — pass T1 with Spell Snare mana and counter their T2 spell while developing your land base. Teferi on T3 (with mana available) locks them down before they assemble their plan.
On the Draw
Extra Card Finds Force of Negation
On the draw you see 8 cards, dramatically improving Force of Negation odds. Keep looser hands on the draw if they have 3 lands + 2 interaction pieces. Force of Negation on the draw is most valuable in the early turns when you’re behind — free counter of their key spell while developing.

When Disrupted

Teferi Removed
Snapcaster Rebuys
Teferi killed: opponents interact at instant speed again. Hold Cryptic Command mana open (tap all their creatures before their attack step). Snapcaster Mage rebuy Counterspell or Day’s Undoing for hand refill. The control plan still functions without Teferi — it just requires more mana efficiency.
Lands Disrupted
Brainstorm + Force Holds
Blood Moon or Spreading Seas disrupts your mana. Pivot: Force of Negation handles the key threats for free. Brainstorm shuffles bad draws away. Keep basic lands in hand until Blood Moon is answered with Prismatic Ending or Cryptic Command bounce.
The Control Mode
Your Reactive Stance
You hold up interaction every single turn. Dovin's Veto ({W}{U} uncounterable noncreature counter), Absorb ({1}{W}{U} counter + 3 life), and Temporary Lockdown ({2}{W} exile MV ≤ 2) are your primary three plays. Your game plan is: answer their threat, hold mana open, untap, answer their next threat. You never commit a threat you can't protect until you're ahead on resources.
Narset Lock
Your Resource Denial
Narset, Parter of Veils prevents opponents from drawing additional cards. You deploy Narset when they tap for their draw-heavy spell (Collected Company, Pore Over the Pages). Under Narset: their Opt draws 0 extra cards, their Company hits but draws 0, their Pore draws only their main card. You follow Narset with a threat they must spend removal on instead of their draw engine.
Teferi Advantage
Your Card Engine
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria pluses to draw a card and untap two lands — you end up with +1 card AND more mana than you started with. Each Teferi activation draws you deeper while maintaining interaction. Your T5+ is: Teferi plus → draw card → two lands untap → hold mana for counter. You generate advantage while keeping interaction open simultaneously.
Supreme Verdict Line
Your Reset Button
Supreme Verdict ({2}{W}{W}) can't be countered. You deploy it when they have 3+ creatures and you need a clean board. After Verdict resolves, you untap with an empty board and (usually) a full hand. You immediately deploy your first threat after Verdict — Brimaz if you have him, or Teferi if you need more resources first. Their board is zero; yours is about to be something.

A
Orim's Chant + Isochron Scepter Lock
The primary lock — {2}{T} every turn to deny all spells; kicked version also stops all attacks
Setup
Turn 2–3
Need: Isochron Scepter in hand, Orim's Chant in hand. Orim's Chant has MV 1 — it is imprint-eligible (Scepter imprints MV ≤ 2). Cast Scepter on turn 2, imprint Chant from hand. Turn 3: pay {2}{T} to create a copy of Chant. Cast the copy without paying mana cost — target them, they can't cast spells this turn. The kicker {W} can be paid when casting the copy for the "creatures can't attack" clause.
Execution
The Loop
Step 1: Cast Isochron Scepter. ETB imprint — exile Orim's Chant from hand.

Step 2: Each turn: tap Scepter + pay {2}. Scepter ability resolves: copy Orim's Chant. Cast the copy targeting opponent — they cannot cast spells this turn.

Step 3: Pay the {W} kicker whenever you have white mana available. Creatures can't attack this turn.

Key: Scepter's activation is an activated ability, not a spell. Once the ability resolves and creates the copy, the copy can still be countered. But Scepter itself cannot be countered once it's on the battlefield.
Result
Full Lock
They cannot cast spells or attack on any of their turns. You develop freely. Narset ticks down every turn finding pieces. Teferi, Hero of Dominaria generates a card per turn toward the emblem. The game ends when their library runs out or Teferi's emblem fires. Losing Scepter to removal breaks the lock — protect it with Counterspell on artifact removal.
B
Narset + Day's Undoing Draw Lock
You draw 7; they draw 1; turn ends — the most lopsided 3-card combo in the format
Setup
Narset in Play
Need: Narset, Parter of Veils on the battlefield (any loyalty), Day's Undoing in hand, your own main phase. Narset's static: each opponent can't draw more than one card each turn. This applies to any draw — including the 7 from Day's Undoing. Result: they draw exactly 1 card from Undoing regardless of their hand size.
Execution
Cast on Your Turn
Step 1: On your main phase with Narset in play, cast Day's Undoing ({2}{U}).

Step 2: Each player shuffles hand + GY into library, then draws 7. Narset's static fires — opponent draws 1.

Step 3: "If it's your turn, end the turn." Damage wears off. Stack exiles. Your main phase was just spent on this card — your extra mana is now irrelevant since the turn ended.

Key: Day's Undoing ends your turn when cast on your turn. Cast it when you have no other spells left to cast. Do NOT cast it on their turn — on their turn, Day's Undoing doesn't end the turn (it only ends "your" turn when it's your turn).
Result
+6 Net Card Advantage
You drew 7. They drew 1. Net: +6 cards in your favor in a single action. Their graveyard is emptied (both GYs shuffled in). Your graveyard is also emptied — Day's Undoing is effectively non-reusable. This is a one-time "win the game from behind" reset that also replenishes your counter suite when you've run out of interaction.
C
Tune the Narrative + Wrath of the Skies
Bank energy with cantrips; deploy Wrath to destroy any MV threshold across artifacts, creatures, and enchantments
Setup
Tune Then Wrath
Need: At least one Tune the Narrative cast (2 energy banked), Wrath of the Skies in hand. Wrath provides its own energy (X value becomes X energy on resolution), then you pay any stored energy including from Tune. Total energy = X (from Wrath) + 2 (from one Tune) = X+2 effective MV destruction threshold. Two Tunes banked = X+4.
Execution
Calibrate the X
Step 1: Cast Tune the Narrative earlier in the game. Draw a card, bank 2 energy.

Step 2: When Wrath is needed: determine the MV of the most threatening permanent. Cast Wrath at X = target MV minus banked energy.

Step 3: Wrath resolves: you get X energy. You now have X + (previously banked) energy. Spend all energy. Destroy all artifacts, creatures, and enchantments with MV ≤ energy paid.

Key: Wrath of the Skies is NOT like Supreme Verdict — it destroys artifacts and enchantments too. Isochron Scepter, energy sources, Ocelot Pride — all die to Wrath. Do not cast Wrath if your own Scepter lock is active.
Result
Board Wipe + Card
The entire threat-generating portion of their board is destroyed in one shot — including enchantments and artifacts that Supreme Verdict ignores. The earlier Tune drew a card and pre-loaded the Wrath for free damage. 4 Tunes cast in a game = Wrath kills everything up to MV 4 higher than X at no additional cost. Combine with Narset lock to fire Wrath while they can't develop a new board state.

Lines & Tricks

  • StackScepter activation creates a copy — the copy can still be countered: When you pay {2}{T}, the Scepter ability goes on the stack. When it resolves, a copy of Orim's Chant is created and you may cast it. That cast goes on the stack and CAN be countered by their Counterspell. The solution: cast the Chant copy when they're tapped out or hold Scepter activation for after they've used their counter mana.
  • StackNarset's static replaces drawing — it is not a triggered ability: When Day's Undoing says "draw 7," Narset's replacement effect modifies the draw event directly. They draw exactly 1. This cannot be responded to with instants — it's a replacement effect on the draw, not a trigger. Even if they have a Teferi, Time Raveler in play, Narset's static still applies.
  • SequenceThe Wandering Emperor at flash speed into a combat: The Wandering Emperor has flash. Cast her during their attack step. As long as she entered this turn, you may activate her loyalty abilities at instant speed. -2 exiles a tapped attacking creature; -1 creates a 2/2 vigilance blocker. This is how she generates maximum value — deploying her during their combat step means she impacts the board immediately as both removal and defense.
  • SequenceGeier Reach Sanitarium forces discard under Narset: Geier Reach Sanitarium: {2}{T}: each player draws a card, then discards a card. With Narset in play, they already drew their one permitted card this turn. If you activate Sanitarium and they've already drawn with Narset's limit: the draw from Sanitarium is replaced (they don't draw). But they still discard. Net: you draw + discard; they just discard. Used to strip key combo pieces from their hand.
  • AvoidNever cast Day's Undoing if your GY has spells you want to rebuy: Day's Undoing shuffles your GY into your library. If you've got valuable spells in the yard — Counterspell, Supreme Verdict — Day's Undoing dilutes your library rather than letting you access them. Cast Day's Undoing when your hand is the problem (empty or low) and your GY is not a resource you're protecting.
  • AdvancedWan Shi Tong punishes every opposing fetchland: Once Wan Shi Tong is in play, each time they crack a fetchland (which says "search your library"), Wan Shi Tong's trigger fires — +1/+1 counter and draw a card. A single game in which they crack 4 fetchlands = 4 free cards and Wan Shi Tong as a 9/9 blocker. Deploy Wan Shi Tong early against fetchland-heavy opponents.

Tactics

"Force of Negation timing and Cryptic Command modes are where pilots give away percentage points."

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Critical
Force of Negation is NOT Free On Your Turn
Force of Negation can only be cast for its alternate cost (exile a blue card) if it is NOT your turn. On your own turn, you must pay {1}{U}{U}. This is the most common rules error with Force. If you’re in your own main phase or combat, Force costs full mana. This means you cannot Force your opponent’s response to your own Wrath of God on your turn — you’d need to pay {1}{U}{U}. Hold Force for their turn.
Critical
Teferi +1 Lets You Cast Spells at Flash Speed
Teferi, Time Raveler’s +1 says "until your next turn, you may cast sorcery spells as though they had flash." This lets you cast Supreme Verdict, Day’s Undoing, and other sorceries at instant speed until your next turn. Cast Verdict during their end step after they’ve committed their board. Cast Day’s Undoing during their upkeep when they have no mana. The +1 also bounces a noncreature, nonland permanent at +1 resolution.
Critical
Cryptic Command: Use the Return Mode
Cryptic Command’s 4 modes: counter spell, return permanent, tap all creatures, draw a card. The return mode is the most underused: Counter + Return bounces your own Snapcaster Mage to rebuy a spell AND counters their response in one Cryptic. Tap All + Return resets their board AND bounces a threat. Return your own Vendilion Clique mid-combat to get another ETB (see their hand again). Know all 6 two-mode combinations before sitting down.
Line 01
Your Dovin's Veto Timing
You save Dovin's Veto for their uncounterable threats: Supreme Verdict (their board wipe), Indomitable Creativity (their combo piece), any spell that wins them the game on resolution. You never Veto a cantrip or a creature — Absorb handles those. You identify their key spell and keep {W}{U} open specifically for it. Veto is uncounterable itself — it always resolves.
Line 02
Your Narset Lock
You deploy Narset, Parter of Veils when they're about to draw extra cards. Their Opt draws nothing extra. Their Pore Over the Pages draws nothing extra. Their Company hits creatures but draws nothing. You protect Narset with your remaining interaction. The lock combined with a clock (Teferi, Brimaz) closes the game while they can't draw into answers.
Line 03
Your Teferi Minus Line
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria's −3 puts a permanent on top of their library. You use the −3 on their most dangerous permanent: Sheoldred, Kalitas, a resolved Creativity threat. "On top of library" means they draw it next turn — not back to hand. You follow the −3 with your own clock (Brimaz attack). They must redraw the removed permanent and cast it again.
Line 04
Your Approach Closing
Approach of the Second Sun ({6}{W}): you cast it first (costs 6). Next turn, you draw and cast it again for 7 life gain. Your goal: cast Approach on T7, survive one more turn, draw and cast Approach again on T8. You protect yourself with counters during the intervening turn. You never cast Approach unless you can cast it again next turn — never waste the first cast.

Timing Windows

When to Hold Mana vs Spend It
The core timing decision for this deck: every land drop and every spell cast is a commitment. Know before your upkeep which lines require open mana this turn and which don’t. Holding mana = passing priority; spending it = committing to a plan. Make this decision in your upkeep, not in response to their action.
When to Cast at Their End Step vs Your Main Phase
End-step casts maximize information: you’ve seen their full turn, they’re often tapped out. Main-phase casts maximize mana availability for follow-up. Know which of your key spells gain most from end-step timing and which need main-phase mana backup.
Chapter 05

Role Assignment

UW Control has one role in almost every matchup: the controller. The deck never applies pressure. The rare exception is when the Scepter lock is online and you win with Teferi, Hero of Dominaria or Wan Shi Tong, Librarian as the threat while they can't interact. The distinction between the three modes is how you allocate your interactive resources — not whether you play offense or defense.

Survive + Stabilize
vs aggro. You are behind on board from turn 1. Every answer buys a turn. Supreme Verdict on turn 4 is your reset button. Don't tap out without Verdict mana. Solitude via evoke is free removal — use it aggressively to buy turns. Wrath of the Skies and Celestial Purge from the board provide additional removal angles.
vs: Boros Energy · Izzet Prowess · Any aggro deck
Counter + Outvalue
vs combo and graveyard. Hold Counterspell and Force of Negation for the key piece. Teferi TRav locks Persist to sorcery speed. Rest in Peace from the board removes the GY permanently. The goal is a hard stop on the combo before it assembles.
vs: Glockulous · Amulet Titan · Any combo
Answer + Teferi Hero
vs midrange and control mirrors. Answer their threats efficiently, generate card advantage with Narset, Parter of Veils and Stock Up, close with Teferi Hero at 5+ loyalty. Mystical Dispute is the edge in blue mirrors — {U} to counter any blue spell when they control an Island. Whoever resolves Teferi Hero with backup wins.
vs: Jeskai Blink · UW Control Mirror · Dimir Midrange

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Tapping out on your turn without a reason. Every turn you tap out is a free development turn for them. The mana must accomplish something worth the risk — Teferi Hero, Verdict into a wide board, or a Snap that generates immediate value. Tapping for Stock Up on your main phase when you could cast it later — or activating the Scepter when they have mana up to counter the Chant copy — are the most common sequencing errors.
  2. Countering the wrong spell. When they cast Faithless Looting into Persist in the same turn, Counterspell Persist, not Faithless Looting. The Looting filters their hand — the Persist is the game-losing play. Always identify which spell wins them the game if it resolves and protect your answer for that spell.
  3. Deploying Teferi Hero into open mana without counter backup. A Teferi Hero that gets immediately Spell Pierced or Force of Negated with no backup is a 5-mana do-nothing. Only deploy Teferi Hero when you can protect him — hold Force of Negation or Counterspell in hand when you cast him.
  4. Breaking the Scepter lock with your own Wrath of the Skies. Wrath of the Skies destroys artifacts including your own Isochron Scepter. Once the Scepter lock is active, switch to a non-Wrath route to close — Teferi, Hero of Dominaria or Wan Shi Tong. Only fire Wrath before the lock is established or after you're ready to win without it.

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Know exactly what your cards do — and exactly what they don't."
Consign to Memory — What It Hits and What It Doesn't Consign to Memory ({2}{U}) counters triggered abilities or colorless spells only. It CANNOT counter: Force of Negation (blue spell), Persist (black sorcery), Counterspell (blue instant), Griselbrand's draw activation (activated ability). It CAN counter: Archon of Cruelty's ETB trigger, Summoner's Pact's upkeep trigger (they lose the game), Urza's Saga chapter III tutor trigger, Arcbound Ravager's modular death trigger. If you are unsure whether something is a triggered ability, look for the words "when," "whenever," or "at the beginning of." Those are triggers.
Supreme Verdict and Wrath of the Skies Don't Target — Hexproof Is Irrelevant Hexproof says "this permanent can't be the target of spells or abilities your opponents control." Supreme Verdict and Wrath of the Skies say "destroy all creatures" — no targeting. Non-targeting sweepers bypass hexproof, shroud, and protection entirely. Scion of Draco with full domain hexproof, Slippery Bogle, Leyline Binding's ward — none of it matters against a non-targeting board wipe. This is your primary line against Domain Zoo and any hexproof creature strategy.
Teferi, Time Raveler Sorcery Lock — What It Stops and What It Doesn't Teferi, Time Raveler's static ability: "Each opponent can only cast spells during their own turn." This stops opponents from casting instant-speed spells (counters, removal, Goryo's) during YOUR turn or at end of turn. It does NOT stop: your own instant-speed spells, triggered abilities (which are placed on the stack, not "cast"), activated abilities (which are "activated," not "cast"), or Ruby Storm (which plays everything at sorcery speed anyway). Under TRav, Goryo's Vengeance is locked to sorcery speed — meaning they cannot cast it at your end step or in response to your Rest in Peace.
Force of Negation — Only on the Opponent's Turn Force of Negation has an alternative cost: "During an opponent's turn, you may exile a blue card from your hand rather than pay this spell's mana cost." The key word is "during an opponent's turn." On YOUR turn, you must pay {1}{U}{U}. You cannot pitch-counter on your own turn. This means: if you want to counter something for free, it must be their turn. If you tap out to cast a planeswalker on your turn and they try to counter it, you CANNOT Force for free to protect it — you must have {1}{U}{U} available.
Solitude Exile — Opponent Gains Life Equal to Exiled Creature's Power Solitude's oracle text: "Exile up to one target creature. Its controller gains life equal to its power." When you evoke-exile their 5/5 Territorial Kavu, they gain 5 life. This is not always relevant, but in the Zoo matchup it matters: exiling their 8/8 Scion of Draco gives them 8 life. Track the life total swing carefully. Against aggro decks at 8 life, exiling a 6/6 that gives them 6 life may not be the correct line if it prolongs the game.
Storm Triggers — Counter Grapeshot Before Copies Exist Storm creates copies as a triggered ability when you cast the storm spell. The sequence: (1) you cast Grapeshot, (2) Storm triggers — "copy this spell for each spell cast before it this turn," (3) copies are placed on the stack. If you counter Grapeshot in step (1), the storm trigger in step (2) never fires and no copies are created. If you wait until copies are on the stack, countering one copy does nothing — the others still resolve. Counter the original when first cast, before priority passes to trigger resolution.
Summoner's Pact Upkeep Kill — Consign to Memory Wins the Game Summoner's Pact has an upkeep trigger: "At the beginning of your next upkeep, pay {2}{G}{G}. If you don't, you lose the game." This upkeep trigger is a triggered ability — Consign to Memory counters it. If they cast Summoner's Pact to tutor a key piece and you let the Pact resolve (search is on the stack, you don't have a counter for the spell), then on THEIR next upkeep you can counter the "pay or lose" trigger with Consign. They don't pay, they lose. This is the correct sequencing: let Pact resolve if you have Consign for the upkeep trigger.

Pilot Principles

  1. Never tap out without a reason. Every turn you tap out is a free development turn for them. The mana must accomplish something that justifies the risk — Teferi TRav, Verdict into a wide board, or Snapcaster generating immediate card advantage. "Developing" without an answer in hand is a trap.
  2. Keep (minimum hand): 3–4 lands, one answer, one way to refuel or a second answer. One-land hands are never keepable. Hands with only lands and card draw and no interaction are risky on the play. Any hand that cannot answer a turn-2 threat should be mulliganed.
  3. Teferi, Time Raveler's sorcery lock is a static ability — it is always active. It does not come from the +1. When Teferi TRav enters the battlefield, opponents can immediately only cast spells at sorcery speed. The +1 gives YOU sorcery flash. Know the difference — it matters every turn he's in play.
  4. Teferi Hero +1 untaps two lands at the BEGINNING OF THE NEXT END STEP, not immediately. Cast Teferi Hero. +1 draws a card. Pass turn. At the beginning of YOUR end step (before you actually move to your opponent's turn), the untap trigger fires. Use those untapped lands to hold counters on their turn.
  5. Teferi Hero -3 puts a nonland permanent THIRD from top — not exiled, not on top. They can draw into it within three turns. Don't -3 a permanent if they have Stock Up, Jace, the Mind Sculptor, or other topdeck manipulation — they'll find it. Use -3 to answer an immediate threat, knowing it's a temporary answer.
  6. Stock Up should be cast at end of their turn, not your main phase. You have the information of their full turn before deciding what to find. You cannot cast Snapcaster and then decide what to target after seeing how they respond. Decide before casting.
  7. Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities and colorless spells only. It cannot counter: Counterspell, Force of Negation, Persist, Faithless Looting, or any colored spell. Its targets: Archon's ETB trigger, Emperor's "counters placed" trigger, Summoner's Pact upkeep trigger, Snapcaster's ETB trigger. Know this list cold before deploying Consign.
  8. Supreme Verdict cannot be countered and destroys ALL creatures including yours. Wrath of the Skies also destroys artifacts and enchantments — Verdict does not. Use Verdict when the uncounterable clause matters; use Wrath when artifacts or enchantments (Ocelot Pride, Goblin Bombardment) also need to die.
  9. Force of Negation only counters noncreature spells and is only free on the opponent's turn. On your own turn, Force costs {1}{U}{U}. On their turn, you may pitch a blue card instead. If you have to Force on your own turn for some reason, you need the mana. Force also exiles the countered spell (not GY) — meaningful vs Snapcaster and Flashback cards.
  10. Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities on the stack, not prevention. After a creature enters and its ETB trigger is on the stack (in the casting trigger window), Cast Consign targeting the ETB trigger after it appears on the stack. Do not act during the casting window — Consign counters triggered abilities, not creature spells. Wait for the trigger, then counter it.
  11. Mystical Dispute costs {U} vs an Island — hold it for their Teferi Hero in mirrors. Do not waste Veto on a minor noncreature spell. In blue mirrors, Veto is the most important card in your 75 postboard. Its targets in priority order: (1) Teferi Hero, (2) Persist (vs Glockulous — though Counterspell is better), (3) Any other key noncreature spell.
  12. Tournament clock management: UW games run long. Play briskly. RC rounds are 50 minutes. A typical UW game takes 25-30 minutes. You have time for two games if both are efficient. Never slow-play your own decision trees — know your lines before the card hits the table. Deliberation happens before the game, not during it.

Common Mistakes

  • MistakeCountering the wrong spell when they have two threats in hand: When they cast Faithless Looting into Persist in the same turn, counter Persist (the winning play) not Faithless Looting (the setup play). Identify which spell wins them the game if it resolves. Always counter the game-winning spell, not the setup spell. Looting is a sorcery — if TRav is in play, they can only cast it on their main phase and you see it in sequence. Counterspell the Persist on the chain.
  • MistakeDeploying Teferi Hero into open counter mana without backup: A Teferi Hero that gets immediately Spell Pierced or Force of Negated with no response is a 5-mana do-nothing. The golden rule: you need to hold at least one Counterspell, Force of Negation in hand when you deploy Teferi Hero. If you don't have backup, wait. Drawing for two turns with Snap is fine.
  • MistakeUsing Consign to Memory on Persist: Consign cannot counter Persist. Persist is a black sorcery. Consign only counters triggered abilities and colorless spells. The error usually happens when players panic-fire Consign at whatever is on the stack. Before you cast Consign, verbally confirm: "Is this a triggered ability or a colorless spell?" If neither, Consign does nothing.
  • MistakeConfusing Consign to Memory timing vs ETB triggers: Cast Consign to Memory AFTER the ETB trigger is on the stack. You can't counter the creature spell itself with Consign — it only counters triggered and activated abilities. The correct window is: Archon enters → ETB trigger goes to stack → respond with Consign. This window is wider than most players expect.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand with 3 lands + 2 interaction pieces. Pure draw hands without answers are mulligans.
  • Counter rule: Save Force of Negation for the card that wins them the game. Don't Force their cantrips.
  • Verdict rule: Always keep Verdict mana up from T4. Never tap out. The deck can't win without Verdict backup.
  • Teferi rule: TRav sorcery lock is ONE-SIDED. You keep full instant speed. Don't confuse your own restrictions.
  • FoN rule: Force of Negation is free ONLY on the opponent's turn. On your own turn, you pay {1}{U}{U}.
  • Sideboard rule: Rest in Peace exiles all GYs on entry AND ongoing. Deploy it the FIRST turn you safely can.
