Chapter 01

Deck Identity

Domain Zoo is Modern's best aggro deck when it draws Leyline of the Guildpact. It's still a solid aggro deck when it doesn't. That's the whole advantage.

Domain Zoo plays the longest continuous history of any aggro archetype in Modern — creatures, burn, and land synergies dating back to the format's beginning. What makes the 2025–26 version dangerous is the Leyline of the Guildpact package: a zero-mana enchantment that, when in your opening hand, enters the battlefield before the game begins, instantly giving your lands all five basic land types and making every nonland permanent you control all five colors.

The payoffs are immediate and compounding. Scion of Draco reduces to free (costs {2} less per basic land type — five types = {10} off its {12} base cost, leaving {2}, then with Leyline it's free as your permanents are already all colors). Territorial Kavu becomes a 5/5 on turn two. Leyline Binding exiles any nonland permanent for {W}. Tribal Flames deals 5 damage for {1}{R}. All before turn two, all for almost no mana.

The deck's identity is aggressively slanted: you want to kill by turn four through a combination of large creatures backed by burn and soft counterspells. Stubborn Denial (ferocious hard counter) and Consign to Memory (colorless/free spells) protect your threats from removal and counterspells. Doorkeeper Thrull shuts down ETB-dependent decks entirely — landing it against Esper Blink or Jeskai Blink is frequently a free win.

Chapter 03

Gameplan

Every game starts the same question: did I draw Leyline? The answer changes how you sequence, but not what you're trying to do — put 5-power bodies on the board and protect them long enough to win.

With Leyline of the Guildpact (Optimal)

Turn 0 / Pregame
Leyline of the Guildpact enters the battlefield before the game begins. All your lands immediately have every basic land type. All your nonland permanents are all five colors. Full domain is already online before either player has taken a turn.
Turn 1
Fetch a dual (Steam Vents, Sacred Foundry) or play Spara's Headquarters or Indatha Triome. Drop Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer or hold up a land. Ragavan on T1 pressures immediately and generates Treasure + exile draw when it connects. With Leyline already active, Ragavan can attack into any 1/1 token and live.
Turn 2
Cast Territorial Kavu for {R}{G} — enters as a 5/5 (Leyline gives full domain). Or cast Scion of Draco for {2} (full domain reduces it by {10}). With Scion in play, all your creatures gain hexproof, lifelink, first strike, trample, and vigilance. Kavu as a 5/5 with all five keywords is essentially unbeatable at this mana cost.
Turn 3–4
Apply lethal pressure. Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury closes. Leyline Binding answers any blocker for {W}. Stubborn Denial with ferocious protects your threats for {U}. If they've stabilized: Tribal Flames (if in list) deals 5 for {1}{R}. A single untapped land usually represents Leyline Binding for zero.

Without Leyline (Fair Mode)

Domain Zoo is still a solid deck without Leyline — it just requires a slightly slower ramp to full domain. The optimal two-land sequence to reach full domain with this manabase is: Spara's Headquarters (Forest/Plains/Island) → Indatha Triome (Forest/Plains/Black). Two lands gives you all five land types, putting Territorial Kavu at 5/5 and Leyline Binding at {W} by turn two. Two lands gives you all five land types, putting Territorial Kavu at 5/5 and Leyline Binding at {W} by turn two with a fetch in hand.

Alternatively: Fetch into Temple Garden (Forest/Plains) → fetch into Steam Vents (Island/Mountain) → one more land for the fifth type (Swamp). You can also sequence fetch into Breeding Pool, then Arid Mesa into Sacred Foundry or similar. The key principle: you need at least one Island, Swamp, Mountain, Forest, and Plains among your lands simultaneously for full domain. Triomes (Spara's Headquarters, Indatha Triome, Thundering Falls) give three types per card — they're the most efficient path to domain without Leyline.

Mulligan Framework

Keep hands that have: at least two lands that together provide multiple land types, and at least two creatures (or one creature + Leyline). Leyline in the opening hand is an automatic keep regardless of the other six cards — it provides so much free value that even a mediocre six-card hand with Leyline beats a good seven without it.

Be wary of: hands with five or more lands (flood risk), hands with three or more Leylines (active liability — you only need one on the battlefield), and hands with no creatures and no Ragavan to apply early pressure. Territorial Kavu without domain is just a 0/0 that dies on ETB — make sure you have a land plan before keeping a Kavu hand.

Lines and Tricks

Two Leylines in Hand
If you have two Leylines in your opening hand and your opponent can't destroy enchantments, put only one into play and use the second as a discard target for Territorial Kavu's rummage on attack. If they can destroy enchantments (Nature's Claim, Wear//Tear), put both in — redundancy protects against the first being destroyed.
Doorkeeper Thrull Timing
Flash Doorkeeper in response to an opponent casting a creature they want to ETB-trigger. They still pay the mana and the creature resolves — it just enters without triggering anything. Against Esper Blink: cast Doorkeeper in response to Solitude being cast (not evoked — evoke means paying the evoke cost to cast it). The ETB exile trigger is silenced.
Scion + Hexproof Sequencing
Cast Scion of Draco before casting your other creatures. Once Scion is in play, every subsequent creature you play enters with hexproof immediately. If you cast Kavu first, it's briefly targetable before Scion hits. The correct order: Scion → then all other threats under the hexproof umbrella.
Consign to Memory vs Leyline Binding Return
When your Leyline Binding is destroyed and a dangerous permanent returns (Atraxa, Primeval Titan), you cannot counter the return — it's not a triggered ability, it's part of Binding's own clause. Instead: counter the destroy spell targeting your Binding with Stubborn Denial (ferocious = hard counter for {U}). Or, re-bind the returning permanent immediately with another Leyline Binding from hand ({W} with full domain).
Psychic Frog + Kavu Loop
Kraven's power = greatest MV among permanents you control. Leyline of the Guildpact has MV 4 (even in play). Scion of Draco has MV 12. Phlage has MV 3. With Scion on the battlefield, Kraven is a 12/4 vigilance. With only Leyline: 4/4. Always check your highest-MV permanent before swinging — Kraven's attack math changes significantly.
Arena of Glory Haste Grant
Arena of Glory: {R}, {T}, exert this land — add {R}{R}. If that mana is spent on a creature spell, it gains haste. Use to cast Phlage or Territorial Kavu with haste, letting them attack the turn they enter without summoning sickness. Exert means Arena can't tap for mana again until your next untap step — plan around this.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 Kavu Is Everything
On the play, T1 Territorial Kavu is your single most important action. A 3/3+ on T1 with Leyline (or 4/4+ with Leyline + T2 Scion) puts immediate pressure that most decks can’t answer cleanly. Mulligan aggressively for T1 Kavu hands on the play. Missing T1 on the play means your T3 window is tighter and opponents have had time to develop stabilizers.
On the Draw
Leyline Hands Are Better
On the draw you see 8 cards, which dramatically improves Leyline odds. Keep slightly looser Leyline hands on the draw — even a slow start with Leyline is reasonable because it gives all your creatures hexproof and makes Kavu enormous. T2 Scion of Draco on the draw is a natural curve that still applies pressure on T3 before most opponents have fully stabilized.

When Disrupted

Leyline Answered
Pivot to Scion Beats
Leyline Binding or Wear // Tear removes Leyline. Kavu and Psychic Frog lose their massive power boosts. Pivot: rely on Scion of Draco’s hexproof grant (creatures with a colour of Scion’s colours have hexproof) and Tribal Flames at full Domain (5 damage to any target). You can still win through raw power — Kavu is still a 3/3 with ferocious counters, Frog still grows on discard. The ceiling is lower but the floor is still real.
Scion Removed
Race with Tribal Flames
Scion of Draco removed: hexproof protection is gone. Opponents can now target your Kavu and Frog with removal. Pivot to burn plan: Tribal Flames at 5 damage each + Lightning Bolt is 8 damage from hand. A 3-creature board + 2 Tribal Flames = 21+ damage. Race before they can clear your board. Bring in Consign to Memory post-board to protect your key pieces when they untap with removal available.

Engine 02 — The Ferocious Counter Wall
Setup
Territorial Kavu (5/5) or Scion of Draco (4/4) is in play. You have {U} or {1}{U} available. Ferocious is enabled — either creature qualifies as a 4+ power permanent.
Execution
Stubborn Denial ({U}): counter any noncreature spell (removal, sweepers, counterspells) for one blue mana — hard counter with ferocious, no pay-{1} escape clause. Cannot counter creature spells, but Leyline Binding ({W} with full domain) covers creatures. Three Stubborn Denial main + one SB gives consistent protection on your combo turn. With Ragavan connecting and Kavu applying pressure, your opponent is already using resources reactively — Denial keeps them from recovering.
Result
Your opponent must fight through {U} countermagic on every removal spell they cast while you have ferocious active. Combined with Scion's hexproof grant, targeted removal becomes effectively impossible. The counter wall protects your threats, advances your clock, and costs only incidental mana.
Engine 03 — Phlage Recursion Loop
Setup
Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury has died and is in the graveyard. You have 5 cards to exile from your graveyard and {1}{R}{W} available. The game has gone long — your initial threats have been answered.
Execution
Cast Phlage via Escape: exile 5 GY cards, pay {1}{R}{W}. Phlage's ETB deals 3 damage to any target and gains you 3 life. With Scion in play: Phlage enters as a 6/6 with hexproof, lifelink, first strike, trample, vigilance. A 6/6 first-striking trampling lifelinker is nearly impossible to profitably block.
Result
Phlage can be escaped repeatedly as long as you have graveyard cards to exile. Each recurrence triggers 3 damage + 3 life. In long games, Phlage is the engine that prevents opponents from ever stabilizing — they answer it, it comes back, they answer it again, and eventually run out of answers. With hexproof from Scion, they can't even target it.

Burn face: They have 10 or less life and 2 Tribal Flames in hand = cast both, win. Don’t attack into blockers.

Mixed: One Bolt + one attack per turn until they’re in Tribal Flames range.

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Line 01 — Critical
Kavu Counts Colors Among Lands You Control
Territorial Kavu gets a +1/+1 counter for each “different color” among lands you control — not each land. With Leyline, all your lands become all colors. But Kavu only counts up to 5 (WUBRG). Even with 10 lands under Leyline, Kavu is a 5/5 from the land color ability — not a 10/10. The size comes from other sources (ferocious, exert). Know the ceiling before calculating lethal.
Line 02 — Critical
Hexproof Doesn’t Stop Sacrifice or Sweepers
Scion of Draco grants hexproof to your creatures. Hexproof means they can’t be targeted by opponent’s spells or abilities. It does NOT protect against: Goblin Bombardment (sacrifice effect, no target), Wrath of God effects (doesn’t target), Monstrous Rage targeting itself. Against Boros Energy, always watch for Goblin Bombardment — Leyline Binding is your answer (exiles Bombardment for {W} with Leyline in play).
Line 03 — Critical
Leyline Binding Costs {W} Under Leyline
Leyline Binding normally costs {5}{W} but has domain: subtract 1 from its cost for each basic land type you control. With all 5 basic land types (via Leyline of the Guildpact), it costs {W} — one white mana to exile any nonland permanent. This is your primary permanent answer. Cast at instant speed during opponent’s attack or in response to ETB triggers. A T1 Binding on their Amulet of Vigor or Ragavan is a real play.

Tribal Flames — 5 Damage for {1}{R}
"Domain — deals X damage where X = basic land types you control." With 5 domain: 5 damage for {1}{R}. This is Lightning Bolt + 2. Hits any target (creature, planeswalker, player). 5 to face × 4 copies = 20 damage from Tribal Flames alone. Without Leyline: domain depends on fetching correctly. Fetch Triomes early (Zagoth Triome = Swamp Forest Island, 3 types from 1 land). Two different Triomes + a basic = 5 domain.
Territorial Kavu Attack Trigger — Discard or Exile
Whenever Kavu attacks, choose: discard then draw, OR exile a card from a GY. The discard-draw filters your hand (pitch lands, draw gas). The exile mode is GY hate — disrupts opponent's delve, escape, Unearth plans. Against Dimir Oculus: exile their Oculus from GY before they can Unearth it. Against Living End: exile their cycled creatures. Kavu attacks as a 5/5 minimum with Leyline — first strike + trample means it beats almost anything in combat.
Flare of Cultivation — Free Ramp by Sacrificing a Green Creature
"You may sacrifice a nontoken green creature rather than pay this spell's mana cost." Sacrifice a mana dork that's done its job → search for 2 basics (1 to battlefield tapped, 1 to hand). Free ramp + domain fixing. With Leyline: ALL your nonland permanents are all colors (including green) → any creature can be sacrificed. Without Leyline: only naturally green creatures qualify.
Leyline Removal Collapses Everything
If Leyline of the Guildpact is destroyed or exiled: all nonland permanents revert to their printed colors, all lands lose extra basic types. Domain count drops to whatever your actual basic land types are (often 2-3 without Leyline). Scion's keywords update IMMEDIATELY — your creatures lose hexproof, lifelink, etc. mid-combat if Leyline dies. Kavu shrinks. Binding's cost doesn't retroactively change (already on battlefield), but future Bindings cost more. Protect Leyline or accept the risk.
Chapter 05

Roles

Zoo wants to be the aggressor. But the ferocious counter-wall and Leyline Binding's flash exile give you a tempo game that most pure aggro decks can't access.
Stompy Aggressor
Primary role — Leyline draw, most matchups. Deploy large domain creatures with absurd efficiency. Kavu at 5/5 for {R}{G}, Scion for {2} flying. Apply a clock that demands immediate answers. Hold Stubborn Denial to protect threats. Win before they can stabilize. This is the Leyline game — most opponents have no realistic line of play against a T2 5/5 with hexproof.
Tempo Disruptor
Secondary role — vs. combo and ETB decks. Flash Doorkeeper Thrull to silence key ETB triggers. Use Leyline Binding at flash speed to exile threats on their turn. Counter key spells with Stubborn Denial (ferocious = hard counter for {U}). This mode slows their game while yours continues advancing. Against Esper Blink, Jeskai, Goryo's: your flash-speed disruption is more valuable than raw speed.
Burn Finisher
Tertiary role — Phlage escape + Lightning Bolt + Tribal Flames. When the board has stalled and creatures can't attack profitably, shift to direct damage. Phlage's escape ETB deals 3 damage. Lightning Bolt deals 3. Tribal Flames deals 5 with domain. Combined: 11 damage from non-combat sources at relatively low mana. Closes games where combat is stalemated.

When to Shift Roles

Against aggro (Boros Energy, Prowess): stay stompy. Your 5/5 Kavu with all five keywords wins combat against almost everything they can field by turn three. Don't try to be clever — apply pressure and hold up one Denial for their key removal.

Against ETB combo (Esper Blink, Jeskai Blink, Goryo's): shift to tempo disruptor. Doorkeeper Thrull is a first-round pick from your sideboard if it isn't already in your main. Deploy Thrull on their turn to silence the ETB they're telegraphing. Use Leyline Binding as a flash exile on their key permanent before it threatens.

Against control (Dimir, Azorius): the burn finisher role is most relevant. Pressure with hexproof threats they can't target, then burn them out when they find ways to stop combat. Consign to Memory is relevant here — counters their triggered abilities.

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • 01
    Not leading Kavu on T1 with a Leyline hand. Leyline is already in play at T0. T1 Kavu is immediately a 5/5+ or larger. Some pilots T1 a land and pass looking for more setup. There is no better T1 play than Kavu with Leyline — the clock starts now and every turn you delay gives them a turn to answer Leyline before Kavu connects.
  • 02
    Using Leyline Binding on a creature when a non-creature permanent is the real threat. Binding exiles any nonland permanent. Against Boros Energy: Bind Goblin Bombardment first, not Ragavan (which Pest Control handles). Against Amulet Titan: Bind Amulet of Vigor, not Primeval Titan (which your creatures race). The permanent that’s hardest to answer otherwise is the Binding target.
  • 03
    Casting Tribal Flames on a creature when you should be pointing it at face. Tribal Flames deals 5 damage — same as most Modern creatures’ toughness. Using Flames as removal burns a kill card for a trade. Only Flames a creature when it’s specifically stopping your attacks (a 5/5 blocker, Solitude) or when you have lethal damage without it. Default to face.
  • 04
    Playing into sweepers without Consign backup post-board. Post-sideboard they bring Wrath effects. Don’t tap out your whole hand for the board if you have no counter available. Present 2–3 threats and hold Consign to Memory — if they Wrath, counter it. One untapped blue source + Consign is worth more than the creature you’d deploy with that mana.

Role-Switching Signals

Switch to Burn Plan When
Board Is Contested
They’ve cleared your creatures twice and stabilized at 12 or below. Don’t rebuild the board — two Tribal Flames + Lightning Bolt = 13 damage from hand. Hold mana for burn rather than deploying more creatures into their removal. This plan requires 3 mana and 3 cards but bypasses their entire board.
Switch to Control When
Leyline Combo Is Gone
Leyline answered. Your creatures are now fair-sized (3/3–4/4 range). Pivot: deploy one threat at a time backed by Consign to Memory. Use Leyline Binding at flash speed as interaction. Don’t overextend into sweepers — one threat + open mana is the correct posture when you’re in fair mode.
Never Switch When
Leyline + Scion Are Both Active
Leyline in play + Scion in play = all your creatures are hexproof. There is no reason to play around anything — attack with everything. They can’t use targeted removal, they can’t block profitably. This position wins. Commit the board, attack, close.
Always Re-Evaluate
After Each Untap
Domain Zoo requires re-identifying its role every turn based on: do I have Leyline? Do I have Scion? What’s their life total? Am I in Tribal Flames range? This deck has three distinct win modes — pilots who autopilot one lose to disruption. Pilots who read each turn and switch cleanly win close games.

Your Leyline hand is a near-auto-win in most matchups. Your non-Leyline hand is a solid aggro deck that needs a plan.

The most-played deck in the format. Boros Energy operates on a similar aggro axis but with a different engine: Guide of Souls energy generation + Galvanic Discharge + Ocelot Pride token flooding. Their removal (Galvanic Discharge, Thraben Charm) is energy-costed and hits your smaller creatures efficiently. The critical difference: Scion of Draco gives your creatures hexproof — Galvanic Discharge, Fatal Push, and removal spells can't target Kavu or other creatures under Scion's protection. Goblin Bombardment is their kill condition that bypasses this hexproof (sacrifice doesn't target). Post-board they bring Wrath of the Skies (an energy sweeper) and Clarion Conqueror (shuts activated abilities — stops Phlage's escape? No — Escape is a casting alternative cost, not an activated ability. Clarion doesn't stop Phlage from escaping).

OutIn
−2 Psychic Frog+2 Pyroclasm (sweeps tokens + Ragavan)
−2 Tribal Flames+2 Pest Control

Pyroclasm sweeps their token board (Ocelot Pride 1/1 tokens, Ragavan, Guide of Souls all die to 2 damage). Pest Control destroys all MV≤1 permanents including Ragavan and Guide of Souls. Note: Goblin Bombardment is MV 2 — use Leyline Binding ({W}) to exile it. Doorkeeper Thrull doesn't help here — Boros has few ETB triggers.

Key needle: Goblin Bombardment (sacrifice a creature → deal 1 damage) can't be stopped by hexproof — it doesn't target. Pest Control from the sideboard or Leyline Binding (exiling Bombardment for {W}) is the answer. Always Bind Goblin Bombardment before it can chain kills.

Tameshi Belcher — 11.1% meta · Unfavorable

Your hardest matchup. Tameshi Belcher wins on turn four with a Charbelcher activation dealing 40+ damage. You can't race a turn-four kill with an aggro clock that often takes five or six turns to finish. Game one is poor — your only interaction is Stubborn Denial (counters Whir of Invention or Charbelcher activation? No — Charbelcher's activation is an activated ability, not a spell, and can't be countered). Stubborn Denial can counter Whir of Invention (instant, not a creature spell) or Disrupting Shoal. Post-board: four Consign to Memory provide dense disruption against their search and tutor spells. Stubborn Denial (ferocious) hard-counters Whir of Invention and other noncreature setup spells.

OutIn
−3 Doorkeeper Thrull+4 Consign to Memory (counters colorless artifacts)
−1 Tribal Flames

Consign to Memory ({1}{U} with ferocious) counters Whir of Invention (colorless {X}{U}{U}{U}) and Disrupting Shoal. Four copies of Consign means high density against their search package. Aggress fast — your clock is your primary plan.

Key needle: Stubborn Denial cannot counter Charbelcher's activated ability (activations are not spells). Consign to Memory counters Whir of Invention (the tutor that finds Charbelcher). Counter the search spell, not the activation. Apply maximum pressure — your aggro clock is the primary plan, disruption is backup.

Eldrazi Tron — 10.5% meta · Favorable

Eldrazi Tron is slow and colorless. Their threats (Thought-Knot Seer, Devourer of Destiny, Sire of Seven Deaths) come down on turn three or four at the earliest. You're attacking with a 5/5 hexproof on turn two. Leyline Binding exiles any of their threats for {W}. Thought-Knot Seer's ETB exiles a card from your hand — Doorkeeper Thrull silences this ETB if you flash it in response. Karn, the Great Creator's static ability shuts off activated abilities of artifacts — irrelevant to you since your creatures aren't artifacts. Chalice of the Void at 2 counters: Lightning Bolt (MV 1 — not hit at 2), Kavu (MV 2 — hit). Chalice at 1 hits Ragavan, Lightning Bolt.

OutIn
−2 Doorkeeper Thrull+4 Consign to Memory (counters colorless Eldrazi)
−2 Tribal Flames+2 Pest Control
−2 Psychic Frog

Wear//Tear destroys Chalice of the Void (artifact) or Ensnaring Bridge. Doorkeeper Thrull silences Thought-Knot Seer's exile ETB. Celestial Purge exiles a black or red permanent (Kozilek's Command is black? No — colorless. Purge doesn't hit Eldrazi.) Use Purge mainly for escape value. Leyline Binding is your best card here — exiles any Eldrazi for one white mana.

Key needle: Flash Doorkeeper Thrull in response to Thought-Knot Seer being cast. TKS resolves, enters without its ETB triggering — you don't exile a card. A 4/4 that just eats your creatures isn't as dangerous as one that also strips your hand.

Esper Goryo's — 5.7% meta · Favorable

Esper Goryo's depends on ETB triggers: Atraxa, Grand Unifier drawing 8+ cards on ETB, Ephemerate blinking for value. Doorkeeper Thrull shuts off Atraxa's ETB — Atraxa enters as a 7/7 flying body with no card draw. This is frequently a free win. Their disruption (Thoughtseize, Force of Negation, Solitude) can stop your threats, but Scion of Draco's hexproof grant means Solitude can't target your creatures and Thoughtseize takes from hand before Scion is in play. Territorial Kavu's attack rummage lets you exile their Atraxa from the graveyard — remove the reanimation target entirely.

OutIn
−2 Tribal Flames+4 Consign to Memory (counters FoN + free spells)
−1 Psychic Frog
−1 Doorkeeper Thrull

Consign to Memory ({1}{U} with ferocious) counters Force of Negation (free-cast), Goryo's Vengeance (if cast for free somehow), or any free spell enabling the combo. Doorkeeper Thrull silences Atraxa's card-draw ETB — she enters as a 7/7 with no cards drawn. Keep Thrulls for this matchup.

Key needle: Territorial Kavu's attack trigger can exile Atraxa from their graveyard before they reanimate it. Attack with Kavu early even if not profitable for damage — use the trigger to exile their graveyard. Remove Atraxa and the deck has no engine.

Izzet Affinity — 5.7% meta · Even

Izzet Affinity goldfishes quickly with Kappa Cannoneer growing into a massive unblockable threat. Doorkeeper Thrull shuts off Kappa's "whenever an artifact enters" counter trigger — Kappa enters but generates no counters from artifacts. Pinnacle Emissary's ETB token also gets silenced. Without Thrull, you need to race — your 5/5 Kavu with first strike and trample can't be profitably blocked by most affinity creatures. Post-board they bring Blood Moon (converts your fetchable duals to Mountains — you can't make blue/green) and Force of Negation.

OutIn
−2 Tribal Flames+2 Pyroclasm (sweeps their small artifacts)
−2 Psychic Frog+2 Thraben Charm (answers artifacts)

Pyroclasm sweeps their small artifact creatures simultaneously. Thraben Charm destroys an artifact at instant speed ({W}). Doorkeeper Thrull (from main) shuts Kappa Cannoneer's counter engine — Kappa still enters but gains no counters. Two Thrulls post-board means maximum ETB suppression.

Key needle: Flash Doorkeeper Thrull in response to any artifact creature entering the battlefield. Kappa Cannoneer's counter trigger fires on artifacts entering — Thrull silences it immediately. Without the counter engine, Kappa is just a 1/1 that costs 4 mana.

Esper Blink — 5.7% meta · Favorable

Your best structured matchup post-board. Esper Blink's entire engine runs on ETB triggers: Solitude exile, Overlord of the Balemurk mill, Phelia bounce, Quantum Riddler card draw. Doorkeeper Thrull silences all of them. Solitude can't exile creatures — it enters, does nothing, just sits as a 3/2. Overlord enters without milling. The deck's interaction collapses. Your hexproof creatures from Scion can't be targeted by Thoughtseize (black sorcery, targets a player not a permanent — Thoughtseize still works) or Solitude (targets a creature — can't target hexproof creatures). Note: Thoughtseize targets a player, not a creature, so it still strips combo pieces from hand even under hexproof.

OutIn
−2 Tribal Flames+4 Consign to Memory (counters Solitude evoke + FoN)
−2 Psychic Frog+2 Clarion Conqueror
−2 Doorkeeper Thrull

Doorkeeper Thrull is your MVP — flash it in response to any ETB creature they cast. Wrath of the Skies sweeps on a chosen energy level — you get the energy and choose MV threshold, which helps clear their smaller blink targets without hitting your own threats. Lightning Bolt is weak — their threats have higher toughness.

Key needle: Scion's hexproof grant means Solitude (target another creature) and Prismatic Ending (exile target permanent with MV ≤ X) cannot target your hexproof creatures. But Ephemerate can still blink their own creatures — Doorkeeper only stops the ETB trigger, not the blink itself.

Izzet Prowess — 5.2% meta · Favorable

Prowess kills by buffing Dragon's Rage Channeler and Monastery Swiftspear with cheap spells. Their 3/3 or 4/4 pumped creatures lose races to your 5/5 Kavu with first strike — first strike means Kavu deals damage before their 4/4 deals back, killing their attacker without dying. Mutagenic Growth (pay 2 life for +2/+2) can make their creatures survive first strike math — always be aware of the +2 life payment trick when attacking with Kavu. Lava Dart flashback (sacrifice a Mountain, deal 1 damage) can finish off Ragavan or a 1-toughness creature. With Scion: your creatures have hexproof, making their DRC and Swiftspear's go-wide strategy irrelevant.

OutIn
−2 Doorkeeper Thrull+2 Pyroclasm (sweeps Swiftspear, DRC)
−2 Tribal Flames+2 Pest Control

Pest Control destroys all permanents with MV 1 or less — kills Ragavan, Monastery Swiftspear, and any 1-drop threats they have. Wrath of the Skies sweeps at the right energy threshold. Consign to Memory is weak — Prowess has almost no triggered abilities. Doorkeeper Thrull is marginal — Prowess has no key ETB triggers.

Key needle: Mutagenic Growth costs 2 life, not mana — they can cast it at any time to save a creature from first strike damage. When attacking with Kavu into a 3/3, assume they have Mutagenic Growth and plan combat math for a 5/5 blocker rather than a 3/3.

Domain Zoo Mirror — 2.6% meta · Even

The mirror comes down to two things: who has Leyline of the Guildpact and who is on the play. With Leyline in play, both players have hexproof creatures — targeted removal is irrelevant. Leyline Binding for {W} is the key interaction — exile their Scion of Draco, their Territorial Kavu, before they can attack. First player to resolve Scion gets the first hexproof grant, but both players' Scions give hexproof to their own creatures. Note: Scion gives YOUR creatures hexproof, not their creatures — it's not a symmetrical effect. With both players on Leyline, combat is determined by sequencing and attack ordering with first strike.

OutIn
−2 Doorkeeper Thrull+2 Thraben Charm (answers their Leyline Binding)
−1 Tribal Flames+1 Stubborn Denial

Thraben Charm ({W}) destroys your opponent's key artifact or enchantment — answers their Leyline Binding targeting your creature at instant speed ({W}). Stubborn Denial (ferocious = hard counter) protects your own Scion or Binding from their interaction. If you can exile their Leyline with Leyline Binding, their creatures lose all keywords and shrink dramatically.

Key needle: In the mirror, two Leylines in your opening hand: put both into play if you think they have Wear//Tear. One Leyline destroyed still leaves the second. If they don't have enchantment removal, put only one in play and use the second as Kavu rummage fodder.

Ruby Storm — 3.9% meta · Unfavorable

Like Tameshi Belcher, Ruby Storm can kill faster than your aggro clock. They combo off with Desperate Ritual + Pyretic Ritual chains into Grapeshot or Empty the Warrens around turn three or four. Your clock is four to five turns. Stubborn Denial with ferocious hard-counters Past in Flames (sorcery, not a creature spell) for {U} — this stops their combo cold. Apply aggression AND hold counter mana. One Denial on Past in Flames can break their Storm turn entirely. Post-board: four Consign to Memory plus the extra Stubborn Denial give you dense interaction vs their chain.

OutIn
−3 Doorkeeper Thrull+4 Consign to Memory (counters PiF + rituals)
−1 Tribal Flames

Consign to Memory with ferocious ({1}{U}) counters Past in Flames directly (which requires cards in GY to cast at reduced cost). Endurance flashes in at instant speed to exile their entire graveyard — devastating against a Storm turn. Doorkeeper Thrull is useless — Storm has no ETB creatures.

Key needle: Counter Past in Flames with Consign to Memory ({1}{U} with ferocious) — it's a sorcery (noncreature spell). Without Past in Flames their storm chain fizzles. Hold counter mana open every turn from turn 2 onward once Kavu is in play.

Amulet Titan — 2.7% meta · Favorable

Amulet Titan needs three to four turns to assemble Amulet of Vigor + bounce lands + Primeval Titan. You're attacking with a 5/5 on turn two. Leyline Binding exiles Primeval Titan for {W}. Leyline Binding exiles Amulet of Vigor for {W}. Territorial Kavu's attack exile ability removes their graveyard cards (disrupting Spelunking or Titan rebuys). Their main deck has no interaction for your early creatures — no removal, no counters. Summoner's Pact can find Endurance or Arboreal Grazer — not threats you care about. Easily one of your best matchups.

OutIn

Pest Control destroys Amulet of Vigor (MV 1 artifact) and any MV≤1 permanents. Leyline Binding is your best card — flash exile of Primeval Titan ({W} with full domain). Keep {W} open on their key turn. Doorkeeper Thrull silences Titan's ETB land-fetch if you flash it in before Titan resolves.

Your hexproof creatures from Scion can't be targeted by Fatal Push or Murktide Regent's block (well — Murktide blocks but can't be Fatal Pushed either). Thoughtseize targets you (the player) not a creature — it strips your hand despite hexproof. Psychic Frog discards your hand to pump itself, but with hexproof creatures you can race it. Key: Leyline Binding exiles Psychic Frog or Murktide Regent at flash speed for {W}. Stubborn Denial counters their Counterspell (a noncreature spell) with ferocious. Chapter 08

Heuristics

Sixteen rules to carry into every round.
  1. 01
    Leyline in hand = automatic keep, always. Even a weak six-card hand with Leyline outperforms a strong seven without it. The free domain engine is worth a card. You can win off five cards if Leyline is one of them.
  2. 02
    Cast Scion of Draco before your other creatures. Scion gives every creature you control hexproof, lifelink, first strike, trample, and vigilance. Creatures cast after Scion enter under this protection immediately. Casting Kavu before Scion means it's briefly targetable. Sequence Scion first, then deploy all threats under the hexproof umbrella.
  3. 03
    Leyline Binding exiles the most dangerous permanent, not the most convenient one. Binding is your cleanest removal at {W}. Use it on Goblin Charbelcher, Amulet of Vigor, Psychic Frog, Primeval Titan — the cards that win the game if unanswered. Don't waste Binding on a 2/2 token when a game-ending threat is in play.
  4. 04
    Stubborn Denial cannot counter creature spells — use Leyline Binding ({W} with full domain) for creatures. Ferocious Stubborn Denial is a hard counter for any noncreature spell: removal, sweepers, counterspells, planeswalkers. Keep one blue open whenever Kavu or Scion is in play. Consign to Memory from the SB handles colorless spells and free-spell casts that neither Denial can counter.
  5. 05
    Doorkeeper Thrull must be in play when the creature enters, not before it's cast. Flash Thrull in response to the creature spell after it's been cast (on the stack) but before it resolves. Thrull's ability is a static effect — "while Thrull is in play, creatures entering don't trigger abilities." The window is between casting and resolution.
  6. 06
    Territorial Kavu's attack trigger is mandatory — choose carefully. On attack, you must choose: rummage (discard, draw) or exile a GY card. Against graveyard decks, always exile their key creature. Against non-GY decks, rummage to dig for missing pieces. You cannot decline both choices — pick the relevant one.
  7. 07
    Two Leylines in hand: play one, use the other as Kavu rummage fuel, unless they have enchantment removal. One Leyline establishes full domain. The second is redundant on the battlefield and better used as a discard card for Kavu's rummage to draw into threats. Exception: if you know they board in Wear//Tear or Nature's Claim, put both in for redundancy.
  8. 08
    Psychic Frog gets +1/+1 and flying when you discard — chain with Kavu's rummage attack. When Kavu attacks, choose to rummage (discard then draw). If Frog is in play, the discard triggers Frog into a 2/2 flier attacking alongside Kavu. This split attack — Kavu through ground defenses, Frog over the top — is often unblockable. The discard also fuels Phlage's escape cost and clears excess Leylines from hand.
  9. 09
    Scion's hexproof protects YOUR creatures, not THEIR creatures. "Each creature you control has hexproof if it's blue." Hexproof means opponents can't target those creatures. Your own targeting spells (Leyline Binding exiling your own creature? Doesn't happen normally) still work. Your opponent's Scion doesn't protect your creatures from their removal.
  10. 10
    Mutagenic Growth costs 2 life, not mana — Prowess opponents can cast it at zero mana. When attacking with Kavu into a 3/3, assume Mutagenic Growth can make it a 5/5. That means Kavu's 5/5 first strike trades instead of winning. Calculate worst-case combat math before committing to attacks where the opponent can pay life to survive.
  11. 11
    Pest Control AND Pyroclasm both hit your own creatures — sequence carefully. Pest Control destroys ALL MV 1 permanents including your Ragavan. Pyroclasm deals 2 damage to EVERY creature including yours. Cast sweepers before deploying your Kavu and Scion (both survive), or accept the friendly-fire if the board clear is more important. Kavu (5/5) and Scion (4/4) survive both sweepers. Ragavan and Psychic Frog do not.
  12. 12
    Wear//Tear at instant speed in response to opponent targeting your Leyline Binding. If they cast Nature's Claim or Wear//Tear targeting your Binding (which would return the exiled permanent), respond by casting your own Wear//Tear targeting their key artifact or enchantment. Get value on the way out — don't let them trade one removal for one binding without cost.
  13. 13
    Against Eldrazi Tron: flash Doorkeeper Thrull in response to Thought-Knot Seer being cast. Thrull silences TKS's ETB exile effect. TKS resolves as a 4/4 with no hand disruption. This single interaction frequently wins the matchup — an unanswered TKS that strips your combo piece is devastating; a muted TKS is just a large body.
  14. 14
    Against Goryo's: attack with Kavu before casting threats, even into blocks. Territorial Kavu's attack exile can remove Atraxa or Griselbrand from their graveyard before they reanimate it. It's often correct to attack Kavu into a 2/2 chump blocker to exile their combo piece from the GY — losing Kavu to a block is worth it if you exile their win condition.
  15. 15
    Consign to Memory counters colorless spells and free-cast spells — the SB card the whole deck is built around post-board. With four copies in the SB, you board into a soft-lock vs Eldrazi, Belcher, Storm, Living End, and Esper Blink. The ferocious reduction ({1}{U} with Kavu or Scion) makes it essentially free once your board is developed. Always hold one blue open whenever Consign to Memory is in your hand and you're facing a key combo turn.
  16. 16
    Leyline Binding can be cast at any time including your opponent's turn — use it as flash exile. Flash on Binding means you can exile their threat on their attack step, in response to them casting something, or at any point you have {W} available. Don't telegraph by casting it on your main phase. The best Binding is the one that blanks their key action, cast in response.

Tuning Notes

The core 20 (4 Leyline, 4 Kavu, 4 Scion, 4 Leyline Binding, 4 Lightning Bolt) is locked. Everything else is meta-dependent.
If Combo Is Dominant (Belcher, Storm, Neoform)
+2 Mystical Dispute MD
When three or more fast combo decks make up 15%+ of the field, main-decking 1–2 Mystical Dispute is correct. Trim Consign to Memory (1–2 copies) and possibly Doorkeeper Thrull (1 copy). Dispute hits Whir of Invention, Goryo's Vengeance, and any blue combo piece for {U}.
If GY Decks Are Prevalent (Goryo's, Living End, Storm)
SB: Consign to Memory x4 is your anchor
The four-copy Consign to Memory package is the deck's primary post-board identity: it answers colorless Eldrazi, free-cast Living End, Storm's Past in Flames, Force of Negation, and Evoke Solitude all with one card. Don't trim below 4 copies unless your meta has zero of these. Against pure creature aggro, board them all out for Pyroclasm and Pest Control.
Tribal Flames vs Psychic Frog Count
3 Flames + 3 Frog (current) or adjust
Tribal Flames (5 damage for {1}{R}) provides reach vs life-gain decks and a burn-close plan. Psychic Frog provides an evasive threat and discard synergy with Kavu's rummage. In faster metas where closing quickly matters: keep 3 Flames. In creature-heavy metas where board presence matters more: cut 1 Flames for a fourth Phlage or third Ragavan.
Tribal Flames — Include or Exclude
0 or 2-3 copies
Some lists cut Tribal Flames entirely for more creatures; others run 2–3. Include Tribal Flames when: (1) the meta has lots of life-gain making burn necessary, (2) you face many stall-based strategies, (3) you want more reach to close games without attacking. Exclude when creature redundancy is more important than direct damage.
Phlage Count: 3 vs 4
3 copies (current) or 4
Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury as the top-end threat: the current 3-copy count balances opening hand pressure with late-game escape recursion. In slower, grindier metas, run 4 Phlage — the escape engine wins long games. In faster metas where turns 1-3 decide, keep 3 and use the slot for additional Doorkeeper Thrull or a second Thraben Charm.
Thraben Charm Flex Slot
1 main (current) or 2
Thraben Charm ({W}) is the most flexible card in the 75: destroy artifact, create token, or pump +2/+2. In artifact-heavy metas (Izzet Affinity prevalent), move to 2 main-deck copies and reduce Doorkeeper Thrull to 2. In token-heavy metas, the token mode adds to your board width. In creature-combat metas, the +2/+2 mode wins combat math. The one SB Thraben Charm becomes a 2nd copy if artifact answers are consistently needed.
