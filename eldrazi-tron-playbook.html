Chapter 01

Overview

"You don't need to interact with them. You need to be faster than their interaction."

Eldrazi Tron is Modern's premier big-mana strategy — a deck that combines the explosive mana acceleration of Urza's Tron lands with the raw power of colorless Eldrazi threats and the flexible toolbox of Karn, the Great Creator. When it works, it presents threats on turn 3 that most decks in the format simply can't answer. Thought-Knot Seer strips the opponent's key piece and applies a 4/4 clock. Ugin, Eye of the Storms exiles colored permanents as a cast trigger then again for every subsequent colorless spell. Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger eats two permanents on cast and attacks for indestructible 10/10 value.

The deck's strongest attribute is inevitability — in long games, colorless threats scale better than most decks in Modern can handle. Its weakness is speed: against combo decks that kill on turn 2–3 and against aggressive decks with removal that answers early creatures, Eldrazi Tron can be too slow. The post-board Karn toolbox partially addresses this, letting you fetch specific hate artifacts (Ensnaring Bridge, Trinisphere, Disruptor Flute) to answer any game plan.

The current build, piloted by Lucas Giggs to 5 consecutive MTGO Challenge Top 8s in December 2025, dropped Glaring Fleshraker for Sowing Mycospawn — a massive upgrade that ramps, provides a relevant body against aggro, and punishes slow opponents by exiling their lands when kicked.

Deck Identity

Primary Win Condition
Karn Toolbox + Threats
Karn fetches silver bullets from the sideboard for any game plan, while threatening a +1 that turns any artifact into an attacker. The actual threats (Ulamog, Ugin, TKS) close games that Karn stabilizes. Together they form a complete game plan.
Mana Acceleration
Three Layers
Urza Tron (7 mana when assembled), Ugin's Labyrinth (ETB exile MV 7+ for {C}{C}), and Eldrazi Temple ({C}{C} for colorless Eldrazi). With any two of these plus a Talisman, you can have 5–7 mana on turn 3 without Tron assembled.
Disruption Package
Karn Static + TKS
Karn's static shuts off activated abilities of opponents' artifacts. Thought-Knot Seer strips a card from hand on entry. Together they disrupt nearly every game plan in Modern without spending additional mana after they resolve.
Blind Spot
Linear Combo Speed
Ruby Storm kills turn 2–3. Tameshi Belcher kills turn 4. Simic Neoform kills turn 2–3. Eldrazi Tron's clock is too slow to race these in game 1 without Karn + hate piece. Post-board Disruptor Flute and Trinisphere dramatically improve these matchups.
Key Strength
Resilient to Removal
Colorless threats are immune to color-based hate. Ulamog has indestructible. Karn's artifacts survive Supreme Verdict. The deck plays through most removal suites by simply casting another threat — the mana is always there to refill.
Meta Position
Tier 2 · 5.5%
Consistent performer at RCQs. Excellent against midrange and fair blue decks. Struggles against fast combo and Affinity. LucasGiggs' December 2025 run (5 top 8s) validated the Sowing Mycospawn build as optimal for the current meta.

Oracle Accuracy — Critical Interactions

Karn, the Great Creator
{4} · Planeswalker
Static: activated abilities of artifacts YOUR OPPONENTS control can't be activated. This shuts Mox Opal (opponent's), Springleaf Drum, Aether Spellbomb, etc. Your own Mox Opal/artifacts still activate normally. −2: reveal artifact from outside the game (your SB) OR choose face-up artifact in exile → put into hand. Karn can fetch artifacts you exiled with Devourer of Destiny, Relic of Progenitus, or Ugin's Labyrinth return.
Ugin, Eye of the Storms
{7} · Planeswalker
Two exile triggers: (1) cast trigger exiles up to 1 colored permanent, (2) whenever you cast a colorless spell, exile up to 1 colored permanent. Every Eldrazi, every Talisman activation being paid by tapping for colored mana... wait — the trigger says "cast a colorless spell." Talismans aren't spells. But Expedition Map, Kozilek's Command, Thought-Knot Seer, Sowing Mycospawn (devoid = colorless) all trigger it. Ugin doesn't trigger on lands.
Ugin's Labyrinth
Land
ETB: you MAY exile a colorless card with MV 7+ from your hand. If you do, {T} adds {C}{C}. Without an exiled card, it only adds {C} — a basic wastes slot. The exiled card can be returned at any time by tapping Labyrinth. Return the card when you want to cast it or to reuse Labyrinth's "exile" slot with a different card. Critically: Ulamog, Ugin, Devourer are all MV 7+ and qualify.
Sowing Mycospawn
{3}{G} · Devoid
Devoid = colorless spell (triggers Ugin). When you CAST (not ETB): search library for a land, put it onto battlefield, shuffle. Kicker {1}{C}: when cast if kicked, exile target land. The land search is unconditional — even unkicked, this is a {3}{G} 3/3 that ramps. Kicked, it also permanently removes an opponent's land — stunning in Amulet, Living End, and Storm matchups where the opponent has few lands.
Devourer of Destiny
{5}{C}{C} · 6/6
Reveal from opening hand → at your first upkeep, look at top 4, exile 3, keep 1. The exiled 3 cards go to exile, not hand — Karn can retrieve them with −2 later. When you cast Devourer, exile target colored permanent. Karn can fetch the exiled Devourer-exiled cards back. This creates a two-card loop: Devourer opens exiling their permanent → Karn retrieves useful exile zone cards.
Kozilek's Command
{X}{C}{C} · Kindred Instant
Choose TWO of four modes: create X Spawn tokens, scry X then draw, exile creature MV ≤ X, exile up to X cards from graveyards. Minimum cost {2}{C}{C} for X=0 (two modes at 0). With Eldrazi Temple: {C}{C} + {2} from land = X=2 on turn 2. Against aggro: X=2 creates 2 tokens (blockers) AND exiles the biggest threat. Versatile removal + ramp + GY hate in one card.

Why One Forest?

The single Forest is the green mana source for Sowing Mycospawn ({3}{G}). It's fetchable via Expedition Map when needed. The minimal green splash means you almost never need to sequence around it — Talismans provide {G} in a pinch via their colored output.

Chapter 03

Game
Flow

Eldrazi Tron's game plan has one core requirement: get to 7+ mana before your opponent kills you or locks you out. Everything else — the threats, the disruption, the toolbox — follows from having a dominant mana advantage. The three accelerants (Tron, Labyrinth, Temple) each contribute differently, and learning when to prioritize each is the deck's fundamental skill.

Mana Acceleration Priority

Priority 1
Urza's Tron
All three Urza lands = 7 mana from 3 land slots. Expedition Map tutors any piece. Assembled by turn 3, Tron enables Karn, Ugin, and Ulamog all in the same turn. Without Tron, the deck is a slower Eldrazi deck. With Tron, it's the most mana-efficient deck in Modern. Always map for the missing piece when you have two.
Priority 2
Ugin's Labyrinth
Exile a colorless MV 7+ card → {C}{C} per turn. With Ulamog or Devourer of Destiny in opening hand: Labyrinth turn 1 = 2 mana immediately. The exiled card returns to hand when you tap Labyrinth to return it — don't forget this. Sequence Labyrinth before other lands when holding a qualifying card.
Priority 3
Eldrazi Temple
{C}{C} for casting colorless Eldrazi spells or activating abilities of colorless Eldrazi. Critically: this mana can pay for Kozilek's Command (colorless cost) and for Thought-Knot Seer's {C} requirement. With Temple + a Talisman turn 2, Thought-Knot Seer drops on turn 2.

Turn-by-Turn Blueprint

Turn 1
Play your best mana land. Priority: Ugin's Labyrinth if holding Ulamog/Ugin/Devourer. Otherwise Eldrazi Temple for the immediate {C}{C}. Reveal Devourer of Destiny if in opening hand — your upkeep, look at top 4 and exile 3 (find missing Tron piece, Karn, or key threat). Map if you have two Tron pieces and need the third immediately.
Turn 2
Ideal play: Talisman of Resilience + pass. With Temple: Thought-Knot Seer ({3}{C} = Temple {C}{C} + Talisman {1} + one land {1}) — this is the fastest disruption play. With two Labyrinth + Talisman: 5 mana available. Expedition Map if you have two Tron pieces and need the third. Play Sowing Mycospawn if ramping is more important than disrupting.
Turn 3
With Tron assembled: 7 mana. Cast Karn (4 mana, fetch hate artifact or +1 to make blocker). OR cast Ugin, Eye of the Storms (7 mana, immediately exile colored permanent on cast, then exile more on each colorless spell). With Mycospawn ramp but no Tron: still 5-6 mana available for Karn or multiple smaller threats.
Turn 4+
Ulamog on turn 4 (10 mana from Tron + Temple + Talisman). Cast trigger exiles two permanents. Indestructible 10/10 that mills 20 cards per attack. At this point the game should be over or close to it. Ugin's −11 (exile top of library, cast all for free) closes unwinnable board states.

Mulligan Framework

Keep any hand that can assemble Tron by turn 3 OR present a meaningful threat by turn 2. The deck can function without Tron assembled — Temple + Talisman + Thought-Knot Seer on turn 2 is a legitimate non-Tron game plan. Always keep hands with Expedition Map + two Tron pieces.

Keep
Tron Hands
Any hand with 2+ Tron pieces and Expedition Map. Even with slow other cards, the Map finds the third piece on turn 2, enabling Karn/Ugin on turn 3. Karn from turn 3 wins most games singlehandedly — it doesn't need support.
Keep
Temple + Threat Hands
Eldrazi Temple + Talisman + Thought-Knot Seer is a legitimate turn-2 Seer hand. Strip their key piece before they can act. Even if Tron never assembles, a sequence of TKS → Karn → Ugin pressures most decks out of the game.
Mulligan
Pure Threat Floods
4+ expensive threats with no acceleration. Ulamog in hand without Labyrinth or Tron pieces means you can't cast it until turn 10. Always ship back hands that can't develop mana — the threats are only good when you can cast them.
Conditional Keep
Karn Hands
Karn on turn 3 is often enough to win. Keep if you have 4+ mana available by turn 3 and Karn in hand. Even without Tron, Temple + Talisman + a third source = 4 mana on turn 3. Karn fetching Ensnaring Bridge wins most aggro games immediately.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 Chalice or Map
On the play, T1 Chalice of the Void on 1 shuts down every 1-CMC spell your opponent planned. T1 Expedition Map tutors a Tron piece for T2 assembly. Both are correct depending on their deck — if you saw their hand via T1 Duress in their SB, deploy accordingly. Against aggro: Chalice at 1 immediately. Against combo or control: Expedition Map to race to Tron.
On the Draw
Extra Card Finds Tron Piece
On the draw you see 8 cards, dramatically improving Tron assembly odds. Keep slightly looser hands on the draw — two Tron pieces + Map is a keep on draw even without a third piece. Against aggro on the draw: Chalice at 1 is urgent — they’ve had a T1 and may have deployed already. Prioritize Chalice over Tron development if their board is ahead.

When Disrupted

Tron Disrupted
Map + Karn Rebuild
Spreading Seas or Blood Moon disrupts Tron. Pivot: Expedition Map finds Eye of Ugin or the missing Tron piece anyway (basic land types don’t apply to colorless utility lands). Karn, the Great Creator fetches any artifact from SB including Ensnaring Bridge or Trinisphere. Eldrazi still cast from colorless mana — you can still function on colorless even without full Tron assembly.
Key Threats Answered
Karn Tutor Chain
TKS and Ulamog both answered: Karn, the Great Creator’s -2 fetches any artifact from outside the game (your SB). Fetch Ensnaring Bridge to lock out their attacks, Trinisphere to tax their spells, or Relic of Progenitus for GY hate. Karn’s +1 makes their artifacts unable to activate — shut off their Mox Opal, Aether Vial, or Chalice they set against you.
Chapter 04

Core
Engines

1
Tron Assembly Engine
Primary Mana Engine
Setup
Find All Three Pieces
Urza's Mine + Urza's Power Plant + Urza's Tower = 7 mana from 3 land slots. Run 4 of each (12 total). Expedition Map ({1} artifact, sacrifice for {2}: search any land) finds the missing piece. Devourer of Destiny's opening-hand ability lets you look 4 deep on upkeep to find missing pieces. With 12 Tron lands + 4 Maps, consistency is high.
Execution
Turn-3 Seven Mana
Turn 1: one Tron piece. Turn 2: second Tron piece + Map. Turn 3: sacrifice Map, find third piece, play it. Now 7 mana available. Cast Karn, the Great Creator (4 mana) + activate, OR cast Ugin, Eye of the Storms (7 mana) for an immediate exile of their best permanent. Tron makes both of these possible on the same turn.
Result
Dominant Mana Advantage
With Tron assembled, you're generating 7+ mana every turn while opponents generate 3–5. Each subsequent turn allows a bigger threat or more toolbox fetches from Karn. Blood Moon destroys this engine — plan alternative lines (Eldrazi Temple + Talisman) for Moon matchups, and always fetch a basic Forest with Sowing Mycospawn when Moon is a threat.
2
Karn Toolbox Engine
Adaptable Answer Engine
Setup
Resolve Karn on Turn 3
Karn, the Great Creator costs {4}. With Tron: 3 mana remaining after Karn for +1 or activations. Karn's static immediately shuts off all activated abilities of opponents' artifacts — Mox Opal stops tapping, Aether Spellbomb can't bounce, Springleaf Drum stops tapping. Then the −2 fetches the perfect answer from the sideboard.
Execution
Fetch the Right Silver Bullet
−2 puts the SB artifact into your hand. Immediately cast it (you still have 3 mana after Karn on Tron). Ensnaring Bridge vs aggro (they can't attack with 0 cards in hand). Trinisphere vs Storm/Storm-adjacent combo. Disruptor Flute vs Kappa Cannoneer or specific combo pieces. Damping Sphere vs Amulet, Tron mirrors, and Storm. Walking Ballista as removal or finisher.
Result
Game-Specific Lockout
Each artifact fetched by Karn addresses a specific matchup's win condition. Ensnaring Bridge combined with zero cards in hand (discard Labyrinth target, spend hand) = they literally cannot attack. Trinisphere = Storm spells cost 3+ mana each, combo chains collapse. The toolbox means Eldrazi Tron has an answer to almost every game plan in the format.
3
Ugin Exile Cascade
Board Clear Engine
Setup
Resolve Ugin on Turn 3–4
Ugin, Eye of the Storms costs {7} — requires Tron or significant acceleration. The cast trigger immediately exiles one colored permanent (opponent chooses which). Ugin enters with loyalty. Every subsequent colorless spell you cast exiles another colored permanent. This includes Talismans, Map activations producing mana — no wait, those are abilities not spells. Actual colorless spells: Eldrazi, Dismember, Kozilek's Command, Map itself.
Execution
Cascade of Exiles
Turn 3 Ugin (cast trigger: exile their best permanent). Turn 4: cast Thought-Knot Seer (colorless spell → exile another colored permanent) + Kozilek's Command (another colorless → exile another). In 2 turns, you've exiled 3 of their permanents while presenting a board of threats. Ugin's +2 gains 3 life and draws a card — strong in longer games. −11 ultimate ends games immediately.
Result
Opponent's Board Evaporates
Against non-colorless decks (everything except the mirror), Ugin creates an asymmetric board state where their permanents disappear while yours remain. Against Affinity (mostly colorless artifacts), Ugin's exile triggers mostly whiff — this is one of the rare times Ugin underperforms. Against Boros Energy, Blink, or any heavily colored deck, Ugin is devastating.

Lines & Tricks

  • Devourer of Destiny + Karn retrieval: Reveal Devourer from opening hand. At upkeep, look at 4 cards, exile 3, keep 1. The 3 exiled cards sit in exile. Later, Karn, the Great Creator −2 can retrieve artifact cards among them. This lets you effectively "choose 1 and store 3 useful artifacts for Karn" from your opening hand look.
  • Relic of Progenitus + Karn recovery loop: If an artifact goes to your graveyard (destroyed by artifact removal), exile it with Relic. Then Karn −2 retrieves it from exile. This recovers destroyed Karn toolbox pieces that would otherwise be gone permanently.
  • Ugin's Labyrinth return timing: You can return the exiled card by tapping Labyrinth as a cost. Do this on the turn you want to cast Ulamog — tap Labyrinth, return Ulamog to hand (now Labyrinth produces only {C}), then play another land and cast Ulamog normally with Tron mana. The "lose {C}" from Labyrinth is worth it to cast the threat.
  • Eldrazi Temple for Kozilek's Command: Kozilek's Command is {X}{C}{C}. Eldrazi Temple provides {C}{C} for colorless Eldrazi spells. Command is a Kindred Instant — Eldrazi. So Temple's {C}{C} applies. With Temple + one mana source on turn 2: X=1, two modes at 1 = create 1 Spawn AND exile 1-MV creature (kills Ragavan on turn 2).
  • Sowing Mycospawn land sequencing: The land Mycospawn searches goes directly onto the battlefield (no summoning sickness for a land). Use this to find the missing Tron piece mid-turn, then play another land drop normally. This gives you Tron on the turn Mycospawn resolves even if you were one piece short.
  • Prioritize early Eldrazi Temple over Tron piece 1: Sometimes your turn-1 choice is Temple vs a Tron land. If you're planning to cast Thought-Knot Seer or Kozilek's Command on turn 2, Temple is the better opening — it accelerates your actual plays. If you're setting up for Karn on turn 3, a Tron piece is correct since you need two pieces for Map to function.
  • Disruptor Flute as a Karn fetch vs combo: In game 1 vs Storm or Belcher, Karn can fetch Disruptor Flute from the SB. Name Ral, Monsoon Mage to shut down the flip trigger (Ral's activated abilities can't be activated). OR name Goblin Charbelcher to shut off the activation. Flute's static makes chosen cards cost {3} more AND their activated abilities are locked — two different effects.

Engine Priority Order

Priority 01
Assemble Tron
Urza’s Mine + Urza’s Power Plant + Urza’s Tower = 7 colorless mana on T3. This is the foundation of everything. Before deploying threats or interaction, always advance Tron assembly. Expedition Map tutors any land — fetch the missing Tron piece. Ancient Stirrings finds any colorless card including Tron lands. Prioritize assembly over any other action.
Priority 02
Karn/Ugin Planeswalker
Once Tron is assembled, the T3 play is Karn, the Great Creator or Ugin, the Spirit Dragon. Karn immediately +1s to lock their artifacts or -2s to fetch from SB. Ugin at -X wipes all colored permanents. These are your primary threats and engines — Eldrazi cast alongside them but the walkers generate ongoing value the Eldrazi don’t.
Priority 03
TKS / Ulamog Clock
Thought-Knot Seer (4 mana) exiles a card from hand and provides a 4/4 body. Ulamog (10 mana) exiles two permanents on cast and attacks with annihilator 4. TKS is your primary disruptive threat and clock. Ulamog is the game-ending threat. Deploy TKS when Tron is online and you need to strip their answer before your Karn resolves.

Lines & Tactics

Lines &
Tactics

"Chalice, TKS, and All Is Dust all have non-obvious rules. Know them before you sit down."

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Critical
TKS Leaving Gives Them a Draw
When Thought-Knot Seer enters, it exiles a card from their hand. When TKS LEAVES the battlefield, the opponent draws a card — any card, not the exiled one. Killing your own TKS (sacrifice to dodge removal) gives them a card. Bouncing TKS returns the exiled card AND triggers the draw. Never bounce TKS with a card exiled unless it’s worth giving them a card.
Critical
Chalice at 1 Stops Your Own Interaction Too
Chalice of the Void on 1 counters ALL spells with MV 1, including your Walking Ballista at 0 (pays 0 then enters as a 0/0), your Dismember, and any MV-1 SB card. Set Chalice after you’ve deployed your own MV-1 pieces. Chalice at 0 stops free spells: Force of Negation alternate cost, suspend spells being cast for free. Know what each setting stops before placing.
Critical
All Is Dust Hits Only Colored Permanents
All Is Dust makes each player sacrifice all colored permanents. Your Eldrazi, your lands, your Chalice, your Karn, your Ulamog — all colorless, all survive. Their colored creatures, planeswalkers, enchantments die. This is not a symmetrical Wrath. Cast it when they have a colored board and you have a colorless one. Your colorless board survives their response to it.

Timing Windows

When to Hold Mana vs Spend It
The core timing decision for this deck: every land drop and every spell cast is a commitment. Know before your upkeep which lines require open mana this turn and which don’t. Holding mana = passing priority; spending it = committing to a plan. Make this decision in your upkeep, not in response to their action.
When to Cast at Their End Step vs Your Main Phase
End-step casts maximize information: you’ve seen their full turn, they’re often tapped out. Main-phase casts maximize mana availability for follow-up. Know which of your key spells gain most from end-step timing and which need main-phase mana backup.
Chapter 05

Role
Assignment

RAMP & THREATEN
Your default role vs midrange and control. Assemble Tron, resolve a threat that demands an immediate answer, then follow up with another. The opponent can't keep up with the mana advantage. Don't overcommit with both Karn and Ugin before seeing how they respond — resolve one, see their answer, then apply the second pressure.
vs Boros Energy, UW Control, Jeskai Blink
DISRUPT & LOCKOUT
Vs combo, aggro, and speed. Prioritize Karn → silver bullet over raw threats. Ensnaring Bridge locks out creature decks before they assemble a lethal board. Trinisphere stops Storm/Belcher before they can chain. Thought-Knot Seer strips the combo piece. The Karn toolbox is a answers the specific deck you're facing, not a generic threat.
vs Ruby Storm, Belcher, Affinity, Aggro
PIVOT
When Tron isn't assembling and you're behind on board, pivot to Eldrazi Temple + Talisman mana for turn-2 Thought-Knot Seer. This non-Tron plan is slower but still viable. Kozilek's Command on turn 2 via Temple is a legitimate early-game stabilization play. Don't sacrifice all your resources trying to force-assemble Tron when the Temple plan is more immediate.
vs fast clocks when Tron is offline

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • 01
    Deploying TKS when Tron isn’t assembled yet. TKS costs 4 mana and gives them a card when it dies. Casting TKS on 4 non-Tron mana when you could spend that turn assembling Tron is usually wrong. The exception: you need to strip their GY hate or counter before they can use it on your next turn.
  • 02
    Chalice at 1 when you still need to cast MV-1 cards yourself. Set Chalice after you’ve deployed all your MV-1 spells (Expedition Map, Relic of Progenitus). A Chalice at 1 that counters your own Map is worse than no Chalice at all.
  • 03
    Bouncing TKS without checking the draw trigger. When TKS leaves the battlefield, opponent draws a card. If you bounce your own TKS (vs removal), the draw trigger still fires. Evaluate whether the draw they get is worse than losing TKS permanently.
  • 04
    Not fetching Ensnaring Bridge with Karn vs aggro. Karn -2 fetches Ensnaring Bridge from the SB when your hand is empty. Bridge locks out all attackers with power greater than the number of cards in your hand. The moment you empty your hand, all their attacks are stopped. Karn + empty hand + Bridge = locked board.

Role-Switching Signals

Switch to Lockdown When
Their Board Is Faster Than Yours
Tron isn’t assembled by T3 and they have a creature board. Karn -2 fetches Ensnaring Bridge immediately. Trinisphere taxes every spell they cast. Chalice at 1 stops their interaction. Switch from developing Tron to deploying hate pieces that slow them enough to assemble on T4.
Switch to Full Gas When
Tron Is Online
Tron assembled, 7+ mana available. Stop developing, start threatening. Karn T3, TKS T4, Ulamog T5 if the game goes long. Every turn you have 7+ mana is a turn they should be losing. Don’t pass without casting something impactful.
Never Slow Down When
Karn + Empty Hand Available
Karn in play + 0 cards in hand = fetch Ensnaring Bridge immediately. This position is often a clean lock — they can’t attack (Bridge), you have Karn generating ongoing value. Execute this line the moment the conditions are met.
Identify Before T1
Race vs Lock
Their deck races you (Boros, Prowess, Domain) → Chalice T1, lock them out of interaction. Their deck combos you (Storm, Living End) → Map T1, race to Tron faster than they kill. Their deck controls you (Jeskai, UWC) → TKS T4 to strip counters, Ulamog T5 to close.

The Matchup: Boros Energy is generally favorable — your threats are too large for their removal package (Lightning Helix, Galvanic Discharge). Lines & Tactics

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Tron wins on inevitability. Understand exactly what stops you and what doesn't."
Chalice of the Void — Counters on Cast, Not on Resolution
Chalice of the Void says "counter target spell with mana value equal to X." This counter fires when a spell with that MV is CAST — before it resolves. Chalice at 1 counters every MV-1 spell your opponents cast. Important: Chalice only counters SPELLS — it doesn't stop activated or triggered abilities, and it doesn't stop creatures entering the battlefield via non-spell means (Aether Vial, reanimation). Against humans with Vial: Chalice at 2 stops them casting 2-drops, but Vial at 2 puts them directly onto the battlefield without casting — Chalice doesn't care. Set Chalice at the MV where their most critical spells cluster.
Thought-Knot Seer — Two Separate Triggers
TKS has two triggered abilities: (1) "When this enters the battlefield, look at target opponent's hand. You may exile a nonland card from it" — fires on ETB, (2) "When this leaves the battlefield, target opponent draws a card" — fires on leaving. These are two separate triggers that resolve independently. If TKS is exiled or bounced, the second trigger fires and they draw. If you flicker TKS with Ephemerate, BOTH triggers fire — they lose a card on the first ETB and draw a card when TKS blinks out, netting zero. Never flicker TKS frivolously. The ETB exile trigger can be countered by Consign to Memory (it's a triggered ability).
Reality Smasher — Discard Only When Targeted, Not When Swept
Reality Smasher says "Whenever Reality Smasher becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, counter it unless its controller discards a card." This ONLY fires when something TARGETS Smasher specifically. Non-targeting removal bypasses it entirely: Supreme Verdict ("destroy all creatures" — no target), All Is Dust ("each player sacrifices" — no target), Wrath of God, bounce effects that hit "all" or "each." Against Smasher: use sweepers over targeted removal to avoid the discard tax. If you must use targeted removal, have a disposable discard fodder ready.
All Is Dust — Only Colored Permanents; Artifacts and Lands Survive
All Is Dust says "each player sacrifices all colored permanents they control." The key word is COLORED. Colorless permanents are unaffected — your own Chalice of the Void, Expedition Map, Sol Ring, Tron lands, Walking Ballista all survive All Is Dust. Opponents lose their colored creatures, enchantments, and planeswalkers, but keep any colorless permanents. Against decks with colorless threats (Eldrazi mirrors, other Tron): All Is Dust doesn't help. Against every fair colored deck: it's a one-sided board wipe that preserves your entire mana base and artifacts.
Karn Liberated — -3 Permanently Exiles; Ult Restarts the Game
Karn's -3 exiles target permanent. Unlike most exile effects, there's no "until Karn leaves" clause — the exile is permanent. The ultimate (+14 to 5 loyalty, then restart the game with exiled cards in play) is not just a game-restart — any cards exiled by Karn's -3 ability (from any game state, including cards opponents exiled from your hand on previous turns) enter the new game under your control. In long games, track what's been exiled. Karn's -3 can also exile your own permanents to set up the ultimate, pulling back key pieces like Ulamog or Emrakul that may have been answered.
Ulamog's Cast Trigger Fires on CAST, Not ETB
Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger says "when you cast this spell, exile the top twenty cards of target player's library." This triggers on CAST — if Ulamog is put onto the battlefield without casting (reanimation, Goryo's Vengeance, Show and Tell), the cast trigger DOES NOT fire. You get the body but not the exile. However, Ulamog's annihilator 4 and indestructibility are ETB-independent — those are static abilities that are always active when the creature is in play. The cast trigger is also a triggered ability — opponents with Consign to Memory can counter it (preventing the exile while Ulamog still enters).

Heuristics

  1. Karn's static only affects opponents' artifacts. Your own Mox Opal, Talisman, and other artifacts still activate normally under your own Karn. Only the opponent's artifact activated abilities are locked. This is why Karn is safe to cast even when you have active artifacts on board.
  2. Expedition Map on turn 2, always, with two Tron pieces. If you have any two Tron lands in hand or play plus a Map, activate Map on turn 2. Don't wait to "save" it. Tron on turn 3 enables Karn or Ugin — delaying Map by one turn costs you an entire combo-level play.
  3. Ugin's Labyrinth: exile your most powerful card, not your most convenient one. Ulamog gives you {C}{C} per turn and can be recast from hand later via the return ability. Always prioritize the highest-MV card you'll want to cast eventually. Exiling a Talisman in Labyrinth is wrong — Talismans cast for 2, not 7+.
  4. Sowing Mycospawn land search before playing your land drop. Mycospawn's land search triggers on CAST, and the land ETBs immediately. Play Mycospawn before your land drop — the searched land counts as a land entering this turn and can be the land drop you would have played anyway, saving you one land from hand.
  5. Thought-Knot Seer gives them a card when it LEAVES, not when it enters. This means bouncing TKS with your own effects (or replaying it) triggers the leave ability, giving them a card. Don't blink or bounce TKS carelessly. In the mirror, if they TKS you and you kill their TKS, they draw a card — factor this into your combat math.
  6. Karn can fetch exile zone artifacts via Devourer of Destiny interaction. Devourer reveals from opening hand, exiles 3 cards to exile zone. Later, Karn −2 can retrieve artifact cards from that exile zone. This creates a slow hand-selection engine: keep 1 card from 4 with Devourer, retrieve useful artifacts later with Karn.
  7. Disruptor Flute: name cards, not archetypes. You name a specific card name. "Izzet Affinity" is not a card. Name the specific card that wins them the game: "Kappa Cannoneer" (their key threat), "Pinnacle Emissary" (their key enabler), "Ruby Medallion" (their key reducer). Think one card ahead of what they need to execute their game plan.
  8. Eldrazi Temple + Kozilek's Command on turn 2 vs aggro. Temple {C}{C} + one land {1} + one mana source {1} = {X}{C}{C} with X=2. Mode 1: create 2 Spawn tokens (0/1 blockers). Mode 4: exile 2 cards from graveyard. Against Prowess, this creates blockers AND strips Lava Dart from GY in the same turn for {4} total mana.
  9. Prioritize Temple over Tron piece 1 when planning T2 TKS. Temple provides {C}{C} for TKS's colorless mana requirement. A hand of Temple + Talisman + TKS + Tower can cast TKS on turn 2 ({3}{C} = Tower {1} + Talisman tap {1} + Temple {C}{C}). This disruption play is often more valuable than a slow Tron build that presents no threat until turn 3.
  10. Relic of Progenitus recovers destroyed artifacts for Karn. If an important artifact (a Karn toolbox piece you already cast) gets destroyed and goes to your GY, tap Relic to exile your own GY. Then Karn −2 retrieves the artifact from exile. This two-card loop recovers key pieces that would otherwise be permanently lost.
  11. Blood Moon destroys your Tron mana base — always Mycospawn for Forest. Under Blood Moon, all your nonbasic lands become Mountains. Only your basic Forest produces green mana. Sowing Mycospawn can find the Forest before Moon resolves (or after if you have green from Talisman). Talisman of Resilience produces {G} as a colored option — use this to cast Mycospawn under Moon.
  12. Kozilek's Command is Eldrazi tribal — Eldrazi Temple pays {C}{C} for it. Kozilek's Command is an Eldrazi spell. Eldrazi Temple's {C}{C} applies. This means on turn 2, Temple + any one-mana source = {X=1}{C}{C} for Command (X=1). Mode 3 exiles a 1-MV creature (Ragavan, DRC, Monastery Swiftspear). This is the cleanest turn-2 answer to the most dangerous 1-drops in the format.
  13. Ugin, Eye of the Storms doesn't trigger on mana abilities or activated abilities. The trigger says "whenever you cast a colorless SPELL." Tapping Talisman for mana isn't casting a spell. Playing a land isn't casting a spell. Only actual casting of colorless cards (Eldrazi, artifacts, colorless instants) triggers Ugin's exile ability. Plan your turns to maximize the number of colorless spells cast while Ugin is in play.
  14. Torpor Orb shuts off YOUR Thought-Knot Seer too. Don't fetch Torpor Orb if you're planning to cast Thought-Knot Seer. With Orb in play, TKS enters as a 4/4 with no ETB — they don't reveal their hand, you don't exile a card. Only fetch Orb when your game plan no longer requires TKS's ETB, or when the matchup's threats are entirely ETB-based.
  15. Sowing Mycospawn's land search is on CAST, not on resolution. If Mycospawn gets countered or removed in response, you've already searched for and put the land onto the battlefield (the land search triggered and resolved before the creature entered or the counter could stop the land trigger). Only the creature entering is prevented — the land is already in play.
  16. In the mirror, first Karn wins. Whoever resolves Karn first gets to fetch Disruptor Flute (naming opponent's Karn) or Ensnaring Bridge. Either play creates a game-deciding lock. Race to Karn by prioritizing Tron assembly over threats in the first 3 turns. Don't play TKS over developing Tron unless TKS steals their missing Tron piece.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand that assembles Tron by T3 OR has a T2-3 threat. Don't keep all Eldrazi and no Tron.
  • Tron rule: Urza's Mine + Tower + Power Plant. You need all three. Map and Expedition Map find missing pieces.
  • Chalice rule: Chalice at 1 on T1 is your best G1 play against most Modern decks. Lead with it when possible.
  • TKS rule: Thought-Knot Seer on T2 via Sol land + Tron piece. Take their best card. Always.
  • All Is Dust rule: Sorcery speed — attack BEFORE casting All Is Dust if you want to deal combat damage first.
  • Sideboard rule: Karn's wish: name a specific answer. Know your Karn targets for each matchup before G2.
