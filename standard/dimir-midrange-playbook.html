Chapter 01

Deck
Identity

"On the play, develop Kaito and force them to have the answer. On the draw, deploy Enduring Curiosity and punish them for attacking into it."

Dimir Midrange is the premier tempo-control strategy in Standard. Every threat in the deck operates at instant speed or generates immediate value on entry — Floodpits Drowner stuns their best creature, Tishana's Tidebinder locks out ETBs and planeswalker abilities, Kaito, Bane of Nightmares becomes a hexproof 3/4 Ninja the moment it resolves on your turn. The opponent is always reacting. You are always the one asking questions.

The deck sits between aggro and control — not slow enough to fold to pressure, not so fast it runs out of gas. Enduring Curiosity is the card that enables the grind: once it's in play as an enchantment it's essentially permanent card draw, and the opponent has no clean answer. Every creature that deals combat damage draws a card. The deck generates more resources than it spends and wins long games against midrange and control while having just enough tempo tools to survive aggro.

The Zuxas build leans toward the aggro end of the spectrum. Azure Beastbinder and Floodpits Drowner represent a flash creature package that operates at instant speed on the opponent's turn, and Cecil, Dark Knight provides a high-risk/high-reward {B} 2/3 deathtouch that transforms into a 4/4 lifelink when your life drops to half — a unique life-pressure payoff that rewards attacking aggressively with the rest of your threats.

What This Deck Does

Primary Plan
Flash Threats + Card Draw
Deploy threats at instant speed on the opponent's end step. Each threat generates value immediately. Enduring Curiosity turns every successful attack into a card. Kaito's 0 ability generates a card every turn. You're always drawing into more answers.
Secondary Plan
Tempo Disruption
Tishana's Tidebinder counters their key ability and strips the permanent. Floodpits Drowner stuns their best attacker and threatens to shuffle it away. Azure Beastbinder blanks their best permanent on attack. Every creature is also disruption.
Win Condition
Out-Resource + Close
Generate card advantage until the opponent runs out of answers, then close with Restless Reef attacks, Cecil transforming into a 4/4 lifelink, or Kaito's emblem making all your creatures bigger. The deck rarely goldfishes — it wins by exhausting opponent options.

Why Play This Deck

Play This If
You Want Flexibility
The deck shifts roles game-to-game and even turn-to-turn. Against aggro you're the control deck using flash blockers and removal. Against control you're the beatdown deck attacking through counters and protecting threats. Against midrange mirrors you're the tempo deck with more instant-speed plays. No two games feel the same.
Skip This If
You Want Simple Lines
This deck has a high decision density. When to deploy Kaito vs. hold mana for Tidebinder. When to attack with Cecil knowing you'll lose life to Darkness. When to promise the fish for Into the Flood Maw. When to blight your own creature for Requiting Hex. Every turn has meaningful choices. It rewards reps.

Meta Position

As of April 2026, Standard's top tables are shaped by three Tier 1 pillars: Mono-Green Landfall (11.09%), Izzet Prowess (9.82%), and Izzet Lessons (9.81%). Dimir occupies a unique position — split into two distinct archetypes: Dimir Excruciator (6.85%, the combo-control build that won Pro Tour Lorwyn Eclipsed) and Dimir Midrange (6.23%, the tempo-value engine you're reading about). Combined, Dimir is the most represented color pair in Standard at ~13%. Below Dimir sit Izzet Spellementals (5.32%), Simic Rhythm (4.12%), Azorius Flash (3.04%), and Mono-Red Aggro (3.02%).

The post-Pro Tour metagame shakeup was seismic: Badgermole Cub strategies crashed from 45% of the PT field to single digits, replaced by Landfall, Izzet variants, and the Excruciator combo. Dimir Midrange's core cards (Kaito, Enduring Curiosity) remain legal despite repeated ban speculation. The deck is favorable against Dimir Excruciator (~57% WR per tournament data) and control strategies (~64%), but struggles against Mono-Green Landfall (38%) and Mono-White Aggro (37%). This makes it a metagame-dependent choice: when the field tilts toward midrange mirrors and control, Dimir thrives. When aggro surges, you need precise sideboarding.

Zuxas's build is tuned for the current meta: Requiting Hex ×3 main handles aggressive early creatures, and the flash creature package at 1-2 mana lets you develop on their end step while holding mana for removal.

Chapter 03

Gameplan

"Turn 1: set up mana. Turn 2: apply pressure. Turn 3: deploy Kaito or Enduring Curiosity and never let them breathe."

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turns 1–2
Setup + First Pressure
Turn 1: Fetch untapped dual or play basic. Spyglass Siren for a 1/1 flier + Map token. On the play: Siren → surveil/explore on turn 2 is your fastest start. Cecil, Dark Knight at {B} on turn 1 — instant 2/3 deathtouch but remember Darkness drains you each hit. Requiting Hex held for their turn-2 threat. Spell Snare open if on the draw vs known 2-drop openings.
Turn 3
Deploy the Engine
On the play: Kaito, Bane of Nightmares — immediately becomes a 3/4 hexproof Ninja. Force them to have an answer before their next turn. On the draw: Enduring Curiosity — flash it at their end step to set up turn-3 attacks that draw cards. Kaito on the draw is also correct if they haven't deployed a threat; the hexproof on your turn means they can't target it at all on your turn.
Turns 4–5
Lock + Close
Floodpits Drowner at their end step — stun their attacker, hold shuffle threat. Tishana's Tidebinder in response to any ability. Azure Beastbinder attacks blanking their best permanent while being unblockable by most creatures. Restless Reef activates as a 4/4 deathtouch attacker when you have spare mana. The game typically ends on turn 6-7 with the opponent having exhausted their removal on redundant threats.

Key Decision Points

Kaito vs Enduring Curiosity
The Turn-3 Choice
Play Kaito when on the play — you can deploy him on your turn and he immediately has hexproof while you have priority. Opponents can't answer him at instant speed on your turn. Play Enduring Curiosity when on the draw — you're a turn behind, so flash it in at their end step. Curiosity as a 4/3 enchantment creature is difficult to answer, and the draw engine operates even without attacking. Both plans are correct in their context — the mistake is playing Kaito on the draw when you needed a draw engine, or playing Curiosity on the play when you had tempo advantage.
Cecil's Darkness Trigger
The Life Math
Cecil at {B} is an outstanding rate but every point of damage he deals also comes off your life. Do the math before attacking: if Cecil deals 3 damage to their creature through blocking, you also lose 3. Plan when you want to flip him — at 10 life (half of 20) he transforms into the Redeemed Paladin. Don't flip him when you're already losing — 4/4 lifelink needs attacks to gain life back, and if their board is too wide the lifelink won't recover fast enough. Deploy Cecil when you can absorb 4-6 life loss and expect to flip on your own schedule.
The Into the Flood Maw Gift
When to Promise the Fish
No gift: bounce any creature (sorcery speed if they have one). Gift: bounce any nonland permanent AND they get a 1/1 Fish. Always promise the gift when bouncing: enchantments, artifacts, planeswalkers (Enduring Curiosity, a resolved walker). The 1/1 Fish is irrelevant if you're bouncing something worth 3+ mana. Don't promise the gift when the 1/1 Fish is relevant — they're at low life, flood the board against tokens, or they have empty-board value from the Fish token.
Floodpits Drowner Timing
Stun First, Shuffle Later
ETB stun goes on the stack when Drowner enters. The activated shuffle ability ({1}{U}{T}: shuffle self and stunned creature) is a separate ability. Sequence: Deploy at their end step → stun their threat → on your turn, either attack around the stunned creature or pay {1}{U} to shuffle both away. Don't immediately shuffle — assess whether the stunned creature poses a greater threat than a 2/1 body on your board. Sometimes keeping Drowner to attack with is better than trading both away.

Mana Management

This deck is greedy with mana. The key discipline: never tap out on your own main phase if their threat could require an instant-speed answer. Hold {B} for Requiting Hex or Long Goodbye. Hold {1}{U} for Floodpits Drowner at their end step. Hold {2}{U} for Tishana's Tidebinder in response to their ETB. The deck plays as a control deck on the opponent's turn and an aggro deck on your turn. Gloomlake Verge is your most important land — always plays as {U} immediately, then {B} once you have any Island or Swamp in play.

Multiversal Passage enters tapped unless you pay 2 life — you almost always pay the life. This deck has enough life gain access (Cecil lifelink, Requiting Hex blight, Qarsi Revenant lifelink) to absorb the early life payments. Don't sacrifice speed for life on Passage. Starting Town is your turn-1 fixer — enters untapped in turns 1-3, fixes any color for 1 life.

Disruption
Lines

"Present a threat. Be hella annoying. Every interaction is a two-for-one when you hold flash mana and make them play into it."

This chapter documents the specific instant-speed interaction sequences that define how Zuxas's build plays against creature-based strategies — especially Mono-Green Landfall. The deck's flash package lets you develop your board on their end step while holding mana for disruption on their turn. These lines are the reason you keep mana up even when deploying threats looks more profitable.

The Cub Trap

Badgermole Cub is Mono-Green's primary ramp engine. Their T2 Cub into T3 Ascension or Sapling Nursery is the nut draw that wins them the game. How you handle it depends on whether you're on the play or the draw — these are two completely different plans.

On the Play
Flash Drowner → Upkeep Stun
On the play you have tempo — use it proactively. If you can hold {2}{U} during their T3 (when Drowner becomes affordable on your end of T2 into T3), flash Drowner during their upkeep before their main phase. Their Llanowar Elves and Badgermole Cub untap at the start of their turn, then immediately get tapped by Drowner before they can use them. They walk into their main phase with zero mana from dorks. Their T3 Ascension or Nursery gets delayed a full turn.

On the play your Spyglass Siren → Kaito curve is also live. Develop Kaito to apply pressure and let the Drowner follow on their T4-T5 when they try to stabilize.
On the Draw
Hold Hex or Spell Snare for Cub
On the draw you're a step behind — lean reactive. Badgermole Cub costs {1}{G} = MV 2. Spell Snare ({U}) hard counters it as it's being cast. Requiting Hex ({1}{B}{W}) exiles it on ETB before the earthbend resolves. Both answers are available the turn they play Cub on T2 if you kept mana open.

The key discipline on the draw: don't tap out on your T2. Hold {U} for Snare or {1}{B}{W} for Hex even if it means passing with nothing deployed. Letting Cub resolve unmolested on the draw gives them T3 Ascension for free, which sets up a board state you can't catch up to efficiently.
Line A — Kill the Cub, Keep the Land
Requiting Hex on Cub ETB
Sequence: They cast Badgermole Cub ({1}{G}) on T2. ETB triggers — "earthbend 1: target a land you control." Before the earthbend resolves, cast Requiting Hex ({1}{B}{W}, exile target creature MV ≤ 2). Cub is exiled. The earthbend trigger is on the stack with no legal target (Cub is gone and the ability was "earthbend 1" — it resolves harmlessly with no effect since the land selection hasn't locked in yet). Wait — actually the trigger says "target a land you control" at casting, not at resolution. If you hex the Cub after it's entered but the ETB is still on the stack, the ETB resolves but there's no Cub to operate with. The land just doesn't get earthbent.

Result: They spent {1}{G} for nothing. No earthbent land, no mana acceleration this turn.
Line B — Bounce the Earthbent Land
Into the Flood Maw (No Gift) Targets the Creature
Oracle ruling: Into the Flood Maw's no-gift mode says "return target creature an opponent controls to its owner's hand" — no land restriction. The earthbent land is simultaneously a land AND a creature, so it qualifies as a creature target. You can bounce it.

Sequence: Cub ETBs, earthbend resolves — their Fabled Passage is now a 1/1 creature with haste. Cast Into the Flood Maw (no gift). Target the earthbent Fabled Passage as a creature. It returns to their hand as a plain land — the earthbend effect ends as it leaves the battlefield. They lose the earthbent creature AND their Fabled Passage disappears from play for this turn.

When to gift vs not gift: Don't gift if you just want to bounce the earthbent creature — the no-gift creature mode handles it for free. Gift the fish only if you need to hit a non-creature enchantment like Sapling Nursery or Earthbender Ascension instead.

Result: Their ramp land is gone. Cub's earthbend generated nothing useful. The 1/1 that was about to attack is back in their hand as a Fabled Passage. You gave them nothing.
Line C — Full Suppression
Hex Cub + Drowner Board Tap
The premium sequence against T2 Cub:
  1. They play Badgermole Cub. ETB earthbend on stack.
  2. You cast Requiting Hex in response — Cub is exiled, earthbend fizzles.
  3. On their T3 (they play Icetill Explorer or Ascension), flash in Floodpits Drowner EOT.
  4. ETB taps their Explorer + any other creatures. They untap into a tapped board.

Two cards (Hex + Drowner) destroyed two of their turns. Your Kaito on T3 now enters a board they can't use.
Line D — Delayed Cub Kill
Let Earthbend Resolve, Then Hex
When you let the earthbend resolve first: Cub earthbends onto a Fabled Passage (two landfall triggers happen — they get value). THEN you Hex the Cub afterward. You lose the double-trigger prevention, but you still exile their primary mana doubler. The earthbent Passage returns tapped at EOT anyway.

When is this better? When you want to see which land they earthbend (tells you their hand contents — earthbend onto fetch = they have another land drop). When you're saving the response mana for their more dangerous T3 play and letting T2 Cub resolve is acceptable.

The Fabled Passage Ambush

This is the most under-known interaction in the matchup. Tishana's Tidebinder counters activated and triggered abilities. Fabled Passage has an activated ability: {T}, Sacrifice this land: Search for a basic land, put it into play tapped (then untap if 4+ lands). The sacrifice is a cost — it's paid before the ability goes on the stack. If you Tidebinder the activated ability after they sacrifice the Passage, they lose the Passage AND don't get the land.

The Interaction
Tidebinder Counters Fabled Passage
Sequence:
  1. They tap and sacrifice Fabled Passage to activate it (sacrifice is the cost — Passage is now gone).
  2. The ability goes on the stack: "Search for a basic land card, put it onto the battlefield tapped, then shuffle. Then if you control 4+ lands, untap that land."
  3. You flash in Tishana's Tidebinder ({1}{U}{G}), ETB: counter target activated or triggered ability.
  4. Target the Fabled Passage activated ability. It's countered.

Result: They sacrificed a Fabled Passage for literally nothing. They're now down one land they planned to use as a landfall trigger + fetch, and the search effect is gone. Their life total takes 3 from Tidebinder's ETB.
Other Tidebinder Targets vs Green
Activated & Triggered Abilities
Tidebinder can counter any activated or triggered ability — not just Fabled Passage:
  • Escape Tunnel unblockable mode — they sacrifice Tunnel to make Harmonizer unblockable. Counter it. Tunnel is gone, creature is still blockable. Combo stops.
  • Mightform Harmonizer landfall trigger — counter the "double target creature's power" triggered ability. Their combo doubling doesn't happen this land drop.
  • Earthbender Ascension quest counter trigger — counter the +1/+1 + trample granted trigger (at 4+ counters). Their creature doesn't get the boost this land drop.
  • Ba Sing Se earthbend activation — their land doesn't become a threat creature.
  • Sapling Nursery indestructible activation — counter it in response to your sweeper. The indestructible clause doesn't apply. Their Treefolk board is now vulnerable.

The Drowner Tap-Out Punish

Floodpits Drowner is your most powerful flash creature vs Mono-Green because it can tap any number of targets simultaneously. The earthbent land is a creature — Drowner's ETB can tap it too, which is the correct call when you want to lock it down without bouncing it. Three distinct timing windows, each with a different goal:

Line: Upkeep Stun
Flash During Their Upkeep
On the play, their creatures untap at the start of their turn — then during upkeep (before draw, before main phase) you can flash Drowner in. Their Llanowar Elves just untapped. Drowner ETB taps it again before they ever get to use it for mana. They go into their main phase with Elves tapped, Badgermole Cub tapped, anything else tapped. They lose a full turn of mana without being able to respond during their own upkeep. This is the correct play on the play whenever you can afford it — deny their T3 mana acceleration before it happens, not after.
Line: EOT Tap
Their End Step Drowner
Flash Drowner at their end step after they've deployed their board and passed. ETB taps: Icetill Explorer, Badgermole Cub, the earthbent land-creature, Sazh's Chocobo — everything. They pass to your turn with a full board that can't block. You untap, attack freely. The earthbent land is a creature — it's a valid Drowner tap target and also can't generate mana while tapped, cutting off any mana tricks they planned.
Line: Combat Tap
Flash During Their Attack Step
After they declare attackers, flash Drowner. ETB taps everything that isn't attacking — their non-attackers can't block, Badgermole Cub can't tap for mana this turn, the earthbent land-creature is locked. Their attack is isolated to exactly what they declared. No tricks, no backup, no mana. Block efficiently and swing back with your full board next turn.

Into the Flood Maw — Full Application Map

Into the Flood Maw has two modes that are easy to underuse because the gift mechanic makes the card look like a tradeoff. It isn't — both modes are strong and serve different purposes. The no-gift mode is a clean, unconditional creature bounce that's available every time without giving them anything. Don't reflexively gift the fish just to hit enchantments when the creature mode already solves your problem.

No Gift — Default Mode
Return Any Creature (Free)
Oracle text: "Return target creature an opponent controls to its owner's hand."

No conditions, no cost, no 1/1 fish. Targets any creature they control. This is your primary use case in almost every matchup because the creatures are almost always the more urgent problem:
  • Earthbent land-creature — the no-gift mode targets any creature they control, and the earthbent land qualifies. Bounce it, it returns to their hand as a plain land. Their earthbend effect ends. They lose the 1/1 haste creature and the land disappears from play for this turn — the best of all worlds, and you gave them nothing.
  • Badgermole Cub — bounce before they tap it for mana. They spent {1}{G}, get it back in hand. No earthbend, no ramp.
  • Icetill Explorer — bounce the engine piece. They lose their extra land play and GY recursion for the turn. Every land they played from GY this turn still happened, but the continuous effect ends.
  • Mightform Harmonizer — bounce it mid-combo. They're about to double a creature's power with the next land drop — bounce Harmonizer in response, doubling doesn't happen, combo reset.
  • Leatherhead, Swamp Stalker — bounce before she attacks and removes her hexproof counter. Her hexproof counter means you can't target her, but you can bounce her in response to their attack declaration before combat damage is dealt.
  • Mossborn Hydra — bounce a Hydra with 8 counters before they can land the killing blow. They spent several turns growing it, now it's back in hand at base stats (all +1/+1 counters are on the permanent, which goes to hand — but counters on creatures are removed when they go to hand).
  • Fecund Greenshell — bounce the 10-land anthem before it can buff their entire team.
Gift the Fish — Upgraded Mode
Return Any Nonland Permanent (They Get a Tapped 1/1)
Oracle text (gift promised): "Instead return target nonland permanent an opponent controls to its owner's hand." They create a tapped 1/1 blue Fish token first.

I. The Double-Kaito Ninjutsu Trick

One of the most powerful and under-utilized sequences in the deck. When you have two copies of Kaito, Bane of Nightmares:

★
Double Kaito Sequencing
Step 1
Activate First Kaito
Use your in-play Kaito's ability (0: surveil 2, draw for each damaged opponent, OR −2: tap + double stun counter on a creature, OR +1: emblem giving Ninjas +1/+1). This uses up the Kaito's ability for the turn. He's now a creature (hexproof on your turn) ready to attack.
Step 2

Declare Kaito (or any unblocked creature) as an attacker. II. Play/Draw Engine Selection Framework

The single highest-impact decision in Dimir Midrange happens before the first spell resolves: which engine do you lean on based on play/draw?

On the Play
Kaito Is Your Engine
You're one turn ahead. The optimal curve is T1 Spyglass Siren or Cecil → T2 hold open for Requiting Hex / interaction → T3 Kaito. Deploying Kaito before their 3-mana interaction comes online means hexproof protects him from anything they could answer with. Kaito on the play generates an average of +10.3 points of win rate. Against aggro on the play, keep Kaito and trim Enduring Curiosity. You can be proactive because the tempo advantage lets you present threats before they answer. If you end up in a topdeck war on the play with Kaito active, you're drawing extra cards every turn and they're not.
On the Draw
Curiosity Is Your Engine
You're a tempo behind. Flash Enduring Curiosity at their end step — it arrives as a 4/3 with the draw trigger active, and you untap with your full turn to attack with it and generate cards. Against aggro on the draw: Curiosity provides a large body that blocks their threats AND draws cards when your creatures connect. The opponent is ahead on board — Curiosity's card advantage catches you up. If Curiosity dies, it returns as an enchantment they can't remove. On the draw vs control: Curiosity at their end step dodges their sorcery-speed answers and forces them to use a counterspell or bounce at instant speed — if they don't have it, you've established an engine they can never answer.

III. Cecil Life-Math Decision Tree

Cecil, Dark Knight is one of the most impactful but most misplayed cards in the deck. His Darkness trigger — dealing damage to you equal to the damage he deals — creates a risk/reward calculation every attack step.

Safe to Attack
Life ≥ 14
At 14+ life, Cecil attacking as a 2/3 deals 2 to them and 2 to you (Darkness), leaving you at 12+ with deathtouch threatening blocks. This is almost always profitable — the damage pressure + deathtouch threat outweighs the self-drain. Attack freely.
Calculate First
Life 8–13
Danger zone. Ask: "If I take 2 from Darkness AND they burn me for 3 on their turn, am I dead?" Against Mono-Red with 3 cards in hand at 10 life: attacking with Cecil puts you to 8, and two burn spells kill you. Don't attack. Against Midrange at 10 life: no burn in their deck, attack freely. Context is everything.
Strategic Play
Life ≤ Half (Flip Zone)
When you're at half your starting life (10 in Standard), Cecil transforms into a 4/4 with lifelink. The transformation is the payoff — a 4/4 lifelink swings the race by 8 points (4 damage dealt + 4 life gained). The trick: you can manipulate your life total to flip Cecil using shock lands, Multiversal Passage (pay 2 life), or Bitter Triumph (pay 3 life). Sometimes it's correct to deliberately take damage from your own lands to flip Cecil when the board state favors a 4/4 lifelink.

IV. Mandatory Draw Awareness

Enduring Curiosity's card draw trigger is mandatory — you cannot choose not to draw. In grindy matchups (Dimir Mirror, Control), this creates a real deck-out risk. With 4 creatures connecting each turn under Curiosity, you draw 4 extra cards per combat. A 20-card library becomes a 4-turn clock on yourself.

When to Track Library Size
Library Watch Points
Start counting your library when it drops below 15 cards. With Curiosity active and 3+ creatures, you're drawing 4–5 cards per turn cycle (draw step + combat triggers). At 15 cards remaining: you have ~3 attack steps before decking. At 10 cards: ~2 attack steps. If the game is close but not won, consider intentionally NOT attacking with some creatures to reduce draw triggers — a 1/1 Siren not attacking costs you 1 damage but saves 1 card. Win the game before your library runs out.
Emergency Measures
Preventing Self-Deck
If you're ahead on board but low on library: (1) stop attacking with non-essential creatures — only attack with enough to be lethal in 1-2 turns, (2) use Floodpits Drowner's shuffle ability — shuffling Drowner and their creature back into libraries effectively adds 2 cards to your library, (3) don't flash in additional creatures if you don't need more attackers — each new creature is another mandatory draw. The most common Dimir Midrange self-loss in the mirror comes from pilots who don't track their library and draw themselves out while "winning."

V. Mana Sequencing — Turn-1 Traps

Dimir's manabase has hidden complexity that trips up even experienced pilots. Gloomlake Verge is the primary culprit.

Trap #1
Verge Without a Basic
Gloomlake Verge taps for {U} unconditionally, but only produces {B} if you control an Island or Swamp. On turn 1 with ONLY Verge in play: you can cast Spyglass Siren ({U}) but NOT Cecil ({B}) or Requiting Hex ({B}). If your hand has Cecil + Verge as your only land: you cannot cast Cecil turn 1. Solution: sequence your land drops so turn-1 Swamp/Watery Grave enables {B}, then Verge on turn 2 produces both colors.
Trap #2
Multiversal Passage Life Cost
Multiversal Passage enters as any basic land type but costs 2 life (enters as a copy of a basic, pays life like a shock land). Against aggro at 12 life: paying 2 to fix your mana puts you to 10 — exactly the threshold where their burn range kills you. Against control: the 2 life is irrelevant. Sequence accordingly: vs aggro, play basics and Verge first, Passage only when necessary. Vs control, Passage freely to fix for double-blue Enduring Curiosity.

VI. The Over-Sideboard Trap

"The most common way Dimir loses post-board is drawing the wrong half of its deck. Every card you board in should have a clear target — if you can't name the card or ability you're bringing it in for, leave it out."

Data shows that Dimir Midrange pilots lose more games post-board from over-sideboarding than from having wrong cards. The deck is synergistic — every small creature enables Kaito and Enduring Curiosity. Remove too many creatures and your engines lose power. The field data confirms this:

Rule of 4
Max 4 Cards In/Out
In most matchups, board 3-4 cards maximum. The deck's synergies (evasive creatures → Kaito ninjutsu → Curiosity draws) break down when you cut too many pieces. Even against aggro, keep enough creatures to enable your card advantage engines.
Data Warning

Side-Board Out Win Rate (SBOWR) vs Side-Board In Win Rate (SBIWR). When a card's SBOWR > SBIWR, you win MORE when it stays out. Example: Essence Scatter has 55.9% SBOWR vs 51.6% SBIWR — you're statistically better leaving it in the board. Track this metric for every SB card.
Common Mistake

81.5% of Dimir players bring Soul-Guide Lantern in vs Mono-Green Landfall. The card has a 32.7% side-in WR in that matchup — the worst SB card possible. Lantern does nothing meaningful against Landfall triggers. Only bring GY hate when the opponent's GY is their actual win condition.

VII. Ninjutsu Timing Windows

Kaito's Ninjutsu ability creates unique timing windows that even experienced opponents misread.

Post-Damage Ninjutsu
Draw off Curiosity, Then Deploy
You can use Ninjutsu during the end of combat step, AFTER damage has been dealt. This means: attack with Spyglass Siren → Siren deals 1 damage → Enduring Curiosity triggers, draw a card → if you draw Kaito off the trigger → use Ninjutsu to put Kaito in attacking (even though damage already happened) → Kaito can still use an ability post-combat. You effectively got the damage + draw trigger from Siren AND deployed Kaito in the same combat.
Defensive Ninjutsu
Saving Creatures from Removal
When an opponent targets your attacking creature with removal after blocks: if the creature is unblocked, you can Ninjutsu it back to hand in response, dodging the removal AND putting Kaito into play. The removal fizzles (no legal target), and Kaito arrives safely. This turns their removal spell into a 2-for-0 in your favor (they spent a card on nothing, you gained a Kaito). Always hold Ninjutsu mana when you have Kaito in hand and creatures attacking.

VIII. Matchup Role Transitions

Push lethal before they draw out of it.

IX. Reading the Opponent's Hand

Dimir Midrange's instant-speed nature gives you information advantages that most opponents don't realize they're leaking.

Signal #1
They Hold Mana Open
If the opponent passes with {1}{U} open: they likely have a counterspell. Deploy your less important creature first to bait it. If they counter: deploy Kaito next turn when they're tapped out. If they don't counter: they either don't have it or are saving it for something bigger — probe with the smaller threat and hold Kaito.
Signal #2
They Attack Into Open Mana
When the opponent attacks knowing you have flash creatures available: they either have removal for your flash blocker or they're bluffing. If you've seen their removal suite from Duress: deploy the flash blocker. If you haven't: sometimes it's correct to NOT block with the flash creature and instead deploy it post-combat for value. The 2 damage you take is worth the creature surviving.
Signal #3
They Don't Play a Land
If the opponent misses a land drop: they're flooding or stuck. Ramp up aggression immediately. Deploy threats on curve rather than holding for value. A mana-screwed opponent can't answer your threats efficiently — every turn they miss is a turn your Kaito generates free cards. Press the advantage before they draw out of it.

Into the Flood Maw — No-Gift Mode Targets Any Creature (Including Earthbent Lands)
Into the Flood Maw has two modes: (1) return target creature to hand, (2) no-gift mode — "You may return target creature an opponent controls to its owner's hand." The no-gift mode can target ANY creature an opponent controls — including a land that has been made into a creature by Earthbender Ascension's ability (earthbent lands are creature permanents while animated). This is the strongest use of the no-gift mode: returning an earthbent land returns it as a land to their hand, removing both their threat and mana source simultaneously.
Floodpits Drowner — Flash During Opponent's Upkeep for Maximum Mana Denial
Floodpits Drowner has flash and its ETB taps target creature an opponent controls. The optimal timing window: flash Drowner in during your opponent's upkeep step — after their creatures untap (untap step) but before their main phase. When Drowner's ETB taps a creature like Llanowar Elves, that creature is now tapped and cannot produce mana for the ENTIRE upcoming main phase. If you wait until their main phase, they can use the Elves for mana before you can tap it. Upkeep is the correct timing window for this interaction.
Kaito, Bane of Nightmares — Can't Activate the Turn It Enters
Kaito, Bane of Nightmares is a planeswalker with loyalty abilities. Planeswalkers enter with summoning sickness equivalent — you cannot activate a loyalty ability on the same turn a planeswalker enters the battlefield. Kaito enters, you cannot immediately use his -2 or any other ability until your NEXT turn. This means: deploying Kaito on turn 4 means his abilities are available on turn 5. Play around this by calculating whether Kaito's presence threatens anything before he can act — opponents should kill him before turn 5.
Phantom Interference — Soft Counter, Opponent Can Pay Through
Phantom Interference says "Counter target spell unless its controller pays {2}." This is a SOFT counter — the opponent has the option to pay {2} to let their spell resolve. This is meaningfully different from a hard counter like Counterspell (which cannot be paid through). Against a well-mana'd opponent in late game, Phantom Interference may not stop their key spell — they just pay {2}. Use Phantom Interference when you know they can't pay, or when the {2} tax forces them to leave your threat unanswered. Never count on Phantom Interference as a hard answer to a critical spell.
Psychic Frog — Flying Ability Uses YOUR Graveyard, Not Any Graveyard
Psychic Frog's flying ability: "Pay {0}: Exile three cards from YOUR graveyard. Psychic Frog gains flying until end of turn." The exile is specifically from YOUR graveyard — you cannot exile from opponent's. This matters: (1) you need 3 cards in your own GY to activate, (2) activating it removes your own resources (cards you might need for other plans), (3) under Rest in Peace there is no graveyard and this cannot activate. Don't use Frog's flying ability carelessly — those 3 exiled cards won't come back.
Badgermole Cub and Earthbender Ascension — Spell Snare Hard Counters Both
Spell Snare counters target spell with mana value exactly 2. Badgermole Cub has mana value 2 ({1}{G}). Earthbender Ascension has mana value 2 ({1}{G}). Spell Snare hard counters both for a single {U} with no pay-through clause. This is relevant in your sideboard guide: when boarding in Spell Snare against Mono-Green, you're getting a perfect answer to their two most critical pieces. Heuristics

"The most common way Dimir loses is drawing the wrong half of the deck. Heuristics prevent that."
  1. 01
    On the play, Kaito at 3. On the draw, Enduring Curiosity at 3. On the play you can develop Kaito before they have answers available — he has hexproof on your turn and they can't answer him at instant speed while you're taking your turn. On the draw you're a tempo behind; Enduring Curiosity flashed in at their end step sets up a draw engine that operates regardless of whether you're ahead or behind.
  2. 02
    Tishana's Tidebinder goes on the stack after the ability, not after the permanent resolves. Flash Tidebinder in response to an activated or triggered ability — not in response to the spell being cast. The sequence: opponent casts creature → creature resolves and enters the battlefield → ETB ability goes on the stack → THEN flash Tidebinder → counter the ETB ability → the permanent also loses all abilities while Tidebinder is on the battlefield. If you flash Tidebinder in response to the creature spell, it's already in play by the time you target an ability.
  3. 03
    Enduring Curiosity as an enchantment cannot be answered by creature removal. Once Curiosity returns from dying as a creature, it's an enchantment — not a creature. Shoot the Sheriff, Long Goodbye, Tishana's Tidebinder's ability countering, Essence Scatter — none of these work. The opponent needs bounce (Into the Flood Maw, similar) or exile to remove it. Recognize when their removal suite can't answer your enchantment and play accordingly — no need to protect something that can't be targeted.
  4. 04
    Don't attack with Cecil if Darkness damage will put you in burn range. Cecil's Darkness trigger means every point of damage he deals also drains you. Do the math before every Cecil attack: if Cecil deals X damage (to their creature or player), you lose X life. Only attack with Cecil when you can absorb that life loss without falling into opponent's reach. Exception: when Cecil is at exactly half-life threshold and you want to flip him — calculate whether the flip is worth the life lost to trigger it.
  5. 05
    Blight a −1/−1 counter from Loch Mare when paying Requiting Hex's additional cost. Loch Mare enters with three −1/−1 counters on it (effectively a 1/2 until counters are removed). Requiting Hex's optional blight cost puts a −1/−1 counter on a creature you control. Instead of weakening a threat, blight removes one of Loch Mare's built-in counters — effectively paying the blight cost to activate Loch Mare's draw ability simultaneously. This is a 2-for-1 on the Hex payment.
  6. 06
    Promise the gift on Into the Flood Maw when bouncing nonland permanents worth more than a 1/1. No gift: bounce any creature. Gift mode: bounce any nonland permanent but they get a 1/1 Fish. The calculation is straightforward — if what you're bouncing is worth more than a 1/1 token (enchantment, planeswalker, artifact, expensive creature), always promise the gift. The 1/1 Fish is irrelevant if you're returning a Kaito or Enduring Curiosity. Only skip the gift when the 1/1 token matters to them (low life, token synergies).
  7. 07
    Gloomlake Verge always provides {U} first. Check for Island or Swamp before assuming {B}. Gloomlake Verge produces {B} only if you already control an Island or Swamp. On turn 1 with only Verge in play, you can only make {U} — not {B}. Plan your mana carefully on turn 1: if you need {B} for Cecil or Requiting Hex, you need a Swamp, Watery Grave, or Starting Town already in play. Multiversal Passage entering as a Swamp (pay 2 life) is the fixer.
  8. 08
    Floodpits Drowner's shuffle ability: assess before paying {1}{U}. The stun + shuffle is powerful but optional. After ETB stun, evaluate whether shuffling Drowner + their creature is correct. Sometimes keeping a 2/1 vigilance attacker on the board is better than a two-for-one shuffle — particularly when you're behind on board. The shuffle also removes your Drowner permanently. Most often the shuffle is correct: their stunned 5/5 disappearing is worth more than your 2/1 body. But against aggro where you need blockers, leaving Drowner on board as a 2/1 vigilance wall is sometimes better.
  9. 09
    Kaito's hexproof applies only during your turn while he has loyalty counters. On the opponent's turn, Kaito is a normal planeswalker with no hexproof — they can target him freely. The hexproof applies during YOUR turn as long as loyalty counters remain. This means: attack with Kaito, use his ability on your turn (he's safe), but expect them to remove him on their turn with targeted removal. Plan turns accordingly — don't leave Kaito at low loyalty expecting hexproof to protect him on their turn.
  10. 10
    Azure Beastbinder's ability-stripping lasts until your next turn, not end of turn. "Until your next turn" is longer than "until end of turn." Attack with Beastbinder on your turn — target creature or permanent loses all abilities AND if a creature it's base 2/2 — until the START of your next turn. This means on their turn (between your attack and your next turn) the permanent still has no abilities. A walker you stripped on your turn can't activate on their turn. A creature you reduced to 2/2 stays 2/2 through their untap step, upkeep, and main phase.
  11. 11
    Restless Reef activates at {2}{U}{B} — don't forget the creature lands. Restless Reef becomes a 4/4 Shark with deathtouch for {2}{U}{B} and mills 4 when it attacks. In late-game grinds or stalled boards, activating Reef as an additional attacker is often correct — a 4/4 deathtouch creature trades profitably with anything, and the mill is a bonus. Soulstone Sanctuary at {4} becomes a 3/3 vigilance all-creature-types attacker. Both land activations are frequently overlooked and can close games that feel stalled.
  12. 12
    Spider-Sense's web-slinging mode: return Floodpits Drowner to get double ETB value. Spider-Sense can be cast for {U} if you return a tapped creature to hand. The ideal target: Floodpits Drowner after it attacked and tapped. Return Drowner to hand to pay the web-slinging cost → counter their instant/sorcery/triggered ability → redeploy Drowner on your next turn for another ETB stun. You get Spider-Sense for {U} AND set up a second Drowner ETB trigger. This sequence requires Drowner to be tapped (it attacked or used the shuffle ability).
  13. 13
    Long Goodbye can't be countered — save it for high-value targets behind counterspells. Against control decks that hold countermagic: deploy your regular threats first to exhaust their counters, then use Long Goodbye to remove any blocker or planeswalker that resolves. The uncounterable property is most valuable when you know they have open mana for countermagic — your removal resolves regardless. MV 3 or less hits their Kaito, most 2-drops, and all 1-drops. Don't waste it on targets your counterable removal could handle.
  14. 14
    Day of Black Sun's "loses all abilities" clause removes indestructible before destruction. Day of Black Sun strips abilities and then destroys. A creature with indestructible (from a Paladin's Protect trigger, Jirina, or similar) loses indestructible first — then is destroyed by the second clause. This is an interaction many players miss. Against decks running indestructible effects, Day of Black Sun answers them cleanly where other removal fails.
  15. 15
    Shoot the Sheriff can't hit your own Spyglass Siren (Siren Pirate = outlaw). Outlaws are: Assassins, Mercenaries, Pirates, Rogues, and Warlocks. Spyglass Siren is a Siren Pirate — a Pirate is an outlaw. Sheriff can't target outlaws. This cuts both ways: your Siren can't be killed by your own Sheriff (you'd rarely want to), but more importantly, opponents running Shoot the Sheriff can't kill your Siren either. Factor this into blocking decisions — your Siren is protected against Sheriff-based removal.
  16. 16
    Never over-sideboard. Trim dead cards, bring in targeted answers, preserve curve. The most common Dimir loss condition is drawing the wrong half of the deck: too much removal against control, too many counters against aggro. Sideboard principle: remove only what's completely dead in the matchup, bring in only what has a clear target. Don't board in 8 cards against control because "more options is better" — you dilute your threat density and draw too many answers to nothing. One focused swap beats three speculative ones.
  17. 17
    Without Kaito in hand you win less than 46% of games. Mulligan harder for him on the play than any other card. Untapped.gg data: Kaito's Not In Hand WR is 45.9% — below 50%. No other card in the list has this kind of gap (+10.3 pts differential). A 6-card hand with Kaito beats a 7-card hand without him in most non-aggro matchups on the play. The data makes the keep/mull decision for you: if you have Kaito and reasonable mana, keep.
  18. 18
    Requiting Hex has a 61% side-out win rate — take it out against non-aggro opponents, not just the obvious ones. Across thousands of games, the field wins 61% when they remove Requiting Hex post-board. It's a pure aggro card: {B} that destroys MV ≤ 2 creatures. In G2/G3 vs. midrange, control, or mirrors where their threats are MV 3+, Hex is a dead card. The data says players are leaving it in too long. When uncertain whether to take it out — take it out.

Testing Framework

Block 01
Psychic Frog Attacks
Practice evaluating when Frog can attack safely. They need a blocker with 3+ power to kill Frog in combat. Identify their blockers, calculate combat, attack when safe — and keep mana open to protect with Counterspell.
Block 02
Flash Creature Sequencing
Practice deploying Floodpits Drowner at their EOT, not your main phase. EOT bounce lets you untap with tempo. Main phase bounce gives them a full draw step to recast the bounced threat — always deploy at their EOT.
Block 03
Counterspell Priority Drills
Practice deciding between countering their key spell vs developing your board. The rule: counter the thing that wins them the game, develop when nothing threatens to win immediately. Wrong counter assignment is the most common mistake.
Block 04
Spell Snare Matchup Drills
Practice Spell Snare targets by matchup. Mono-Green: Badgermole Cub MV 2 and Earthbender Ascension MV 2 — both hard-countered by Snare for {U}. Know every relevant MV-2 spell in the format and whether Snare catches it.
