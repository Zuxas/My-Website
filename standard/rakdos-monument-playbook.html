Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"Three paths to victory in one 60-card shell: T1-T3 aggressive curve, recursive creatures that never stay dead, and Monument to Endurance grinding out 1-damage triggers they can't race."

Rakdos Monument is Standard's most resilient aggro-midrange deck. It wins three distinct ways simultaneously: pure aggro pressure from Moonshadow and Marauding Mako, graveyard recursion from Bloodghast and Flamewake Phoenix, and Monument to Endurance converting every discard into 1 damage + 1 life. Opponents who try to answer one axis find themselves losing to another.

The deck's defining card is Monument to Endurance ({2}): at the beginning of each end step, you may discard a card. If you do, Monument deals 1 damage to each opponent and you gain 1 life. The engine runs on free or near-free discard sources — Iron-Shield Elf discards for {0}, Bloodthorn Flail discards to equip, Restless Vents discards as an activated ability. Discard payoffs compound: discarded Bloodghast returns on your next land drop. Discarded Flamewake Phoenix returns to attack if you have a 4-power creature. Discarded Moonshadow or Marauding Mako grows their own power for future turns.

Lorwyn Eclipsed added exactly what Monument needed: Iron-Shield Elf ({G} — wait, it's {1B}) provides a consistent free discard outlet that also gains indestructible (letting it survive most combat and removal), and Moonshadow ({B}) — a 1-drop that scales with permanents in your GY — gives the deck a better T1 threat than anything it had before.

Why Rakdos Over Jund or Mono-Black Monument

Red Payoff
Flamewake Phoenix + Bitter Triumph
Red provides Flamewake Phoenix (recursive flying 2/2 haste for {1RR} that returns from GY if you attacked with 4-power or greater) and Bitter Triumph ({1B}: exile target creature or PW, discard a card or lose 3 life). Bitter Triumph is unconditional exile removal that also fuels Monument. Without red the removal suite relies on non-exile options, and Flamewake Phoenix is the deck's best recursive flying threat.
Blood Crypt Baseline
Reprinted in Lorwyn Eclipsed
Blood Crypt's reprint in Lorwyn Eclipsed gives Rakdos Monument a clean, consistent mana base. The deck now runs 4 Blood Crypt + 4 Blazemire Verge (enters untapped if you control a swamp) + Starting Town (cycles) + Multiversal Passage (flexible MDFC). The two-color mana base is more consistent than three-color Monument variants. Every land enters untapped most of the time.
Sunspine Lynx
Punishes Greedy Mana Bases
Sunspine Lynx ({1R}) 2/1 first strike: whenever an opponent plays a nonbasic land, Lynx deals 1 damage to them. Standard's mana bases are greedy — Mono-Green, Izzet Lessons, Dimir Excruciator all run 10-15 nonbasics. Lynx in the SB punishes this brutally in slower matchups. Rakdos runs relatively few nonbasics itself (Blood Crypt + Blazemire Verge are the main dual sources) — be aware you still have nonbasics.

Matchup Overview

Favorable
Fair Midrange, Slow Control
Dimir Midrange (resilient threats they can't permanently answer), slower control builds (Monument generates sustained damage they can't race), Izzet Spellementals (your creatures are in a different size class post-Monument). Your recursive threats exhaust their removal eventually.
Unfavorable
Graveyard Hate, Fast Combo
Soul-Guide Lantern exiles Bloodghast and Flamewake Phoenix — your recursion engine turns off. Izzet Prowess races before Monument gets going. Mono-Green Landfall generates boards too large to race through once Earthbender Ascension triggers. Dimir Excruciator can bounce your Monument permanently.

Chapter 03

GAME
FLOW

"T1 threat. T2 Monument. T3 recursive attack. Repeat until they're at 0. The deck has no dead draws — every discard fuels the engine."
Ideal Line
T1 Moonshadow → T2 Monument + Flail
T1: Moonshadow ({B}) — 1/1 with evasion that grows with each permanent you put in GY. T2: Play Monument to Endurance ({2}), equip Bloodthorn Flail discarding Bloodghast. EOT: Monument triggers (discard Flail'd card) — 1 damage + 1 life. T3: Bloodghast returns on land drop (Landfall). Iron-Shield Elf provides a free discard for Monument each turn from here. By T5 with Monument pinging 1 per EOT + Moonshadow + Phoenix swinging, their life total collapses.
Fair Mode
No Monument — Pure Aggro
Without Monument: play the aggro game. Moonshadow into Iron-Shield Elf (indestructible blocker) into Flamewake Phoenix (haste flyer). Tersa Lightshatter handles large blockers. Bitter Triumph exiles their key permanent. Inti, Seneschal of the Sun generates card advantage whenever you discard. Even without Monument the deck is a legitimate aggro deck — creatures that recur, fly, and generate evasion win games purely on combat damage.
Mulligan
Need: 2 Lands + Monument OR 3 Threats
Keep: any hand with Monument + 2 lands + one discard outlet. OR any hand with 3 creatures + 2 lands (aggro the Monument draws). Mulligan: 1 land hands (too risky), 5+ land hands with no threats, hands with Monument but zero discard sources. At 6: 2 lands + Monument + Iron-Shield Elf = snap keep. At 5: 2 lands + Moonshadow + Bloodghast = keep (they recur, you need momentum).
Monument Priority Order
Draw → Damage → Treasure
Monument to Endurance at each end step: first choose Draw (draw a card) unless you already have too many cards. Then choose Damage (deal 1 to opponent + gain 1 life) to accrue reach and life buffer. Treasure only when you need the mana for a key play next turn. The default is Draw → Damage on subsequent triggers. Never reflexively take Treasure — the card advantage and reach from Draw + Damage is almost always better.
Chapter 04

CORE
ENGINES

"Monument is the engine. Bloodthorn Flail is the fuel line. Bloodghast and Phoenix are the renewable fuel."
Engine 1 — Value Generator
Monument to Endurance
Monument ({2}) artifact: at the beginning of each end step, choose one — you draw a card, Monument deals 1 damage to each opponent and you gain 1 life, or you add {C}. The 1-damage trigger doesn't look like much but across 8-10 turns it generates 8-10 damage + 8-10 life. Combined with recursive creatures providing pressure, it creates a clock opponents can't ignore. Use Monument's triggers to set up your turns — Draw when your hand is low, Damage when their life is in range, Treasure when you need mana for a key play.
Engine 2 — Free Fuel
Iron-Shield Elf + Bloodthorn Flail
Iron-Shield Elf ({1B}) 2/2: whenever you discard a card, Iron-Shield Elf gains indestructible until end of turn. Tap it for {0}, discard a card — Iron-Shield Elf becomes indestructible and Monument sees the discard for its EOT trigger. Bloodthorn Flail (equipment): equip by discarding a card, giving +1/+0 and menace. Equip at instant speed during combat. These two are free discard outlets — Iron-Shield Elf in particular enables the Monument loop at zero mana cost every turn.
Engine 3 — Recursive Threats
Bloodghast + Flamewake Phoenix
Bloodghast ({BB}) 2/1 haste: can't block. Returns from GY to battlefield whenever a land enters under your control (Landfall). Discard Bloodghast to Monument/Flail → play a land next turn → Bloodghast returns → attacks → discard again. Flamewake Phoenix ({1RR}) 2/2 haste flying: returns from GY at upkeep if you control a 4+ power creature. With Moonshadow growing large or Marauding Mako as a 4/3, Flamewake recurs every turn it's in GY. Both together make the board extremely hard to permanently clear.
Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Best opening: Moonshadow → Bloodthorn Flail equip discarding Bloodghast → Bloodghast swings as a 3/1 menace with haste."
Line 01 — T1 Moonshadow + T2 Bloodthorn Flail = T2 4-Power Attacker
T1: Moonshadow (1/1). T2: Bloodthorn Flail. Equip by discarding Bloodghast (Moonshadow grows: +1/+0 for each permanent put in your GY this turn → now 2/1). Flail gives +1/+0 and menace. Moonshadow attacks as a 3/1 with menace on T2. Bloodghast returns on T3 land drop as a 2/1 haste attacker. This opening applies 5+ damage by T3 and establishes Monument fuel immediately.
Line 02 — Flamewake Phoenix Returns at Upkeep, Not EOT
Flamewake Phoenix returns from the GY at the beginning of your upkeep if you controlled a creature with power 4 or greater at the beginning of upkeep. Check your board state BEFORE upkeep starts. If Moonshadow has grown to 4+ power (via GY permanents) or Marauding Mako is 4/3, Flamewake returns. This means: don't block with your 4-power creature to save a different creature — protect the 4-power permanent that enables Phoenix recursion. Phoenix attacks the same turn it returns (haste).
Line 03 — Iron-Shield Elf Gains Indestructible When You Discard — Use Combat
Iron-Shield Elf gains indestructible until end of turn whenever you discard a card. Activate its tap ability (discard a card) in your combat step — before blockers are declared. Iron-Shield Elf attacks as an indestructible 2/2, meaning it can't be killed in combat. They can't block it profitably. Use this line every attack step: tap → discard → indestructible → attack. The discarded card fuels Monument EOT trigger simultaneously.
Line 04 — Inti, Seneschal of the Sun Doesn't Draw — It Impulses
Inti, Seneschal of the Sun ({RW}) 2/1 first strike: whenever you discard a card, exile the top card of your library. Until end of your next turn, you may play it. This is an impulse draw, not a real draw — the exiled card must be played by the end of your next turn or it's gone. Keep track of what's exiled. If you impulse into a land, play it that turn. If you impulse into a creature, decide if you need it now or can afford to lose it. Don't discard recklessly with Inti — make sure you have mana to use what it finds.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"vs Aggro: be the bigger aggro. Your creatures recur. Theirs don't. vs Control: Monument and recursive threats are attrition machines."
vs Aggro (Prowess, Mono-Red)
Recursive Beatdown
Your creatures recur from the GY — theirs don't. Bloodghast comes back every land drop. Flamewake Phoenix comes back every upkeep. Trade freely in combat: you have more threats coming back than they can generate. Iron-Shield Elf with indestructible is an unkillable blocker in aggressive mirrors. Monument gaining 1 life per trigger buys you enough time to establish the recursion loop. Their removal doesn't stick — your GY refills your board every turn.
vs Midrange (Dimir, Simic)
Pressure + Monument Reach
Against Dimir Midrange: Bloodghast is hard for them to answer permanently (no exile in most main decks). Flamewake Phoenix flies over Psychic Frog. Monument's sustained damage kills around their interaction plan. Against Simic Rhythm: you're the faster deck. Sunspine Lynx in the SB punishes their nonbasic-heavy mana base. Requiting Hex removes their Earthbender Ascension or Badgermole Cub at instant speed.
vs Control (Dimir Excruciator)
Grind With Monument
Monument generates value every turn for free — they can't counter it. Bloodghast bypasses their countermagic (lands trigger it from GY, not spells). Flamewake Phoenix returns through Floodpits Drowner bounce — they bounce it, it goes to GY, comes back at upkeep. Case of the Crimson Pulse (SB) fuels Monument every turn even when your hand is empty. Vengeful Possession (SB) gives your creatures ward — more resistance to their targeted removal.

SIDEBOARD
GUIDE

"Sunspine Lynx for mana-hungry decks. Soul-Guide Lantern for GY mirrors. Requiting Hex for enchantments. Case of the Crimson Pulse for long games."
P1 — vs Nonbasic-Heavy Decks
Sunspine Lynx ×3
Sunspine Lynx ({1R}) 2/1 first strike: whenever an opponent plays a nonbasic land, deals 1 damage to them. Standard's greedy mana bases (10-15 nonbasics per deck) mean Lynx generates 3-5+ damage just from their land drops across a game. Against Izzet Lessons (Botanical Sanctum, Steam Vents, etc.), Dimir Excruciator (Watery Grave, Underground Mortuary), and Mono-Green Landfall (Stomping Ground, Breeding Pool): Lynx forces them to take damage for doing nothing wrong. Boards in whenever opponent has 8+ nonbasics.
P2 — vs GY Decks / Mirrors
Soul-Guide Lantern ×3
{1} artifact: when it enters, exile target card from a GY. Tap, sacrifice: exile all cards from target GY. Against Sultai Reanimator and GY-dependent strategies: exile their graveyard entire in one activation. Against decks using Doom or Spider-Man (superior spider-man) with GY recursion: exile the key card they're targeting. Also relevant in the mirror — exile their Bloodghast and Flamewake Phoenix when they've discarded them, turning off their recursion engine.
P3 — vs Enchantments
Requiting Hex ×3
Requiting Hex ({1B}) instant: destroy target enchantment. Deals damage equal to its MV to its controller. Against Mono-Green Landfall: destroys Earthbender Ascension (MV 2) for {1B} at instant speed, dealing 2 damage. Against Izzet Lessons: destroys Artist's Talent. Against Simic Rhythm: destroys Badgermole Cub's enchantment payoffs. Requiting Hex is strictly better than generic enchantment removal because it deals damage simultaneously — relevant for Monument's reach math.
P4 — Long Games

Case of the Crimson Pulse ({1R}) enchantment: at the beginning of your upkeep, discard a card. Case is solved when you've discarded 3+ cards. While unsolved: draw an extra card and discard. Vengeful Possession ({2B}) aura: enchanted creature has ward — "when target, opponent discards a card." Makes your key creatures cost extra to interact with. Stacks with Monument's attrition game.

HEURISTICS

"Default Monument: Draw → Damage → Treasure. Iron-Shield Elf taps before attackers. Don't exile Bloodghast yourself — let it die and return."
  1. 01Monument trigger order: Draw first, Damage second, Treasure only when needed. Draw refills your hand for more discard fuel. Damage accrues reach and life buffer simultaneously. Treasure is lowest priority — you usually have enough mana. Deviate from this only when you have lethal reach from Damage or need mana for a specific next-turn play.
  2. 02Activate Iron-Shield Elf's tap ability BEFORE declaring attackers. The indestructible gained triggers on the discard — do it before combat so Elf attacks as indestructible. They can't kill it in combat math. The discard also triggers Monument at EOT simultaneously. One tap, two payoffs.
  3. 03Don't use exile removal on your own Bloodghast. Bloodghast returns from GY via Landfall. Never use Bitter Triumph or other exile effects on it — put it in the GY through combat, discarding (Flail, Iron-Shield Elf, Monument), or letting it die to removal. Once exiled, it never returns. This sounds obvious but comes up when you're deciding whether to "cycle" it with Tersa.
  4. 04Flamewake Phoenix needs a 4-power creature at the START of your upkeep — check before upkeep triggers resolve. If your 4-power creature died on their turn, Phoenix doesn't return. Don't count on Phoenix returning through Bloodthorn Flail's temporary pump (pump wears off at EOT before your next upkeep). The 4-power creature must be naturally 4 power — Moonshadow with GY permanents, Marauding Mako (4/3), or a Flail-pumped creature that lived to your upkeep.
  5. 05Inti, Seneschal of the Sun impulses — play the exiled card immediately or lose it. Whenever you discard, Inti exiles the top card of your library. You may play it until end of your NEXT turn. Track what's exiled. If it's a land, play it immediately for Monument fuel + Bloodghast Landfall trigger. If it's a creature, deploy it if you need board presence. If you can't use it, it's lost — don't discard recklessly with Inti when your exile zone already has an unplayed card.
  6. 06Sunspine Lynx (SB) triggers on THEIR nonbasic land plays — board it in versus 8+ nonbasic decks. Izzet Lessons, Dimir Excruciator, Temur Landfall, Simic Rhythm all run 12-15 nonbasics. Lynx deals 1 per land drop without you doing anything. In a 7-turn game against these decks Lynx generates 5-7 "free" damage. Always board it in against three-color or dual-heavy mana bases. Be aware: you also run Blood Crypt and Blazemire Verge — you take damage too, but far less than them.
  7. 07Bloodthorn Flail equips at instant speed — use it in their attack step to make a blocker into a menace attacker next turn. Equip Bloodthorn Flail during their attack step after they've declared attackers. Your creature can't attack this turn (summoning sickness or already declared as non-attacker) but next turn it attacks with menace from the Flail. Also: equip discards a card in the process — triggering Monument at EOT and potentially enabling Flamewake Phoenix recursion.
  8. 08Restless Vents is both a land and a Monument fuel source — use its activated ability last, not first. Restless Vents can tap to discard and loot. Use it after Monument's EOT trigger resolves in scenarios where you want to set up your hand for next turn. Don't use it on your main phase casually — the timing matters for Monument's "beginning of end step" trigger window.
  9. 09Moonshadow grows with EACH permanent put in your GY this turn — combo with Bloodthorn Flail discards. Moonshadow ({B}) gets +1/+0 for each permanent card put into your GY this turn. Bloodthorn Flail discard = one permanent in GY = Moonshadow +1/+0. Multiple Flail equips in one turn stack. With two discards via Flail + Iron-Shield Elf, Moonshadow is a 3/1 on T2 before it attacks. Know the math before you attack — an unexpected 4/1 Moonshadow enables Flamewake Phoenix recursion immediately.
  10. 10Soul-Guide Lantern exiles ALL cards from a GY in one activation — hold it for when their GY is fully loaded, not early. Soul-Guide Lantern sacrifices to exile an entire GY. Don't fire it after they've discarded two cards. Wait until they've fully loaded their GY with all their recursion targets, then exile the whole thing at once. Timing: use it at the end of your upkeep, before Phoenix's return trigger resolves, if you're in a mirror — exile their GY before Phoenix comes back.

"4 Monument and 4 Moonshadow are locked. Bloodghast and Flail are locked. Everything else adjusts to your expected meta."
Lock
4 Monument + 4 Moonshadow + 4 Bloodghast + 4 Bloodthorn Flail
These 16 cards are the deck's engine. Monument is the payoff. Moonshadow is the scaling T1 threat. Bloodghast is the free recursive fuel. Bloodthorn Flail is the free discard outlet. Never reduce any below 4.
Flex 3–4
Flamewake Phoenix
3 is correct for most metas. Go to 4 in midrange-heavy fields where your 4-power creature is reliably in play every upkeep. Cut to 2 in aggro-heavy metas where you need more main deck interaction (Tersa Lightshatter counts) rather than recursive threats.
Flex 2–4
Iron-Shield Elf
3 main is the reference. Go to 4 in Monument-centric builds where you want maximum free discard triggers. The indestructible blocker effect is also relevant against creature-heavy fields. Cut to 2 in graveyard-hate-heavy metas where you want faster threats.
Meta Call
Sunspine Lynx (0–2 main)
1 main is the current reference. Go to 2 main in metas where 80%+ of decks run 8+ nonbasics (current Standard is close to this). 0 main if you're expecting Boros energy or Mono-Red (basics-heavy). Never more than 2 main — it's a punisher card, not a proactive threat.
Flex
Inti, Seneschal of the Sun (2–3)
3 is the reference. Cut to 2 in matchups where you want more direct interaction (Bitter Triumph slots). Inti generates card advantage but requires careful tracking. In fast metas where games end T4-5, the impulse draw doesn't have time to generate value — cut to 2 and add Requiting Hex or Tersa Lightshatter.
Consideration
Case of the Crimson Pulse
Currently SB only (2 copies). Move 1 to main in control-heavy metas where the sustained Monument fuel across 10+ turns generates crushing advantage. Don't main it in aggro-heavy metas — it's too slow. 2 SB is the right home for most environments.
Rule 01
Monument Mode Selection
You select Monument to Endurance's trigger mode before declaring which mode. Early game (20+ life): Damage mode (1 damage + 1 life). Mid game (resources depleted): Draw mode (1 card). Late game (closing): Creature mode (4/4 token). You map your life total to the correct mode each turn.
Rule 02
Moonshadow Discard Value
You discard via Bloodthorn Flail to grow Moonshadow (Ayara) intentionally. Each discard fuels your Moonshadow's power while also creating cards in GY for Flamewake Phoenix's return condition. You discard excess lands and redundant copies — never discard your key interaction pieces.
Rule 03
Phoenix Return Threshold
You maintain a 4+ power creature every turn for Flamewake Phoenix's return condition. Moonshadow at 5+ power enables Phoenix. If your board drops below 4 power (Moonshadow removed), Phoenix doesn't return next upkeep. You track your largest creature's power every turn.
Rule 04
Iron-Shield Elf Protection
When they target Iron-Shield Elf with removal: you discard a card to make Elf indestructible until EOT. You do this automatically in response. Elf + discard outlet always in hand means you can protect Elf from removal indefinitely as long as you have cards to discard.
Tune 01
Sunspine Lynx Count
You run more Sunspine Lynx copies in metas with many nonbasic-heavy decks. Against 3-color metas: 3-4 copies (many nonbasics per opponent). Against mono-color metas: 0-1 copies (few nonbasics to punish). You audit your expected nonbasic count per opponent before each tournament.
Tune 02
Pyroclasm vs Slagstorm
Pyroclasm ({1}{R}: 2 damage to each creature) handles 2-toughness threats. Slagstorm ({1}{R}{R}: 3 damage or 3 to each player) handles 3-toughness threats but costs more. You run Pyroclasm in fast metas with small creatures (RDW, aggressive tokens). You run Slagstorm in metas with 3-toughness creatures where Pyroclasm misses too often.
Tune 03
Nihil Spellbomb vs Soul-Guide Lantern
Nihil Spellbomb ({1}: exile target player's GY; when sacrificed, draw for {B}) and Soul-Guide Lantern ({1}: exile target GY; when put in your GY, draw for {B}) have similar effects. Spellbomb targets one player's GY specifically. Lantern targets any GY. You run Spellbomb when you specifically need to exile THEIR GY (not your own). Run Lantern when flexibility matters.
Tune 04
Case of the Crimson Pulse Flex
Case of the Crimson Pulse fuels Monument every upkeep while generating card draw. You run more copies in control matchup slots where the game going 7+ turns makes Case's slow drip of value profitable. You cut Case in fast matchups where the game ends before Case generates enough value.
Tune 05
Iron-Shield Elf Copies
Iron-Shield Elf (indestructible via discard) is your resilience piece against removal-heavy metas. You run 3-4 copies in metas with many targeted removal spells. You cut Elf copies in metas where sweepers (not targeted removal) are the primary threat — Elf doesn't survive sweepers. You adjust based on single-target vs sweeper ratio.
Tune 06
Roiling Vortex Quantity
You run Roiling Vortex in metas with many lifegain sources (UW Control, Selesnya, Azorius). Vortex at {R} denies all lifegain and deals 1 per spell. In non-lifegain metas: cut Vortex from your SB. The card is narrowly metagame-specific — when lifegain is common, run 3-4; when rare, run 0.
Tune 15
Disciple of the Vault
Disciple of the Vault (1/1: whenever an artifact is put into a GY, target opponent loses 1 life) triggers on each Monument artifact token creation and each Blood token sacrifice. Multiple Disciples create a drain engine: Monument + 3 Disciples + 3 artifact triggers = 9 life drain passively per turn.
Tune 14
Monument Synergy Stack
Monument to Endurance's Damage mode and your creatures' life drain stack. Monument deals 1 and gains 1 each trigger. Your Flamewake Phoenix is a free recurring threat. Moonshadow's menace forces blocks. All three together make winning through combat or life gain extremely difficult for opponents.
Tune 11
Ob Nixilis Finisher
Ob Nixilis, the Adversary (planeswalker: casualty 1 copies him; +1 makes them discard; −2 makes Imp token; −7 wins) is your planeswalker finisher. With casualty 1: you sacrifice a small creature to make two Ob Nixilis. Two Ob activations per turn rapidly drains their hand.
Tune 12
Tenacious Underdog Cycling
Tenacious Underdog (2/3 with blitz: pay {B}{R} to cast as a hasty 2/3 that draws a card when it dies) cycles through your deck at 2 mana. You blitz Underdog when you need a card draw immediately — pay blitz cost, attack, Underdog dies at EOT, draw a card.
Tune 13
Rix Maadi Reveler Cycle
Rix Maadi Reveler (2/2: ETB discard and draw for each discarded) refuels your hand while triggering Monument damage and growing Moonshadow. With spectacle (paid when opponent lost life this turn): draw 3 instead of 1. You cast Reveler after dealing damage to trigger spectacle.
Tune 07
Bloodthorn Flail Activation
Bloodthorn Flail equips to a creature and lets you discard for effects. You activate Flail when you have excess lands in hand or duplicate cards. Every Flail activation: grows Moonshadow + fuels Flamewake Phoenix return + creates Monument triggers.
Tune 08
Case Solve Trigger
Case of the Crimson Pulse solves when you have a creature with power ≥ 4. After solving: you draw a card every upkeep. Your Moonshadow at 4+ power triggers Case solve. Once solved, Case generates one card per turn passively.
Tune 09
Ayara Sacrifice Mode
Ayara, Widow of the Realm (Moonshadow's other face): you can sacrifice a non-token creature to give target creature −X/−X until EOT where X = sacrificed creature's power. You sacrifice expendable creatures to kill their key threat in a pinch.
Tune 10
Kolaghan's Command Recursion
Kolaghan's Command's "return creature from GY to hand" mode recurs your key creatures. Against removal-heavy opponents: return Flamewake Phoenix from GY to hand — then discard it to Flail to put it back in GY for haste recursion.
Monument Accumulation
Every Turn Adds Up
Monument to Endurance's damage triggers stack. At 1 damage per trigger × 1 trigger per turn × 10 turns: 10 accumulated damage before a creature attacks. You think of Monument as a slow but unstoppable clock — your opponent takes 10 damage just from you having Monument in play for 10 turns.
Flamewake Phoenix Value
Nearly Free Threat
Flamewake Phoenix costs {1}{R}{R} initially. After it dies and returns from GY at your upkeep (if you control a 4+ power creature), it's free. A Phoenix that dies in combat returns next upkeep — it's never truly gone. You attack with Phoenix freely because the cost to return it is zero.
Moonshadow Growth
Track Your Discard Count
Moonshadow (Ayara, the Widow) gets larger each time you discard a card. You track your total discard count: each Bloodthorn Flail activation grows Moonshadow by 1. At 5 discards: Moonshadow is a 6+ power menace creature requiring two blockers every combat.
Case of the Crimson Pulse
Slow But Reliable Draw
Case of the Crimson Pulse generates a card draw every upkeep when its condition is met. You deploy Case when you expect the game to go 7+ turns and value card advantage over speed. Against aggressive decks: skip Case and run Pyroclasm instead.
Monument Mode Selection
Early Damage, Late Creature
You select Monument to Endurance's trigger mode before declaring which mode. At 20+ life: Damage mode (accumulate a life buffer). At 10-15 life: Draw mode (find answers). At 8 life or below: Creature mode (4/4 token races the clock). You never default to one mode — you evaluate your life total each trigger.
Moonshadow Discard Protocol
Discard Lands and Copies
You discard excess lands and redundant card copies to grow Moonshadow (Ayara). You never discard your interaction pieces or your live threats. Lands after your 5th: always discard. Second copies of Monument: discard if the first is active. You maximize Moonshadow's power through strategic discard.
Iron-Shield Elf Defense
Always Protect In Response
When they target Iron-Shield Elf with removal: you discard a card immediately in response to make Elf indestructible until EOT. You never let Elf die to targeted removal — your discard supply is almost always sufficient to protect it. Discard a land, never a threat.
Flamewake Phoenix Return
Maintain 4+ Power
Flamewake Phoenix returns from your GY at the beginning of combat each upkeep if you control a creature with 4+ power. You track your largest creature's power before your combat phase. Moonshadow at 5+ power: Phoenix returns. If Moonshadow was removed: you need another 4+ power creature or Phoenix doesn't return.
Sunspine Lynx Timing
T3 Before Their Nonbasics
You deploy Sunspine Lynx on T3 before they've played their T3 nonbasic land. Lynx must be in play BEFORE their nonbasic land drop to trigger 3 damage on that land. You calculate: how many nonbasics do they have left to play? If 2-3: deploy Lynx immediately for maximum damage.
