Chapter 01

Deck
Overview

"Jeskai Control is an Azorius Control variant that splashes red to take advantage of how valuable that color is in many matchups — particularly the current meta centered around creatures and combat."

Jeskai Control runs zero creatures in the main deck — the entire 60 is removal, counters, and card draw. The win condition is Jeskai Revelation, which as an instant bounces a threat, deals 4 damage, creates two 1/1 Monk tokens with prowess, draws 2 cards, and gains 4 life — essentially resetting a board state and refueling simultaneously. Lightning Helix at instant speed deals 3 damage and gains 3 life, stabilizing against aggro. Inevitable Defeat exiles any nonland permanent, uncounterable, with life swing. Against creatures, this package is nearly impossible to fight through.

The deck's weakness is creature-light strategies — Izzet Prowess flood with tokens, Mono-Green's enchantment-based threats, and combo strategies that don't use creatures. The sideboard is built around filling those gaps (Torpor Orb vs ETB-heavy, High Noon vs aggro, Pyroclasm vs tokens).

Why Study This Deck

Jeskai Control is ~5% of the Cincinnati meta — you'll face it 1-2 rounds. As Dimir Midrange, this is your best matchup (64% WR per Untapped). Knowing their counterspell suite (No More Lies, Spell Snare), their sweepers (Day of Judgment, Ultima), and their uncounterable finisher (Inevitable Defeat + Jeskai Revelation) helps you sequence correctly — hold key spells through their removal window and deploy Surrak, Elusive Hunter (can't be countered) when they tap out.

Card Notes

The Lifegain Removal
Lightning Helix
{R}{W} Instant. 3 damage to any target + gain 3 life. The best red-white spell in the format. At instant speed, it answers their T1-T3 creatures AND stabilizes your life total. Against Boros Aggro, 4 Helixes = 12 damage absorbed and 12 life gained — effectively a 24-point life swing. Against Prowess, it answers Slickshot Show-Off at any stage. The most important card in the deck against aggro.
The Uncounterable Exile
Inevitable Defeat
{2}{B}{W} Instant. Can't be countered. Exile target nonland permanent. Its controller loses 3 life, you gain 3 life. The answer to everything — Ojer Axonil, Mossborn Hydra, Sapling Nursery, Stormchaser's Talent. Can't be countered means it resolves through their Spell Pierce and Get Out. The life swing (+3 / −3 = 6-point swing) is meaningful in close races. 4 copies because this is the primary removal engine.
The Win Condition
Jeskai Revelation
{2}{U}{R}{W} Instant. Return target spell or permanent to hand + deals 4 damage to any target + create two 1/1 Monk tokens with prowess + draw 2 cards + gain 4 life. Five effects in one instant-speed card. Cast at end of opponent's turn after they've tapped out: bounce their key permanent, create two prowess threats, refuel your hand, gain 4 life. Those Monk tokens are your win condition — they grow with Stock Up and Helix triggers on your turn.
The Hard Counter
No More Lies
{U}{W} Instant. Counter target spell unless its controller pays {3}. If countered, exile it. The soft counter that's effectively hard when they're on curve. T2 Elusive Otter requires they pay {3} extra = it costs them {U}+{3} = {3}{U}. They can rarely pay {3} extra in the early turns. The exile clause prevents GY recursion for Esper Origins, Wild Ride Harmonize, and any flashback effects.
The Early Counter
Spell Snare
{U} Instant. Counter target spell with mana value 2. Hard counter for one mana specifically targeting the most dangerous turn-2 plays: Stormchaser's Talent, Warleader's Call, Shocking Sharpshooter, Earthbender Ascension. The {U} efficiency means you can hold it while deploying another spell. Only 2 copies because it's dead against 3+ mana threats.
The Sweepers
Day of Judgment / Ultima
Day of Judgment: {2}{W}{W} Sorcery — destroy all creatures. 2 main + 1 SB. The primary board reset. Ultima: {4}{W}{U} Sorcery — destroy all artifacts and creatures, then end the turn (exile all spells/abilities from stack, current player discards to max hand size, damage wears off, EOT effects end). Ultima destroys Warleader's Call (enchantment... wait — check: Ultima destroys artifacts AND creatures, not enchantments). Day of Judgment handles creatures only. Both are sorcery speed — must play proactively.
The Card Engine
Consult the Star Charts / Stock Up
Consult the Star Charts: {1}{W} Instant. Kicker {1}{U}. Look at top X cards (X = lands you control), put 1 (or 2 if kicked) in hand. With 6 lands, unkicked = look at 6 pick 1. Kicked with 6 lands = look at 6 pick 2 for {2}{U}{W}. Stock Up: {1}{U} Sorcery — look at top 5, put 2 in hand. Consult at instant speed enables reactive digging — find the sweeper or Helix you need on their end step.
The Mana Base
Thundering Falls / Mistrise Village
Thundering Falls: Island + Mountain, enters tapped, ETB surveil 1. Fixing + library manipulation. Mistrise Village: enters tapped unless you control Mountain or Forest. {U}{T}: your next spell can't be countered. The uncounterable clause on Mistrise is critical in the control mirror — deploy key Revelation or Inevitable Defeat through their countermagic. Note: Ultima can't be countered via Mistrise to blow up their board even through open blue mana.
Chapter 03

Gameplan

"Your job for the first 4 turns: don't die. Your job turns 5-8: answer everything. Your job after that: cast Revelation and win."

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turns 1–3: Survive
Hold Mana Open
Hold Lightning Helix and No More Lies in hand. Deploy land + hold up {R}{W} for Helix response on their attack step. Against aggro: kill their key threats at instant speed (Sharpshooter, Slickshot Show-Off). Against midrange: counter their value engines (Stormchaser's Talent, Sapling Nursery). Never tap out unless you're drawing a land and have nothing to hold up.
Turns 4–6: Reset
Sweep + Refuel
Once their board is threatening: Day of Judgment to wipe, then Consult the Star Charts kicked to refuel. Inevitable Defeat for their indestructible threats (Ojer Axonil) that Day of Judgment doesn't hit. Stock Up at instant speed on their end step before your turn. The goal: enter turn 7 with 3+ cards in hand and their board clear.
Turns 7+: Win
Revelation into Monks
Cast Jeskai Revelation at instant speed (end of their turn): bounce their key permanent, deal 4 damage, create 2 Monk tokens with prowess, draw 2 cards, gain 4 life. Now you have 2 prowess 1/1 threats on board. Cast Lightning Helix next turn while attacking: each Helix is also +1/+1 on each Monk until EOT. They become 3/3s after one Helix. Win in 2-3 swings.
Mid (T4–T6)
Your Key Plays
Your T4 is Voice of Victory. You attack immediately — it can't be targeted while attacking. Your T5 holds Dovin's Veto for their board wipe attempt. Your T6 is Stormplain Detainment if they've developed a wide board. You generate advantage through sustained pressure (Voice) combined with reactive answers (Aven Interrupter, Seam Rip) every turn.
Counter Suite
Your Interaction Depth
Your counters each answer a specific threat type. Seam Rip ({1}{U}): counters sorceries exactly. Aven Interrupter (flash 5/4 ETB): hard counters any instant/sorcery. Dovin's Veto ({W}{U} uncounterable): noncreature spells. High Noon: prevents all sorceries for both players. You hold the right counter for their specific threat — never use Seam Rip on an instant or waste Dovin's Veto on a creature.
The Clock
Your T4+ Win Condition
Voice of Victory is your clock. You deploy it at 5+ lands and attack every turn. At 3 damage per turn, you need 7 attack steps to win from 20 life. Their board wipe is the threat — you hold Dovin's Veto specifically for Supreme Verdict. Your Aven Interrupter is your backup counter for whatever their other win condition is. You attack, they spend resources answering, you counter their answer.
Mana Management
Your Open Mana Discipline
Your most important skill: never tap out. Your T3: one land + {W}{U} open (Dovin's Veto). Your T4: Voice into {W}{U} or {1}{U} open (Seam Rip). Your T5+: hold the most relevant counter for their top 3 plays. You pre-identify what they can cast on their next turn and keep the mana to answer exactly that. Never tap out for development when interaction is available.

Ultima's End the Turn Clause

Ultima ends the turn after resolution. This means: all spells and abilities currently on the stack are exiled (including Ultima itself). The current player discards to maximum hand size. All "this turn" and "until end of turn" effects end — including their prowess buffs, their creature's haste (they untap next turn), and their temporary pump spells. Use Ultima when they've tapped out to attack and pumped their creatures — you destroy everything and their pump effects vanish with the turn ending. Note: Ultima destroys artifacts AND creatures, not enchantments. Warleader's Call survives Ultima.

Chapter 04

Answer Suite

"Know which spell answers which threat. Running out of answers because you used the wrong tool is how Jeskai Control loses."
Creatures (any size)
Lightning Helix / Inevitable Defeat
Helix: instant, 3 damage, gain 3 life. Best against 1-3 toughness creatures early (all of Boros' 1/1-2/3 threats, Elusive Otter, Slickshot Show-Off). Inevitable Defeat: exile any nonland permanent, uncounterable. Use for: indestructible creatures, high-toughness threats Helix can't reach (Eddymurk Crab 5/5), planeswalkers, key enchantments. The rule: Helix kills efficiently (2 mana for 3 damage + 3 life), Defeat exiles anything that doesn't die to Helix.
Enchantments
Inevitable Defeat / Get Lost
Inevitable Defeat exiles any nonland permanent — the only main-deck answer to enchantments like Sapling Nursery, Warleader's Call, Stormchaser's Talent. Get Lost: {1}{G} instant — destroy target creature, enchantment, or planeswalker; controller gets 2 Map tokens. The Map tokens are a concession (they get minor card advantage), but instant-speed enchantment removal is critical. Use Get Lost when Helix's 3-damage clause is irrelevant and the enchantment must die immediately.
Counterspells
No More Lies / Spell Snare
No More Lies: soft counter at {U}{W}, hard counter at MV ≤ 3 effectively. Use for key threats you can't answer in play (combo pieces, uncounterable threats, things without answers in your main deck). Spell Snare: hard counter for MV 2 exactly — optimal against Stormchaser's Talent, Warleader's Call, Shocking Sharpshooter. Hold Snare when their T2 play is dangerous. Never tap out to cast a proactive spell when Snare is your only response.
Wide Boards
Day of Judgment / Ultima
Day of Judgment: {2}{W}{W} sorcery, destroy all creatures. Use when they have 3+ creatures and Helix can't answer them individually. Ultima: {4}{W}{U} sorcery, destroy all artifacts and creatures, end the turn. Use when they have artifacts AND creatures threatening simultaneously (Boros Aggro with artifact-matters, any multitype board). Remember: Ultima doesn't hit enchantments — Warleader's Call survives.

What Jeskai Cannot Answer in Main Deck

Main Deck Gap
GY-Based Strategies
The main deck has no graveyard hate. Izzet Spellementals using GY for Eddymurk Crab cost reduction, Reanimator returning large threats, Icetill Explorer playing lands from GY — all operate without main deck interference. Plan: counter or exile before they hit the GY. Post-board: Rest in Peace (hard exile everything) and Torpor Orb (stops ETB triggers from reanimated threats).
Main Deck Gap
Fast Token Floods
A resolved Stormchaser's Talent at level 3 generating tokens per spell is difficult to answer — each Inevitable Defeat on the Talent costs {4} and they've already gotten value. Day of Judgment clears the tokens but not the Talent. Annul from the SB counters it on the way in. Post-board: Annul for Talent, Torpor Orb blanks Crab and other ETB threats. The gap: no instant-speed enchantment removal in the main.
Engine: Voice Lock
Your Uncounterable Clock
Voice of Victory (3/3 flying) can't be targeted while attacking. You deploy Voice at 5+ mana and attack every turn. Your payoff: they can't point targeted removal at Voice during your attack step — they must answer it with board wipes or sacrifice effects. You hold your most relevant counter (Dovin's Veto) specifically for their board wipe attempt after Voice resolves. Voice + Veto is your primary two-card closing combination.
Engine: Aven Interrupter
Your Flash Counter Creature
Aven Interrupter ({3}{W}{U} = 5 mana, 5/4 flash, ETB counters a spell) is your most efficient card. You deploy it in response to their most important noncreature spell — Earthbender Ascension, Sunderflock, their board wipe. Your payoff: their spell is countered AND you have a 5/4 in play. You follow Aven with an attack — they must spend removal instead of advancing their plan.
Engine: Stormplain Detainment
Your Board Lockout
Stormplain Detainment ({2}{W}) taps all their creatures until EOT. You deploy it at their attack step when they have 3+ attackers. Your payoff: their entire attack is cancelled, they're tapped down, and you attack freely next turn. You combine it with Voice of Victory attacks: Stormplain taps their blockers, Voice attacks through them. You generate "free" attack steps every turn you cast Stormplain.
Engine: High Noon
Your Sorcery Shield
High Noon prevents all sorcery-speed plays until EOT — for both players. You deploy it the turn before their key sorcery. Your payoff: they can't cast Earthbender Ascension, sweepers, or their key sorcery on their turn. You plan your own sorcery-speed actions for before High Noon resolves. You never cast High Noon blindly; you know their specific sorcery-speed threat first.
Chapter 05

Role Assignment

Jeskai Control is always the reactive player — you never have a proactive "I must do X by turn Y" plan. Your role is defined by the opponent:

vs Aggro
Life Total Manager
Use Lightning Helix at instant speed to both kill threats and gain life. Never tap out when they have open attack mana. Day of Judgment as the reset button. Goal: reach turn 6 with 8+ life, then Revelation stabilizes completely.
vs Midrange
Answer Engine
Inevitable Defeat for their key permanents. No More Lies for their value spells. Counter their draw engines before they generate card advantage. Revelation when they're topdecking — bounce their last threat and refuel.
vs Control Mirror
Resource War
Hold counterspells for their key spells. Mistrise Village makes your Inevitable Defeat uncounterable — use it to land the uncounterable exile. Revelation at instant speed EOT lets you maintain card parity while generating the Monk clock they can't answer.
Priority Rule
Your Interaction Hierarchy
You pre-establish your interaction hierarchy before each game: Dovin's Veto for their board wipe, Seam Rip for their key sorcery, Aven Interrupter for their key instant, Stormplain Detainment for their wide attack. You never use your highest-priority counter on a lower-priority threat. You identify their top 3 threats and hold the right answer for each. Wrong counter assignment loses games.
Clock Rule
Your Attack Commitment
Once you deploy Voice of Victory, you attack every turn. You never miss an attack step with Voice — every attack is 3 guaranteed damage through targeted-removal immunity. You hold your remaining mana for answers, not attacks. Your threat (Voice) attacks automatically; your job after Voice resolves is holding the right counter for their board wipe attempt.
Counter Sequencing
Your Mana Discipline
You hold {W}{U} open on their turn every turn from T3 onward. Against a spell-heavy deck: you hold Seam Rip or Dovin's Veto every turn. You tap down to 0 only when you're deploying your own threat AND you have a Veto in hand that will protect it. You never tap out speculatively. Your power comes from untapped mana — every tapped out turn is a turn they can resolve their win condition.
High Noon Timing
Your Sorcery Window
You cast High Noon when you've identified the specific sorcery you're stopping. You hold High Noon for the turn before they cast Earthbender Ascension or their sweeper. You don't cast High Noon when they have no sorceries — you save it. Once High Noon resolves, you attack freely knowing they're locked out of their key sorcery-speed play for that turn.

Matchup Guide

Written from the Jeskai Control pilot's seat. You are the reactive deck. Every matchup is about surviving long enough to execute Revelation + Monks.

You are the underdog at 36% — their flash creatures react to your sweeper timing and Kaito has hexproof on your turn. But you have the tools to fight back if you sequence correctly. The matchup is decided by whether you can land Revelation + Monks before they rebuild their hand via Enduring Curiosity. One successful Revelation — bounce their Kaito, deal 4 to their face, create two 1/1 prowess Monks, draw 2, gain 4 — sets you ahead on every axis: card parity, life, and board.

Kaito is uncounterable on their turn: Kaito has hexproof during your turn. But on their turn, when they attack with Kaito's minus ability or when Kaito first resolves, you can Inevitable Defeat it — Inevitable Defeat is an instant and doesn't require targeting during your turn. You're exiling "target nonland permanent" which doesn't have the hexproof restriction. Get Kaito off the board with Inevitable Defeat the turn it resolves or the turn after they use his +2 ability.

Torpor Orb is your game-winner: Their disruption package is entirely ETB-based: Floodpits Drowner taps your board, Tishana's Tidebinder counters your abilities, Deep-Cavern Bat strips your hand. Their disruption suite is completely blank.

The Mana Hold Rule

Control loses games in one specific way: tapping out during your own main phase when the opponent then untaps and attacks or resolves an uncounterable threat. Every turn, before you decide what to cast, ask: what is the worst thing they can do if I tap out right now? If the answer is "kill me" or "resolve something I can't answer," hold the mana.

What to Hold Mana For
Priority Response Mana
{R}{W} = Lightning Helix — hold vs any aggro opponent with open attack mana. One Helix per turn is 6-point swing ({R}{W} mana + 3 damage + 3 life).

{2}{B}{W} = Inevitable Defeat — hold whenever their key permanent could resolve. Against Dimir: Kaito. Against Boros: Ojer Axonil. Against Mono-Green: Sapling Nursery.

{U}{W} = No More Lies — hold vs any key 3-drop you can't let resolve. Counters unless they pay {3} — on T3 with exactly 3 lands they almost never can.
When to Spend Mana
Safe Windows to Tap Out
Their end step is your safest window — they've declared no blocks, used their mana, and can't respond to your sorcery-speed cards until their next turn. Use their end step for: Consult the Star Charts (instant — dig for the key card you need), Stock Up on T4+ (sorcery but you're already in their end step... wait, Stock Up is sorcery, you can't cast it at instant speed). Correct: use end step for Consult. Cast Stock Up only when you have nothing else to do on your own main phase with mana open.

Consult the Star Charts — Instant Speed Dig

Consult ({1}{W} instant, kicker {1}{U}: look at top X lands you control, put 1 or 2 into hand) is your most efficient card selection tool because it's at instant speed. Use it on their end step, not on your main phase, so you untap with full mana available:

Correct Timing
Their End Step (After They Pass)
They pass priority in their end step. You respond: cast Consult kicked ({1}{U}{1}{W} = {2}{U}{W}). Look at top (lands you control) cards, put 2 in hand. Now you untap with a full hand and full mana. They get no response because it's still their end step — they're not untapping yet. This is the cleanest dig cycle in the deck.
Critical Oracle Rule
X = Lands You Control at Cast Time
You look at the top X cards where X is the number of lands you control at the time you cast Consult. With 6 lands = look at 6. With 8 lands = look at 8. This gets dramatically better as the game goes on — by turn 8 you're looking at 8 cards for {2}{U}{W}. Always wait until you have maximum lands before casting Consult for full dig depth.

Revelation — When and What to Bounce

Jeskai Revelation is your most powerful card but it costs {2}{U}{R}{W}. The five effects (bounce + 4 damage + 2 Monks + draw 2 + gain 4) all happen simultaneously. The bounce target is the highest-leverage decision:

Best Bounce Target
Their Key Permanent When They're Tapped
Cast Revelation on their end step when they've spent all their mana. Bounce their most dangerous permanent — not any permanent, the one they relied on most this turn. Sapling Nursery (back to hand, no tokens next turn unless they recast it). Kaito (loses all loyalty counters going back to hand). Enduring Curiosity (stops their draw engine). Time the bounce to the moment they can't immediately recast it.
The Monk Clock
Two Prowess 1/1s That Grow With Your Spells
Your Revelation Monks have prowess — every spell you cast makes them +1/+1 until EOT. Lightning Helix cast while Monks are attacking = both Monks are 2/2. Consult while they're attacking = 2/2. Stock Up + Consult = 3/3. Your instants that you were going to cast anyway are free combat pump on the Monks. Revelation creates a clock that accelerates with your existing game plan.
The Life Swing
+4 Life at Instant Speed
Against any aggro deck, the 4 life from Revelation plus the 3 life from a Lightning Helix in the same turn = 7 life at instant speed. That's often the turn you stabilize from "dead to their next attack" to "comfortable." Don't hold Revelation looking for the perfect bounce target — casting it purely for the life + draw + Monks is often correct against aggressive opponents.

Inevitable Defeat vs Kaito Hexproof

Kaito, Bane of Nightmares has "hexproof during your turn" — you cannot target him with spells or abilities you control during your own turn. But Inevitable Defeat is an instant. You can cast it during their turn (when Kaito's hexproof is inactive) or during their end step. The correct line:

The Timing Window
Cast Inevitable Defeat During Their Turn
Their main phase — after they deploy Kaito but before they end their turn. Or their end step. During their turn Kaito has NO hexproof. Inevitable Defeat: exile him. He's uncounterable (Inevitable Defeat's own text) and instant speed. They can't protect Kaito during their own turn because his hexproof only applies during YOUR turn. This is the single most important interaction rule vs Dimir.
Mistrise Village Setup
Make Defeat Doubly Uncounterable
Inevitable Defeat is already uncounterable by its own text. But Mistrise Village ({U}{T}: your next spell this turn can't be countered) adds a second layer. In the control mirror where they might try to counter your Defeat anyway — activate Mistrise first, then cast Defeat. It's now protected by both its own text AND the Village trigger. Use this against any opponent running countermagic who might try to get cute.

Ultima — The End Turn Timing

Ultima destroys all artifacts and creatures then ends the turn. "Ending the turn" means: exile all spells and abilities from the stack (including Ultima), the current player discards to max hand size, damage wears off, and "this turn" / "until end of turn" effects end. Critical timing implications:

Correct Use: Their Combat Step
Cast Ultima When They Attack
They declare attackers. You cast Ultima. Their attacking creatures die (destroyed). Their non-attackers die. The turn ends — their attack step is over, they go to their end step with an empty board and no damage dealt. This is the only sweeper in the format that also stops the attack that triggered it. Critical: they don't get to deal combat damage because the turn ending cancels the combat damage step.
What Ultima Does NOT Hit
Enchantments Survive Ultima
Ultima destroys artifacts AND creatures. It does not destroy enchantments. Sapling Nursery survives. Stormchaser's Talent survives. Warleader's Call survives. Earthbender Ascension survives. After Ultima, their enchantments are still in play and will resume generating value next turn. Plan for what survives — if Nursery is in play when you cast Ultima, you've swept their board but Nursery immediately starts making tokens on their next land drop.

Voice of Victory — They Cannot Cast Spells During Your Turn

Boros Aggro runs Voice of Victory whose text includes "your opponents can't cast spells during your turn." This is their turn ability on your turn — during the Boros player's turn after Voice attacks, you cannot cast anything. All interaction must happen during Boros's main phase BEFORE Voice attacks. The specific windows:

Your Response Windows vs Voice of Victory
Their Main Phase (OPEN)
You can cast any spell. Helix kills Sharpshooter here. Inevitable Defeat hits Ojer here. This is your last chance before Voice attacks and shuts you out.
After Voice Attacks (LOCKED)
You cannot cast spells at all. Warrior tokens enter, Sharpshooter pings, Ojer amplifies — all without response. Sit and take the damage.
Their End Step (OPEN)
Voice's "your turn" restriction ends. You can now cast Helix on their end step. But any damage from this turn's combat and ETBs has already been dealt.
Line 01
Your High Noon Line
You cast High Noon on T3 after identifying their key sorcery. High Noon prevents ALL sorcery-speed plays until EOT. You calculate: if their key play is a sorcery (Earthbender Ascension, their sweeper), you cast High Noon. If their key play is an instant (Counterspell, flash creatures), you hold High Noon. You never cast High Noon blindly — you know their specific threat first.
Line 02
Your Voice + Counter Line
You deploy Voice of Victory on T5 with {W}{U} or {1}{U} open (Dovin's Veto or Seam Rip). You attack immediately — Voice can't be targeted while attacking. They must cast their board wipe. You fire Dovin's Veto on their board wipe. If they don't have a wipe, you attack every turn until they're dead. You count their outs before committing Voice.
Line 03
Your Aven Interrupter Flash
You hold Aven Interrupter mana ({3}{W}{U} = 5 mana) from T5 onward. You flash Aven in when they tap for their most important non-creature spell. Aven ETB counters the spell. You now have a 5/4 in play. You follow with attack — they must spend removal on Aven instead of advancing their plan.
Line 04
Your Stormplain Detainment Line
You deploy Stormplain Detainment ({2}{W}) at their combat step when they declare attackers. All their creatures are tapped until EOT — their attack is cancelled. You untap and attack with your Voice of Victory. They cannot attack back that turn. You reset the board state from "them attacking" to "you attacking" in one instant-speed card.

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

No More Lies — Exiles If Countered, Not Just Counter
{W}{U} instant: "Counter unless they pay {3}. If countered this way, exile it instead of putting into graveyard." The exile matters against: flashback, escape, and GY recursion. If they DON'T pay {3}: their spell is exiled permanently. If they DO pay: their spell resolves normally and goes to GY. The {3} tax is steep early game but easy to pay T5+. Board out against decks with excess mana.
Sunfall — Exile, Not Destroy. Incubate Scales with Board Size
{3}{W}{W}: "Exile all creatures. Incubate X where X = creatures exiled." Exile bypasses indestructible, undying, death triggers, and "when dies" effects. The Incubator token has X +1/+1 counters — in a board stall with 6 creatures, you get a 6/6 for {2} to transform. Sunfall hits YOUR creatures too — don't play it with a board advantage unless the Incubator is worth more.
Tishana's Tidebinder — Permanent Ability Removal on Flash Body
{2}{U} 3/2 Flash Merfolk. ETB: "Counter up to one target activated or triggered ability. If countered this way, that permanent loses all abilities for as long as Tidebinder remains on the battlefield." This is permanent ability suppression — not until end of turn. A Tidebinder that counters Ob Nixilis's loyalty ability makes Ob lose ALL abilities while Tidebinder lives. Kill Tidebinder: Ob gets abilities back. The "up to one" means you can flash Tidebinder in with no target if you just need a 3/2 blocker.
Scrollshift as Protection — Exile Your Own Creature in Response
"Exile up to one target artifact, creature, or enchantment you control, then return it." In response to opponent's removal: Scrollshift your creature → it's exiled (removal fizzles, no legal target) → it returns. The creature is a new object: no longer targeted by anything. Also draws a card. This is a {2}{W} protection spell + cantrip that dodges ALL targeted removal.
Ob Nixilis Casualty — Copy with Lower Loyalty
"Casualty X: sacrifice creature with power X. Copy this spell. Copy isn't legendary, has starting loyalty X." If you sacrifice a 3-power creature: you get the original Ob (starting loyalty from card) AND a copy with loyalty 3. Both are planeswalkers named Ob Nixilis but the copy isn't legendary — no legend rule conflict. Two Obs on T3 is devastating. The copy can use +1 or -2 immediately.

Heuristics

  1. 01
    Never tap out when they have open attack mana — hold response mana always. Control's golden rule. Tapping out for a sorcery-speed Consult or Stock Up when they have 3 open mana means they untap and attack into an unguarded Jeskai player. The loss condition is almost always "I tapped out and they attacked for lethal." Hold {R}{W} for Helix or {U}{W} for No More Lies every single turn.
  2. 02
    Inevitable Defeat is your primary removal — use it on indestructible, high-toughness, and enchantment threats that other spells miss. Helix answers toughness 3 or less. Inevitable Defeat answers everything else. Reserve Defeat for: Ojer Axonil (4 toughness, indestructible when transformed), Sapling Nursery (enchantment), Mossborn Hydra (large toughness), Eddymurk Crab (5 toughness). Don't waste Defeat on a Shocking Sharpshooter when Helix handles it.
  3. 03
    Jeskai Revelation's bounce is a tempo gain, not permanent removal — bounce their best permanent, not their most permanent. Revelation returns "target spell or permanent to its owner's hand." Bouncing a Sapling Nursery delays it one turn but they replay it next turn. Bouncing a planeswalker when they have the mana to re-cast is poor value. Best targets: bounce their spell in response to ETB before it resolves (tempo gain + 4 damage + tokens + 2 draws + 4 life), or bounce their key permanent when they're at 1 land and can't re-cast.
  4. 04
    Mistrise Village's uncounterable activation must be used the same turn you cast the spell — it applies to the next spell you cast this turn. Activate Mistrise Village ({U}{T}: next spell can't be countered) immediately before casting Inevitable Defeat or Jeskai Revelation in the control mirror. The activation is at sorcery speed — use it before moving to cast the spell you want to protect. The uncounterable window doesn't carry to next turn.
  5. 05
    Ultima ends the turn after destroying artifacts and creatures — your own combat damage and effects end too. If you had your own permanents with "until end of turn" effects, they end. More importantly: if you were planning to attack after Ultima, you can't — the turn ends immediately after Ultima resolves. Cast Ultima BEFORE your combat step if you want to destroy their board and still attack with your Monks. Or cast it on their end step to take out their board before untapping.
  6. 06
    Voice of Victory prevents opponents from casting spells during your turn — plan your removal before their attack step. When facing Boros Aggro, after Voice attacks and creates Mobilize tokens, you cannot cast Lightning Helix or Inevitable Defeat in response during their turn because Voice's ability prevents opponents from casting spells DURING their turn. You must deploy removal BEFORE they enter their attack step. If they attack with Voice and you're holding Helix, you've missed the window.
  7. 07
    Consult the Star Charts at instant speed is your most efficient card selection — use it on their end step. Consult: {1}{W} instant, unkicked = look at top (lands you control), put 1 in hand. Kicked {1}{U} = put 2 in hand. On their end step when they've passed priority: cast kicked Consult to dig 6 cards deep and take 2 into your full untap. This is your primary draw engine — it's better than Stock Up (sorcery) because you can respond and dig during their turn.

Revelation: 3 vs 4 copies
4 is correct for RC
Jeskai Revelation is your win condition and your stabilizer. Some lists run 3 to fit more interaction, but 4 ensures you reliably find it in games 2-3 when they've boarded in more threats. The {2}{U}{R}{W} cost is heavy but you're running 26 lands with extensive fixing. Never cut below 3.
Day of Judgment: 2 main + 1 SB
Match the meta
2 main deck is correct for the current meta. In aggro-heavy metas (Boros Aggro up in meta share), run 3-4 main. In midrange-heavy metas, trim to 1 main and add more targeted removal. The SB copy gives access to 3 total against token-heavy strategies without dead draws against control.
Ultima: 2 main deck
Use vs artifact decks
Ultima's value is the artifact+creature wipe combined with ending the turn. If artifact decks (Krang, Izzet Artifacts) are absent from the meta, Ultima is usually worse than a 3rd Day of Judgment. The "end the turn" clause is uniquely powerful in some situations but mostly irrelevant in the current meta. Trim to 1 if facing mostly creature-only decks.
Counterspell ratio
6 total (4 NML + 2 Snare)
4 No More Lies + 2 Spell Snare is the correct ratio for a primarily reactive control deck. Against heavy countermagic metas (more control): add Get Out (modal — also returns your own permanents). Against creature-heavy metas: trim a Spell Snare for a Get Lost. The deck shouldn't run more than 8 total counterspells or it becomes too clunky in the aggro matchups.
Gloomlake + Godless Shrine splash
Black access for Dimir SB
The Gloomlake Verge + Godless Shrine package provides black mana access for the sideboard. Tishana's Tidebinder is {1}{U}{G} but some builds add Duress or Thoughtseize access. The splash is minimal and doesn't affect main deck consistency. If you cut black from the SB, trim the Gloomlake Verges to 0-1.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any 7 with 2 lands + Voice of Victory OR 2 lands + Aven Interrupter + a counter. Never keep a hand that can't interact by T2.
  • Voice of Victory rule: Voice of Victory (3/3 flier) can't be targeted during attacks. Attack immediately — they need a board wipe or sacrifice effect to remove it while attacking.
  • Aven Interrupter rule: Aven Interrupter (5/4 flash) counters a target instant/sorcery on ETB. Flash it when they tap for their best spell. The counter resolves when Aven enters.
  • High Noon rule: High Noon prevents all sorcery-speed plays. Deploy it when they're about to cast their key sorcery. Both players are affected — plan your own sorceries accordingly.
  • Stormplain Detainment rule: Stormplain Detainment taps all their creatures until EOT. Deploy at their attack step before they attack — they're tapped down and can't attack this turn.
  • Board rule: Spell Snare ({U}) hard counters MV exactly 2. Know their MV-2 targets: Earthbender Ascension MV 2, Badgermole Cub MV 2. Both are Spell Snare targets.

Testing Framework

Block 01
Voice Attack Lines
Practice the Voice lock: they can't target it while attacking. Identify what removes Voice — board wipes and sacrifice effects vs targeted removal.
Block 02
Aven Interrupter Flash Timing
They cast a key instant: flash Aven in response. Aven ETB counters the spell. Know that Aven's ETB trigger counters on resolution, not on the stack.
Block 03
High Noon Windows
Cast it when they're about to cast their key sorcery. Too early = they play around it. Too late = damage is done.
Block 04
Stormplain Detainment Timing
They commit creatures to attack: tap them at their declare-attackers step. They can't attack AND they're tapped down for your swing.

Matchday Routine

  1. 01
    Voice of Victory: attack immediately when it resolves. Every turn you wait they're developing against an uncontested clock.
  2. 02
    Aven Interrupter: know exactly which spell you're countering before deploying. It counters on ETB — a hard counter at instant speed.
  3. 03
    High Noon: optimal window is when they're tapped and about to cast their key sorcery. Early High Noon is often wrong.
  4. 04
    Between rounds: count Stormplain Detainment efficiency — preventing full attacks vs wasting it on 1-2 creatures.
  5. 05
    T1 plan by matchup: vs Mono-Green (hold Spell Snare for MV 2), vs Izzet (Aven for Slickshot), vs Dimir (Duress first).
