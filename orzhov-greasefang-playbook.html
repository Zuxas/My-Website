Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"Turn 3 Greasefang is the fastest combo in Pioneer. One card in the GY plus Greasefang equals 13 damage. You don't need anything else."

The plan is brutally simple: discard Parhelion II to any of your eight discard outlets on turns 1–2, cast Greasefang, Okiba Boss on turn 3. Greasefang's ETB returns Parhelion II from your GY to the battlefield. Greasefang has power 4, which meets Parhelion II's crew cost. You attack with a 9/9 flying vehicle with first strike, vigilance, and haste — creating two 4/4 Angel tokens with flying and vigilance. That's 13 damage and a follow-up 8 damage from the Angels. Opponents need to be at 8 or below for the kill to be complete, which is most of the time by T4–5.

What makes the modern Orzhov build superior to older Greasefang shells is the Monument to Endurance engine. Monument generates mana acceleration every time you discard a card — the same discards you're already doing to put Parhelion II in the GY. With The Mycosynth Gardens copying Monument, you can ramp into Greasefang on turn 2, threaten both the combo kill and a resilient midrange gameplan simultaneously. The deck isn't fragile GY combo anymore — it's a two-axis threat.

Cryptcaller Chariot is the backup vehicle. When you discard a card while the Chariot is in play, it creates Zombie tokens. Greasefang can reanimate the Chariot instead of Parhelion II, letting the Chariot attack while generating more tokens. Against GY hate decks, you can sometimes go wide on Zombie tokens without ever needing the GY at all.

Why It's #2 at ~11%

Speed
T3 Kill Is Real
Eight discard outlets across four cards (Seasoned Hallowblade, Guardian of New Benalia, Fleeting Spirit, plus Monument looting). The combo reliably assembles by turn 3. With Monument mana acceleration, you can threaten a turn-2 Greasefang in nut draws. No other Pioneer deck presents a T3 13-damage attack this consistently.
Resilience
Two-Axis Threat
Against GY hate: pivot to Cryptcaller Chariot Zombie tokens + Monument ramp. Against creature hate: Parhelion's Angels close from the air. Against hand disruption: Sheltered by Ghosts protects Greasefang. You're not a one-trick combo deck — you have a legitimate midrange backup that wins games independently.
Disruption
Thoughtseize + Fatal Push
The best interaction package in Pioneer. Thoughtseize answers anything before it resolves. Fatal Push hits every relevant creature at one mana. Sheltered by Ghosts gives one of your key pieces protection from a color. You're proactive AND interactive — opponents can't just goldfish past you; you're stripping their answers while assembling your combo.

How the Combo Kills

Step 1 — T1/T2
Discard Parhelion II
T1: Cast Seasoned Hallowblade or Guardian of New Benalia. Use their activated/ETB ability to discard Parhelion II to GY. Or T2: Monument to Endurance generates mana when you discard — discard Parhelion II for both the GY setup AND Monument value. Parhelion must be in GY before Greasefang resolves.
Step 2 — T3
Cast Greasefang, Attack for 13
Greasefang, Okiba Boss ETB: return a Vehicle from your GY to the battlefield under your control — it gains haste. Parhelion II enters with haste. Greasefang (power 4) crews it (crew 4). Attack with the 9/9 Parhelion II: deal damage + create two 4/4 Angel tokens with flying and vigilance. Parhelion II returns to GY at EOT. Angels stay.
Step 3 — T4
Angels + Repeat
Two 4/4 Angels with flying and vigilance attack for 8. If Parhelion is back in GY (it returned at EOT), cast Greasefang again for another 13-damage swing. Total over two turns: 13 + 8 + 13 = 34 potential damage. Most opponents are at 0 by turn 4. The Angels also block during their turn since they have vigilance.
Plan B — Monument
Token Flood Without GY
Monument to Endurance + Cryptcaller Chariot: each discard triggers Monument (mana) and Chariot (Zombie token). With four discard outlets in hand, flood the board with Zombies in a single turn. This plan bypasses all GY hate — no GY needed, just discards. Against Hearse or RIP, pivot immediately to this gameplan games 2–3.

Key Card Notes

The Engine
Greasefang, Okiba Boss
3/4 legendary creature for {2}{B}. ETB: return target Vehicle from your GY to the battlefield. That Vehicle gains haste until EOT. At the beginning of your EOT, sacrifice that Vehicle if it's still on the battlefield. Key rule: Greasefang has power 4 — exactly enough to crew Parhelion II (crew 4) by itself. You can play Greasefang, reanimate Parhelion II, and immediately crew and attack, all in the same turn.
The Target
Parhelion II
9/9 Vehicle artifact with crew 4, flying, first strike, vigilance. When it attacks, create two 4/4 white Angel creature tokens with flying and vigilance. The combo attack deals 9 (Parhelion) + creates two 4/4 Angels = 13 total damage potential (Parhelion attacks, Angels are created). Next turn Angels swing for 8. Parhelion returns to GY at EOT — ready for another Greasefang next turn.
The Ramp
Monument to Endurance
Artifact that triggers whenever you discard a card: add mana (usually {W} or {B}) equal to the number of Monuments you control. The Mycosynth Gardens can copy Monument as an artifact land — stack two Monuments and each discard adds {2}. Four discard outlets in one turn generates enormous mana for Greasefang or other threats. This accelerates your combo turn by a full turn in ideal draws.
The Backup
Cryptcaller Chariot
Vehicle artifact. Whenever you discard a card while Cryptcaller Chariot is on the battlefield, create a 2/2 Zombie creature token. Multiple discard outlets + Cryptcaller = a board flooded with Zombies in one turn — no GY required. Greasefang can also reanimate the Chariot itself as a backup attack. Against GY hate (Rest in Peace, Grafdigger's Cage), pivot to this plan immediately.

Standard Draw
T3 Greasefang
T1: Seasoned Hallowblade or Guardian of New Benalia. T2: Activate discard outlet — discard Parhelion II to GY. T3: Cast Greasefang (3 mana). ETB returns Parhelion II. Crew with Greasefang (power 4). Attack with 9/9 flying first strike vigilance + haste. Two 4/4 Angels created. Leave opponent at 7. T4: Angels + Parhelion again = lethal.
Disrupted Draw
Monument Backup
Opponent kills Greasefang / exiles your GY. Shift to Monument plan. Cryptcaller Chariot in play: discard your whole hand via four outlets = four Zombie tokens in one turn. Monument generates mana with each discard. Build a 4–6 creature board by T4–5 with zero GY involvement. This plan legitimately beats decks that overboard on GY hate.
Mulligan
Keep: Outlet + Parhelion + Greasefang
Keep: any hand with one discard outlet, Parhelion II (or Monument to find it), and Greasefang, on 2–3 lands. Mulligan: hands without Greasefang AND without a clear path to it by T3. At 6: keep outlet + Parhelion + lands — Greasefang can be found via looting. At 5: only keep if you have Greasefang + Parhelion in hand with 2 lands.

CORE
ENGINES

"Eight discard outlets. Four Monuments. Four Mycosynth Gardens. Discarding is never a cost — it's always a benefit."
Engine 1 — Primary Combo
Discard → Greasefang → 13 Damage
Discard Parhelion II. Cast Greasefang. Reanimate Parhelion II with haste. Crew (Greasefang power 4 = crew 4). Attack for 9, create two 4/4 Angels. Parhelion returns to GY at EOT. This is the fastest kill in Pioneer at 13 damage per Greasefang cast. With two Greasefangs across two turns: 13 + 13 + 8 (Angels) = 34 potential damage.
Engine 2 — Mana Acceleration
Monument + Mycosynth Stack
Monument to Endurance: whenever you discard a card, add {1} (or color-appropriate mana) for each Monument in play. The Mycosynth Gardens copies Monument as an artifact, creating a second copy. Each discard now adds {2}. Four outlets in one turn generates {8} extra mana — enough to cast Greasefang, a spell, and still have gas. This enables T2 Greasefang in nut draws.
Engine 3 — Token Flood
Cryptcaller Chariot + Outlets
Cryptcaller Chariot: whenever you discard a card, create a 2/2 Zombie token. Each of your eight discard outlets creates a Zombie. With Chariot in play, discarding your hand to five outlets creates five 2/2 Zombies in one turn. On your end step using Fleeting Spirit or Hallowblade = Zombies your opponent doesn't see coming before their turn. No GY required — this plan beats every GY hate card in the format.
Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Greasefang reanimates on ETB — it doesn't target. There's no window to 'respond to the vehicle entering' once Greasefang resolves."
Line 01 — Parhelion II Returns to GY at EOT — Set Up T4 Repeat
Parhelion II is sacrificed at the beginning of your EOT after Greasefang reanimates it. It goes back to your GY — not exiled. This means if you cast a second Greasefang next turn (or replay the same one), you can reanimate Parhelion again. This is the full kill: T3 Greasefang (13 damage + 2 Angels created), T4 the two 4/4 Angels attack (8 damage) + second Greasefang reanimates Parhelion again (9 more damage). Plan for this line rather than just hoping the first swing kills.
Line 02 — Sheltered by Ghosts Gives Protection After Resolution, Not Before
Sheltered by Ghosts enchants a creature and gives it protection from a chosen color. This means: (1) existing targeted removal resolves before you can enchant, (2) you must name the color BEFORE they choose to remove your creature. The correct play: anticipate their removal color (black vs Fatal Push, red vs Lightning Axe) and preemptively Shelter the creature before they can use it. Don't wait for them to cast — name the color you expect and Shelter early. Also: Shelter gives protection which means the creature can no longer be targeted by that color. Enchantments of that color fall off. Very strong against black removal and red burn.
Line 03 — Greasefang's ETB Doesn't Target — It's a May Effect On Resolution
Greasefang says "when Greasefang enters, you may return target Vehicle card from your GY to the battlefield with haste." This is a triggered ability that goes on the stack when Greasefang enters. Opponents can respond to Greasefang before the trigger resolves (kill Greasefang, exile your GY). However: once the trigger resolves, the vehicle enters with haste — there's no additional trigger to respond to. The common mistake: thinking they can "respond to the vehicle entering." They cannot. If the trigger resolves, Parhelion II is on the battlefield.
Line 04 — Cryptcaller Chariot Can Be Reanimated as a Blocker, Not Just an Attacker
Greasefang can reanimate Cryptcaller Chariot instead of Parhelion II. The Chariot gets haste, you crew it (crew cost is low), and it attacks. More importantly: in defensive scenarios, reanimating the Chariot and using it as a blocker via Greasefang's ETB is legitimate. Chariot reanimates, you don't have to crew it immediately — you can hold it. It also creates Zombies on your discard triggers during the same turn. Use this line when Parhelion is exiled or unavailable, or when you need a blocker more than an attacker.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"You are the fastest combo deck in the room. You also have the best hand disruption. Play accordingly."
vs Aggro (UR Cutter, RDW)
Race — Priority on Speed
Against UR Cutter: they kill on T3–4, you kill on T3. You must go first or match their speed. Don't slow down to use Fatal Push — only kill creatures that would block Greasefang or prevent your attack. Dead aim: assemble the combo as fast as possible. If you're on the draw and they have a board by T2, Parhelion's first strike blocks favorably, buying turns. The 13-damage swing + two 4/4 Angels racing their 1/1 Monks usually wins.
vs Control (UW Control)
Force Countermagic
Against UW Control: they counter Greasefang. Board in Thoughtseize + Duress — strip their counterspell before you cast Greasefang. The correct play: T2 Thoughtseize to see their hand, take their counter or sweeper. T3 Greasefang resolves uncountered. Sheltered by Ghosts on Greasefang after it resolves makes it very difficult to remove. If they sweep, re-reanimate next turn — Parhelion is back in GY.
vs Midrange (Rakdos, Selesnya)
Combo + Interaction
Against midrange: use Fatal Push + Thoughtseize to clear the path for Greasefang. Their removal (Fatal Push, Push) answers your creatures, not your vehicles — Parhelion in GY is safe from most hate. Strip their key disruption pieces (Graveyard Trespasser, their hate bears) before combo turn. The midrange clock is slower than your combo, so you have turns to interact first.

UR CUTTER (IZZET PROWESS)

Unfavorable

They are faster than you on the draw. UR Cutter's T3 kill clock matches your T3 combo, but they're more resilient to disruption — 12 cantrips means stripping one threat isn't enough. Game 1 is a straight race. Parhelion II's first strike means it favorably trades or blocks their Monk tokens. The two 4/4 Angels are often bigger than everything they have.

Priority: Maximize speed. Don't use Fatal Push on small Monks — only use it on their Vivi Ornitier or Stormchaser's Talent that would prevent you from attacking profitably. Aim for the T3 combo even if they have some board presence. Your 9/9 first strike flies over or tramples their board.

Needle: Reidane, God of the Worthy (SB) taxes noncreature spells costing 3 or less — hits all their cantrips and Treasure Cruise at instant speed. This single card completely disrupts their engine. Board it in immediately.

P1 — The Discard Suite
Thoughtseize ×4 + Duress ×3
Thoughtseize hits any card. Duress hits noncreature spells. Together they strip counterspells before Greasefang, remove combo pieces from opponent's hand, and let you see exactly what disruption they have. Board in vs: control (takes their counters/sweepers), combo (takes their key pieces), midrange (takes their best removal before your combo turn). The best seven cards in your SB by a wide margin.
P2 — GY Hate Response
Go Blank ×2
Go Blank: sorcery, each opponent discards two cards and exiles their GY. This serves two functions: (1) it strips opponents' GY-based threats (Phoenix, Cruise fuel), (2) it exiles their GY in response to their own GY manipulation. Board in vs: Izzet Phoenix, any Treasure Cruise shell. Also excellent vs control to strip their hand further after a Thoughtseize chain.
P3 — Transformational Plan
Waste Not ×2
Waste Not: whenever an opponent discards a card, get a benefit based on card type (creature = Zombie token, noncreature spell = mana, land = draw). Combine with Thoughtseize + Duress to generate massive resources. Against control: T2 Waste Not, T3 Thoughtseize their hand — each discard creates Zombies and mana. This plan doesn't require GY at all, bypassing hate entirely. Board in when GY plan is shutdown.
P4 — Removal & Hosers
Anguished Unmaking ×2 + Reidane ×2
Anguished Unmaking: exile any nonland permanent for {1}{W}{B} at instant speed. Answers Graveyard Trespasser, Teferi, planeswalkers, anything. Reidane, God of the Worthy: legendary 2/3 flying creature for {2}{W}. Her god side taxes noncreature spells costing 3 or less — directly hits UR Cutter's entire cantrip suite and Treasure Cruise. Board Reidane vs any cantrip-heavy strategy.

Chapter 09

HEURISTICS

"Thoughtseize before Greasefang. Always. No exceptions."
  1. 01Thoughtseize the turn before Greasefang, never the same turn. If you Thoughtseize on T2 and cast Greasefang on T3, you know exactly what interaction they have. If you wait and try to do both on T3, you either spend mana awkwardly or resolve Greasefang into unknown countermagic. T2 Thoughtseize → T3 Greasefang is the correct sequencing 90% of the time.
  2. 02Discard Parhelion II as early as possible — don't hold it. Parhelion II in your hand is dead weight. You cannot cast it from hand (9 mana). It only matters in the GY. The moment you have a discard outlet, put Parhelion in the GY. Even if you don't have Greasefang yet — Parhelion in GY is always better than Parhelion in hand.
  3. 03Sheltered by Ghosts protects from targeted removal, not board wipes. Name black vs Fatal Push/Dreadbore/Thoughtseize. Name red vs Lightning Strike/Fiery Impulse. Name white vs Fatally Push effects. Shelter does NOT protect from Supreme Verdict (doesn't target) or exile-all effects. Pre-name the color based on their mana base: Godless Shrine = name black, Steam Vents = name red.
  4. 04Monument to Endurance + Mycosynth Gardens stack — play Gardens as Monument immediately when Monument is in play. The Mycosynth Gardens enters as a land and can tap for any color. Activate it to copy Monument before your first discard of the turn. Now each discard adds {2} instead of {1}. This acceleration changes your curve by a full turn. Always check if Gardens is in play when you're about to discard.
  5. 05Parhelion II returns to GY at YOUR EOT — plan for turn 4 repeat. After Greasefang's attack, Parhelion returns to GY at your EOT. It's not exiled — it's ready for another Greasefang ETB. Track whether a second Greasefang or a second cast is achievable. Two Greasefang attacks total 26 damage; the Angels contribute 8. You rarely need both — know exactly when the game is over.
  6. 06Against GY hate, pivot immediately — don't try to fight through it. If they have Rest in Peace, Grafdigger's Cage, or Unlicensed Hearse active, your GY plan is off. Pivot to Cryptcaller Chariot Zombies or Waste Not (SB) plan immediately. Don't spend resources trying to remove their hate piece when you have a fully functional Plan B that doesn't require the GY at all.
  7. 07Fleeting Spirit's protection ability can be activated at instant speed. Fleeting Spirit can discard a card to gain protection from a chosen color until EOT — at instant speed, at any priority window. Use this to dodge removal being cast on it, or to trigger Cryptcaller Chariot on their turn. A Spirit with protection from black is very difficult for Rakdos to answer. Don't just use it to discard on your main phase — use it reactively.
  8. 08Cryptcaller Chariot Zombie triggers on EACH discard, not per turn. Each discard outlet activation triggers Chariot separately. Seasoned Hallowblade (discard 1) + Guardian ETB loot (discard 1) + Fleeting Spirit (discard 1) = three Zombie tokens in one turn. With four outlets available, you can flood the board in a single turn. Count your available discard activations before deciding whether to pivot to the Zombie plan.
  9. 09Geier Reach Sanitarium loots at instant speed for both players — use it at EOT. The Sanitarium activates at instant speed and makes BOTH players loot. Use it on your EOT after you've done everything else — your opponent gets no benefit from the card they draw before your untap. Also: you can use it to discard Parhelion II if your discard outlets aren't available. The fact it also triggers Monument to Endurance makes it a dual-purpose utility land.
  10. 10Takenuma, Abandoned Mire channels to return a legendary from GY — gets Greasefang back. Channel Takenuma: discard it and pay {2}{B} to return a legendary creature from your GY to hand. If Greasefang is killed, channeling Takenuma recovers it for the combo next turn. This is the most important utility land activation — know its cost ({2}{B}) and have it available when Greasefang is in your GY.

The entire engine. Finding it consistently on T3 requires four copies. Running three introduces too much variance — games where you don't find it by T4 are almost always losses. Four copies means you find it in your opening 7 about 40% of the time and by turn 3 in the vast majority of games.
Lock — Never Cut
4 Parhelion II
You must have Parhelion in your GY when Greasefang ETBs. Four copies plus eight discard outlets plus Geier Reach Sanitarium ensures Parhelion is in the GY by T3 consistently. Running three risks games where you have Greasefang but no Parhelion to target — an embarrassing failure state.
Lock — Never Cut
8 Discard Outlets (4+4)
Seasoned Hallowblade + Guardian of New Benalia form the base. Both are independently powerful two-drops that also crew vehicles and have relevant stats. The two-drop slot must have eight copies to guarantee a T1–T2 discard outlet in opening hands consistently. Cutting to six creates too many slow hands.
Flex 2–4
Monument to Endurance
Four copies is standard — the mana acceleration is excellent and it's a payoff for discards you're doing anyway. In very aggro-heavy metas where the Monument plan is irrelevant and speed is everything, trim to two. In slower metas where Monument's ramp and the transformational SB plan are valuable, keep four.
Flex 2–4
Cryptcaller Chariot
Four copies is correct in any meta with significant GY hate. If GY hate is light, trim to two — it's the fourth most important piece and draws awkwardly in multiples. Always keep at least two for the backup Zombie plan against hate-heavy opponents.
Register When
Midrange-Light Metas
Greasefang is best when the meta is control or aggro — no disruption to your GY and you race them. It's worst when Rakdos Midrange is over 15% (Thoughtseize + Graveyard Trespasser is a brutal combination). If Rakdos is dominant, shift to a different Pioneer strategy. Otherwise Greasefang is correct at ~11% meta share.

Know Cold
The Kill Sequence
Memorize: (1) discard outlet discards Parhelion, (2) Greasefang ETB returns Parhelion with haste, (3) Greasefang crews (power 4 = crew 4), (4) Parhelion attacks — 9 damage + two 4/4 Angels created, (5) Parhelion returns to GY at EOT. You must execute this in under 20 seconds per tournament rules. Practice until it's automatic.
Know Cold
Pivot Trigger: Is GY Hate Active?
Check at the start of every game: do they have Rest in Peace, Grafdigger's Cage, or Unlicensed Hearse on the battlefield? If yes: go immediately to Cryptcaller Chariot + discard outlet Zombie plan. Do not spend any resources trying to fight the hate piece unless you have Anguished Unmaking. Monument + Chariot + four outlets is a real kill.
Verify
T1 Land Read
Steam Vents = UR Cutter (board Reidane + Thoughtseize G2, race G1). Hallowed Fountain = UW Control (board discard suite, Thoughtseize before Greasefang). Overgrown Tomb = Rakdos Midrange (board Unmaking + Thoughtseize). Forest + land = Selesnya Company (board Thoughtseize + Unmaking). Mountain only = RDW (board Reidane, speed up your clock).
Round 1 Decision
Go First or Second?
Almost always choose to go first. Thoughtseize on T1 on the draw gets you a card but you're behind on tempo. On the play: T1 outlet, T2 discard Parhelion, T3 Greasefang = fastest possible line. The deck is better on the play than any deck in the format except UR Cutter. Only consider going second if you have a very specific hand that needs the extra card.
