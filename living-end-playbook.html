Chapter 01

Deck
Identity

"Living End is the perfect weapon for anyone who wants to play a true combo deck while keeping the ability to interact. When it's working, nothing in Modern feels as clean or as devastating as a turn-3 cascade into a full board wipe and immediate rebuild." — BtB Jericho

Living End is a cascade combo deck built around one card: Living End, a sorcery with Suspend 3 and no mana cost. Because it has no mana cost, cascade spells (which look for "a nonland card that costs less" than themselves) will always find and cast it for free. You spend turns 1–2 cycling large creatures into your graveyard, then cascade on turn 3 via Violent Outburst (instant-speed) or Ardent Plea. Living End resolves: all creatures are exiled and sacrificed, then all your GY creatures enter the battlefield. Your opponent loses their board. You gain 3–6 large creatures.

The deck has evolved significantly from its original "easy win" form. The printed elements include interaction (Force of Negation, Subtlety, Grief), a resilient payoff in Overlord of the Balemurk (mills 4 each trigger, filling the GY faster), and Violent Outburst as an instant-speed cascade at end of opponent's turn. This protects against sorcery-speed hate like Relic of Progenitus activations and Rest in Peace at your combat step.

Deck Identity

The Kill Mechanism
Cascade → Living End
Cast Violent Outburst or Ardent Plea (MV 3). Cascade exiles cards until it finds a nonland card costing less than MV 3. Living End has no mana cost — MV 0 — so cascade finds it immediately. Cast Living End for free. Board wipe + graveyard refill.
Why MV 0?
No Mana Cost = No MV
Living End has "Suspend 3—{2}{B}{B}" but no printed mana cost at all. Cards with no mana cost have MV 0. The cascade rule says "exile cards until you exile a nonland card that costs less." Less than MV 3 = MV 0, 1, or 2. Living End at MV 0 qualifies. This is why the deck runs ZERO other nonland spells with MV 0, 1, or 2 in the main deck — any such spell could be hit by cascade instead.
Instant Speed
Violent Outburst EOT
Violent Outburst is an INSTANT. Cast it at end of opponent's turn to dodge sorcery-speed hate. Rest in Peace, Relic of Progenitus activations, Grafdigger's Cage — many GY hate pieces can only be deployed at sorcery speed. Casting Violent Outburst at end step also means you hold up mana for Subtlety or Force of Negation on the same turn.
Free Cycling
Street Wraith — Pay 2 Life
Street Wraith cycles for "pay 2 life" — zero mana. On turn 1 with no lands, you can cycle Street Wraith freely if you started at 20 life. Four copies means 8 free life spent cycling by turn 3. This is how the deck fills the GY while still playing on curve. Monstrous Carabid cycles for {B/R} (one mana), Horror for {B}.
Modern Constraint
Zero Sub-3-MV Spells
The deck cannot run any nonland spell with MV 2 or less in the main deck — cascade would hit it instead of Living End, wasting the cascade trigger. This shapes every card choice: every noncreature interaction piece (Force of Negation, Subtlety, Grief, Endurance) is either a creature with cycling or a "free" spell that doesn't go in the main cascade sequence.
Meta Position
2.8% · Consistent Threat
BtB Jericho: "A rewarding experience for those players who like graveyard and combo strategies alike." Living End is a strong metagame choice when opponents haven't prepared GY hate. The sideboard is heavily tailored to beat GY hate since every opponent brings it in. Expert pilots routinely top8 Modern challenges with the deck.

Mill 4 on entry = 4 more cards in GY for Living End. Also recurs a key creature if Living End has been countered.

The Zero-MV Constraint

Every nonland spell in the main deck must have MV 3 or higher. Cascade exiles from the top until it finds a nonland card costing less than the cascade spell's MV. For Violent Outburst (MV 3), that means any MV 0–2 nonland spell would be hit. This is why interaction like Force of Negation (MV 3) and Subtlety (MV 4) live here — they're above the threshold. Never put a 1-drop or 2-drop spell in this deck's main. Ever.

Chapter 03

Game
Flow

Every Living End game has the same structure: spend turns 1–2 cycling, cascade on turn 3 at end of opponent's turn, resolve Living End. The variance is in how many creatures you cycle, whether your opponent has disruption, and how many creatures you're bringing back.

The Standard Line

Turn 1
Cycle freely. Street Wraith — pay 2 life, cycle (put Wraith in GY, draw a card). Find a land or another cycler. Monstrous Carabid cycles for {B/R} = one mana. With any land and a cycler, always cycle on turn 1. Priority: cycle as many creatures as possible. Each creature in the GY is a creature you get back with Living End. Overlord of the Balemurk: play it early for its impending cost {1}{B} — mills 4 on entry even before it becomes a creature.
Turn 2
Continue cycling. Horror of the Broken Lands ({B} cycle): cycles cheaply and gets larger when you cycle other cards (irrelevant since it's going to the GY anyway — you want it there). Curator of Mysteries ({U} cycle): scries 1 whenever you cycle or discard. Stack Curator cycling last to get maximum scry benefit. With Subtlety or Grief in hand: hold them until needed for protection on turn 3.
Turn 3 — Kill
At END OF OPPONENT'S TURN: cast Violent Outburst. Cascade triggers — exile from library until you find Living End (MV 0, always first nonland hit since the main deck has no MV 0–2 nonland cards). Cast Living End for free: all creatures from all GYs enter your battlefield (plus opponents' if they have any), all creatures on the battlefield are sacrificed/exiled first. Board wipe complete. Your cycled creatures enter. With 5–7 creatures in GY: immediate board of attackers.
The Hat Trick
Cycle creatures with Living End on the stack. Once you've cast Living End via cascade, its resolution is on the stack. Before it resolves, cycle more creatures — they go to the GY. When Living End resolves, it picks up ALL creatures in your GY at that moment, including the ones just cycled. This can add 2–3 more creatures to the board. This is an advanced play for maximizing the Living End trigger.

Protecting the Combo

Before Cascading
Grief Disruption
Cast Grief (evoke — exile a black card) before cascading to strip the opponent's counter or GY hate piece. Grief ETB: each opponent reveals their hand, you choose a nonland, nonblack card to discard. Stripping a Force of Negation, Rest in Peace, or Teferi before your cascade is often game-winning. Grief dies from evoke but the discard effect already resolved.
On the Cascade Trigger
Hold Up Force / Subtlety
Cast Violent Outburst and cascade into Living End. Before Living End resolves, opponents can respond. Hold Force of Negation to counter opposing responses. Subtlety can be cast in response to an opponent's Endurance (trying to shuffle your GY) — Subtlety puts Endurance back on top/bottom of library, protecting your GY before Living End resolves.

When to Play Ardent Plea vs Violent Outburst

Violent Outburst
Instant — Default
Always prefer Violent Outburst when possible. Cast at end of opponent's turn — they must hold up interaction during their own turn, can't tap out, can't play sorceries in response. The instant speed is the strongest protection the deck has against sorcery-speed hate.
Ardent Plea
Sorcery — Redundancy
Ardent Plea is sorcery speed. Cast on your main phase turn 3 when you have 3 mana. Useful when Violent Outburst is countered or unavailable. The exalted trigger is irrelevant — you're cascading into a mass resurrection, not attacking alone. Running 3 copies ensures consistency.
Stack Management
Which Cascade Finds What
Both cascades into Living End (the only legal hit in the deck). If you have both, hold Violent Outburst for instant speed. After Living End resolves and you have a second cascade spell: the second cascade finds… Living End again if it's still in the library. If Living End was already cast (it goes to exile via cascade), the second cascade finds nothing above MV 0 and whiffs.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
Cycle Aggressively T1–2
On the play you want to cycle creatures into the graveyard as fast as possible. T1 cycle Deadshot Minotaur or Street Wraith (free cycle). T2 cycle another or cast Violent Outburst if you have 3 mana. On the play: count GY creatures before T3 — you need 3+ for a meaningful Living End. With 5+ creatures you win combat by a wide margin after resolution.
On the Draw
Extra Cycle Finds the Cascade
On the draw you see 8 cards, increasing cascade spell odds. Keep any hand with 2+ cyclers on the draw even without cascade — the extra card often finds Violent Outburst or Ardent Plea. Against aggressive decks on the draw: Grief hands (T1 discard their key threat) become more valuable since you’re a turn behind on the clock and need to disrupt their development.

When Disrupted

Living End Countered
Second Cascade
They counter Living End on the cascade trigger: your creatures stay cycled and you need a second cascade spell. Deploy Grief or Subtlety to attack their hand / remove their next counter. Then Ardent Plea or second Outburst next turn. With 8 cascade spells in the deck, you often have a second line. The key: don’t tap out casting Outburst into obvious open counter mana.
GY Exiled
Commandeer Their Threat
Nihil Spellbomb or Endurance exiles your GY. You have no creatures to reanimate. Pivot: Commandeer steals their best permanent (Titan, Solitude, planeswalker) for free using 2 blue cards from hand. Inevitable Betrayal (suspend) also takes their creature eventually. In the worst case, your Grief and Subtlety bodies are still 3/1 and 3/3 flying threats. You can win fair.

Setup

Even if Living End is countered once, your 4–6 cycled creatures are still in GY. Find the next cascade spell and cast again. If no cascade spells left: the cycled creatures are a GY threat that opponents must handle with additional hate. Overlord + cycled creatures = persistent pressure even without the instant kill.

Protection Engine
Setup
Free Pitch Spells
Force of Negation (pitch blue, counter noncreature spell on opponent's turn), Subtlety (pitch blue, put creature/PW spell on top/bottom), Grief (pitch black, opponent discards), Endurance (pitch green, shuffle GY). All can be cast for free on opponent's turn by pitching a colored card. This gives the deck interaction without tapping mana, critical when you need mana for your own cascade on the same turn.

When you evoke Subtlety or Grief (die immediately after ETB), they go to GY. Living End picks them up! Evoke Subtlety to put an opposing creature spell on top of library → Subtlety dies → GY. Living End returns Subtlety as a 3/3 flying body. This is the "Subtlety from GY" line: cascade in response to opponent's big creature → Living End returns Subtlety → ETB puts their creature on top/bottom → you get a free 3/3.
Result
Both Modes Matter
The pitch spells serve dual purpose: protect your combo AND become creatures in your GY for Living End to return. Grief stripping a counter from opponent's hand → dying to evoke → returning as a 3/2 with "enter: opponent discards" again. Your interaction is also your win condition. This is the design elegance of the deck: every free spell is a future attacker.

Lines & Tricks

  • The Hat Trick — cycle on the stack: After cascading into Living End, Living End is on the stack and awaiting resolution. Before it resolves, cycle creatures from your hand. They hit the GY. When Living End resolves, it picks them up. Net: extra 2–3 creatures added to your board without spending extra mana. This is the highest-skill play in the deck.
  • Foundation Breaker enters from hand — Cage doesn't stop it: Grafdigger's Cage says creatures can't enter from GYs or libraries. Foundation Breaker is cast normally (from your hand), not from GY. It enters from hand. Its ETB "destroy target artifact or enchantment" fires and destroys Cage. Then Foundation Breaker itself is in play — it cycles: discard it to GY, draw a card. Now it's in GY for Living End to return.
  • Force of Negation can't counter Endurance: FoN counters noncreature spells only. Endurance is a creature. An opponent casting Endurance to shuffle your GY cannot be stopped by Force of Negation. Use Subtlety instead — Subtlety puts creature spells (including Endurance) on top or bottom of library before they resolve.
  • Commandeer steals their spell for free: Commandeer — {5}{U}{U}, or "pitch 2 blue cards" for free — gains control of target noncreature spell. Used against Kozilek's Command (which exiles GYs), sweepers, or any spell that would ruin your board. Pitching 2 blue cycling creatures is free — Curator of Mysteries goes to GY and comes back with Living End.
  • Clarion Conqueror shuts Yawgmoth activated abilities: Clarion Conqueror (3/3 for {2}{W}) — activated abilities of artifacts, creatures, AND planeswalkers can't be activated. Yawgmoth's sacrifice ability is an activated ability. Clarion Conqueror = Yawgmoth becomes a 2/4 with no text. Living End brings it back as a resilient threat.
  • Inevitable Betrayal — steal their Titan for free: Inevitable Betrayal — {4}{U}, or "pitch 3 blue cards" — search opponent's library for a creature card and put it onto the battlefield under YOUR control, then shuffle. Against Amulet Titan (steal their Primeval Titan), Eldrazi Tron (steal Ulamog), or any creature-based combo. Pitching 3 blue cards costs: 3 cyclers in GY for Living End to return later.
  • At low land counts, triomes cycle too: Zagoth Triome, Ketria Triome — these lands have cycling. When facing a GY wipe and needing to rebuild: cycle a triome to dig for cyclers. The triome goes to GY (it's a land, so Living End ignores it — Living End only returns creature cards).
  • Living End in cascade goes to exile: When cascaded into, Living End is "cast" from exile (cascade puts it there). If countered, it goes to GY. If resolved, it goes to... wait — Living End has no mana cost. Cards cast via cascade go to normal zones on resolution. Living End resolves → it goes to the graveyard (sorceries go to GY after resolving). The next cascade CAN hit Living End again if it went to GY after resolving and then somehow got back to library — but normally once resolved, it's in GY. Plan for 4 copies: run them all.

Violent Outburst cascades at instant speed on their end step. This is the primary cascade vehicle because they can’t Counterspell at instant speed efficiently and they’re often tapped out. Lines & Tactics

Lines &
Tactics

"The cascade trigger and the Living End trigger are two different things. Counter the right one."

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Line 01 — Critical
Counter the CASCADE Trigger, Not Living End
When you cast Violent Outburst, cascade triggers and goes on the stack. You THEN reveal cards until you hit Living End and cast it for free. At this point there are TWO things on the stack: the cascade trigger result (Living End) and the original Outburst. Counter Living End on the stack and the Outburst still resolves as a +1/+1 spell. This is why opponents should counter the cascade trigger, not Living End — but they usually don’t know this.
Line 02 — Critical
Living End Returns Your Creatures THEN Kills Theirs
Living End has two clauses that happen in order: (1) each player sacrifices all creatures they control, (2) then each player returns all creature cards from their GY to the battlefield. Your creatures come back AFTER theirs die. This means if they have no creatures and you have 5 in the GY, the board is yours cleanly. But if they have creatures, they also die — don’t reanimate into a board where they rebuild faster than you.
Line 03 — Critical
Grief Evoke Targets Nonland Cards Only
Grief’s evoke ability (discard a black card, target player discards a nonland card from their hand) targets a specific player and forces them to discard any nonland card of your choice. You see their whole hand. This is why Grief is your T1 disruption — strip their counter, Endurance, or key combo piece before T2 cascade. Note: Grief’s body is a 3/1 with menace even if you evoke it immediately. The body matters in fair games.
"Cascade is the most rules-dense mechanic in Modern. Every timing window matters. Every interaction has a ruling."

Living End Resolution — Three Steps in Order

Step 1
Each Player Exiles Their GY
ALL creature cards from ALL graveyards are exiled simultaneously. Your cycled creatures (Striped Riverwinder, Street Wraith, etc.) are exiled. Opponent's creatures in GY are also exiled. Non-creature cards (instants, sorceries, lands) stay in the graveyard — only creature cards are exiled.
Step 2
Each Player Sacrifices All Creatures
ALL creatures in play are sacrificed simultaneously. Your cascade creature (Violent Outburst doesn't leave a body, but Shardless Agent is a 2/2 that dies here). Opponent's entire board is wiped. Sacrifice — not destroy. Indestructible doesn't save them. Death triggers fire (Blood Artist, etc.).
Step 3
Exiled Creatures Return
ALL cards exiled in Step 1 enter the battlefield simultaneously. Your 5/5 Riverwinder, 7/7 Waker of Waves, 6/5 Troll, 3/4 Street Wraith — all enter at once. ETB triggers fire (Architects of Will, Foundation Breaker, Grief). ONLY cards exiled in Step 1 return — creatures sacrificed in Step 2 that got exiled by replacement effects (Leyline of the Void) do NOT return.

Oracle-Verified Edge Cases

Cascade Timing — The Trigger Is on Cast, Not Resolution
Cascade triggers when you CAST the cascade spell. The cascade trigger goes on the stack ABOVE the cascade spell. Cascade resolves first: you exile cards until you hit a nonland card with lower MV, then you may cast it without paying its mana cost. If you cast Living End this way, it goes on the stack above everything. Opponent can respond to Living End with instants/abilities BEFORE it resolves. Key: the cascade spell (Violent Outburst) is still on the stack below Living End — it resolves after Living End.
Living End Has No Mana Cost — MV Is 0
Living End has no mana cost (not {0} — literally no cost). Its mana value is 0. This matters for: Chalice of the Void on 0 COUNTERS Living End (MV 0 spell). Spell Snare does NOT counter it (MV 0, not MV 2). Force of Negation CAN counter it (counters any noncreature spell). Cascade finds it because you exile until you find a nonland card with MV LESS than the cascade spell (MV 3) — Living End at MV 0 qualifies.
Leyline of the Void vs Living End — Replacement Effect Interaction
If opponent has Leyline of the Void ("Cards that would be put into opponent's GY are exiled instead"): Step 1 still exiles YOUR creatures from your GY (they return in Step 3). Step 2 sacrifices all creatures — but YOUR sacrificed creatures are exiled by Leyline instead of going to GY. Per Scryfall ruling: "those cards were not exiled by Living End's first effect and will not be returned." So opponent's Leyline means your creatures that were already in GY return, but creatures sacrificed during resolution do NOT. This is why Living End boards in enchantment removal aggressively vs Leyline decks.
Nihil Spellbomb in Response to Living End
Opponent casts Nihil Spellbomb targeting you in response to Living End. Your GY is exiled by Spellbomb BEFORE Living End resolves. When Living End's Step 1 happens, your GY has no creature cards to exile. Step 2 still sacrifices all creatures. Step 3 returns nothing (for you) but opponent's creatures come back. Devastating. Counter-play: hold Subtlety or Force of Negation to protect Living End from GY hate on the stack.
Endurance in Response to Living End
Opponent flashes Endurance targeting you: your GY goes to bottom of library. Living End resolves: Step 1 finds no creatures in your GY. Step 2 sacrifices all creatures (including Endurance itself). Step 3 returns nothing for you. Brutal. If they target THEMSELVES: their GY goes to library, but Endurance is a creature that entered the battlefield — it gets sacrificed in Step 2. Their creatures from the library don't come back (they weren't exiled from GY).
Grief Evoke + Living End — Free Hand Disruption Then It Returns
Evoke Grief (exile a black card from hand): Grief enters, ETB triggers (opponent discards), then Grief is sacrificed (evoke clause). Grief goes to your GY as a 3/2 creature. When Living End resolves: Grief comes back from GY with its ETB — opponent discards AGAIN. You get a free Thoughtseize effect pre-combo AND Grief returns post-combo for a second disruption. This is the deck's primary pre-combo interaction line.
Subtlety vs Cascade Spell — Putting It on Top or Bottom
Opponent casts Subtlety targeting your Violent Outburst: "owner puts it on their choice of the top or bottom of their library." BUT cascade already triggered when Outburst was CAST. Subtlety bounces Outburst to your library, but the cascade trigger is already on the stack and still resolves. Living End still fires. Subtlety is BAD against cascade spells. To stop Living End, you must counter Living End ITSELF (on the stack after cascade resolves) or counter the cascade trigger with Consign to Memory.
Force of Negation Exiles Living End Permanently
FoN: "Counter target noncreature spell. If countered this way, exile it instead of putting it into GY." Living End is a noncreature spell. FoN counters it AND exiles it. Living End goes to exile permanently — it can never be suspended from exile (suspend only works from hand), and it's removed from the game entirely. In a deck running 4 Living End, losing one to FoN exile means only 3 remain. Two FoN exiles = only 2 left in the deck.
Suspend from Hand Only — Can't Suspend from GY or Exile
Suspend: "Rather than cast this card from your hand, pay {2}{B}{B} and exile it with three time counters." Suspend ONLY works from your hand. If Living End is in your GY (milled, discarded), you cannot suspend it. If it's in exile (FoN'd, Surgical'd), you cannot suspend it. The only way to cast Living End outside of cascade is to suspend it from hand — a 3-turn delay that's almost never used in competitive play because cascade is instant-speed.
Consign to Memory Counters the Cascade Trigger
Consign to Memory ({U} instant, Replicate): "Counter target triggered ability or colorless spell." Cascade is a TRIGGERED ability. Consign can counter the cascade trigger itself — Living End never gets cast. This is the cleanest answer: one card, one mana, cascade never resolves. With Replicate: {U}{U}{1} counters TWO cascade triggers in the same turn.
Cycling Is Not Casting — Can't Be Countered by Counterspells
Cycling ({cost}, Discard this card: Draw a card) is an ACTIVATED ABILITY, not a spell. You can't counter cycling with Counterspell, Spell Pierce, or Force. The only way to stop cycling: Stifle (counter activated ability), Damping Sphere/Drannith Magistrate/Chalice (prevent casting, not cycling). Cycling puts the creature in your GY for Living End while drawing you closer to your cascade spell.
Street Wraith — Cycling Costs 2 Life, Not Mana
Street Wraith: "Cycling—Pay 2 life." No mana cost for cycling. Can be cycled on T0 before you play a land. Puts a 3/4 swampwalk creature in your GY for free (well, 2 life). This is essentially "pay 2 life: draw a card and put a 3/4 in your GY." The life cost is real against aggro — cycling 3 Wraiths = 6 life, which can cost you the game against Burn or Prowess.
Violent Outburst Is an Instant — Cascade at Instant Speed
Violent Outburst ({1}{R}{G} instant): cascade + "creatures you control get +1/+0 until EOT." The +1/+0 is irrelevant — you're casting this for cascade. The key: it's an INSTANT. You can cascade into Living End at instant speed. End of opponent's turn: cast Outburst, cascade into Living End, wipe their board, return your army. They untap facing 20+ power of creatures.
Demonic Dread Requires a Target — Can Target Your Own Creature
Demonic Dread ({1}{B}{R} sorcery): cascade + "target creature can't block this turn." It REQUIRES a target creature. If no creatures are in play, you can't cast it. In the early game before Living End: you may need to cast a creature just to have a target for Dread. You CAN target your own creature. Post-board: if opponent has no creatures and you have none, Dread is dead. Outburst doesn't have this restriction.

vs Control, Jeskai, Blue-based Decks

When Living End gets countered and the game extends: Overlord of the Balemurk mills 4 and recurs creatures each turn. The cycled creatures that are already in GY present a persistent threat. vs Prepared Opponents, Games 2–3 with Heavy GY Hate

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • 01
    Cascading into open blue mana without a Grief setup. Two open blue mana on their side means Force of Negation is possible. If you didn’t Grief first, you’re walking Living End into a free counter. Either wait for them to tap out, or T1 Grief strips the Force before you cascade T2–3.
  • 02
    Cycling expensive creatures (Architects of Will, Waker of Waves) when you need mana. These cyclers cost 2–3 mana to cycle. In the early game, Street Wraith (free) + Deadshot Minotaur ({2}{R/G}) are your priority cycles. Don’t tap out cycling Waker of Waves on T2 and miss the T3 cascade window because you’re out of mana.
  • 03
    Not tracking Endurance timing. Endurance can be cast at instant speed (flash) to exile all cards in a GY and shuffle them in. If they have Endurance in hand and you cascade, they can respond to Living End resolving by Endurance-ing their own GY OR your GY. Always ask: do they have flash GY interaction before committing to the cascade. Grief strips Endurance if you see it.
  • 04
    Forgetting Commandeer in the pivot plan. When GY is exiled and Living End is offline, Commandeer (pay 2 blue cards from hand) steals any spell target opponent controls for the rest of the game. This takes their Primeval Titan, Solitude, or key permanent. Pilots who forget about Commandeer concede games they should have stolen.

Role-Switching Signals

All-In Cascade When
No GY Hate Visible
G1, no sideboard: cascade as fast as possible. The correct line is almost always T3 cascade on their EOT. You’re fastest without GY hate and every turn you wait gives them a draw step to find it.
Grief First When
Counter or Hate Visible
Open blue mana, they held mana for something, you see Endurance or Nihil Spellbomb from a previous game. T1 Grief strips the interaction, T3 cascade safely. Trading T1 for protection is worth it every time.
Pivot to Commandeer When
GY Is Exiled
Nihil Spellbomb or Endurance cleared your GY and you can’t rebuild in time. Commandeer their best threat. Inevitable Betrayal suspends for a free steal later. Your Grief + Subtlety bodies attack. You can still win — don’t scoop to GY hate.

Below 3 Creatures in GY
Living End with 1–2 creatures in GY is often a losing play — they return 1–2 bodies and their board may be bigger. Wait until you have 3+ (ideally 5+) in GY before cascading. Cycle aggressively on T1–2 to hit the threshold before committing.

Heuristics

  1. Never put a nonland spell with MV ≤ 2 in the main deck. Ever. Cascade exiles cards until it finds a nonland card costing less than MV 3. Any MV 0–2 nonland would be hit before Living End (MV 0). Zero exceptions. This includes "harmless" cyclers, cantrips, and removal spells. The entire deck construction principle is built on this constraint. Violation = failed cascade = lost game.
  2. Violent Outburst at end of opponent's turn — always prefer instant speed. Casting cascade as an instant protects against sorcery-speed GY hate (Rest in Peace, Relic of Progenitus activation). Opponents must hold up mana during their own turn, can't tap out, can't respond freely. The single biggest mechanical advantage the deck has is instant-speed cascade.
  3. Foundation Breaker enters from hand — Grafdigger's Cage doesn't stop it. Cage says creatures can't enter from GYs or libraries. Foundation Breaker is cast from your hand normally. Its ETB triggers on entering and destroys an artifact or enchantment — including Cage itself. After ETB resolves (Cage gone), cycle Foundation Breaker to put it in GY for Living End to return.
  4. Force of Negation cannot counter Endurance — it's a creature. FoN counters noncreature spells only. When an opponent casts Endurance to shuffle your GY before Living End resolves, FoN doesn't stop it. Use Subtlety (counters creature spells) — evoke Subtlety in response to Endurance, put Endurance on top of library. Or don't let them reach it: cascade at EOT when they have to tap for Endurance, then FoN their response if it's noncreature.
  5. The hat trick: cycle on Living End's stack before it resolves. After cascading into Living End, it's on the stack waiting to resolve. Cycle additional creatures from your hand before Living End resolves. They reach the GY. When Living End resolves, they enter the battlefield. This adds 2–3 extra bodies to your board for free. The sequence: cascade → Living End on stack → cycle Street Wraith (pay 2 life) → cycle Monstrous Carabid ({B/R}) → Living End resolves → all creatures including freshly cycled ones enter.
  6. Consign to Memory can counter Living End — it has no colored mana. Living End has no mana cost. Consign to Memory counters spells that have no colored mana in their mana costs. Living End qualifies. Jeskai Blink players know this — pre-strip their Consign with Grief before cascading, or hold Force of Negation to protect Living End from Consign.
  7. Grief before cascade — strip their counter, then chain into Violent Outburst. On your cascade turn: evoke Grief first (exile a black card, opponent reveals hand, discard a nonblack nonland card). Strip Force of Negation or Consign. Then cast Violent Outburst for the cascade. With their counter gone, Living End resolves cleanly. The sequence is: Grief evoke → Grief ETB → discard their counter → Grief dies → now cascade.
  8. Living End resolves → exile happens simultaneously for all players. All players exile all creatures from their GYs simultaneously, then all sacrifice all creatures simultaneously, then all enter simultaneously. If both you and an opponent have creatures in GY, both sets enter. This is relevant in the mirror: Living End returns YOUR creatures and THEIR creatures. If they have more creatures in GY, it might benefit them. Counter their cascade before it resolves, or cascade yourself first to clear GYs.
  9. Street Wraith cycles for paying 2 life — track your life total when cycling. Four Street Wraiths = 8 life paid if all cycled. Against Boros Energy or Domain Aggro (burn-heavy), don't cycle all four Street Wraiths if you're at risk of being in range of burn lethality. Preserve life intelligently. Against combo or control, cycle freely — life totals don't matter until they do.
  10. Subtlety from GY: cascade in response to opponent's creature spell. If Subtlety was evoked earlier (now in GY), and opponent casts a creature spell, cascade in response: Living End resolves, Subtlety ETBs and puts their creature spell on top/bottom of library. You stop their creature and get a 3/3 flying. This is a powerful line vs Endurance — their Endurance never ETBs, and you have a 3/3 flier.
  11. Don't over-cycle green sources if you need Force of Vigor. Force of Vigor requires exiling a green card from hand to cast for free. If you've cycled all your green creatures (Monstrous Carabid, Foundation Breaker) and hold no green cards, FoV costs its full {2}{G}{G}. Against decks with Leyline of the Void, keep at least one green card in hand until you know you can handle the Leyline.
  12. Overlord of the Balemurk mills 4 on entry even as an enchantment (impending). Play Overlord early via impending ({1}{B}). It enters as an enchantment with 5 time counters — not a creature yet. But "when this permanent enters or attacks" still fires on entry. You mill 4 on turn 2 and return a creature from GY to hand. That creature goes back to hand to cycle again. This doubles your GY filling rate when Overlord is out early.
  13. Cascade in response to opponent's cascade (mirror). When your Living End mirror opponent casts Violent Outburst, their cascade trigger goes on the stack. In response, cast your own Violent Outburst. Your cascade trigger goes on stack first, resolves first. Your Living End resolves first — exile both GYs, enter your creatures. Then their Living End resolves — both GYs are already empty. You win the mirror by being faster on the stack.
  14. Living End goes to GY after resolving — it can't be re-cast from GY by cascade. After Living End resolves, it goes to the graveyard (normal sorcery destination). The next cascade spell will exile cards looking for another nonland MV 0–2 card. If all 4 Living Ends are in GY (not library), cascade finds nothing. Don't cascade a second time if no Living Ends remain in library. Instead, attack with your board.
  15. Teferi, Time Raveler means you cascade on your main phase — plan accordingly. Teferi's static ability stops you from casting spells at instant speed. Violent Outburst at EOT becomes impossible. You must cascade on your main phase, where opponents have full mana and full access to interaction. Grief to strip Teferi first, or Commandeer to steal it. Otherwise, cast Ardent Plea (also sorcery speed) at main phase and accept the worse conditions.
  16. Clarion Conqueror shuts ALL activated abilities of creatures — yours too. Clarion Conqueror's ability shuts activated abilities of artifacts, creatures, AND planeswalkers — indiscriminately. This includes your own creatures' activated abilities. Confirm you have no creatures with important activated abilities before bringing Clarion. Your cyclers have cycling as activated abilities — but you can still cycle by paying the cost as a special action, not an activated ability. Actually, cycling IS an activated ability. With Clarion, you can't cycle. Only bring Clarion in matchups where the tradeoff is worth it (Yawgmoth).
  17. Inevitable Betrayal can only be cast for free on your turn by pitching blues. Inevitable Betrayal's alternate cost is "exile three blue cards from your hand." This is a cost, not a timing restriction — you can cast it any time you could cast a sorcery (your main phase). The pitched blue cards go to exile (not GY). They won't come back with Living End. Only pitch blue cards you don't need back, or that you've already cycled to GY copies.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any hand with a cascade spell + Living End in GY, or a hand that can cycle to get there.
  • Cascade rule: Violent Outburst and Shardless Agent cascade. They find Living End because it's the only 0-MV spell in the deck.
  • Misdirection rule: Never cast Living End from hand. Always cascade into it. Casting it directly means it can be countered normally.
  • Endurance rule: Opponent flashing Endurance in response to cascade: their GY gets exiled and returns. Your Street Wraiths are safe — exile, not GY.
  • Force rule: Hold Force of Negation for their GY hate (Nihil Spellbomb, Endurance). Don't trade it for cantrips.
  • Sideboard rule: Consign counters the cascade trigger itself. They counter the cascade, not Living End. Know the difference.

The Cascade Constraint — Print and Never Forget

Main Deck Rule
Zero MV ≤ 2 Nonland Spells
Cascade finds the first nonland card costing less than MV 3. Living End (MV 0) must always be that card. Any MV 0–2 nonland spell = cascade hits it instead. Zero exceptions. Cycling creatures cost ≥ 3 to cast (not cycle cost — cast cost). Force of Negation is MV 3. Subtlety is MV 4. All legal.
Standard Kill Line
T1–T2 Cycle → T3 EOT Cascade
Turns 1–2: cycle Street Wraith (free), Horror ({B}), Monstrous Carabid ({B/R}), Curator ({U}), Waker of Waves (cost). T3 EOT: Violent Outburst (instant) → cascade → Living End → hat trick (cycle more on stack) → resolve → 5–7 creature board. Attack for lethal T4.
