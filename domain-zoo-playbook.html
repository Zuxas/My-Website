Chapter 01

Deck Identity

Domain Zoo is Modern's best aggro deck when it draws Leyline of the Guildpact. It's still a solid aggro deck when it doesn't. That's the whole advantage.

Domain Zoo plays the longest continuous history of any aggro archetype in Modern — creatures, burn, and land synergies dating back to the format's beginning. What makes the 2025–26 version dangerous is the Leyline of the Guildpact package: a zero-mana enchantment that, when in your opening hand, enters the battlefield before the game begins, instantly giving your lands all five basic land types and making every nonland permanent you control all five colors.

The payoffs are immediate and compounding. Scion of Draco reduces to free (costs {2} less per basic land type — five types = {10} off its {12} base cost, leaving {2}, then with Leyline it's free as your permanents are already all colors). Territorial Kavu becomes a 5/5 on turn two. Leyline Binding exiles any nonland permanent for {W}. Tribal Flames deals 5 damage for {1}{R}. All before turn two, all for almost no mana.

The deck's identity is aggressively slanted: you want to kill by turn four through a combination of large creatures backed by burn and soft counterspells. Stubborn Denial and Spectral Denial protect your threats from removal and counterspells. Doorkeeper Thrull shuts down ETB-dependent decks entirely — landing it against Esper Blink or Jeskai Blink is frequently a free win.

Oracle Accuracy — Critical Interactions

Leyline of the Guildpact — The Engine
"If this card is in your opening hand, you may begin the game with it on the battlefield. Each nonland permanent you control is all colors. Lands you control are every basic land type in addition to their other types." With Leyline in play before the game starts: (1) All your lands are Forest/Island/Swamp/Mountain/Plains simultaneously — full Domain from land drops alone. (2) All your nonland permanents are all five colors — including Scion of Draco, making all his abilities active immediately. You do not need to cast Leyline for it to enter — it's a replacement effect in your opening hand.
Scion of Draco — Domain Payoff
"Domain — This spell costs {2} less to cast for each basic land type among lands you control." Base cost {12}. With full domain (5 basic land types) = costs {2}. With Leyline in opening hand, your lands already have all five types on turn one — Scion on turn two for {2} (pay {1}{G} or similar). Scion then gives each creature you control: white = vigilance, blue = hexproof, black = lifelink, red = first strike, green = trample. Hexproof is applied to each creature individually — Kavu with hexproof cannot be targeted by Fatal Push, Path to Exile, or Thoughtseize while Scion is in play.
Territorial Kavu — The Flagship
"Domain — Territorial Kavu's power and toughness are each equal to the number of basic land types among lands you control." With full domain = 5/5 for {R}{G}. On attack: choose rummage (discard, draw) or exile a card from a graveyard. With Leyline in opening hand, Kavu is a 5/5 on turn one before either player has cast a land. Kavu attacks trigger a choice — you must choose one. You cannot decline both without targeting.
Leyline Binding — Instant Exile
"Flash. Domain — This spell costs {1} less to cast for each basic land type among lands you control." Base cost {5}{W}. With full domain = {W}. Flash — cast on any turn at any time. Exiles any nonland permanent until Binding leaves the battlefield. This is how Zoo answers Primeval Titan, Yawgmoth, Griselbrand, and any other threat that would otherwise dominate the board. If Leyline Binding leaves the battlefield, the exiled permanent returns. Destroying Binding (Nature's Claim, Wear // Tear) returns their permanent.
Stubborn Denial + Spectral Denial — Ferocious Counterspells
Stubborn Denial ({U}): counter noncreature spell unless pay {1}. With ferocious (4+ power creature you control) — hard counter instead. Territorial Kavu (5/5) and Scion of Draco (4/4) both enable ferocious. Spectral Denial ({X}{U}): costs {1} less per creature you control with power 4+. With two 4-power creatures, effectively {X-2}{U}. Counter spell unless pay {X}. Unlike Stubborn Denial, Spectral Denial can counter creature spells. The combination gives Zoo soft-to-hard counterspells that frequently cost zero mana.
Doorkeeper Thrull — ETB Shutdown
"Flash. Flying. Artifacts and creatures entering don't cause abilities to trigger." Flash — deploy at instant speed in response to an opponent casting a creature. Thrull prevents: Solitude's exile trigger, Overlord of the Balemurk's mill trigger, Kappa Cannoneer's token trigger, Yawgmoth's enter trigger, and all ETB effects on artifacts and creatures. This does NOT affect spells — Ephemerate still blinks, and the creature still enters. The entry just doesn't trigger anything. Thrull is a 1/2 flying body, dies to Lightning Bolt (3 damage kills it).
Tribal Flames — Domain Finisher
"Domain — Tribal Flames deals X damage to any target, where X is the number of basic land types among lands you control." With full domain = 5 damage for {1}{R}. Can target players, creatures, or planeswalkers. At sorcery speed only. With Leyline, Tribal Flames on turn two deals 5 — enough to kill most creatures or put any opponent on a lethal clock. Some lists cut Tribal Flames in favor of more creatures; others keep it as the primary burn finisher.

Chapter 03

Game Flow

Every game starts the same question: did I draw Leyline? The answer changes how you sequence, but not what you're trying to do — put 5-power bodies on the board and protect them long enough to win.

With Leyline of the Guildpact (Optimal)

Turn 0 / Pregame
Leyline of the Guildpact enters the battlefield before the game begins. All your lands immediately have every basic land type. All your nonland permanents are all five colors. Full domain is already online before either player has taken a turn.
Turn 1
Fetch a dual (Steam Vents, Lush Portico) or play Xander's Lounge. Drop Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer or hold up a land. Ragavan on T1 pressures immediately and generates Treasure + exile draw when it connects. With Leyline already active, Ragavan can attack into any 1/1 token and live.
Turn 2
Cast Territorial Kavu for {R}{G} — enters as a 5/5 (Leyline gives full domain). Or cast Scion of Draco for {2} (full domain reduces it by {10}). With Scion in play, all your creatures gain hexproof, lifelink, first strike, trample, and vigilance. Kavu as a 5/5 with all five keywords is essentially unbeatable at this mana cost.
Turn 3–4
Apply lethal pressure. Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury closes. Leyline Binding answers any blocker for {W}. Stubborn Denial with ferocious protects your threats for {U}. If they've stabilized: Tribal Flames (if in list) deals 5 for {1}{R}. A single untapped land usually represents Leyline Binding for zero.

Without Leyline (Fair Mode)

Domain Zoo is still a solid deck without Leyline — it just requires a slightly slower ramp to full domain. The optimal two-land sequence to reach full domain is: Xander's Lounge (Island/Swamp/Mountain) → Lush Portico (Forest/Plains). Two lands gives you all five land types, putting Territorial Kavu at 5/5 and Leyline Binding at {W} by turn two with a fetch in hand.

Alternatively: Fetch into Temple Garden (Forest/Plains) → fetch into Steam Vents (Island/Mountain) → one more land for the fifth type (Swamp). You can also sequence fetch into Breeding Pool, then Arid Mesa into Sacred Foundry or similar. The key principle: you need at least one Island, Swamp, Mountain, Forest, and Plains among your lands simultaneously for full domain. Triomes (Xander's Lounge, Lush Portico) give three types per card — they're the most efficient path to domain without Leyline.

Mulligan Framework

Keep hands that have: at least two lands that together provide multiple land types, and at least two creatures (or one creature + Leyline). Leyline in the opening hand is an automatic keep regardless of the other six cards — it provides so much free value that even a mediocre six-card hand with Leyline beats a good seven without it.

Be wary of: hands with five or more lands (flood risk), hands with three or more Leylines (active liability — you only need one on the battlefield), and hands with no creatures and no Ragavan to apply early pressure. Territorial Kavu without domain is just a 0/0 that dies on ETB — make sure you have a land plan before keeping a Kavu hand.

Lines and Tricks

Two Leylines in Hand
If you have two Leylines in your opening hand and your opponent can't destroy enchantments, put only one into play and use the second as a discard target for Territorial Kavu's rummage on attack. If they can destroy enchantments (Nature's Claim, Wear//Tear), put both in — redundancy protects against the first being destroyed.
Doorkeeper Thrull Timing
Flash Doorkeeper in response to an opponent casting a creature they want to ETB-trigger. They still pay the mana and the creature resolves — it just enters without triggering anything. Against Esper Blink: cast Doorkeeper in response to Solitude being cast (not evoked — evoke means paying the evoke cost to cast it). The ETB exile trigger is silenced.
Scion + Hexproof Sequencing
Cast Scion of Draco before casting your other creatures. Once Scion is in play, every subsequent creature you play enters with hexproof immediately. If you cast Kavu first, it's briefly targetable before Scion hits. The correct order: Scion → then all other threats under the hexproof umbrella.
Consign to Memory vs Leyline Binding Return
When your Leyline Binding is destroyed and a dangerous permanent returns (Atraxa, Primeval Titan), you can use Consign to Memory to counter the triggered ability that returns it. Wait — the return isn't a triggered ability, it's part of Binding's own "until Binding leaves" clause. It resolves automatically. Counter the permanent before it becomes a problem instead — use Spectral Denial or Stubborn Denial in response to the opponent's destroy spell targeting Binding.
Kraven's Power Scaling
Kraven's power = greatest MV among permanents you control. Leyline of the Guildpact has MV 4 (even in play). Scion of Draco has MV 12. Phlage has MV 3. With Scion on the battlefield, Kraven is a 12/4 vigilance. With only Leyline: 4/4. Always check your highest-MV permanent before swinging — Kraven's attack math changes significantly.
Arena of Glory Haste Grant
Arena of Glory: {R}, {T}, exert this land — add {R}{R}. If that mana is spent on a creature spell, it gains haste. Use to cast Phlage or Territorial Kavu with haste, letting them attack the turn they enter without summoning sickness. Exert means Arena can't tap for mana again until your next untap step — plan around this.
Chapter 04

Core Engines

Engine 01 — The Leyline Combo (Turn 0 → Turn 2 Win Condition)
Setup
Opening hand contains Leyline of the Guildpact. It enters the battlefield before the game begins. All lands immediately have all five basic land types. All nonland permanents are all five colors. Full domain is established before turn one.
Execution
Turn 1: play a fetch or dual, drop Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer. Turn 2: fetch a second color, cast Territorial Kavu for {R}{G} — enters as 5/5 with full domain active. Or cast Scion of Draco for {2} (full domain reduces its {12} cost by {10}). Scion's ETB gives all creatures hexproof, lifelink, first strike, trample, vigilance.
Result
Turn two: 2/1 Ragavan + 5/5 Kavu with five keywords, or Ragavan + 4/4 Scion making Ragavan have hexproof and lifelink. Either board state demands two or more answers simultaneously. With Stubborn Denial or Spectral Denial holding up: your threats are effectively uncounterable and removal-proof.
Engine 02 — The Ferocious Counter Wall
Setup
Territorial Kavu (5/5) or Scion of Draco (4/4) is in play. You have {U} or {1}{U} available. Ferocious is enabled — either creature qualifies as a 4+ power permanent.
Execution
Stubborn Denial ({U}): counter any noncreature spell (removal, sweepers, counterspells) for one blue mana — hard counter with ferocious, no pay-{1} escape clause. Spectral Denial ({X}{U}, reduces by {1} per 4-power creature): counter any spell including creature spells. With two 4-power creatures = costs {X-2}{U}, often just {U} for a hard counter on any spell.
Result
Your opponent must fight through {U} countermagic on every removal spell they cast while you have ferocious active. Combined with Scion's hexproof grant, targeted removal becomes effectively impossible. The counter wall protects your threats, advances your clock, and costs only incidental mana.
Engine 03 — Phlage Recursion Loop
Setup
Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury has died and is in the graveyard. You have 5 cards to exile from your graveyard and {1}{R}{W} available. The game has gone long — your initial threats have been answered.
Execution
Cast Phlage via Escape: exile 5 GY cards, pay {1}{R}{W}. Phlage's ETB deals 3 damage to any target and gains you 3 life. With Scion in play: Phlage enters as a 6/6 with hexproof, lifelink, first strike, trample, vigilance. A 6/6 first-striking trampling lifelinker is nearly impossible to profitably block.
Result
Phlage can be escaped repeatedly as long as you have graveyard cards to exile. Each recurrence triggers 3 damage + 3 life. In long games, Phlage is the engine that prevents opponents from ever stabilizing — they answer it, it comes back, they answer it again, and eventually run out of answers. With hexproof from Scion, they can't even target it.
Chapter 05

Roles

Zoo wants to be the aggressor. But the ferocious counter-wall and Leyline Binding's flash exile give you a tempo game that most pure aggro decks can't access.
Stompy Aggressor
Primary role — Leyline draw, most matchups. Deploy large domain creatures with absurd efficiency. Kavu at 5/5 for {R}{G}, Scion for {2} flying. Apply a clock that demands immediate answers. Hold Stubborn Denial to protect threats. Win before they can stabilize. This is the Leyline game — most opponents have no realistic line of play against a T2 5/5 with hexproof.
Tempo Disruptor
Secondary role — vs. combo and ETB decks. Flash Doorkeeper Thrull to silence key ETB triggers. Use Leyline Binding at flash speed to exile threats on their turn. Counter key spells with Stubborn/Spectral Denial. This mode slows their game while yours continues advancing. Against Esper Blink, Jeskai, Goryo's: your flash-speed disruption is more valuable than raw speed.
Burn Finisher
Tertiary role — Phlage escape + Lightning Bolt + Tribal Flames. When the board has stalled and creatures can't attack profitably, shift to direct damage. Phlage's escape ETB deals 3 damage. Lightning Bolt deals 3. Tribal Flames deals 5 with domain. Combined: 11 damage from non-combat sources at relatively low mana. Closes games where combat is stalemated.

When to Shift Roles

Against aggro (Boros Energy, Prowess): stay stompy. Your 5/5 Kavu with all five keywords wins combat against almost everything they can field by turn three. Don't try to be clever — apply pressure and hold up one Denial for their key removal.

Against ETB combo (Esper Blink, Jeskai Blink, Goryo's): shift to tempo disruptor. Doorkeeper Thrull is a first-round pick from your sideboard if it isn't already in your main. Deploy Thrull on their turn to silence the ETB they're telegraphing. Use Leyline Binding as a flash exile on their key permanent before it threatens.

Against control (Dimir, Azorius): the burn finisher role is most relevant. Pressure with hexproof threats they can't target, then burn them out when they find ways to stop combat. Consign to Memory is relevant here — counters their triggered abilities.

Chapter 06

Matchup Deep Dives

Your Leyline hand is a near-auto-win in most matchups. Your non-Leyline hand is a solid aggro deck that needs a plan.

Boros Energy — 15.8% meta · Even

The most-played deck in the format. Boros Energy operates on a similar aggro axis but with a different engine: Guide of Souls energy generation + Galvanic Discharge + Ocelot Pride token flooding. Their removal (Galvanic Discharge, Thraben Charm) is energy-costed and hits your smaller creatures efficiently. The critical difference: Scion of Draco gives your creatures hexproof — Galvanic Discharge, Fatal Push, and removal spells can't target Kavu or other creatures under Scion's protection. Goblin Bombardment is their kill condition that bypasses this hexproof (sacrifice doesn't target). Post-board they bring Wrath of the Skies (an energy sweeper) and Clarion Conqueror (shuts activated abilities — stops Phlage's escape? No — Escape is a casting alternative cost, not an activated ability. Clarion doesn't stop Phlage from escaping).

OutIn
−2 Consign to Memory+2 Wrath of the Skies
−2 Spectral Denial+2 Pest Control

Wrath of the Skies sweeps their 1/1 Cat tokens and small energy creatures. Pest Control destroys all permanents with MV ≤ 1 (hits Ragavan, Ocelot Pride, Guide of Souls, Goblin Bombardment). Consign and Spectral Denial are weak — Boros has few triggered abilities or relevant counters to interact with.

Key needle: Goblin Bombardment (sacrifice a creature → deal 1 damage) can't be stopped by hexproof — it doesn't target. Pest Control from the sideboard or Leyline Binding (exiling Bombardment for {W}) is the answer. Always Bind Goblin Bombardment before it can chain kills.

Tameshi Belcher — 11.1% meta · Unfavorable

Your hardest matchup. Tameshi Belcher wins on turn four with a Charbelcher activation dealing 40+ damage. You can't race a turn-four kill with an aggro clock that often takes five or six turns to finish. Game one is poor — your only interaction is Stubborn Denial (counters Whir of Invention or Charbelcher activation? No — Charbelcher's activation is an activated ability, not a spell, and can't be countered). Stubborn Denial can counter Whir of Invention (instant, not a creature spell) or Disrupting Shoal. Post-board: Mystical Dispute counters their blue spells (Whir of Invention costs {X}{U}{U}{U}, Dispute makes it cost {1}{U}{U}{U} more). Wear//Tear destroys Goblin Charbelcher.

OutIn
−3 Doorkeeper Thrull+2 Mystical Dispute
−1 Nishoba Brawler+2 Wear // Tear

Wear//Tear destroys Goblin Charbelcher (artifact) or Lotus Bloom (artifact). Mystical Dispute counters Whir of Invention at reduced cost. Doorkeeper Thrull is useless — they have almost no ETB creatures. Aggress hard and hope they stumble.

Key needle: Stubborn Denial cannot counter Charbelcher's activated ability. Your best interaction is destroying Charbelcher before they activate it (Wear//Tear) or countering Whir of Invention (the tutor that finds it). Be as aggressive as possible — every turn they don't have Charbelcher is a turn you can take their life total lower.

Eldrazi Tron — 10.5% meta · Favorable

Eldrazi Tron is slow and colorless. Their threats (Thought-Knot Seer, Devourer of Destiny, Sire of Seven Deaths) come down on turn three or four at the earliest. You're attacking with a 5/5 hexproof on turn two. Leyline Binding exiles any of their threats for {W}. Thought-Knot Seer's ETB exiles a card from your hand — Doorkeeper Thrull silences this ETB if you flash it in response. Karn, the Great Creator's static ability shuts off activated abilities of artifacts — irrelevant to you since your creatures aren't artifacts. Chalice of the Void at 2 counters: Lightning Bolt (MV 1 — not hit at 2), Kavu (MV 2 — hit). Chalice at 1 hits Ragavan, Lightning Bolt.

OutIn
−2 Consign to Memory+2 Wear // Tear
−2 Spectral Denial+1 Doorkeeper Thrull
−1 Stubborn Denial+2 Celestial Purge

Wear//Tear destroys Chalice of the Void (artifact) or Ensnaring Bridge. Doorkeeper Thrull silences Thought-Knot Seer's exile ETB. Celestial Purge exiles a black or red permanent (Kozilek's Command is black? No — colorless. Purge doesn't hit Eldrazi.) Use Purge mainly for escape value. Leyline Binding is your best card here — exiles any Eldrazi for one white mana.

Key needle: Flash Doorkeeper Thrull in response to Thought-Knot Seer being cast. TKS resolves, enters without its ETB triggering — you don't exile a card. A 4/4 that just eats your creatures isn't as dangerous as one that also strips your hand.

Esper Goryo's — 5.7% meta · Favorable

Esper Goryo's depends on ETB triggers: Atraxa, Grand Unifier drawing 8+ cards on ETB, Ephemerate blinking for value. Doorkeeper Thrull shuts off Atraxa's ETB — Atraxa enters as a 7/7 flying body with no card draw. This is frequently a free win. Their disruption (Thoughtseize, Force of Negation, Solitude) can stop your threats, but Scion of Draco's hexproof grant means Solitude can't target your creatures and Thoughtseize takes from hand before Scion is in play. Territorial Kavu's attack rummage lets you exile their Atraxa from the graveyard — remove the reanimation target entirely.

OutIn
−2 Lightning Bolt+1 Doorkeeper Thrull
−1 Nishoba Brawler+2 Soul-Guide Lantern
+1 Endurance

Soul-Guide Lantern exiles their GY on ETB (remove Atraxa/Griselbrand targets) and can be sacrificed to exile target card anytime. Endurance flashes in to exile their entire GY at instant speed. Doorkeeper Thrull silences Atraxa's card-draw ETB. Lightning Bolt is weak — their threats are 7+ power.

Key needle: Territorial Kavu's attack trigger can exile Atraxa from their graveyard before they reanimate it. Attack with Kavu early even if not profitable for damage — use the trigger to exile their graveyard. Remove Atraxa and the deck has no engine.

Izzet Affinity — 5.7% meta · Even

Izzet Affinity goldfishes quickly with Kappa Cannoneer growing into a massive unblockable threat. Doorkeeper Thrull shuts off Kappa's "whenever an artifact enters" counter trigger — Kappa enters but generates no counters from artifacts. Pinnacle Emissary's ETB token also gets silenced. Without Thrull, you need to race — your 5/5 Kavu with first strike and trample can't be profitably blocked by most affinity creatures. Post-board they bring Blood Moon (converts your fetchable duals to Mountains — you can't make blue/green) and Force of Negation.

OutIn
−2 Consign to Memory+2 Wear // Tear
−1 Spectral Denial+1 Doorkeeper Thrull
−1 Nishoba Brawler+2 Mystical Dispute

Wear//Tear destroys Blood Moon (enchantment) and key artifacts. Doorkeeper Thrull shuts Kappa Cannoneer's counter engine and Pinnacle Emissary tokens. Mystical Dispute counters Force of Negation and any blue interaction for {U}.

Key needle: Flash Doorkeeper Thrull in response to any artifact creature entering the battlefield. Kappa Cannoneer's counter trigger fires on artifacts entering — Thrull silences it immediately. Without the counter engine, Kappa is just a 1/1 that costs 4 mana.

Esper Blink — 5.7% meta · Favorable

Your best structured matchup post-board. Esper Blink's entire engine runs on ETB triggers: Solitude exile, Overlord of the Balemurk mill, Phelia bounce, Quantum Riddler card draw. Doorkeeper Thrull silences all of them. Solitude can't exile creatures — it enters, does nothing, just sits as a 3/2. Overlord enters without milling. The deck's interaction collapses. Your hexproof creatures from Scion can't be targeted by Thoughtseize (black sorcery, targets a player not a permanent — Thoughtseize still works) or Solitude (targets a creature — can't target hexproof creatures). Note: Thoughtseize targets a player, not a creature, so it still strips combo pieces from hand even under hexproof.

OutIn
−2 Spectral Denial+1 Doorkeeper Thrull
−1 Lightning Bolt+2 Wrath of the Skies
−1 Nishoba Brawler+1 Pest Control

Doorkeeper Thrull is your MVP — flash it in response to any ETB creature they cast. Wrath of the Skies sweeps on a chosen energy level — you get the energy and choose MV threshold, which helps clear their smaller blink targets without hitting your own threats. Lightning Bolt is weak — their threats have higher toughness.

Key needle: Scion's hexproof grant means Solitude (target another creature) and Prismatic Ending (exile target permanent with MV ≤ X) cannot target your hexproof creatures. But Ephemerate can still blink their own creatures — Doorkeeper only stops the ETB trigger, not the blink itself.

Izzet Prowess — 5.2% meta · Favorable

Prowess kills by buffing Dragon's Rage Channeler and Monastery Swiftspear with cheap spells. Their 3/3 or 4/4 pumped creatures lose races to your 5/5 Kavu with first strike — first strike means Kavu deals damage before their 4/4 deals back, killing their attacker without dying. Mutagenic Growth (pay 2 life for +2/+2) can make their creatures survive first strike math — always be aware of the +2 life payment trick when attacking with Kavu. Lava Dart flashback (sacrifice a Mountain, deal 1 damage) can finish off Ragavan or a 1-toughness creature. With Scion: your creatures have hexproof, making their DRC and Swiftspear's go-wide strategy irrelevant.

OutIn
−2 Consign to Memory+2 Pest Control
−1 Doorkeeper Thrull+1 Wrath of the Skies

Pest Control destroys all permanents with MV 1 or less — kills Ragavan, Monastery Swiftspear, and any 1-drop threats they have. Wrath of the Skies sweeps at the right energy threshold. Consign to Memory is weak — Prowess has almost no triggered abilities. Doorkeeper Thrull is marginal — Prowess has no key ETB triggers.

Key needle: Mutagenic Growth costs 2 life, not mana — they can cast it at any time to save a creature from first strike damage. When attacking with Kavu into a 3/3, assume they have Mutagenic Growth and plan combat math for a 5/5 blocker rather than a 3/3.

Domain Zoo Mirror — 2.6% meta · Even

The mirror comes down to two things: who has Leyline of the Guildpact and who is on the play. With Leyline in play, both players have hexproof creatures — targeted removal is irrelevant. Leyline Binding for {W} is the key interaction — exile their Scion of Draco, their Territorial Kavu, before they can attack. First player to resolve Scion gets the first hexproof grant, but both players' Scions give hexproof to their own creatures. Note: Scion gives YOUR creatures hexproof, not their creatures — it's not a symmetrical effect. With both players on Leyline, combat is determined by sequencing and attack ordering with first strike.

OutIn
−2 Consign to Memory+2 Wear // Tear
−1 Nishoba Brawler+1 Celestial Purge

Wear//Tear destroys your opponent's Leyline Binding (enchantment) at instant speed in response to them targeting your permanent. Celestial Purge exiles a red permanent (Ragavan, Phlage, Territorial Kavu — red with Leyline). If you can exile their Leyline with Binding and they've already cast their own creatures, they lose their domain and their creatures shrink dramatically.

Key needle: In the mirror, two Leylines in your opening hand: put both into play if you think they have Wear//Tear. One Leyline destroyed still leaves the second. If they don't have enchantment removal, put only one in play and use the second as Kavu rummage fodder.

Ruby Storm — 3.9% meta · Unfavorable

Like Tameshi Belcher, Ruby Storm can kill faster than your aggro clock. They combo off with Desperate Ritual + Pyretic Ritual chains into Grapeshot or Empty the Warrens around turn three or four. Your clock is four to five turns. Stubborn Denial can counter Past in Flames (sorcery, not a creature spell) with ferocious — this slows their combo significantly. Spectral Denial can also counter Past in Flames. Apply aggression AND hold disruption. Your interaction is thin but meaningful — one Denial on Past in Flames can break their Storm turn.

OutIn
−3 Doorkeeper Thrull+3 Soul-Guide Lantern
−1 Nishoba Brawler+1 Endurance

Soul-Guide Lantern exiles their graveyard, cutting off Past in Flames (which requires cards in GY to cast at reduced cost). Endurance flashes in at instant speed to exile their entire graveyard — devastating against a Storm turn. Doorkeeper Thrull is useless — Storm has no ETB creatures.

Key needle: Soul-Guide Lantern ETB exiles target opponent's graveyard. Activate it in response to Past in Flames being cast — their flashback source disappears. Don't wait — if they have enough mana to Past in Flames, exile the GY immediately before they get to cast it.

Amulet Titan — 2.7% meta · Favorable

Amulet Titan needs three to four turns to assemble Amulet of Vigor + bounce lands + Primeval Titan. You're attacking with a 5/5 on turn two. Leyline Binding exiles Primeval Titan for {W}. Leyline Binding exiles Amulet of Vigor for {W}. Territorial Kavu's attack exile ability removes their graveyard cards (disrupting Spelunking or Titan rebuys). Their main deck has no interaction for your early creatures — no removal, no counters. Summoner's Pact can find Endurance or Arboreal Grazer — not threats you care about. Easily one of your best matchups.

OutIn
−2 Spectral Denial+2 Wear // Tear

Wear//Tear destroys Amulet of Vigor (artifact) or other key artifacts. Spectral Denial is weak — they cast almost no noncreature spells worth countering early. Leyline Binding is your best card: keep mana available for flash exile of any Titan that resolves.

Key needle: Exile Amulet of Vigor with Leyline Binding or destroy with Wear//Tear before they assemble the Titan combo. Without Amulet, bounce lands enter tapped and their mana advantage collapses. Titan without Amulet is a 6/6 that fetches two lands — still large, but much slower.

Dimir Frog / Control — 2.3% meta · Even

Dimir Frog operates through Psychic Frog (grows as you discard, evasive) + Thoughtseize + Counterspell. Your hexproof creatures from Scion can't be targeted by Fatal Push or Murktide Regent's block (well — Murktide blocks but can't be Fatal Pushed either). Thoughtseize targets you (the player) not a creature — it strips your hand despite hexproof. Psychic Frog discards your hand to pump itself, but with hexproof creatures you can race it. Key: Leyline Binding exiles Psychic Frog or Murktide Regent at flash speed for {W}. Stubborn Denial counters their Counterspell (a noncreature spell) with ferocious. The matchup is winnable but requires careful sequencing.

OutIn
−3 Doorkeeper Thrull+2 Mystical Dispute
−1 Nishoba Brawler+1 Celestial Purge
−1 Consign to Memory+2 Soul-Guide Lantern

Mystical Dispute counters any blue spell for {U} (Counterspell, Force of Negation, Consign to Memory). Celestial Purge exiles Murktide Regent (blue, permanent) or Psychic Frog (blue with Leyline active — wait, Frog is black). Celestial Purge exiles black or red permanents. Psychic Frog is black — Purge exiles it. Soul-Guide Lantern disrupts their GY-based plans.

Key needle: Leyline Binding for {W} exiles Psychic Frog or Murktide at flash speed during their attack or in response to pump. This is the cleanest answer. Keep mana open on their turn — blank the Frog mid-swing before it deals damage and discards a key card from your hand.

Chapter 07

Sideboard Guide

The sideboard solves three problems: graveyard decks, combo decks, and mirror enchantment hate. Everything else is gravy.

Sideboard Card-by-Card

Soul-Guide Lantern ×3
ETB: exile target player's graveyard. {0}, sacrifice: exile target card from any GY. Instant-speed GY hate that also has a body. The ETB exile comes in against Esper Goryo's (exile Atraxa), Ruby Storm (exile Past in Flames targets), Living End (exile their cycling creatures). Activate in response to Past in Flames being cast to deny flashback. Comes in vs any GY-dependent deck.
Wear // Tear ×2
Wear: destroy target artifact ({1}{R}). Tear: destroy target enchantment ({W}). Fuse both for {1}{R}{W}: destroy an artifact AND an enchantment. Answers: Goblin Charbelcher (artifact), Amulet of Vigor (artifact), Blood Moon (enchantment), Leyline Binding in the mirror (enchantment), Chalice of the Void (artifact). Instant speed. Versatile enough to earn spots against almost any non-creature problem.
Wrath of the Skies ×2
{X}{W}{W} sorcery: get X energy, pay any amount of energy, destroy each artifact/creature/enchantment with MV ≤ that amount. You control the threshold — pay 2 energy to destroy everything MV 2 or less (hits Ragavan, Ocelot Pride, tokens, Guide of Souls). Pay 3 to include their MV 3 creatures. You keep your Scion (MV 12) and Phlage (MV 3 — hits at threshold ≥ 3). Use when they've flooded with small threats.
Pest Control ×2
{W}{B} sorcery, cycling {2}: destroy all nonland permanents with MV 1 or less. Hits: Ragavan (MV 1), Ocelot Pride (MV 1), Guide of Souls (MV 1), Goblin Bombardment (MV 2 — not hit), Monastery Swiftspear (MV 1), Dragon's Rage Channeler (MV 1). A surgical sweep against the one-mana threat packages of Boros Energy and Prowess. Also has cycling {2} for when the sweeper isn't needed.
Mystical Dispute ×2
Counter any blue spell: costs {U} if target controller has an Island, {1}{U} otherwise. Hits: Counterspell, Force of Negation, Consign to Memory, Goryo's Vengeance, Whir of Invention. Your best answer to blue control and blue combo post-board. Comes in vs Dimir Frog, Esper Goryo's, Tameshi Belcher, any blue-heavy interaction deck.
Celestial Purge ×1
Exile target black or red permanent — opponent can't respond to save it. Hits: Psychic Frog (black), Murktide Regent (blue — not hit), Ragavan (red — hits in mirror), Phlage (red — hits in mirror), Goblin Bombardment (red). Instant speed, uncounterable removal that bypasses hexproof (it exiles, doesn't target a creature in the traditional "can't be targeted" sense — wait, it does say "target permanent." Hexproof from opponents means you can't TARGET it. Celestial Purge targets. Scion's hexproof applies — Purge CAN'T exile a hexproof permanent you control. But it CAN exile your opponent's non-hexproof permanents.
Endurance ×1
Flash 3/4 {2}{G}. ETB: exile all cards from target player's GY, shuffle them into library. Instant-speed GY reset. Better than Lantern when you need to exile a full graveyard in response to a combo turn (Living End cascade, Goryo's with Atraxa ready). Can also be found via combat trick — flash it in as a surprise blocker. Comes in vs any GY-dependent combo deck as the premium Lantern upgrade.
Doorkeeper Thrull ×1 (SB)
A fourth Thrull for matchups where ETB shutdown is critical (Esper Blink, Esper Goryo's, Izzet Affinity). Bringing four total Thrulls into heavy ETB matchups maximizes your chance of having it when needed. The main deck runs three; the fourth in the board is specifically for matchups where Thrull's effect is a near-auto-win.
Stubborn Denial ×1 (SB)
A fourth Stubborn Denial for matchups where you need maximum ferocious counter coverage. Against heavy spell-based interaction (Dimir Control, any blue deck), four hard counters with ferocious provides almost impenetrable coverage for your threats. Bring in when you're cutting Spectral Denial and need the extra Denial count.

What Never Comes Out

Always Stays In
Leyline of the Guildpact ×4
Never sideboard out Leylines. The card that makes your deck from "good aggro" to "best aggro" doesn't leave for any matchup. Even in the mirror where both players have Leyline, yours is still symmetrically powering your side.
Almost Always Stays In
Territorial Kavu ×4
Your best creature in almost every matchup — 5/5 for {R}{G} that also handles graveyard hate on attack. The only matchup where you might trim one is a pure combo matchup where its attack trigger is irrelevant and you need more interaction.
First Cuts
Doorkeeper Thrull / Consign to Memory
These come out against matchups with no ETBs to counter. Vs Eldrazi Tron, Ruby Storm, or Tameshi Belcher: Thrull does nothing. Consign to Memory counters triggered abilities — weak when there are none. Cut these first and bring in relevant sideboard pieces.
Chapter 08

Heuristics

Sixteen rules to carry into every round.
  1. 01
    Leyline in hand = automatic keep, always. Even a weak six-card hand with Leyline outperforms a strong seven without it. The free domain engine is worth a card. You can win off five cards if Leyline is one of them.
  2. 02
    Cast Scion of Draco before your other creatures. Scion gives every creature you control hexproof, lifelink, first strike, trample, and vigilance. Creatures cast after Scion enter under this protection immediately. Casting Kavu before Scion means it's briefly targetable. Sequence Scion first, then deploy all threats under the hexproof umbrella.
  3. 03
    Leyline Binding exiles the most dangerous permanent, not the most convenient one. Binding is your cleanest removal at {W}. Use it on Goblin Charbelcher, Amulet of Vigor, Psychic Frog, Primeval Titan — the cards that win the game if unanswered. Don't waste Binding on a 2/2 token when a game-ending threat is in play.
  4. 04
    Stubborn Denial cannot counter creature spells. Spectral Denial can. Keep this distinction when deciding which counter to hold. Ferocious Stubborn Denial hard counters any noncreature spell. Spectral Denial handles anything. Use Stubborn vs. removal/sweepers; Spectral vs. creature ETBs your Doorkeeper couldn't answer in time.
  5. 05
    Doorkeeper Thrull must be in play when the creature enters, not before it's cast. Flash Thrull in response to the creature spell after it's been cast (on the stack) but before it resolves. Thrull's ability is a static effect — "while Thrull is in play, creatures entering don't trigger abilities." The window is between casting and resolution.
  6. 06
    Territorial Kavu's attack trigger is mandatory — choose carefully. On attack, you must choose: rummage (discard, draw) or exile a GY card. Against graveyard decks, always exile their key creature. Against non-GY decks, rummage to dig for missing pieces. You cannot decline both choices — pick the relevant one.
  7. 07
    Two Leylines in hand: play one, use the other as Kavu rummage fuel, unless they have enchantment removal. One Leyline establishes full domain. The second is redundant on the battlefield and better used as a discard card for Kavu's rummage to draw into threats. Exception: if you know they board in Wear//Tear or Nature's Claim, put both in for redundancy.
  8. 08
    Kraven's power scales with your highest-MV permanent. With Leyline (MV 4) and no other permanents: Kraven is 4/4. With Scion (MV 12): Kraven is 12/4. Check your board before combat — Kraven's first-strike damage calculation changes dramatically based on what's in play. A 12/4 first striker kills almost anything.
  9. 09
    Scion's hexproof protects YOUR creatures, not THEIR creatures. "Each creature you control has hexproof if it's blue." Hexproof means opponents can't target those creatures. Your own targeting spells (Leyline Binding exiling your own creature? Doesn't happen normally) still work. Your opponent's Scion doesn't protect your creatures from their removal.
  10. 10
    Mutagenic Growth costs 2 life, not mana — Prowess opponents can cast it at zero mana. When attacking with Kavu into a 3/3, assume Mutagenic Growth can make it a 5/5. That means Kavu's 5/5 first strike trades instead of winning. Calculate worst-case combat math before committing to attacks where the opponent can pay life to survive.
  11. 11
    Pest Control hits MV 1 or less — don't cast it on turn two if you have your own Ragavan in play. Pest Control destroys ALL nonland permanents with MV 1, including your own Ragavan. Cast it before deploying your own 1-drops, or accept that Ragavan dies to clear their board. Wrath of the Skies gives you more control over the threshold.
  12. 12
    Wear//Tear at instant speed in response to opponent targeting your Leyline Binding. If they cast Nature's Claim or Wear//Tear targeting your Binding (which would return the exiled permanent), respond by casting your own Wear//Tear targeting their key artifact or enchantment. Get value on the way out — don't let them trade one removal for one binding without cost.
  13. 13
    Against Eldrazi Tron: flash Doorkeeper Thrull in response to Thought-Knot Seer being cast. Thrull silences TKS's ETB exile effect. TKS resolves as a 4/4 with no hand disruption. This single interaction frequently wins the matchup — an unanswered TKS that strips your combo piece is devastating; a muted TKS is just a large body.
  14. 14
    Against Goryo's: attack with Kavu before casting threats, even into blocks. Territorial Kavu's attack exile can remove Atraxa or Griselbrand from their graveyard before they reanimate it. It's often correct to attack Kavu into a 2/2 chump blocker to exile their combo piece from the GY — losing Kavu to a block is worth it if you exile their win condition.
  15. 15
    Soul-Guide Lantern: ETB exiles GY immediately — use it the turn you cast it against Storm or Living End. Against Ruby Storm: cast Lantern in response to Past in Flames being cast — the ETB triggers on the stack above Past in Flames. Past in Flames resolves into an empty graveyard. The flashback source is gone. Timing is critical; don't let Past in Flames resolve first.
  16. 16
    Leyline Binding can be cast at any time including your opponent's turn — use it as flash exile. Flash on Binding means you can exile their threat on their attack step, in response to them casting something, or at any point you have {W} available. Don't telegraph by casting it on your main phase. The best Binding is the one that blanks their key action, cast in response.
Chapter 09

Tuning Notes

The core 20 (4 Leyline, 4 Kavu, 4 Scion, 4 Leyline Binding, 4 Lightning Bolt) is locked. Everything else is meta-dependent.
If Combo Is Dominant (Belcher, Storm, Neoform)
+2 Mystical Dispute MD
When three or more fast combo decks make up 15%+ of the field, main-decking 1–2 Mystical Dispute is correct. Trim Consign to Memory (1–2 copies) and possibly Doorkeeper Thrull (1 copy). Dispute hits Whir of Invention, Goryo's Vengeance, and any blue combo piece for {U}.
If GY Decks Are Prevalent (Goryo's, Living End, Storm)
+1 Soul-Guide Lantern MD
Moving one or two Soul-Guide Lanterns main deck makes sense when GY decks are concentrated in the field. The Lantern ETB exiles their GY on entry — proactive hate that costs {0} to cast. Trim Nishoba Brawler (it's the weakest creature slot) for Lanterns.
Nishoba Brawler vs Doorkeeper Thrull Count
3 Thrull / 2 Brawler or 2 Thrull / 3 Brawler
Nishoba Brawler (*/3 trample, domain power) is a redundant domain threat. Doorkeeper Thrull (flash ETB shutdown) is format-dependent hate. Against ETB-heavy metas (Esper Blink dominant), run 4 Thrull (3 MD + 1 SB). Against aggro-heavy metas with few ETBs, cut Thrull to 2 and run more Brawlers for body count.
Tribal Flames — Include or Exclude
0 or 2-3 copies
Some lists cut Tribal Flames entirely for more creatures; others run 2–3. Include Tribal Flames when: (1) the meta has lots of life-gain making burn necessary, (2) you face many stall-based strategies, (3) you want more reach to close games without attacking. Exclude when creature redundancy is more important than direct damage.
Kraven, Proud Predator Count
0, 2, or 4 copies
Kraven is a scaling threat that rewards having high-MV permanents. With Scion (MV 12) in play, Kraven is a 12/4 vigilance. Excellent in slower metas. Cut Kraven against extremely fast aggro where you need bodies that are relevant before Scion resolves. Run 0 in very aggressive fields, 4 in slower midrange-heavy environments.
Spectral Denial vs Consign to Memory
Meta-dependent split
Spectral Denial (counters creatures and spells) vs Consign to Memory (counters triggered abilities and colorless spells). Spectral is better against ETB creature-heavy decks. Consign is better against Eldrazi Tron (colorless spells) and pure combo. In a balanced meta: 2 Spectral, 2 Consign. In an ETB meta: 3 Spectral, 1 Consign. In an Eldrazi/combo meta: 1 Spectral, 3 Consign.
