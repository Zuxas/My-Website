BOROS ENERGY
Chapter 01

Identity &
Philosophy

"Pressure that never stops generating advantage." — Core Mantra

Boros Energy is an aggro-midrange hybrid that wins by applying relentless early pressure while simultaneously generating card advantage through energy synergies. It is not a pure beatdown deck — it punishes stumbles like aggro but grinds like midrange.

I qualified on this deck last Modern season with 17 RCQ Top 8s. The core strength is that it operates ahead of the curve in a way that forces reactive decisions at every turn. The opponent is always responding — never developing their own game plan on their terms.

Core Axes

Axis 01
Resource Generation
Ragavan generates mana and card advantage on attack. Guide of Souls and Ocelot Pride scale into inevitability as the game progresses. You are always ahead on resources.
Axis 02
Punishing Stumbles
Any slow start from the opponent is a fast loss. Galvanic Discharge scales to kill anything relevant. Goblin Bombardment converts tokens and spent bodies into reach and blanks removal. The deck has no "wait and see" mode.
Axis 03
Phlage as Pivot
Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury is the late-game engine. Escaping it resets life totals and applies pressure simultaneously. Games where Phlage resolves and escapes are almost always wins. Fetch/shock decisions change once Phlage is online — racing becomes favorable.
Axis 04
Goblin Bombardment Reach
Goblin Bombardment turns every token and spare body into a free damage spell. Blanks removal, converts Ocelot cats into face damage, and closes games from positions that look stabilized. Always know your Bombardment math before combat.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths
What You Do Well
Fastest closing speed of any fair deck in Modern.

Generates advantage while applying pressure.

Reward-to-rep ratio is excellent — familiar hands close games.

Sideboard is proven and deeply understood.
Weaknesses
What Beats You
Galvanic Discharge doesn't kill everything — energy management matters.

Wrath effects at the right moment can reset your board.

Stabilizing opponents at 8+ life make Ajani harder to close.

The mirror is decided entirely by sideboard quality.

Status This Season

Known quantity — evaluating whether to run back. The deck is still powerful. The question is whether the hate has caught up enough to reduce the margins at the RC level, where every opponent has specifically prepared for you. RCQs may still be correct. Full RC cycle requires deeper evaluation against the expected field.

Chapter 03

The Game Plan

"Never give them a free turn. Every attack either threatens lethal or generates a resource." — Key Principle

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turn 1
Establish Pressure
Ragavan on turn 1 is the ideal start. If no Ragavan, Ocelot Pride or Guide of Souls. Always apply pressure on turn 1 if you have it — tempo losses in the early turns compound.
Turn 2–3
Layer Threats
Add Guide of Souls or Ocelot Pride while continuing to attack. Use Galvanic Discharge to clear blockers. Never let the opponent stabilize behind a single blocker for free.
Turn 3–4
Generate Inevitability
Ocelot Pride triggers create a growing cat army. Guide of Souls generates energy for lifegain and card advantage. By turn 4 you should have multiple threats that scale independently.
Turn 4–5
Ajani Closes
A single Ajani activation with 3+ cats on board represents lethal from 20 life. Never walk into a Wrath or Solitude with Ajani — wait for the window where they're tapped out or empty on interaction.

Critical Decision Points

Galvanic Discharge timing: Discharge scales with energy — always check if you can hit the breakpoint to kill what matters before spending it. Against Blink, don't shave Discharge post-board; it's needed to kill Guide of Souls and DRC before they snowball.

Goblin Bombardment math: Always calculate your Bombardment lines before going to combat. Sac outlets + token count = reach that opponents don't see coming. Converting the last two cats into 2 damage when they're at 2 life closes more games than attacking.

Ragavan attacks: Ragavan should attack every turn it's alive unless blocking saves a race. The gold and mana generation compound. Even connecting once often represents a significant tempo advantage.

Mulligan Philosophy

Snap keep: Any hand with Ragavan or Ocelot Pride + 2 lands + at least one removal spell.

Snap mulligan: No one-drop, no pressure on turns 1–2, or more than 4 lands. This deck cannot afford slow starts.

Borderline: Two-drop heavy hands without a one-drop are keepable on the play, questionable on the draw. Add one card of scrutiny on the draw for every hand.

Chapter 04

Role Assignment

Before the first spell is cast, know whether you are the beatdown or the control. Getting this wrong costs games. Boros Energy shifts roles less than Blink — it is almost always the beatdown — but there are key exceptions.

Beatdown
vs: Control · Midrange · Combo · UW Blink

Default role. Apply relentless pressure. Every attack matters. Do not slow down to play around potential answers — force them to have it. Time is always on the opponent's side in these matchups if you stop pressing.

Tempo Control
vs: Prowess · Burn · Very Fast Aggro

In the rare matchups where you are slower or your life total matters, use removal to buy time. Wrath of the Skies at X=1 is a tempo play that resets wide boards. Galvanic Discharge killing a Swiftspear is higher priority than pressing your own clock.

Read Carefully
vs: Mirror Match

In the mirror, whoever is on the draw should default to a slightly more defensive posture — Ragavan trades are often favourable for the pilot who draws second since they can reestablish faster. Goblin Bombardment decides the mirror post-board — whoever gets it online converts tokens to reach faster. Map energy spending carefully; Galvanic Discharge breakpoints matter a lot in the mirror.

Chapter 06

Sideboard Guide

Per-card notes on what each sideboard card does and when to bring it in. Commit to a plan before games 2 and 3 — don't decide at the table.

Sideboard Card Notes

Blood Moon — 2 copies
Land disruption. Comes in vs Amulet Titan, Eldrazi, and any greedy 3+ color manabase. Joe Leo's verdict: Molten Rain fits the proactive Boros gameplan better in most spots, but Blood Moon earns its keep against decks with fetch/shock bases that can't function under it. Don't board it in against decks with basic-heavy manabases.
Celestial Purge — 2 copies
Exile removal for black or red permanents. Hits Psychic Frog, Archon of Cruelty, Phlage (mirror), and any Goryo's target. Two mana instant — cleanest answer to Glockulous threats post-board. Also hits Ral in Storm. Boards in vs Grixis Reanimator, Prowess, Storm, Zoo (hits Scion of Draco).
Clarion Conqueror — 1 copy
3/3 flyer that counters spells. Boards in vs Belcher (they have fewer 3cmc cards to interact with) and fringe decks like Yawgmoth. Joe Leo: "The difference between 2 and 3 mana is huge" — use carefully. Randomly good against any deck relying on cheap noncreature spells.
Deafening Silence — 1 copy
Hard lock against Storm and Prowess — limits noncreature spells to one per turn. Joe Leo moved away from Orim's Chant toward Silence specifically for Storm. You can play 3 Silence if you know Storm will be at the tournament. Also boards in vs any spell-chain combo.
High Noon — 1 copy
Limits each player to one spell per turn. Shuts down Prowess's entire game plan (they need to chain spells). Also good vs Storm and cascade. Boards in alongside Deafening Silence when you want double hate coverage vs spell-based decks.
Meltdown — 1 copy
Sweeps cheap artifacts for X mana. X=0 kills Leyline of the Void and Amulet of Vigor. X=1 hits Nihil Spellbomb and equipment. Boards in vs Belcher (kills Charbelcher itself at X=4, but more useful vs supporting artifacts), Affinity, and any deck with 0–1 CMC artifact hate pieces.
Surgical Extraction — 2 copies
Graveyard hate that also extracts key combo pieces. Primary use: vs Grixis Reanimator (extract Archon of Cruelty after any copy enters the graveyard). Also boards in vs Amulet Titan (extract Amulet or Titan after seeing it), Grinding Breach, and any graveyard combo. Don't bring in speculatively — know what you're extracting.
The Legend of Roku — 1 copy
Enchantment that grants all your creatures haste and an enters trigger. Boards in vs midrange and control where the extra velocity matters. Evaluate per meta — this is the flex slot that adjusts based on expected field.
Vexing Bauble — 1 copy
Taxes free spells — opponent must pay 1 or you draw a card. Primarily boards in vs decks relying on free interaction: Force of Negation (Blink), Living End (cascade), Eldrazi (free spells). Excellent vs any blue deck leaning on Force. Cantrips when they pay the tax so it's never dead.
Wear // Tear — 1 copy
Artifact and enchantment removal. The split card covers both axes in one slot. Boards in vs Affinity, Belcher, Stony Silence (from opposing sideboard), Sanctifier en-Vec in the mirror, and any problematic enchantment. The reason this is a 1-of over a dedicated card is the split flexibility.
Wrath of the Skies — 2 copies
X-cost sweeper. Boards in vs any wide creature deck — Prowess (X=1 clears Monk tokens and Swiftspear), Blink (X=2 clears their token board), Zoo. Cast it when the board is threatening — don't wait for a perfect X value. X=1 is usually enough.
Chapter 07

Heuristics &
Quick Rules

These are the rules that apply automatically — decisions made before the game starts so you don't have to think about them at the table.

  1. Always lead with Ragavan on turn 1 if you have it and the opponent can't immediately block or kill it. Every stolen mana and card compounds. Don't hesitate.
  2. Galvanic Discharge scales with energy — check the breakpoint before spending it. At 2 energy it hits most creatures; at 4+ it answers anything. Don't spend it without knowing the value.
  3. Never tap out to deploy Ajani if the opponent has Wrath mana up. Wait one turn — they either tap out first or you attack for two turns and close anyway.
  4. Goblin Bombardment math before combat: always count how many damage you can Bombardment through after attackers are declared. It closes games opponents think are safe.
  5. Phlage is your pivot. Once Phlage is online and you're escaping it, racing becomes favorable in almost every matchup. Prioritize getting to 6 mana to escape over other lines.
  6. Joe Leo's rule: if you feel like you need to splash a third color, that's a signal to play a different deck entirely — not a reason to add a color. Play straight Boros.
  7. On the draw, be one turn more defensive than on the play. Don't trade resources you need to close — let the game go one turn longer if you can stabilize the board.
  8. Against combo, your pressure is their clock. Never slow down to interact unless the interaction stops their combo directly. Galvanic Discharge to the face beats Discharge to a creature in the Reanimator matchup.
  9. Ocelot Pride tokens are expendable. Trade them into blockers freely if it keeps Ragavan or Guide of Souls alive — the cat generation will rebuild. The Bombardment reach the tokens represent is the real value.
  10. Know your Ajani math before casting it. With 3 cats on board: each cat gets +3/+3 and creates another cat. That's 6+ attacking power the turn Ajani activates. Know when it's lethal before spending the mana.
