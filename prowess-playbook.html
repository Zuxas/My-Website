Chapter 01

Identity &
Philosophy

"One spell triggers three threats. Two spells end the game." — Core Mantra

Izzet Prowess is an aggro-combo hybrid that wins not through any single powerful card but through the density of interactions between cheap threats and cheap spells. Every noncreature spell simultaneously pumps your Monastery Swiftspear via prowess, surv1 sculpts your Dragon's Rage Channeler, advances your Cori-Steel Cutter's Flurry threshold, and grows your Slickshot Show-Off. A single Lightning Bolt does four things at once.

The deck's identity is spell density. You're not trying to resolve a bomb — you're trying to cast four spells in one turn while your opponent is trying to handle a 4/4 flier, a 3/3 with double strike, and a 3/3 that made a haste token. The format's interaction is designed to answer individual powerful cards. It's not designed to handle the chain.

The critical concept separating 55% pilots from 70% pilots: delirium threshold management. Dragon's Rage Channeler is a 1/1 vanilla until you hit delirium — four card types in the graveyard. At that point it becomes a 3/3 flier that must attack. Knowing exactly when you cross that threshold and what card types you've accumulated is a live tracking task every turn.

Core Engines

Engine 01
The Prowess Package
Monastery Swiftspear (1/2 haste, prowess) and Slickshot Show-Off (1/2 flying haste, +2/+0 per noncreature spell) are your primary attackers. Every noncreature spell turns these into serious damage dealers. With two spells in a turn, Slickshot is a 5/2 flying haste — often an 8+ damage swing before blocks are declared.
Engine 02
Dragon's Rage Channeler
{R} 1/1. Each noncreature spell you cast surv 1 — look at the top card, optionally put it in the graveyard. This actively builds toward delirium while filtering your draws. Delirium active: DRC becomes a 3/3 with flying that must attack each combat. Cannot be held back. Manage your graveyard types consciously.
Engine 03
Cori-Steel Cutter
{1}{R} equipment. Equipped creature gets +1/+1, trample, haste. Flurry: your second spell each turn creates a 1/1 Monk with prowess — you can immediately attach Cutter to it, giving it +1/+1, trample, haste. That Monk is now a 2/2 trampling haste attacker the same turn it was created, and it has prowess for further pumping. The threshold is the second spell, not the first.
Engine 04
Mishra's Bauble System
{0} artifact: tap + sacrifice to look at the top card of any player's library. Draw a card at the beginning of the next turn's upkeep — delayed, not now. Bauble is zero mana, triggers prowess on cast, is an artifact (counting toward delirium), and provides a free delayed card. Four copies means you have four 0-mana spell chains and four guaranteed artifact card types for delirium.

The Delirium System

Delirium requires four or more card types among cards in your graveyard. The card types are: artifact, battle, creature, enchantment, instant, kindred, land, planeswalker, sorcery. The typical Prowess delirium path is instant + sorcery + artifact + land — Lightning Bolt in the yard, Preordain in the yard, Mishra's Bauble in the yard, any fetchland cracked. This is achievable by turn 2 on a normal draw.

Dragon's Rage Channeler's delirium ability is not a triggered ability — it's a continuous static ability checked every turn. The moment you have four card types, DRC is a 3/3 flier. The moment you drop below four (rare, but possible if graveyard hate exiles cards), it reverts. Track your graveyard types on every turn.

The delayed draw means you're always going to be one card ahead the following turn. More importantly: it's zero mana, it's an artifact (delirium card type), and casting it triggers prowess. Chapter 03

The Game Plan

"Don't play one spell — play four. The whole deck fires at once." — Execution Principle

Turn-by-Turn Framework

Turn 1
Deploy + Survey
Play a one-drop threat (DRC, Swiftspear) plus Mishra's Bauble if in hand. Bauble costs zero, puts an artifact in the graveyard immediately, triggers prowess, and gives you a card next turn upkeep. DRC surv 1 on Bauble cast starts graveyard sculpting toward delirium. By end of turn 1 you can already have instant + artifact in the yard.
Turn 2
Establish Delirium
Cast Preordain or Lava Dart to put a sorcery or instant in the yard, cracking a fetchland puts land in the yard. With Bauble already in the yard: artifact + sorcery + land + instant = delirium. DRC flips to a 3/3 flier at this point. Cast Cori-Steel Cutter and equip it to anything — now your attacker has +1/+1, trample, haste.
Turn 3
Chain Spells
This is the turn you're looking for. Two or three spells in one turn while attacking with a delirium DRC and a Cori-Steel Cutter-equipped threat. Lightning Bolt + Mutagenic Growth + attack = 3+2 from burn plus combat damage from a pumped, trampling creature. Slickshot as a 5/2 flying haste attacker. The chain damage often represents 12+ in one turn.
Turn 4+
Close or Murktide
If the game goes past turn 3, Expressive Iteration refills and Murktide Regent provides a large threat that's hard to remove profitably. Murktide with 4 instants/sorceries exiled is a 7/7 flier for around 3 mana. Any removal they cast on Murktide also triggers its counter from instants/sorceries leaving the graveyard — it grows even as they try to kill it.

Delirium Management

Track your graveyard types every turn. The four you need: instant (Lightning Bolt, Lava Dart), sorcery (Preordain, Expressive Iteration), artifact (Mishra's Bauble), land (any cracked fetchland). You typically hit delirium by turn 2.

What disrupts delirium: Surgical Extraction removing your only copy of a card type, Rest in Peace exiling the entire graveyard, Nihil Spellbomb cracking. After any graveyard disruption, immediately assess which card types remain and whether DRC reverted. Adjust your sequencing accordingly — if DRC is back to a 1/1, prioritize rebuilding delirium over attacking.

The Cori-Steel Cutter Flurry Sequence

Cori-Steel Cutter's Flurry triggers on your second spell each turn — not the first. The correct sequencing: cast spell 1 (any), equip Cutter to a threat, attack, then cast spell 2 on your main phase after combat to trigger Flurry and create a Monk you can attach Cutter to. The Monk enters as a 1/1 with prowess, but if you attach Cutter immediately it becomes a 2/2 with trample and haste — an attacker that can swing the same turn.

The most impactful Flurry line: cast Lightning Bolt (spell 1), cast Mutagenic Growth (spell 2, free) → Flurry triggers, Monk enters. Attach Cutter to Monk. Now you have a fresh 2/2 trample/haste creature that also has prowess and is already equipped. This all happened in response to your opponent tapping out.

Lava Dart — The Real Cost

Lava Dart: {R} instant, 1 damage to any target. Flashback: sacrifice a Mountain. The flashback cost is not free — you're spending a land. This means Lava Dart flashback depletes your land count. Never use it carelessly in the early turns. The correct use: fire Dart and immediately flash it back in the same turn when you need to trigger prowess twice, kill a 1-toughness creature at instant speed, or need two spell triggers for Cori-Steel Flurry. Know that you are one Mountain poorer after the flashback.

Mulligan Philosophy

Snap keep: Any hand with 2 threats + 2 spells + 2 lands. You need to be casting spells on turns 1 and 2 — any hand that can't do this is borderline.

Snap mulligan: Hands with 0 one-drop threats, 4+ lands, or hands that are purely cantrips without threats. You need a creature to pump — cantrips with no target are dead cards.

Delirium note: Hands with Mishra's Bauble are stronger than they look because Bauble counts as an artifact in the yard and triggers prowess for free. Prioritize hands that can hit delirium by turn 2.

On the draw: One extra card means more redundancy. You can keep slightly lower-threat hands on the draw because the extra card often provides the missing piece. Lean toward keeping hands with Preordain or Expressive Iteration on the draw — card selection is more valuable when you have extra information.

Play vs Draw

On the Play
T1 DRC or Swiftspear
On the play, T1 Dragon’s Rage Channeler or Monastery Swiftspear is your highest-value opening. DRC on T1 starts surveiling to build delirium while applying pressure. On the play: always lead with a 1-drop threat. A land-pass on T1 means you’re a full turn behind the tempo game and your spells do less damage without a prowess/surveil trigger magnet on the board.
On the Draw
Extra Card Finds the Kill Spell
On the draw, the extra card often provides the burn spell that closes the game or the cantrip that finds it. Keep looser hands on the draw if they have a threat + any cantrip. Against aggro on the draw: Fatal Push or Lava Dart hands are keepable even without a T1 threat because you need to survive to T3 before your clock takes over.

When Disrupted

Threats Removed
Burn to Face
DRC and Swiftspear removed: pivot to burn plan. Lightning Bolt + Light Up the Stage + any damage spell = 8-10 damage from hand. At 20 life this closes in 2-3 turns without any creatures. The burn redundancy means creature removal never fully answers this deck — they’re only removing 5-6 prowess triggers, not the damage output itself.
Burn Countered
Rebuild with DRC Surveil
Burn spells countered: rebuild via DRC’s surveil triggers. Each noncreature spell surveils 1 — dig through the deck for more threats. Murktide Regent in the GY as a finisher means even a swept board recovers to a 4/4+ flier in 2-3 spells. DRC surveil makes every card you draw a choice, not a topdeck.

Three engines make Izzet Prowess the fastest consistent kill in Modern. Each spell you cast advances all three simultaneously.

A
Dragon's Rage Channeler Delirium Engine
DRC becomes a 3/3 flying must-attack creature with delirium. Once active it attacks every turn whether you want it to or not.
Setup
4 Card Types in GY
Delirium requires four or more card types in your graveyard. Your typical type spread: Lightning Bolt (instant), Sleight of Hand (sorcery), a land drop (land), and DRC itself if it dies (creature). Mishra's Bauble is an artifact — it enters the GY immediately when cracked and provides the artifact type for delirium in one card. Cori-Steel Cutter in the GY covers equipment. Four types by turn 2 is consistently achievable.
Execution
DRC Becomes the Clock
DRC with delirium: 3/3 flying, must attack each turn if able. The must-attack clause is a feature, not a drawback — it guarantees 3 damage per turn in the air, ignoring ground blockers. DRC's surveil 1 triggers on each cast, fueling deeper delirium and finding your best spells. A turn-1 DRC that hits delirium on turn 2 deals 3 damage on turn 2 and every turn thereafter.
Result
Guaranteed 3 Damage Per Turn
DRC with delirium plus any other attacker is often 6+ damage per turn. At 20 life with a turn-2 delirium activation, they have 6 turns before DRC alone kills them. Your burn spells shorten that clock dramatically. Against control decks that stabilize on life but can't profitably block a 3/3 flier, DRC is often the primary win condition after their sweeper lands.
B
Cori-Steel Cutter Flurry Chain
The second spell you cast each turn creates a 1/1 Monk with prowess and haste. Chain into infinite Monks.
Setup
Cori-Steel Cutter Resolves
Cori-Steel Cutter (MV 2, equipment): equipped creature gets +1/+1, trample, haste. Flurry ability: whenever you cast your second spell in a turn, create a 1/1 white Monk token with prowess and haste. The Monk enters immediately — you can attack with it this turn. Each subsequent spell after the second in a turn triggers Flurry again if Cutter is equipped and the Monk is the equipped creature.
Execution
Chain Spells, Attack With Monks
Cast two spells — Monk 1 enters with prowess and haste. Cast a third spell — the Monk grows. Cast a fourth spell — another Monk from Flurry if Cutter moved to the first Monk. Each Monk has prowess (gets +1/+1 until EOT for each noncreature spell). A turn with 4 spells and one Monk creates a 4/4 haste attacker that attacks immediately for free. Chain Lava Dart (flashback a Mountain) and Mishra's Bauble (draw at next upkeep) for zero-mana spell triggers.
Result
Explosive Burst Turns
Turns 3-4 with Cutter active: a hand of 4 spells casts for 4 mana total, generates 2 Monk tokens, both with prowess triggers, both attacking with haste. Combined with DRC's 3/3 flying attack and a Swiftspear hitting for 3-4 after triggers, a single burst turn deals 15-20 damage from a stable-seeming board position. This is why Prowess kills on turn 3 consistently.
C
Lava Dart + Mishra's Bauble Free Trigger Engine
Zero-mana spells trigger prowess, Flurry, and DRC surveil for free. They are not just utility — they are engine components.
Setup
Lava Dart + Mountain in GY
Lava Dart costs {R}: deal 1 damage to any target. Flashback: sacrifice a Mountain. The flashback cast is a separate spell for zero mana beyond sacrificing a basic Mountain. Mishra's Bauble costs {0}: tap and sacrifice to look at the top card of a player's library; draw a card at the beginning of the next upkeep. Both are zero or near-zero mana spell casts that count as spells for prowess, Flurry, and DRC surveil.
Execution
Stack Triggers Efficiently
The correct sequence on a burst turn: (1) cast Monastery Swiftspear — prowess creature enters (0/0 + no triggers yet). (2) Cast Mishra's Bauble for 0 — prowess triggers, Swiftspear is 1/2. (3) Cast Lava Dart — prowess triggers again, Swiftspear is 2/3, Dart deals 1 somewhere. (4) Flashback Lava Dart (sacrifice Mountain) — prowess triggers again, Swiftspear is 3/4. Four spells for {R}+{0}+{R}+{land sacrifice}, Swiftspear attacks as 3/4 haste. Add DRC surveil and Flurry Monks to this framework.
Result
Mana-Efficient Storm
Lava Dart flashback and Mishra's Bauble are not support cards — they are the engine. They let you cast 4-6 spells on turns where you only have 2-3 mana, compressing your damage output into a single turn. This is why Prowess sequences feel like Storm. The "free" spells extend your chain far beyond what your land count suggests is possible.

Lines & Tricks

  • StackMutagenic Growth on an attacking creature in response to a block: They declare a blocker — their 3/3 blocks your 2/2 Swiftspear. Cast Mutagenic Growth paying 2 life (not mana). Swiftspear is now 4/4. Their blocker dies, your Swiftspear survives, and Mutagenic also triggers prowess for +1/+1 more. The 2 life is almost always worth the exchange.
  • SequenceSave Lava Dart flashback for a burst turn rather than shooting early: Lava Dart flashback sacrifices a Mountain permanently — it is a one-time resource. Spending it on turn 1 to deal 1 damage to a creature is weak. Saving it for turn 3 as the 4th spell on a burst turn grows your whole board by +1/+1 from prowess and generates a Flurry Monk. Always evaluate burst turns before spending Dart flashback.
  • SequenceDRC surveil order matters: DRC's surveil 1 triggers when you cast any spell. If you're about to draw a card (from Bauble, Sleight), surveil first — put the card you want on top, then draw it. If you need to hit delirium, surveil to put a non-land card in the GY. Plan the surveil destination before the spell resolves.
  • AvoidDon't overcommit to the board against sweepers: Prowess is a burst deck, not a go-wide deck. Casting your entire hand onto the board over multiple turns and losing everything to Supreme Verdict is the wrong line. Deploy one or two threats at a time, keep burn for face rather than removal, and burst-kill them before the sweeper becomes relevant.

Three Lines You Must Know Cold

Critical
DRC Needs 4 Card TYPES for Delirium
Dragon’s Rage Channeler’s Delirium requires 4 different card types in your graveyard, not 4 cards. Card types: Instant, Sorcery, Creature, Land, Artifact, Enchantment. The most common gap: you have Instant (Lightning Bolt), Creature (DRC), Land (from fetch) = 3 types. You need a SORCERY — Preordain or Serum Visions. Without a sorcery in GY, DRC is a vanilla 1/1. Surveil aggressively with DRC’s trigger to find the sorcery type.
Critical
Monastery Swiftspear Prowess Triggers Stack
Monastery Swiftspear gets +1/+1 until end of turn whenever you cast a noncreature spell. Each spell is a separate trigger. Cast Lightning Bolt + Mutagenic Growth + Preordain in one turn — Swiftspear gets 3 separate +1/+1 triggers = 4/5 until EOT. Triggers can be responded to individually and all still resolve. Chain spells before blocks to maximize Swiftspear’s combat size. Count before declaring attackers.
Critical
Lava Dart Flashback Sacrifices a MOUNTAIN
Lava Dart’s flashback cost is to sacrifice a Mountain — not just any land, specifically a Mountain-type land. Steam Vents is a Mountain (it has the Mountain land type). Scalding Tarn after fetching a Mountain leaves no Mountain on board. Basics: only basic Mountain counts. Count your Mountains before counting available Dart flashbacks. Never tap your last Mountain for mana if you need the Dart flashback to kill a blocker.

Timing Windows

When to Hold Mana vs Spend It
The core timing decision for this deck: every land drop and every spell cast is a commitment. Know before your upkeep which lines require open mana this turn and which don’t. Holding mana = passing priority; spending it = committing to a plan. Make this decision in your upkeep, not in response to their action.
When to Cast at Their End Step vs Your Main Phase
End-step casts maximize information: you’ve seen their full turn, they’re often tapped out. Main-phase casts maximize mana availability for follow-up. Know which of your key spells gain most from end-step timing and which need main-phase mana backup.

Role Assignment

Izzet Prowess has three distinct modes. The mistake most pilots make is staying in Burn mode when the game requires Tempo, or playing Tempo when the game required closing. Reading the mode is the highest-skill expression in this deck.

Burn Clock
Default mode. Creatures attack, spells pump them, burn goes to the face. You are racing the opponent's life total. Every decision is evaluated by "does this close the game faster?" Combat damage + direct burn + prowess triggers all simultaneously.
vs: Grixis Reanimator · Amulet Titan · Control · Any slow deck
Tempo Aggro
When they have specific threats you must answer before closing. Remove their key blocker or engine, then race. Lightning Bolt and Lava Dart are removal tools here, not face damage. Unholy Heat at 6 damage kills anything in the format with delirium active.
vs: Boros Energy (kill Goblin Bombardment) · Jeskai Blink (kill Guide of Souls)
Delirium Engine
When the game goes long, switch to Murktide as the finisher and Expressive Iteration as the refill engine. Stop spending burn on face and start deploying Murktide. A 7/7 flier often ends the game in 2 turns from any board state. Consign to Memory protects the chain.
vs: Any matchup that reaches turn 5+ · Any deck that stabilized the board

Role Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning face when you need to kill a blocker. At 8 life against Boros Energy with Goblin Bombardment on board, burning their face with Bolt does less work than killing Bombardment (which removes 4+ damage of reach). Situationally, a removal spell that opens your attack for 8 is worth more than 3 to the face.
  • Holding spells when you should be chaining. The deck generates value by casting multiple spells per turn. A hand of 3 spells played one-per-turn is half as effective as casting all 3 in one turn to maximize prowess pumps, Flurry triggers, and DRC surv 1 selections.
  • Casting Murktide too early. A turn 3 Murktide from a hand without threats is a 3/3 flier for a lot of resources. A turn 5 Murktide after the graveyard is stacked is often a 7/7+. Don't rush the Regent — let it scale.
  • Flashing back Lava Dart carelessly. The flashback cost is sacrifice a Mountain. Recklessly using Dart flashback in the early turns depletes your land base. Count your lands before flashing Dart.

Role-Switching Signals

Switch to Tempo When
They Have a Clock You Can't Race
Opponent presents a threat you can't ignore — a resolved Primeval Titan, a 5/5 Kappa Cannoneer, a Teferi with counters. Stop chaining spells for prowess pumps and deploy targeted removal. Unholy Heat with delirium active (6 damage) is your premium answer. Prioritize surviving over growing your threats.
Stay on Aggro When
Their Life Total Is in Lethal Range
Opponent is below 8 life and you have DRC or Swiftspear with spells in hand. Stop holding back for "better" lines — calculate pump math and attack. Mutagenic Growth + Lava Dart + one spell = 6+ damage from nothing. Commit when the math says lethal is available this turn.
Never Slow Down When
Delirium + Open Board
Delirium is active, DRC is attacking with surveil, and they have no blockers. This is your highest-value position. Don't switch modes — keep casting spells to trigger prowess, keep flipping DRC to 3/3 surveil 1, keep piling on damage. The tempo advantage compounds with every spell.
Identify Before T1
Read Their Opener
Their T1 land tells you a lot: Island = likely counter suite, hold spells until they tap out. Mountain/Plains = aggro mirror or Boros, race them immediately. No land = potential mull to 6, press aggressively. Use this read to sequence your first two spells correctly — the right opener beats good hands by 20%.

Boros Energy — The Dynamic

Boros is a slight dog matchup because their Goblin Bombardment converts token armies into direct damage reach that bypasses your life total drain advantage. Their Galvanic Discharge gets 3 energy on cast, meaning a single Discharge kills almost everything you have at any point in the game. Your path to winning: DRC as a 3/3 flier is hard for them to block efficiently. Lava Dart flashback at instant speed kills their Ocelot Pride tokens and Ragavan without spending your own mana. Your life total will get drained — stay above 8 as a priority.

Edge Cases &
Rules Precision

"Prowess wins on the third spell. Know exactly what triggers when, or the math never adds up."
Prowess — Triggers on Cast, Not Resolution
Prowess says "whenever you cast a noncreature spell, this creature gets +1/+1 until end of turn." This triggers when you CAST the spell — not when it resolves. If your Counterspell counters the spell in response, prowess still triggered on the cast. If the spell fizzles (target becomes illegal), prowess still triggered. This means: you can cast a spell into a counter knowing your creature gets the +1/+1 regardless of whether the spell resolves. Count prowess triggers from the moment of casting, not outcome.
Dragon's Rage Channeler — Delirium Check Is Continuous
Dragon's Rage Channeler has delirium — it's a 3/3 flier that must attack if able, but only while you have 4+ card types in your graveyard. This check is CONTINUOUS and constant — it updates every time your GY composition changes. If during your opponent's turn they use Nihil Spellbomb to exile your GY, DRC immediately loses delirium and reverts to a 1/1. If you then put 4+ types back, it regains delirium. Track your GY types actively: you need land, instant, sorcery, and creature (or enchantment/artifact) for delirium.
Murktide Regent — Size Is Based on Exiled Instants/Sorceries at ETB
Murktide Regent says "Delve. When Murktide Regent enters the battlefield, exile all instants and sorceries from your graveyard. Murktide Regent gets +2/+2 for each card exiled this way." The size is locked in when it enters — it doesn't grow or shrink after that based on spells cast later. The exiled cards fuel the size. Important: if opponents use Nihil Spellbomb before Murktide enters (exiling your GY), Murktide enters as a 0/0 and immediately dies as a state-based action. Cast Murktide before they have mana for GY disruption.
Slickshot Show-Off — Plot Exiles to Cast Later Free, Not for Free From Hand
Plot costs {1}{R}: exile Slickshot Show-Off from your hand. You may cast it from exile for free at any later point. The key distinction: Plotted spells are cast from exile zone, not from hand. Effects that care about casting from hand (Ephemerate rebound) don't apply. Lavinia, Azorius Renegade says "opponents can't cast spells without paying mana" — a Plotted Slickshot casting for free DOES get countered by Lavinia. Plotted spells cannot be cast on the same turn they're plotted.
Cori-Steel Cutter — Equipment Equip Is an Activated Ability
Cori-Steel Cutter's equip cost is an activated ability of an artifact. Under Stony Silence, Cori-Steel Cutter cannot be equipped to a creature (equip is an activated ability of an artifact — Stony Silence shuts off all activated abilities of artifacts). The creatures that already have it equipped keep the bonus, but you can't move the equipment while Stony Silence is in play. Against Humans post-board with Kataki, War's Wage: Cutter must pay {1} per upkeep or sacrifice. Against Consign to Memory: the triggered ability from Cutter generating tokens CAN be countered by Consign.
Lava Dart — Flashback Targets Any Creature, Including Your Own
Lava Dart deals 1 damage to any target. Flashback {R} (sacrifice a Mountain). When you flash it back, you can target any creature — including your own. This is intentional: targeting your own Monastery Swiftspear with Lava Dart flashback gives Swiftspear +1/+1 from prowess (you cast a spell) PLUS the 1 damage resolves on Swiftspear. Net: +1/+1 and -1 toughness = net 0 on toughness but +1 on power. More commonly, targeting your own creatures to trigger prowess multiple times in the same turn is a legitimate and common line.

Why Consign Over Other Counters

Sanctifier en-Vec's protection ability is an ETB triggered ability — Consign counters it. This denies the protection from black and red entirely, leaving Sanctifier as a vanilla 2/1. Without the protection, your entire burn suite is live again. This is the play that turns a lost game 2 into a winning game 2.

Murktide is a slow finisher in a matchup where you need to race Sanctifier landing. The mana investment in Murktide (even with Delve) is better spent on multiple 1-mana spells that each trigger prowess. Blink also has Solitude to answer Murktide at zero mana cost. Replace with density.

The Counter War Plan

Consign counters Teferi's ETB bounce trigger and Snapcaster's flashback trigger. Spell Snare counters their Counterspell on your turn 2 for one mana. Spell Pierce counters their sweeper early. Chapter 08

Heuristics &
Quick Rules

Automatic decisions made before the game starts. Prowess rewards pilots who've internalized these as reflexes — the spell chaining happens too fast for deliberate calculation every time.

  1. Track your graveyard card types every turn. Delirium requires 4 card types. The standard path is instant + sorcery + artifact + land. Know which types you have. Know when DRC flips. Know when graveyard hate removes a type and DRC reverts.
  2. Mishra's Bauble costs zero and draws a card next upkeep — cast it proactively, don't sandbag it. It triggers prowess, it's an artifact in the graveyard immediately, and it gives a delayed card. Sandbagging Bauble "for later" loses the triggers and the delirium contribution.
  3. Lava Dart flashback costs a Mountain. You are one land poorer after flashing it. Count your lands before flashing. Don't use it carelessly in turns 1-3 when you need your mana base intact.
  4. Cori-Steel Cutter's Flurry triggers on the second spell each turn, not the first. Sequence: cast spell 1, then after combat cast spell 2 to trigger Flurry. The Monk enters, you attach Cutter immediately, it becomes a 2/2 trample haste attacker that same turn.
  5. Mutagenic Growth can be cast at any time — use it to save creatures from removal. When they cast removal, respond with Mutagenic Growth to save your threat. Paying 2 life to keep a 3/3 DRC alive is always correct. The pump also triggers prowess on other creatures you control.
  6. Slickshot Show-Off attacks as a 5/2 flying haste with just two spells cast that turn. Know this number. Two spells = Slickshot at 5 power with flying. On a stalled board, casting two cheap spells and swinging with Slickshot often represents 5 flying damage opponents didn't account for.
  7. Violent Urge with delirium gives double strike — this is often a kill setup, not just a pump. A delirium DRC with Violent Urge is a 4/3 with double strike — dealing 4 first strike damage, then 4 combat damage. Through a 3/3 blocker: first strike kills the blocker, then DRC deals 4 unblocked. Combined with any other damage this is often lethal.
  8. Expressive Iteration looks at 3 cards: you keep 1, exile 1 (playable this turn), put 1 on bottom. The card you exile can be played this turn but not kept — it's gone after the turn if you don't cast it. Prioritize exiling a land or a card you'd want to cast immediately rather than saving for later.
  9. Murktide grows when any instant or sorcery leaves your graveyard — including via Expressive Iteration exile. Every Expressive Iteration you cast grows Murktide by 1. Every fetch crack puts a land in the yard (not an instant/sorcery). Track Murktide's power separately from the graveyard card type count.
  10. Consign to Memory cannot counter colored spells. It only counters triggered abilities and colorless spells. Lightning Bolt is safe from Consign. Counterspell is not stopped by Consign. Consign's targets are triggered abilities (ETBs, upkeep triggers, keyword triggers) and colorless spells.
  11. DRC must attack when delirium is active — this is not optional. Delirium ability says "attacks each combat if able." If DRC is under your control and able to attack, it must. This matters when you'd prefer to hold it back as a blocker. Plan around this constraint when delirium is active.
  12. Chain all your spells in a single turn rather than spreading across turns. Three spells in one turn turns your 1/2 Swiftspear into a 4/5, your 1/1 DRC (pre-delirium) into a 4/4, your 1/2 Slickshot into a 7/2. Three spells across three turns makes each creature 2/3 at best. The damage potential is exponentially higher when chained.
  13. Against Sanctifier en-Vec: your blue creatures (DRC, Murktide) are unaffected. Sanctifier protects from black and red sources. DRC and Murktide are blue. They can still be targeted by your blue interaction. They can still deal combat damage to Sanctifier-protected creatures. Pivot to blue damage sources when Sanctifier is down.
  14. Preordain surv 2 then draw — use both surv decisions, don't auto-put both on bottom. Scry 2 means look at top 2 cards, put any number on bottom in any order. If you see a land you need, keep it. If you see a duplicate Bauble, put it on bottom. The decision on each card matters — it's not "take both or leave both."
  15. Unholy Heat at 6 damage kills almost everything with delirium active — it's the premier removal spell in your board. At 6 damage it kills Primeval Titan, Murktide Regent, Archon of Cruelty, Phlage, and every other relevant creature in Modern. But it only hits creatures and planeswalkers — not enchantments (Goblin Bombardment), artifacts, or lands.
  16. Know when to switch from burn clock to Murktide plan. If their life total is above 10 and you've spent your hand, switch to Murktide as the closer. A graveyard with 5+ instants and sorceries means Murktide enters as a 8/8 for essentially free. This is a better clock than trying to Lightning Bolt through a stabilized board.

Non-Negotiable Rules

Rule 01
Track Delirium Every Turn
Know your graveyard card types at all times. DRC is a 1/1 or a 3/3 flier — that's a 12-point power difference over a game. Never be surprised by your own DRC state.
Rule 02
Chain Spells Don't Trickle Them
Three spells in one turn is exponentially better than three spells across three turns. The prowess chain compounds. Always look for the turn you can cast everything at once.
Rule 03
Lava Dart Flashback Costs a Mountain
Not free. You lose a land. Count your lands before every Dart flashback. Never flash it back carelessly in turns 1-3 when you need your mana base for spells.
Rule 04
Consign Targets Triggers Not Spells
Consign counters triggered abilities and colorless spells. Cannot counter Lightning Bolt, Counterspell, or any colored spell. Know your targets before bringing it in.
