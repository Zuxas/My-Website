Chapter 01

Identity &
Philosophy

"Cast spells. All of them. In the right order."— Core Mantra

Izzet Prowess is the highest damage ceiling of any fair deck in Modern. The combination of Monastery Swiftspear, Dragon's Rage Channeler, Slickshot Show-Off, and Cori-Steel Cutter creates explosive turns that other decks simply cannot race. Lava Dart flashing back from the graveyard for free pumps all prowess creatures — creating lethal damage out of nowhere.

The key card to understand entering 2026: Cori-Steel Cutter is the centerpiece two-drop. It creates a 1/1 prowess creature with haste and trample every turn cycle (including the opponent's turn), and the creature can be equipped for trample and extra damage. Described as "an unkillable two-mana Monastery Mentor that's on color." It doesn't require deckbuilding changes — it slots into the existing creature suite.

Axis 01
DRC > Swiftspear (T1 Priority)
Always lead DRC over Swiftspear when you have the choice. You want your non-haste threat attacking as soon as possible. DRC with delirium becomes a 3/3 flying surveiller — close games 2 turns faster than a 1/1.
Axis 02
Cori-Steel Cutter
Creates a 1/1 prowess creature with haste and trample on your turn AND their turn. Equipping to a freshly summoned creature gives surprise haste attackers. Treat it as an unkillable threat generator.
Axis 03
Lava Dart Engine
Lava Dart flashback costs zero mana, triggers prowess on all creatures, and sacrifices a land already used. On the kill turn, flashback after every other spell for maximum pump. Never cast it early for damage.
Axis 04
The Kill Turn
The whole deck is built around one explosive turn. Sequence: filter spells first → free spells (Gut Shot, Lava Dart) → pump → burn. Prowess triggers stack — all creatures get +1/+1 per spell cast.
Strengths
What You Do Well
Highest damage ceiling — wins from nowhere.

Cori-Steel Cutter creates threats every turn cycle, can't easily be answered.

DRC with delirium generates card selection while applying pressure.

Unstoppable through most creature removal.
Weaknesses
What Beats You
Deafening Silence, High Noon, Trinisphere — hate pieces shut off the spell chain.

Solitude at instant speed removes your best threat freely.

Inconsistent hands lose fast with no recovery path.

Tournament clock awareness is critical — sequencing must be automatic.

When to pick this: When the RC field is unprepared for the Cori-Steel Cutter angle. When Deafening Silence is not appearing in more than 20% of sideboards. Best in fields with fair creature matchups where your speed advantage compounds.

Chapter 03

The Game Plan

"Know the kill math before declaring attackers. Never attack without knowing if you can close."— Key Principle

Getting to Delirium

DRC with delirium is a 3/3 flying surveiller. Getting to 4 card types fast is the priority: land + instant + creature + artifact (Bauble). Use DRC's upkeep surveil ability to set up your draw. Cast noncreature spells in your upkeep to surveil towards whatever you need for the turn.

Violent Urge timing: Checks delirium on resolution — if you cast it without delirium but DRC is in play, you can turn on delirium after cast but before resolution with a free spell. Conversely, if they disrupt the stack and you lose delirium, the creature won't get double strike.

Lava Dart Rules

Lava Dart is never cast early for damage unless you need to bin it to enable delirium. It's held for the kill turn where flashback costs zero mana and pumps every prowess creature. Cast all other spells first, then flashback Dart at the end for the final pump. Remember: the opponent can respond to Dart on the stack before you flashback — if they exile your graveyard, Dart won't have a flashback available.

Cori-Steel Cutter Sequencing

You don't always have to equip the new Monk. Sometimes the currently equipped creature having trample is more important than equipping the hasty Monk. Double-spelling at your opponent's end step to create an additional Monk is often game-winning. Plot Slickshot on one turn, cast on the next to dodge interaction.

Mulligan

Keep: Any hand with a prowess creature + 2 lands + 2+ spells including at least one free or one-mana spell. 1-land hands with DRC + Bauble are keepable — Bauble finds the second land and enables delirium.

Mulligan: No creatures, 3+ lands, hands without T2 damage potential.

Chapter 04

Role Assignment

Prowess is always the beatdown. No exceptions. Even against faster aggro, your plan is to race.

vs Everything
Beatdown
Apply pressure from turn 1. Every turn they don't take damage they're stabilizing. Force them to have removal — don't slow down to play around it.
vs Aggro Mirrors
Race, Don't Trade
Don't trade creatures in aggro mirrors. Your reach through burn and Lava Dart is better than theirs. Race through damage rather than managing the board.
vs Control
Go Under
Apply pressure before Verdict mana is online. Cori-Steel Cutter on an empty board after a Verdict creates threats faster than they can answer them.
vs Combo
Your Clock = Their Pressure
You don't need to interact with most combo decks — just kill them first. Consign to Memory is your interaction piece where you can't race.

Chapter 06

Sideboard Guide

Consign to Memory — 4 copies
The best sideboard tool for any blue deck in the format. Provides game against all Eldrazi decks (stops cast triggers), combo decks, and stack-based interaction that can't be raced. This is the reason Izzet splashes blue. Board in liberally against anything that requires stack interaction.
Into the Flood Maw — 2 copies
Bounces a permanent at instant speed. Primary use: Sanctifier en-Vec (shuts off Lava Dart flashback and Lightning Bolt) and Trinisphere. Bounce Sanctifier after they tap to cast it — take the turn back and continue the aggro plan.
Mystical Dispute / Flusterstorm — 2 each
Blue counterspells for blue matchups. Dispute answers anything blue for U in the control matchup. Flusterstorm stops storm counts, Cascade triggers, and counterspell wars for one mana. Board both in against any blue deck with counterspells.
Surgical Extraction — 2 copies
Boards in against graveyard decks (Glockulous, Goryo's) and can hit key combo pieces. Lower priority than Consign. Don't bring in speculatively — know what you're extracting before boarding.
Chapter 07

Heuristics &
Quick Rules

  1. Always lead DRC over Swiftspear on turn 1. You want your non-haste threat attacking as soon as possible.
  2. Lava Dart is never cast for damage. It is held for the kill turn to flashback for zero mana. Only exception: casting to bin it for delirium.
  3. Know the kill math before declaring attackers. With DRC delirium + Swiftspear + 4 spells in hand, calculate exactly how much damage you can represent before going to combat.
  4. Cori-Steel Cutter creates a Monk on their turn too. Double-spelling at their end step to create an extra Monk before your untap is often game-winning.
  5. Gut Shot and Mutagenic Growth trigger prowess for free. Cast them proactively on your turn rather than holding as reactive cards — they're offense tools, not defense.
  6. Mishra's Bauble replaces itself and enables delirium. Cast it proactively. 1-land hands with DRC + Bauble are keepable because Bauble finds the second land.
  7. Sanctifier en-Vec shuts off Lava Dart flashback and Lightning Bolt. Into the Flood Maw is your cleanest answer. Know it's in the sideboard and board it for any white matchup that brings Sanctifier.
  8. Deafening Silence, High Noon, and Trinisphere shut off your entire game plan. Your only answers are Spell Pierce and Into the Flood Maw — both conditional. In fields with heavy hate density, consider whether Prowess is the right pick.
  9. The RC clock is your biggest enemy. Know your lines before casting spells. Prowess decisions look complex but should be automatic after sufficient reps.
  10. Violent Urge checks delirium on resolution, not on cast. With DRC in play, you can cast Violent Urge without delirium and then cast a free spell to enable delirium before Urge resolves.
