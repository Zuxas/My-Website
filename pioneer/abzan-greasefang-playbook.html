Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"Traverse finds Greasefang. Grisly Salvage puts Parhelion in the GY. Esika's Chariot grinds when they answer the combo. Three axes, one deck."

The plan is brutally simple: mill Parhelion II into your graveyard using Grisly Salvage, Witherbloom Command, or Vessel of Nascency on turns 1–2, then cast Greasefang, Okiba Boss on turn 3. Greasefang’s ETB returns Parhelion II from your GY to the battlefield, crewing it immediately — you attack for 13 in the air and leave behind two 4/4 flying vigilance Angel tokens. Most opponents cannot survive this sequence.

What makes Abzan superior to other Greasefang variants is the green toolbox. Traverse the Ulvenwald finds Greasefang directly once you achieve Delirium (trivial with your self-mill), turning every Traverse into a one-mana tutor for your combo piece. Overlord of the Balemurk does everything: mills on ETB, returns Greasefang from the GY, threatens as a late-game body, and enables Delirium with two card types. The green splash gives you consistency that no other Greasefang shell can match.

Esika's Chariot is the backup vehicle. When you discard a card while the Chariot is in play, it creates Cat tokens. Greasefang can reanimate the Chariot instead of Parhelion II, letting the Chariot attack while generating more tokens. Against GY hate decks, you can sometimes go wide on Cat tokens without ever needing the GY at all.

Why It's #2 at ~11%

Speed
T3 Kill Is Real
Eight self-mill effects across four cards (Overlord of the Balemurk, Raffine's Informant, Vessel of Nascency, plus Grisly Salvage mill). The combo reliably assembles by turn 3. With Traverse consistency, you can threaten a turn-2 Greasefang in nut draws. No other Pioneer deck presents a T3 13-damage attack this consistently.
Resilience
Two-Axis Threat
Against GY hate: pivot to Esika's Chariot Cat tokens + Chariot grind. Against creature hate: Parhelion's Angels close from the air. Against hand disruption: Thoughtseize protects Greasefang. You're not a one-trick combo deck — you have a legitimate midrange backup that wins games independently.
Disruption
Thoughtseize + Fatal Push
The best interaction package in Pioneer. Thoughtseize answers anything before it resolves. Fatal Push hits every relevant creature at one mana. Thoughtseize gives one of your key pieces protection from a color. You're proactive AND interactive — opponents can't just goldfish past you; you're stripping their answers while assembling your combo.

How the Combo Kills

Step 1 — T1/T2
Discard Parhelion II
T1: Cast Overlord of the Balemurk or Raffine's Informant. Use their activated/ETB ability to discard Parhelion II to GY. Or T2: Traverse the Ulvenwald generates mana when you discard — discard Parhelion II for both the GY setup AND midrange value. Parhelion must be in GY before Greasefang resolves.
Step 2 — T3
Cast Greasefang, Attack for 13
Greasefang, Okiba Boss ETB: return a Vehicle from your GY to the battlefield under your control — it gains haste. Parhelion II enters with haste. Greasefang (power 4) crews it (crew 4). Attack with the 9/9 Parhelion II: deal damage + create two 4/4 Angel tokens with flying and vigilance. Parhelion II returns to GY at EOT. Angels stay.
Step 3 — T4
Angels + Repeat
Two 4/4 Angels with flying and vigilance attack for 8. If Parhelion is back in GY (it returned at EOT), cast Greasefang again for another 13-damage swing. Total over two turns: 13 + 8 + 13 = 34 potential damage. Most opponents are at 0 by turn 4. The Angels also block during their turn since they have vigilance.
Plan B — Chariot Grind
Chariot Grind Without GY
Esika's Chariot enters with two 2/2 Cats. On attack, copy any token — including 4/4 Angels from a prior Parhelion hit. With your mill spells in hand, flood the board with Cats in a single turn. This plan bypasses all GY hate — no GY needed, just discards. Against Hearse or RIP, pivot immediately to this gameplan games 2–3.

Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Greasefang reanimates on ETB — it doesn't target. There's no window to 'respond to the vehicle entering' once Greasefang resolves."
Line 01 — Parhelion II Returns to GY at EOT — Set Up T4 Repeat
Parhelion II is sacrificed at the beginning of your EOT after Greasefang reanimates it. It goes back to your GY — not exiled. This means if you cast a second Greasefang next turn (or replay the same one), you can reanimate Parhelion again. This is the full kill: T3 Greasefang (13 damage + 2 Angels created), T4 the two 4/4 Angels attack (8 damage) + second Greasefang reanimates Parhelion again (9 more damage). Plan for this line rather than just hoping the first swing kills.
Line 02 — Thoughtseize Gives Protection After Resolution, Not Before
Thoughtseize enchants a creature and gives it protection from a chosen color. This means: (1) existing targeted removal resolves before you can enchant, (2) you must name the color BEFORE they choose to remove your creature. The correct play: anticipate their removal color (black vs Fatal Push, red vs Lightning Axe) and preemptively Vessel the creature before they can use it. Don't wait for them to cast — name the color you expect and Vessel early. Also: Vessel gives protection which means the creature can no longer be targeted by that color. Enchantments of that color fall off. Very strong against black removal and red burn.
Line 03 — Greasefang's ETB Doesn't Target — It's a May Effect On Resolution
Greasefang says "when Greasefang enters, you may return target Vehicle card from your GY to the battlefield with haste." This is a triggered ability that goes on the stack when Greasefang enters. Opponents can respond to Greasefang before the trigger resolves (kill Greasefang, exile your GY). However: once the trigger resolves, the vehicle enters with haste — there's no additional trigger to respond to. The common mistake: thinking they can "respond to the vehicle entering." They cannot. If the trigger resolves, Parhelion II is on the battlefield.
Line 04 — Esika's Chariot Can Be Reanimated as a Blocker, Not Just an Attacker
Greasefang can reanimate Esika's Chariot instead of Parhelion II. The Chariot gets haste, you crew it (crew cost is low), and it attacks. More importantly: in defensive scenarios, reanimating the Chariot and using it as a blocker via Greasefang's ETB is legitimate. Chariot reanimates, you don't have to crew it immediately — you can hold it. It also creates Cats on your discard triggers during the same turn. Use this line when Parhelion is exiled or unavailable, or when you need a blocker more than an attacker.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"You are the fastest combo deck in the room. You also have the best hand disruption. Play accordingly."
vs Aggro (UR Cutter, RDW)
Race — Priority on Speed
Against UR Cutter: they kill on T3–4, you kill on T3. You must go first or match their speed. Don't slow down to use Fatal Push — only kill creatures that would block Greasefang or prevent your attack. Dead aim: assemble the combo as fast as possible. If you're on the draw and they have a board by T2, Parhelion's first strike blocks favorably, buying turns. The 13-damage swing + two 4/4 Angels racing their 1/1 Monks usually wins.
vs Control (UW Control)
Force Countermagic
Against UW Control: they counter Greasefang. Board in Thoughtseize + Duress — strip their counterspell before you cast Greasefang. The correct play: T2 Thoughtseize to see their hand, take their counter or sweeper. T3 Greasefang resolves uncountered. Thoughtseize on Greasefang after it resolves makes it very difficult to remove. If they sweep, re-reanimate next turn — Parhelion is back in GY.
vs Midrange (Rakdos, Selesnya)
Combo + Interaction
Against midrange: use Fatal Push + Thoughtseize to clear the path for Greasefang. Their removal (Fatal Push, Push) answers your creatures, not your vehicles — Parhelion in GY is safe from most hate. Strip their key disruption pieces (Graveyard Trespasser, their hate bears) before combo turn. The midrange clock is slower than your combo, so you have turns to interact first.

HEURISTICS

"Thoughtseize before Greasefang. Always. No exceptions."
  1. 01Thoughtseize the turn before Greasefang, never the same turn. If you Thoughtseize on T2 and cast Greasefang on T3, you know exactly what interaction they have. If you wait and try to do both on T3, you either spend mana awkwardly or resolve Greasefang into unknown countermagic. T2 Thoughtseize → T3 Greasefang is the correct sequencing 90% of the time.
  2. 02Discard Parhelion II as early as possible — don't hold it. Parhelion II in your hand is dead weight. You cannot cast it from hand (9 mana). It only matters in the GY. The moment you have a self-mill effect, put Parhelion in the GY. Even if you don't have Greasefang yet — Parhelion in GY is always better than Parhelion in hand.
  3. 03Thoughtseize protects from targeted removal, not board wipes. Name black vs Fatal Push/Dreadbore/Thoughtseize. Name red vs Lightning Strike/Fiery Impulse. Name white vs Fatally Push effects. Vessel does NOT protect from Supreme Verdict (doesn't target) or exile-all effects. Pre-name the color based on their mana base: Godless Shrine = name black, Steam Vents = name red.
  4. 04Traverse the Ulvenwald + Traverse toolbox — find Greasefang with Traverse once you have Delirium. The Boseiju, Who Endures enters as a land and can tap for any color. Cast Traverse targeting Greasefang once Delirium is activee turn. Now each discard adds {2} instead of {1}. This acceleration changes your curve by a full turn. Always check if Gardens is in play when you're about to discard.
  5. 05Parhelion II returns to GY at YOUR EOT — plan for turn 4 repeat. After Greasefang's attack, Parhelion returns to GY at your EOT. It's not exiled — it's ready for another Greasefang ETB. Track whether a second Greasefang or a second cast is achievable. Two Greasefang attacks total 26 damage; the Angels contribute 8. You rarely need both — know exactly when the game is over.
  6. 06Against GY hate, pivot immediately — don't try to fight through it. If they have Rest in Peace, Grafdigger's Cage, or Unlicensed Hearse active, your GY plan is off. Pivot to Esika's Chariot Cats or Duress (SB) plan immediately. Don't spend resources trying to remove their hate piece when you have a fully functional Plan B that doesn't require the GY at all.
  7. 07Vessel of Nascency's protection ability can be activated at instant speed. Vessel of Nascency can discard a card to gain protection from a chosen color until EOT — at instant speed, at any priority window. Use this to dodge removal being cast on it, or to trigger Esika's Chariot on their turn. A Spirit with protection from black is very difficult for Rakdos to answer. Don't just use it to discard on your main phase — use it reactively.
  8. 08Esika's Chariot Cat triggers on EACH discard, not per turn. Each self-mill effect activation triggers Chariot separately. Overlord of the Balemurk (discard 1) + Informant connive (discard 1) + Vessel of Nascency (discard 1) = three Cat tokens in one turn. With your mill package available, you can flood the board in a single turn. Count your available discard activations before deciding whether to pivot to the Cat token plan.
  9. 09Geier Reach Sanitarium loots at instant speed for both players — use it at EOT. The Sanitarium activates at instant speed and makes BOTH players loot. Use it on your EOT after you've done everything else — your opponent gets no benefit from the card they draw before your untap. Also: you can use it to discard Parhelion II if your self-mill effects aren't available. The fact it also triggers Traverse the Ulvenwald makes it a dual-purpose utility land.
  10. 10Takenuma, Abandoned Mire channels to return a legendary from GY — gets Greasefang back. Channel Takenuma: discard it and pay {2}{B} to return a legendary creature from your GY to hand. If Greasefang is killed, channeling Takenuma recovers it for the combo next turn. This is the most important utility land activation — know its cost ({2}{B}) and have it available when Greasefang is in your GY.

TUNING
NOTES

"4 Greasefang + 4 Parhelion + 8 self-mill effects is the locked core. Everything else is adjustable."
Lock — Never Cut
4 Greasefang, Okiba Boss
The entire engine. Finding it consistently on T3 requires four copies. Running three introduces too much variance — games where you don't find it by T4 are almost always losses. Four copies means you find it in your opening 7 about 40% of the time and by turn 3 in the vast majority of games.
Lock — Never Cut
4 Parhelion II
You must have Parhelion in your GY when Greasefang ETBs. Four copies plus twelve self-mill effects plus Geier Reach Sanitarium ensures Parhelion is in the GY by T3 consistently. Running three risks games where you have Greasefang but no Parhelion to target — an embarrassing failure state.
Lock — Never Cut
8 Discard Outlets (4+4)
Overlord of the Balemurk + Raffine's Informant form the base. Both are independently powerful two-drops that also crew vehicles and have relevant stats. The two-drop slot must have eight copies to guarantee a T1–T2 self-mill effect in opening hands consistently. Cutting to six creates too many slow hands.
Flex 2–4
Traverse the Ulvenwald
Four copies is standard — the mana acceleration is excellent and it's a payoff for discards you're doing anyway. In very aggro-heavy metas where the midrange plan is irrelevant and speed is everything, trim to two. In slower metas where Chariot's grind plan and the transformational SB plan are valuable, keep four.
Flex 2–4
Esika's Chariot
Four copies is correct in any meta with significant GY hate. If GY hate is light, trim to two — it's the fourth most important piece and draws awkwardly in multiples. Always keep at least two for the backup Cat token plan against hate-heavy opponents.
Register When
Midrange-Light Metas
Greasefang is best when the meta is control or aggro — no disruption to your GY and you race them. It's worst when Rakdos Midrange is over 15% (Thoughtseize + Graveyard Trespasser is a brutal combination). If Rakdos is dominant, shift to a different Pioneer strategy. Otherwise Greasefang is correct at ~11% meta share.
Rule 01
Discard Timing Protocol
You discard Parhelion II on T2 via your self-mill effect (Bitter Triumph, Blood token, Conspiracy Theorist). You never discard Parhelion if they have Soul-Guide Lantern mana open — wait until they tap for something else. You check their available mana before committing Parhelion to GY.
Rule 02
Greasefang Blink Line
When they have removal available, you protect Greasefang with Ephemerate or Selfless Spirit. Greasefang leaving play means Parhelion returns to GY — the ETB trigger doesn't fire again until Greasefang re-enters. You hold your protection piece and cast it in response to their removal on Greasefang.
Rule 03
Go Blank Timing
You cast Go Blank (discard 2 + exile their GY) after they've deployed key pieces to their GY but before they can use them. Against UW Control: cast after they've drawn multiple turns to strip their hand. Against mirror: cast after they've discarded Parhelion to exile it permanently. You time Go Blank for maximum impact.
Rule 04
Knight of Autumn Targets
You target their GY hate with Knight of Autumn at instant speed. Lantern activating: respond with Knight of Autumn before the exile resolves. Rest in Peace in play: Knight of Autumn exiles it and their GY reappears. You accept the 3 life cost for instant-speed exile — it's always worth it to enable your combo.
Tune 01
Thoughtseize vs Duress Ratio
You run Thoughtseize (any nonland, 2 life) in metas where creatures threaten you (Sheoldred, Kalitas, combo creatures). You run Duress (nonland noncreature, 0 life) in metas dominated by noncreature answers (counters, board wipes). In mixed metas: split 3/2 Thoughtseize/Duress. The decision depends on whether creatures or spells threaten your combo more.
Tune 02
Knight of Autumn vs Fatal Push
Knight of Autumn (instant exile any nonland permanent, 3 life) removes any permanent including enchantments. Fatal Push (destroy creature MV ≤ 2 or ≤ 4 with revolt) only hits creatures. You run Knight of Autumn when GY hate enchantments (Rest in Peace) are common. You run Push when their key targets are creatures. In most metas: run both.
Discard Timing
Never Into Open Lantern Mana
You never discard Parhelion II when your opponent has Soul-Guide Lantern mana ({1}) open. You wait until they tap for a spell, then discard. You count their available mana before discarding Parhelion — one wrong discard permanently exiles your win condition.
Greasefang Protection
Hold Your Answer
When Greasefang is on the stack or in play: you hold your protection piece (Ephemerate, Selfless Spirit) specifically for their removal. You cast protection in response to their removal on Greasefang. Greasefang leaving play before it ETBs means no Parhelion return.
Knight of Autumn Priority
Answer Their Permanent Hate
When they deploy a permanent that shuts off your combo (Rest in Peace, Grafdigger's Cage): you immediately Knight of Autumn it. You never wait — the longer their hate is in play, the more likely they sequence additional answers. Knight of Autumn immediately is always correct.
Thoughtseize Window
T1 Information Advantage
You Thoughtseize on T1 in game 1 to identify their GY hate cards. If they have Soul-Guide Lantern: take it and discard Parhelion on T2. If they don't: discard Parhelion on T2 anyway (faster combo). You make the discard decision AFTER seeing their hand.
Go Blank Timing
Wait for Full GY
You cast Go Blank (discard 2 + exile their GY simultaneously) when their GY has their key card already. Against mirror: wait until they've discarded Parhelion, then Go Blank to exile it permanently. Against UW Control: cast when their hand is large enough to hurt from the 2-card discard.

20-Minute Crash Course

If you're sitting down in 20 minutes, run through this in order:

  • Mulligan rule: Keep any 7 with a self-mill effect + Parhelion II OR Greasefang + a way to discard Parhelion. Never keep a hand without the full combo within sight.
  • Combo rule: Discard Parhelion II to GY on T2 (via Conspiracy Theorist, Thirst for Discovery, etc.), then cast Greasefang on T3. Greasefang crews Parhelion (a vehicle) using its power — Parhelion swings for 13 on T3.
  • Greasefang ETB rule: Greasefang enters and returns a Vehicle from your GY to play. Parhelion II returns and immediately taps to be crewed by Greasefang (power 2). Parhelion requires 4 power to crew — two Greasefangs work.
  • Soul-Guide Lantern counter: Their Soul-Guide Lantern exiling your GY at instant speed removes Parhelion permanently. Watch for Lantern and plan around it — don't discard Parhelion into a Lantern.
  • Thoughtseize rule: Thoughtseize on T1 strips their GY hate (Lantern, Rest in Peace) before you discard Parhelion. Always Thoughtseize before establishing your GY if you see them hold up interaction.
  • Board rule: Knight of Autumn (instant exile, lose 3 life) is your answer to any permanent they've deployed that blocks your combo. Use it on their Lantern or Rest in Peace.
