Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"You never tap out. Every turn ends with mana available. Every threat they present gets answered. When Teferi resolves, the game is functionally over."

UW Control is Pioneer's premier permission-and-sweeper control deck. The plan is consistent regardless of the matchup: answer every early threat with removal, hold up counterspells every turn, sweep the board when they overcommit, and win by resolving Teferi, Hero of Dominaria and protecting it until the ultimate ends the game. No other deck in Pioneer can match the card advantage Teferi generates each turn.

The interaction package is layered: March of Otherworldly Light removes early threats at instant speed for any converted mana cost. Get Lost removes anything and gives them two Clue tokens (which is fine — you'll kill them before they draw enough to matter). No More Lies is the soft counterspell of choice — a {1}{U} Mana Leak that stops everything early when opponents are mana-constrained. Dovin's Veto is the hard counter for critical spells you cannot afford to let resolve.

Temporary Lockdown is the deck's answer to wide boards — it exiles all nonland permanents with MV 2 or less until it leaves the battlefield. Against UR Cutter's Monk tokens, Selesnya Company's elves, and RDW's one-drops, Lockdown is a sweeper that doesn't need to wait until turn 4. Supreme Verdict is the uncounterable true sweeper for larger boards. The win conditions are all planeswalkers: Teferi closes long games via ultimate, The Wandering Emperor stabilizes at instant speed, and Elspeth, Storm Slayer provides resilient pressure.

Why UW Control At 9% Pioneer

Inevitability
Teferi Wins Every Long Game
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria draws a card and untaps two lands every turn. In a control shell, this means you draw a card and still have two mana available for a counterspell. No fair deck in Pioneer can outgrind this engine. Once Teferi resolves and survives a turn, your hand size and mana advantage grow exponentially.
Layered Defense
Every Threat Has an Answer
Temporary Lockdown for go-wide. Supreme Verdict for go-tall. March of Otherworldly Light for individual threats. No More Lies for spells on the stack. Dovin's Veto for must-counter spells. Farewell for GY-based strategies. Each layer addresses a different axis of attack — the deck has no unwinnable matchup.
Flash Threats
Wandering Emperor at Instant Speed
The Wandering Emperor is a flash planeswalker — you can cast it at any time. This means you never need to tap out on your own turn. Deploy Wandering Emperor at end of their turn, untap, and continue holding up interaction. The Emperor creates tokens, exiles tapped attackers, and protects your life total without ever requiring you to spend your turn proactively.

Key Card Notes

The Engine
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria
5 mana. +1: draw a card, untap two lands. This means after playing Teferi you can +1 and still leave {U}{U} up for a counterspell. Every subsequent turn: draw a card AND maintain countermagic. -3: put a nonland permanent on top of its owner's library — answers anything that slipped through. Ultimate: each turn exile 3 target permanents. Games where Teferi untaps are almost unlosable.
The Flash Threat
The Wandering Emperor
4 mana Flash. +1: creates a 2/2 vigilance Samurai token. +1 (alternate): put a +1/+1 counter and first strike on target creature. -2: exile target tapped creature, gain 2 life. Cast at instant speed at end of their turn. Never tap out for this — it functions exactly like passing the turn with a threat ready. Against aggro the exile mode stabilizes the board. Against control the token threatens lethal.
The Sweeper
Temporary Lockdown vs Supreme Verdict
Temporary Lockdown: 3 mana enchantment, exiles all nonland permanents with MV 2 or less until it leaves. Cheaper, hits non-creatures, and handles token boards. Use for wide aggro. Supreme Verdict: 4 mana sorcery, destroys all creatures, can't be countered. Use for larger creature threats or when you need an uncounterable effect. Know which to cast when — Lockdown is usually correct against most Pioneer decks.
The Hard Counter
Dovin's Veto vs No More Lies
No More Lies ({1U}) is your soft counter — counters unless they pay {3}. Perfect for early game when mana is tight. Dovin's Veto ({WU}) is the hard counter — counters and can't be countered itself. Reserve Dovin's Veto for: Teferi-level threats, their sweepers vs your board, combo-critical spells (Greasefang ETB trigger). No More Lies is the free-roll counter; Dovin's Veto is the nuclear button.
Chapter 03

GAME
FLOW

"T1-T4: survive and answer threats. T5: Teferi with counterspell backup. T6+: their resources are gone."
Early (T1–T3)
Survival Phase
Your only goal in turns 1-3 is to not die. March of Otherworldly Light removes early threats at any mana cost. Consult the Star Charts draws cards and sculpts your hand. Temporary Lockdown on T3 sweeps MV-2 or less permanents. Never commit a threat — hold all your mana for answers. The opponent is trying to establish pressure; you're answering everything.
Mid (T4–T5)
Transition to Control
T4: Supreme Verdict or Temporary Lockdown wipes whatever survived. Wandering Emperor at end of their T4 stabilizes. T5: Teferi with a counterspell backup in hand — this is your target state. Playing Teferi at 5 mana while holding No More Lies means: deploy Teferi, they try to answer it, you counter their answer. Teferi survives and your lead is permanent.
Late (T6+)
Inevitability Phase
Once Teferi is active: +1 every turn draws a card AND leaves 2 mana up. Each turn your hand grows, your options expand, and their resources deplete. Use Teferi -3 to send their threats back to their library one at a time. If they ever cast a creature, Wandering Emperor exiles it tapped. Memory Deluge at instant speed refuels if Teferi dies. The game ends when Teferi ultimates or they concede.
Mulligan
Need: Land + Interaction + Draw
Keep: 3-4 lands, at least 2 interaction pieces, at least 1 draw spell. Mulligan: fewer than 3 lands (you can't play on curve), 0 interaction pieces (you die to aggro), or hands with 2+ Teferi and no way to survive to T5. You can keep 2 lands if both are dual lands and you have Consult the Star Charts to find more. At 6: keep 3 lands + 2 interaction. At 5: 3 lands + 1 instant-speed removal + 1 counterspell.
Mid (T4–T6)
Deploy Your Threat
Your T4 play is Teferi, Hero of Dominaria. You plus Teferi, untap two lands, and immediately have mana open for interaction on their turn. Teferi generates card advantage (+1 draws a card) while keeping counterspells available. Your T5 is Absorb if they threaten to resolve something critical, or another Teferi activation for another draw. You win by generating more cards than they have answers.
Late (T7+)
Seal the Game
You win in the late game by having more interaction in hand than they have threats in theirs. Supreme Verdict refills your threat count from zero. Brimaz, King of Oreskos creates Cat tokens each time it attacks or blocks — your board goes wide faster than they can answer individual pieces. You close with Brimaz beats while holding Dovin's Veto for their board wipe attempt.
Mana Management
Open Mana on Their Turn
Your most important skill is managing your mana to hold interaction open on their turn. Leave exactly the mana you need for your most relevant counter: {W}{U} for Dovin's Veto, {1}{W}{U} for Absorb, {3}{W}{W} for Supreme Verdict. Never tap out unless you're winning immediately. Your power comes from untapped mana — never waste it.
Threat Assessment
What Are You Answering?
Before each of their turns, ask: what are the three most dangerous things they could cast? Know your answer for each. If they cast A, you counter. If they cast B, you march it. If they cast C, you Verdict. Having a pre-planned answer for their top 3 plays means you never hesitate when they tap mana.
Chapter 04

CORE
ENGINES

"Defense layer, sweeper layer, draw engine, win condition. Four systems. None fails if the others are running."
Engine 1 — Removal Suite
Spot + Sweeper Layering
March of Otherworldly Light exiles at instant speed. Get Lost answers anything at sorcery speed for 2 mana. Temporary Lockdown blanks boards at 3 mana. Supreme Verdict wipes boards at 4 mana. Farewell is the reset button for GY and artifact-heavy strategies. Each answers a different threat width — never be caught with the wrong tool by understanding each card's specific role.
Engine 2 — Permission Suite
Hard + Soft Countermagic
No More Lies counters early (soft — {1U}, unless pay {3}). Dovin's Veto counters critical spells (hard — {WU}, can't be countered). Three Steps Ahead is flexible: copy, counter, or draw 2. Together they cover every threat type: early creatures, high-impact spells, and uncounterable threats (Dovin's Veto handles their Dovin's Veto; Supreme Verdict can't be countered back). Always know which counter to use for each threat.
Engine 3 — Draw Engine
Consult + Memory Deluge + Teferi
Consult the Star Charts at 2 mana filters draws. Memory Deluge at 4 mana (or flashback for 5) draws 3-4 cards. Teferi draws a card AND untaps 2 lands every turn. The draw engine ensures you never run out of answers — every turn your hand replenishes while opponents' hands empty. At Teferi mana you're typically drawing more per turn than you're spending.
Engine: Teferi
Your Card Advantage Loop
Teferi, Hero of Dominaria generates +1 card per activation while untapping two lands for free. Your loop: Teferi plus → draw a card → untap two lands → use those lands to hold Dovin's Veto open → their turn ends → repeat. You generate one card per turn cycle while maintaining interaction capability. You protect Teferi with Dovin's Veto on their answers to him.
Engine: Dovin's Veto
Your Uncounterable Shield
Dovin's Veto ({W}{U}) is uncounterable. You use it to protect your key plays from their counters and to stop their key noncreature spells. You hold it specifically for Supreme Verdict (their board wipe), Indomitable Creativity (their combo), or their most impactful noncreature answer. You never use Veto on a creature spell — Absorb handles those for 3 life gain.
Engine: Brimaz
Your Self-Generating Army
Brimaz, King of Oreskos creates a 1/1 Cat with vigilance every time he attacks or blocks. You attack every turn with Brimaz and generate one Cat token per combat. After 3 attacks: Brimaz 3/4 (with vigilance, untaps after attack) + three 1/1 Cats. Your board multiplies without additional card investment. You protect Brimaz with Dovin's Veto on their board wipe.
Engine: Approach
Your Alt Win Condition
Approach of the Second Sun ({6}{W}): you cast it, pay 7 life, it goes to your library 7 from the top. Next turn you draw 7 cards down, find Approach, cast it again — you win instantly. Your Approach line: survive to T7 (hold interaction throughout), cast Approach with Veto backup, survive their response turn, draw and cast Approach again T8. You win on the spot when Approach resolves the second time.
Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Never tap out on your main phase unless casting Teferi with backup. Every other turn ends with mana available."
Line 01 — The Teferi Sequence: Deploy with Counterspell Backup
The ideal Teferi deployment: T5, cast Teferi, hold No More Lies or Dovin's Veto. They must answer Teferi or they lose — and you answer their answer. If they don't have a response, Teferi +1 on T6 draws a card AND leaves {U}{U} up. From this point forward you can counter anything while drawing a card every turn. Never deploy Teferi without backup unless their hand is empty or you're certain they have no answers.
Line 02 — Wandering Emperor Timing: End of Their Turn, Not Your Main Phase
The Wandering Emperor has Flash — never cast it during your main phase. Always wait until the end of their turn. This preserves mana for your turn's counterspells and never wastes a turn on a proactive play when you should be answering. The only exception: using Emperor's -2 to exile an attacking creature that would deal lethal damage. In that case cast it during their combat, before damage.
Line 03 — Temporary Lockdown: Know When It's Wrong
Temporary Lockdown exiles MV-2 or less permanents until Lockdown leaves. This means: (1) if your own Lockdown gets destroyed, everything returns; (2) their MV-3+ permanents survive; (3) your own MV-2 permanents (Beza if in play, Restless Anchorage tokens) are also exiled. The dangerous scenario: you cast Lockdown to clear their board, they destroy it with a card you didn't answer, and everything returns. Always ensure Lockdown survives by having counterspells for their artifact/enchantment removal.
Line 04 — Field of Ruin vs Utility Lands: Force Their Basic Land Draws
Field of Ruin taps for colorless, and {2}: destroy target nonbasic land, then each player searches for a basic. Use it against: Lotus Field (destroys their Lotus before it comes back), Restless lands (creature-lands that can attack you), and Nykthos, Shrine to Nyx. The "each player searches" clause means you also get a basic — you're trading one of your lands for their best land, but replacing yours with a basic that enters tapped. Use it proactively vs combo lands, reactively vs creature-lands.
Line 01
Your T4 Teferi Line
You reach T4 with {1}{U}{U} open and Teferi in hand. You cast Teferi, Hero of Dominaria: draw a card, untap two lands. You immediately have {W}{U} or {1}{U} open for interaction on their turn. You never cast Teferi into open mana — you wait until they've spent their interaction or until you have Dovin's Veto backup.
Line 02
Your Supreme Verdict Line
You hold Supreme Verdict until they have 3+ creatures. You never Verdict into 1-2 creatures. After Verdict resolves, you immediately deploy Brimaz, King of Oreskos — your board goes from 0 to 1 creature with a Cat token the turn he attacks. You follow Brimaz with Dovin's Veto backup for their sweeper response.
Line 03
Your Narset Line
You deploy Narset, Parter of Veils when they are about to draw — their upkeep, or in response to their cantrip. Under Narset: their Opt draws 0 extra cards, their Collected Company hits but draws 0. You follow Narset with a clock (Brimaz) so they must spend removal on Narset instead of advancing their own plan.
Line 04
Your Dovin's Veto Line
You save Dovin's Veto for their uncounterable board wipes (Supreme Verdict from opponents, Anger of the Gods) or their most critical noncreature spell. You never use Veto on a cantrip or a small creature spell — you reserve it for the one spell that wins them the game. Veto is uncounterable itself, so you hold it until it must go on the stack.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"You are always the reactive deck. Every action is a response to their action. Never act without cause."
vs Aggro
Survive to T4 Wrath
Against RDW, Selesnya Company: your goal is staying alive until Temporary Lockdown or Supreme Verdict clears the board. March of Otherworldly Light removes early threats. Beza (SB) gains life. Wandering Emperor exiles key attackers in combat. Your life total is a resource — don't spend it to stop irrelevant damage. A wrath on T4 followed by Teferi on T5 wins the game.
vs Combo
Counter Their Key Piece
Against Greasefang: counter or remove Greasefang itself on the stack (the ETB trigger is the kill). Rest in Peace (SB) answers the GY recursion. Against Lotus Field: Field of Ruin destroys Lotus before it doubles. Get Lost answers Thespian's Stage. High Noon (SB) stops them from untapping lands to generate explosive mana.
vs Midrange/Control
Win the Permission War
Against UR Cutter and Dimir Midrange: play around their interaction by baiting counters on expendable spells before deploying Teferi. Against the UW mirror: the first Teferi to resolve often wins — hold Dovin's Veto for their Teferi, deploy yours when they're tapped out. Wandering Emperor at instant speed functions as a threat that never exposes you to their interaction window.

UR CUTTER

Even

UR Cutter plays fast through cantrips and Treasure Cruise. Temporary Lockdown clears all their Monk tokens and Cutter itself (MV 2). No More Lies counters Stormchaser's Talent (MV 3 after activation cost) — counter it on the stack. Their Treasure Cruise is devastating at instant speed; hold Dovin's Veto for their Cruise or Vivi Ornitier.

Priority: Kill or exile their Cutter before the Otter token engine fires. Temporary Lockdown exiles the Monk tokens and the Cutter (if MV 2) in one sweep. If they've cracked Treasure Cruise, your hand needs to be all interaction — they've seen 3 cards of gas. Deploy Wandering Emperor to pressure their planeswalker loyalty while holding up countermagic.

ORZHOV GREASEFANG

Favorable

Greasefang combo relies on: (1) discarding Parhelion II, (2) casting Greasefang, (3) reanimating Parhelion. Your counters stop any of these steps. March of Otherworldly Light exiles Greasefang before ETB resolves. Get Lost removes Greasefang at instant speed. Rest in Peace (SB) shuts the GY completely — no Parhelion II can be returned. Dovin's Veto is the nuclear button for their key spell.

Priority: Answer Greasefang on the stack before its ETB resolves. Rest in Peace on T2 alongside a land prevents them from setting up. If they've already discarded Parhelion II but haven't cast Greasefang yet, hold removal for when Greasefang enters. Their Angels (4/4 flying vigilance tokens from Parhelion II) are easy targets for Wandering Emperor -2 post-attack since they attack and become tapped.

Lotus Field combo needs Lotus Field + Thespian's Stage to generate 6+ mana for Hidden Strings loops. Field of Ruin destroys either piece early. Brimaz + Caracal + Kaheera

HEURISTICS

"Pass with mana up. Answer on their turn. Deploy on your end step or after their end step."
  1. 01Never tap out on your main phase before T5 unless casting Teferi with backup. Every turn from T1-T4 should end with at least {U} up for a counterspell. If you tap out, you're vulnerable to their most important spell. The deck is built around this principle — every action is reactive until Teferi resolves.
  2. 02Teferi with a counter in hand — that's the target. Teferi alone is not. A resolved Teferi with no backup is one removal spell away from doing nothing. Save a counterspell specifically for the turn you deploy Teferi. No More Lies is the standard — Teferi costs 5, they answer with something 3 or less, you counter for {1U}.
  3. 03Wandering Emperor timing: always end of their turn, never your main phase. The flash ability is the point. Cast it in their end step to create a blocker or threat, then untap with full mana for your turn's counterspells. Casting it on your main phase wastes your turn's interaction window.
  4. 04March of Otherworldly Light can exile any MV at any cost — use it for non-creatures too. March removes enchantments, artifacts, and creatures. It can hit planeswalkers (unlike some removal). It exiles (important vs recursion). You can pay extra white to hit higher MV targets. Don't forget the non-creature applications vs Lotus Field's Thespian's Stage or CoCo matchups.
  5. 05Temporary Lockdown returning permanents is your opponent's primary line vs you. If they can destroy Lockdown, everything comes back. Always hold a counterspell specifically to protect Lockdown after it resolves. Their Naturalize, Abrade, or disenchant effect is the card that wins the game — don't let it resolve.
  6. 06Dovin's Veto is for their Dovin's Veto and their Teferi. Save it. Don't waste Dovin's Veto on a 2-drop creature or a draw spell. Reserve it for: their Teferi, their Dovin's Veto (countering the uncounterable), their Supreme Verdict vs your board, and combo-critical spells. No More Lies handles everything else.
  7. 07Field of Ruin's basic land search triggers for both players — use it early. Field of Ruin forces both players to search for a basic when it destroys a nonbasic. Use it early when they have a utility land (Lotus Field, Restless Anchorage, Nykthos) and you have a basic to spare. Don't wait until late — the tempo of destroying their best land early outweighs the slight mana cost.
  8. 08Supreme Verdict can't be countered — deploy it freely when sweeping is correct. Unlike Temporary Lockdown, Supreme Verdict doesn't care about their counterspells. If you're in a mirror and they have a board with Teferi, Supreme Verdict is the trump that can't be answered. Time it for when they're tapped out to also deploy a threat the same turn.
  9. 09Memory Deluge at instant speed — use it end of their turn to sculpt your hand. Memory Deluge is best cast at the end of their turn when they passed without action. You see X+3 cards, put X-1 back. This ensures your next turn starts with maximum information and the best possible cards. Don't cast it in your main phase unless you need it immediately.
  10. 10In the mirror: the first Teferi to resolve wins. Hold Dovin's Veto for their Teferi specifically. Mirror matches are decided by who resolves Teferi first. Both players know this, so they hold countermagic. The line: bait their counters on an expendable spell, then deploy Teferi with Dovin's Veto backup. Dovin's Veto into their Dovin's Veto on your Teferi is the key interaction pattern.

NOTES

"4 No More Lies and 4 March of Otherworldly Light are the locks. Everything else adjusts to meta."
Lock — Never Cut
4 March of Otherworldly Light
The most flexible removal spell in Pioneer — instant, any MV, exiles. Hits creatures, enchantments, artifacts, and planeswalkers. Never cut below 4. The exile clause is critical vs GY strategies.
Lock — Never Cut
4 No More Lies
The best early counterspell available. Counters anything for {1U} when opponents are mana-constrained (turns 1-4). Never cut to fewer than 4 — your early permission suite depends on it.
Lock — Never Cut
2 Teferi, Hero of Dominaria
Your primary win condition and draw engine. 2 copies ensures you draw it in most games without flooding. 3 copies is too many — Teferi is dead in hand before T5 and bad when you draw multiples.
Flex 2–3
Dovin's Veto
2 main is correct for most metas. In heavy-blue or mirror-heavy metas, move to 3 main. In aggro-heavy metas, trim to 1 and move the rest to sideboard where they arrive with a plan.
Flex 2–3
Temporary Lockdown
3 main in aggro-heavy metas. 2 in balanced metas. The card is too narrow vs control and combo to run 4, but too powerful vs creature decks to run fewer than 2.
Meta Call
High Noon
Main-deck when Lotus Field is popular (prevents the land-untap combo). Board it out in control-heavy metas where it does nothing. The main deck 1 is a hedge — if you never face Lotus, it's a low-impact card that at worst draws off Consult.
Rule 01
Mana Open Discipline
You leave the correct mana open on their turn every turn from T3 onward. T3+: hold {W}{U} for Dovin's Veto. T4+: hold {1}{W}{U} for Absorb. T5+: hold {3}{W}{W} for Supreme Verdict. You pre-plan which counter you're holding before their turn starts. You tap out ONLY when you're winning immediately.
Rule 02
Supreme Verdict Threshold
You cast Supreme Verdict at exactly 3 creatures. Below 3: use smaller answers (March of Otherworldly Light, Absorb). At 3: Verdict. You enforce this threshold strictly. Verdicting into 1-2 creatures wastes 4 mana on inefficient removal.
Rule 03
Brimaz Bravery
You attack with Brimaz every turn, regardless of their board. Brimaz 3/4 with vigilance (untaps after attack) creates a 1/1 Cat on attack. You attack into their 3/3 — Brimaz blocks back with vigilance. After 3 turns of Brimaz attacks: you have 3 Cat tokens and Brimaz untapped. You never hold Brimaz back.
Rule 04
Dovin's Veto Reserve
You save Dovin's Veto for the specific spell that wins them the game. You identify that spell before T1. Against Lotus Field: Veto on Pore Over the Pages. Against Greasefang: Veto on Greasefang the spell. Against RDW: Veto is wrong (they cast creatures) — use March instead. You match Veto to the correct target.

Know Cold
Counter Priority Hierarchy
Use No More Lies on: early creatures and spells when opponent has limited mana. Use Dovin's Veto on: Teferi (mirror), their Dovin's Veto, combo kill spell, sweeper that would destroy your board. Use Three Steps Ahead on: copy a planeswalker ability if you need its effect, counter if flexible, or draw 2 in late game. Never waste Dovin's Veto on a creature.
