Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"The plan is simple: get Phoenixes in the graveyard early, cast three cheap spells in a single turn, and attack with 3/2 haste fliers that come back every turn. Your opponent needs exile effects. Your spells reload you faster than they can answer."

Arclight Phoenix is Pioneer's premier GY-value combo-tempo deck. The namesake card — Arclight Phoenix — is a 3/2 flying haste creature that returns from the graveyard to the battlefield at the beginning of combat if you've cast three or more instant/sorcery spells during your turn. The deck runs 12 one-mana cantrips (Consider, Opt, Sleight of Hand) specifically to enable this recursion consistently from turn 3 onward.

The modern version of the deck runs a dual threat structure: Cori-Steel Cutter provides a secondary win condition that doesn't require the GY. When Cutter transforms into an equipment and you cast spells, an Otter Monk 1/1 token enters each time. Artist's Talent enables both threats simultaneously — each spell you cast while it's in play loots (discard, then draw), filling your GY with Phoenixes and churning through the deck for gas.

Treasure Cruise is the engine that makes the whole deck absurd. After filling your GY with discarded Phoenixes and cantrips, Cruise costs {U} or {1U} by turn 3-4. Drawing three cards for one mana while already having two Phoenixes in your GY means your next turn has six cards worth of action. Picklock Prankster accelerates the GY even faster — its adventure (Pilfer) lets you loot for {U}, and the 2/2 Flash body enters after, providing a creature that also enables spell chains.

Why Phoenix At 5% — And Why You Should Still Register It

The Clock
Consistent T3 Flying Attack
Two Phoenixes attacking on T3 is 6 damage. One Cutter-equipped Otter + one Phoenix is another angle. No other deck in Pioneer consistently presents two haste fliers on T3 that come back from the GY for free. The speed and resilience combination is unique.
The Fuel
Treasure Cruise at 1–2 Mana
Drawing three cards for {U} is what the deck is actually doing. No fair card in Pioneer matches this rate. After one Phoenix recurs and two cantrips fire, your Cruise costs {U} by turn 3. You're drawing 3 cards while they're interacting with one thing.
The Resilience
Threats Return From GY Automatically
Unlike every other deck, your threats don't die permanently — they come back next turn if you can cast three spells. Single removal spells are tempo-neutral. They kill your Phoenix; you cast three cantrips; it returns. They need exile effects specifically. Most decks don't run enough.

Key Card Notes

The Win Con
Arclight Phoenix — GY Recursion Haste Flier
3/2 flying haste for {3R}. Returns from the GY to the battlefield at the beginning of combat on your turn if you've cast 3+ instant/sorcery spells this turn. You rarely hardcast it — the goal is to discard it via Artist's Talent, Lightning Axe (discard), Picklock Prankster looting, or Consider (surveil). Then cast three cantrips and it's back with haste, attacking immediately. Remove + return costs them 2 cards total; you spent zero.
The Fuel
Treasure Cruise — Draw 3 for {U}
Delve: exile cards from your GY, each exiled card reduces the cost by {1}. With 2 Phoenixes and 5 cantrips in the GY (easy by T3), Cruise costs {U}. Drawing 3 cards for 1 mana is the single most efficient draw spell in Pioneer. The tension: exiling GY cards also removes Phoenix — plan which cards to exile (cantrips yes, Phoenixes no).
The Loot Engine
Artist's Talent + Picklock Prankster
Artist's Talent: {1U} enchantment, each spell you cast while it's in play loots (draw, then discard). Every cantrip becomes double-filtering. Picklock Prankster's adventure (Pilfer) loots for {U} at instant speed, then you get a 2/2 Flash body. Both put Phoenixes in GY while drawing cards. Together with 12 cantrips, you reliably have Phoenixes in GY by T2.
The Removal
Lightning Axe + Fiery Impulse
Lightning Axe: {R}, deals 5 damage to target creature, you discard a card. The discard is a feature — pitch a Phoenix to activate it. A 5-damage instant that also loads your GY is the best tempo play in the deck. Fiery Impulse: {R}, deals 2 damage to target creature (3 with spell mastery — any two instant/sorcery in GY). Your 12 cantrips enable spell mastery by T2.
Chapter 03

GAME
FLOW

"T1: cantrip, filter for Phoenix. T2: discard Phoenix to Talent or Lightning Axe. T3: three spells, Phoenix returns, attack for 3. They're at 17."
Ideal Curve
T1–T4 Phoenix Recursion
T1: Opt or Consider (surveil/scry, put Phoenix on top or in GY). T2: Artist's Talent, then cantrip (loots — discard Phoenix). T3: Opt + Consider + Sleight of Hand (three spells, Phoenix returns from GY with haste), attack for 3. T4: Treasure Cruise for {U} (5 cards in GY), draw 3, second Phoenix returns next turn. They're at 14 after T3 attack and facing an impossible card-advantage gap.
Mulligan
Need: 2 Lands + Cantrips + GY Enabler
Keep: 2 lands, 2+ cantrips, and either Artist's Talent or a way to discard Phoenix (Lightning Axe, Picklock). Mulligan: 0 cantrips (can't enable Phoenix recursion), 4+ lands (too slow), or a hand with Treasure Cruise but no way to fill the GY. At 6: 2 lands + 2 cantrips is keepable. At 5: 2 lands + Consider + any spell is fine if you have a Phoenix in hand.
The Recursion Window
Counting to Three Spells
Phoenix returns "at the beginning of combat" if you've cast 3+ instant/sorcery spells "this turn." Every cantrip counts. Every removal spell counts. Lightning Axe + Opt + Consider = three spells, Phoenix back. Into the Flood Maw + Sleight + Opt = three spells, two Phoenixes back. Count carefully — going to combat a spell short means Phoenix stays dead that turn.
Treasure Cruise Priority
Exile Cantrips, Not Phoenixes
When casting Treasure Cruise for delve, choose which GY cards to exile carefully. Exile: spent cantrips (Opt, Consider, Sleight), spent removal (Fiery Impulse), and cards you don't need back. Never exile: Arclight Phoenixes (they need to stay in GY to recur), Lightning Axe (it might be the discard outlet you need). Wrong exile choices cost you a Phoenix activation the next turn.
Chapter 04

CORE
ENGINES

"Cantrip engine fills GY. Loot engine puts Phoenixes in GY. Recursion engine returns them. Cruise refuels everything."
Engine 1 — GY Fill
12 Cantrips + Loot Package
4 Consider, 4 Opt, 4 Sleight of Hand = 12 one-mana cantrips that draw a card and filter your hand/deck. Consider specifically surveil 1 — you can put a Phoenix directly into your GY. Opt scries 1. Sleight sees 2 cards and keeps 1. Together they fill your GY with 4-8 cards by turn 3 while digging toward Treasure Cruise. Every cantrip is also a spell that counts toward Phoenix recursion.
Engine 2 — Phoenix Recursion
3 Spells = Phoenix Returns
Arclight Phoenix returns at the start of combat if you've cast 3+ instant/sorcery spells. Your 12 cantrips + Artist's Talent loots + Picklock's adventure + removal = 25+ instant/sorceries in the deck. Turn 3 onward, three {1} spells means Phoenix attacks with haste. Two Phoenixes in GY: same cost, double the attack. The recursion is free — no mana investment beyond your normal plays.
Engine 3 — Refuel
Treasure Cruise at 1–2 Mana
After filling the GY in turns 1-3, Treasure Cruise costs {U}. Drawing three cards refills your cantrip chain, finds more Phoenixes for the GY, and maintains pressure long after the opponent has stabilized the board. The recursion loop: fill GY with cantrips → Cruise for 1 → fill GY again → Cruise again. No fair deck can match this card draw rate.
Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Discard Phoenixes early and often. Three spells is always the target. Never exile Phoenixes for Cruise."
Line 01 — Consider vs Opt — Which to Cast First
When you have both Consider and Opt in hand and want to fill your GY, cast Consider first. Consider's surveil lets you put a card directly into your GY — including a Phoenix. Opt only scries (puts back on top). Consider → Opt sequence: Consider puts Phoenix in GY (or finds the right card), Opt digs further. Opt → Consider sequence: less value from the surveil since you've already filtered. Consider first, always.
Line 02 — Lightning Axe as Both Removal and GY Enabler
Lightning Axe deals 5 damage and requires discarding a card. The discard cost is a benefit when you're holding Phoenixes. A Phoenix-in-hand + Lightning Axe on a creature = you remove their threat AND put Phoenix in your GY for free. The 5-damage threshold kills every relevant creature in Pioneer (Monastery Swiftspear, Soul-Scar Mage, Thalia, Kellan, Greasefang itself). Time it correctly: cast Axe, discard Phoenix, then cast two more spells this turn, Phoenix attacks with haste.
Line 03 — Artist's Talent Timing — Play It Before Your Cantrips
Artist's Talent loots every spell you cast while it's in play. If you have Talent + three cantrips in hand on T3, the correct sequence: cast Talent first, then cast cantrips (each triggers the loot). Three cantrips while Talent is active = three additional loots = six card interactions on one turn. If you cast cantrips before Talent, you get zero loot triggers. Always Talent before cantrips.
Line 04 — Into the Flood Maw — Bounce as Both Interaction and Tempo
Into the Flood Maw bounces a nonland permanent to its owner's hand. At instant speed for {2U}, it answers threats you can't kill with burn (Teferi, large creatures, enchantments like Rest in Peace). It also counts as a spell for Phoenix recursion — bounce their RIP at end of their turn + two cantrips on your turn = Phoenix returns and their hate piece is gone. The correct response to Rest in Peace: hold Into the Flood Maw + two cantrips, bounce at end of their upkeep, then chain three spells on your main phase.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"You're always the tempo deck. You apply threats and force them to answer. If they don't, you win. If they do, you come back."
vs Aggro
Interact and Race
Against RDW: Lightning Axe kills Eidolon (5 damage, key target), Fiery Impulse + spell mastery kills most one-drops. Your Phoenix trades unfavorably with combat and comes back; their Swiftspear doesn't. Brotherhood's End (SB) sweeps all their one-drops. You race their clock while removing their threats via your already-necessary spells.
vs Combo
Race and Bounce Key Pieces
Against Greasefang: race their T3 kill. Into the Flood Maw bounces Greasefang the turn they cast it — the ETB doesn't trigger without Greasefang surviving. Against Lotus Field: apply pressure, Into the Flood Maw bounces Lotus Field or Thespian's Stage. Spell Pierce (SB) counters their setup spells. You're typically faster than Lotus Field.
vs Control
Force Them to Exile
Against UW Control: your threats recur from the GY — every removal spell they cast is temporary unless it's exile. Force them to use exile effects (March of Otherworldly Light) early on your smaller threats, then Phoenix dodges the rest. Spell Pierce (SB) answers Temporary Lockdown. Brazen Borrower bounces Teferi at instant speed. Never overcommit creatures into a clear Supreme Verdict.

Chapter 09

HEURISTICS

"Three spells is the target every turn. Discard Phoenixes early. Never exile Phoenixes for Cruise."
  1. 01Always cast Artist's Talent before your cantrips on the same turn. Talent loots for each spell while it's in play. Cantrips before Talent get zero loot triggers. Talent first — every subsequent cantrip on that turn filters twice as efficiently.
  2. 02Consider before Opt when you want to put Phoenix in GY. Consider's surveil puts a card directly into GY. Opt scries — the card either stays on top or goes to the bottom, never into GY. For GY loading, Consider is superior. For deck-filtering when you want to draw something, Opt is fine.
  3. 03Lightning Axe + Phoenix in hand = free GY load. When you need removal AND need Phoenix in GY, Lightning Axe solves both at once. Discard Phoenix to pay the Axe's additional cost. One mana, remove their threat, Phoenix enters GY. This is the most efficient play in the deck.
  4. 04Count to three before going to combat — always. Phoenix returns "at the beginning of combat." Before declaring attack, count: have I cast 3+ instant/sorcery spells this turn? If you forgot a spell, cast it before moving to combat. If you're at 2, cast one more cantrip before attacking. Once you declare attackers, it's too late.
  5. 05Treasure Cruise: exile cantrips and removal, never Phoenixes. Each exiled card reduces Cruise's cost by {1}. Exile: spent Opts, Considers, Sleights, Fiery Impulses, Torch the Towers. Never exile: Arclight Phoenixes (they need to stay in GY to recur). Making this mistake once loses the game.
  6. 06Against Rest in Peace: hold Into the Flood Maw and bounce it before casting Cruise. RIP exiles all GYs and prevents Phoenix recursion. Before casting Treasure Cruise (which exiles cards from your GY, leaving nothing to recur even after RIP is gone), bounce RIP with Into the Flood Maw. Then cast three spells on your turn to recur Phoenixes before they can recast RIP.
  7. 07Fiery Impulse for 3 damage requires spell mastery — two instant/sorceries in GY. Fiery Impulse deals 2 damage normally, 3 with spell mastery. By T2, you typically have 2+ instant/sorceries in GY. The extra point matters for: killing Greasefang (2/2), Thalia (2/1 first strike — without the 3, she survives combat), and Monastery Swiftspear when it's pumped.
  8. 08Picklock Prankster's adventure (Pilfer) loots at instant speed — use it at end of their turn. Pilfer is a {U} instant that loots (draw a card, then discard a card). Discard a Phoenix to set up your next turn's recursion without spending your main phase. Then cast Prankster's creature side as a 2/2 Flash on their end step. Two birds, one Prankster.
  9. 09Two Phoenixes in GY is the same cost as one — keep both if possible. Phoenix's recursion doesn't care how many Phoenixes are in your GY — three spells = all of them return. Two Phoenixes in GY attacking is 6 flying damage. Always try to get both into the GY before your recursion turn. Lightning Axe can discard the second one for free removal.
  10. 10Don't keep Cutter on the board vs Supreme Verdict — bounce or protect it. Cutter is one of your best non-GY win conditions. Against UW Control, don't leave Cutter on the board when they might Verdict. Either keep mana up to bounce it with Brazen Borrower (Petty Theft) or accept that Cutter dies and win with Phoenix recursion instead. Protecting Cutter often means not committing it at all.

TUNING
NOTES

"4 Phoenix, 4 Cruise, 12 cantrips is the core. The removal suite adjusts to the meta."
Lock — Never Cut
4 Arclight Phoenix
The deck's identity card. 4 ensures maximum GY density. More copies in GY = more Phoenix attacks per recursion turn. Never cut any — you need to draw them early to discard them early.
Lock — Never Cut
4 Treasure Cruise
The most broken draw spell in Pioneer. Fills your hand every turn in the mid-game for {U}. 4 ensures you draw it consistently in the window when your GY is full. Never cut any.
Lock — Never Cut
12 One-Mana Cantrips
4 Consider + 4 Opt + 4 Sleight of Hand = the recursion enablers AND the GY fillers AND the draw engine. Below 10 cantrips the deck's consistency collapses. Never cut below 12 total.
Flex 3–4
Picklock Prankster
3 is the reference number. 4 in metas where GY filling speed matters more (GY hate heavy). 3 in metas where the 2/2 Flash body is less relevant. The adventure is always good; the creature side is context-dependent.
Flex 2–4
Into the Flood Maw
2 main is correct in most metas. In UW Control or enchantment-heavy metas go to 3. The bounce is your only main-deck answer to Rest in Peace and Teferi. At 1 you'll frequently not see it when needed.
Consider Registering
UR Cutter Instead
UR Cutter runs a similar engine (Cutter + cantrips + Cruise) without the GY dependency. At ~23% meta share vs Phoenix's 5%, the edge comes from avoiding graveyard hate. If the meta has heavy RIP/Leyline of the Void, seriously consider switching to Cutter.
Rule 01
Spell Count Discipline
You count your spells every turn before entering combat. Three spells in one turn = Phoenix returns from GY. You pre-identify which three spells you're casting before committing to the attack. You never attack and then realize you're one spell short of Phoenix recursion — you plan the turn before declaring attackers.
Rule 02

Tune 04
Fatal Push vs Fiery Impulse
You adjust your removal suite based on the meta's creature toughness. Fatal Push hits MV ≤ 2 (or ≤ 4 with revolt) — ideal vs small creatures. Fiery Impulse deals 3 damage (kills 3-toughness) — ideal vs medium creatures. You audit which threats dominate and adjust the removal split accordingly.

Your Post-Board Shell

Clock Priority
Your Fastest Win
Your fastest win is three Phoenixes in play attacking for 9 flying damage. You work toward this: discard Phoenixes to GY, cast three spells in a turn to return all three, attack. You track your Phoenix count in GY throughout every game.
Key Interaction

You always answer Rest in Peace before attempting Treasure Cruise. RIP exiles your GY — Cruise under RIP does nothing. Your priority: resolve the RIP response (Brazen Borrower or Annul), then generate advantage.

You never deploy Brazen Borrower at sorcery speed unless you need the 3/1 to attack immediately. You flash it at their EOT to bounce their key permanent, then untap and attack. The EOT bounce denies them their main phase without giving them a draw step.
