Chapter 01

DECK
IDENTITY

"Every card does damage. Every creature has haste or a relevant ability. Every turn you're threatening lethal. The question is whether they can answer fast enough."

Red Deck Wins is the purest aggro strategy in Pioneer. The plan is the same every game: play one-drop threats, cast burn spells to clear blockers or go face, and end the game before the opponent can stabilize. The deck runs zero cards that don't either deal damage or enable something that deals damage.

Monastery Swiftspear is the engine. Every spell cast after Swiftspear gives +1/+1 until end of turn. A single Swiftspear in an opening with three burn spells presents 6+ damage by turn 2. Soul-Scar Mage provides the same clock but with a different angle — burn spells put -1/-1 counters on creatures, making it excellent vs creature-heavy decks where you need to permanently shrink their threats. Eidolon of the Great Revel is the most punishing card in the list: a 2-mana 2/2 that deals 2 damage to each player whenever they cast a spell with MV ≤ 3. Since almost every spell in the format costs 3 or less, Eidolon taxes every spell they cast for 2 life. In many matchups your opponent takes 6-10 damage just from trying to interact.

Bonecrusher Giant is the cleanest 3-drop in Pioneer aggro — the Stomp adventure deals 2 damage and prevents damage prevention, clearing a blocker at instant speed, then you cast the Giant later as a 4/3 body. Light Up the Stage provides card advantage at {R} with spectacle (after dealing damage, which happens constantly), giving you two extra cards to use next turn without discarding anything.

Why RDW At 10% Pioneer

Speed
Fastest Consistent Kill in Pioneer
UR Cutter kills on T3 sometimes. RDW kills on T3 consistently. A Swiftspear + Wild Slash + Lightning Strike + Wizard's Lightning hand deals 12 damage by turn 2 with zero creatures from the opponent. The nut draw kills on T2 with any two prowess triggers.
Resilience
No Moving Parts
Nothing combos. Nothing needs setup. Every card in the deck works independently. They can take your Swiftspear, your Lightning Strike still deals 3. They can counter your Giant, Stomp already resolved. Every card in your hand is a threat or removal on its own.
Pressure
Eidolon Wins Games by Existing
Many opponents can't kill Eidolon without losing 2 more life to it. Against Greasefang: every discard outlet costs 2. Against control: every interaction costs 2. Against UR Cutter: every cantrip costs 2. Some games you just play Eidolon on T2 and watch them die to their own answers.

Key Card Notes

The Clock
Monastery Swiftspear — Prowess Haste
Haste means it attacks the turn it enters. Prowess gives +1/+1 until end of turn for each noncreature spell — in a deck with 22 spells this means every attack turn is a +2/+2 to +5/+5 swing. A T1 Swiftspear + T2 three spells (Lightning Strike + Wild Slash + Play with Fire) attacks for 4 and deals 6 from spells = 10 damage by the end of turn 2.
The Tax
Eidolon of the Great Revel — 2 Per Spell
Deals 2 damage to each player whenever either player casts a spell with MV 3 or less. You accept losing 2 per spell yourself; your opponent loses 2 per every answer, cantrip, or interaction they cast. In matchups where they need 4-6 spells to stabilize, that's 8-12 free damage from Eidolon alone. The opponent usually dies trying to remove it.
Removal + Creature
Bonecrusher Giant // Stomp
Stomp (the Adventure) deals 2 damage and prevents damage prevention for the turn — critical vs Selesnya Company's protection spells. After casting Stomp for {1R}, cast Bonecrusher Giant later as a 4/3 body. It also has "whenever a spell or ability deals damage to Bonecrusher Giant, counter that spell or ability" — makes it hard to kill with small burn.
The Enabler
Wizard's Lightning — {R} with Wizard
Normally costs {2R}, but costs just {R} if you control a Wizard. Soul-Scar Mage, Ghitu Lavarunner (when you've cast a spell this turn), and Bonecrusher Giant are all Wizards. In virtually every game, Wizard's Lightning costs {R} by turn 2. A {R} Lightning Bolt effect is the best rate on a burn spell in Pioneer.

GAME
FLOW

"T1 Swiftspear. T2 Eidolon + Wizard's Lightning for R. They're at 11. T3 three spells. They're dead."
Ideal Curve
T1–T4 Kill
T1: Monastery Swiftspear (attacks for 1). T2: Eidolon of the Great Revel + one spell — opponent takes 2 from Eidolon, Swiftspear attacks for 3 (prowess). They're at 14. T3: Wizard's Lightning for {R} + two more spells — 3+2+2 = 7 damage. They're at 7. T4: any two spells = lethal. This is a clean T4 kill doing nothing unusual.
Mulligan
Need a T1 Threat + 3 Spells
Keep: any hand with a one-drop (Swiftspear/Soul-Scar/Ghitu) and 3+ burn spells on 2 lands. Mulligan: all Mountains and no action, hands with 4+ lands, hands with only creatures and no burn. At 6: keep any 1-drop + 2 spells on 2 lands. At 5: keep 1-drop + 2 burn + 2 lands — anything less is too slow.
The Spectacle Mode
Light Up the Stage for {R}
Light Up the Stage costs {2R} normally but {R} with Spectacle — triggered when you deal damage to an opponent. Since Swiftspear attacks T1, Light Up the Stage costs {R} on T2 every game. It exiles the top two cards of your library and you may play them until next turn. This is {R} for two cards — extraordinary value that refuels after the initial burst.
Ramunap Ruins
The 21st-22nd Point of Damage
Ramunap Ruins is a land that can deal 1 damage by tapping and paying 1 life. In many games you win at 1 life by activating it for the last point. More importantly: it makes your "land" draws functional damage sources in topdeck mode. At 4-5 mana with a creature on board and no cards in hand, Ramunap Ruins often represents the final 2 damage they can't see coming.
Burn Face Rule
When to Redirect Burn
Your default is always: burn the player, not the creature. Redirect burn to their creature ONLY when: (1) their creature is about to block your best attacker and the blocked damage exceeds the burn damage, OR (2) their creature will win the game if it stays in play (e.g., Sheoldred). In all other cases, their life total is your target. Calculate the math before redirecting.
Eidolon Value
Your Best Card in Mirrors
Eidolon of the Great Revel deals 2 damage whenever they cast a spell with MV ≤ 3. You deploy it on T2 before they can answer it. Against interactive decks: every cantrip (Opt, Consider) costs them 2 life. Every removal spell costs them 2 life. Against creature decks: every one-drop costs them 2 life. Your Eidolon often deals 8-10 damage before they remove it.
Ferocidon Mode
Anti-Lifegain Package
Your Rampaging Ferocidon (SB) stops ALL life gain by all players. Against Greasefang: Parhelion II's lifelink triggers do nothing. Against Selesnya Company: their Voice of Resurgence lifelink, their Knight of Autumn life gain — all negated. You deploy Ferocidon on T3 after their key life gain source is in play. Menace means they need two blockers for it every combat.
Kill Math
Mapping Lethal
Before your attack each turn, map your kill. Current damage: Swiftspear (3/4 after pumps) + Goblin Guide (2/2) = 5 attacking. Burn in hand: Wizard's Lightning (3) + Burst Lightning (2) = 5. At 8 or less life: this is lethal. You always know the exact life total where your hand is lethal before you commit to attacking. Never attack without knowing the number.
Chapter 04

CORE
ENGINES

"Prowess creatures + cheap spells + Eidolon tax. Three engines running simultaneously every game."
Engine 1 — Primary
Prowess Burst
Monastery Swiftspear and Soul-Scar Mage both have prowess (+1/+1 per noncreature spell). Chain three spells on your attack turn and each creature swings for +3/+3. The burn spells that deal direct damage ALSO trigger prowess — they serve dual purpose as removal/reach AND pump. A hand with Swiftspear + three burn spells presents 15+ damage by turn 3.
Engine 2 — Passive Tax
Eidolon Damage Clock
Eidolon of the Great Revel is a passive damage source — every spell your opponent casts costs them 2 life. In most matchups this is 8-16 free damage over the course of a game. The opponent must choose: ignore Eidolon and die to it, or use resources to kill it and die to your burn. Either path accelerates their death. You accept the same tax on yourself, but you deal damage faster.
Engine 3 — Reach
Burn to the Face
22 spells in the main deck, 18 of which deal damage directly. When opponents stabilize the board, you still have direct damage to close. Wizard's Lightning for {R}, Wild Slash, Lightning Strike, Play with Fire — any two of these from topdeck mode close from 6. Ramunap Ruins can contribute the last 1-2 damage without drawing a card. You never truly flood out.
Engine: Eidolon Tax
Your Free Damage Multiplier
Eidolon of the Great Revel makes them pay 2 life per spell they cast with MV ≤ 3. You deploy Eidolon on T2 before they have removal mana available. Your payoff: their Opt costs them 2. Their Fatal Push costs them 2. Their any one-drop costs them 2. Against interactive decks: you deal 10+ damage from Eidolon alone before they finally remove it. You attack aggressively while Eidolon is live — your damage output doubles.
Engine: Monastery Swiftspear
Your Primary Clock
Monastery Swiftspear (1/2 haste prowess) attacks the turn it enters and pumps +1/+1 for each instant/sorcery you cast. Your pump sequencing: cast burn before attacking → Swiftspear pumps from the spell → attack with buffed Swiftspear. You never attack first and pump second — always cast spells first. At 3 spells in one turn, Swiftspear attacks as a 4/5.
Engine: Rampaging Ferocidon
Your Anti-Lifegain Package
Rampaging Ferocidon (3/3 menace SB): opponents can't gain life AND they take 1 damage whenever a creature enters under their control. You deploy it against Greasefang (no Parhelion lifelink), Selesnya Company (each Company creature entering deals 1 to them), UW Control (no Lyra lifelink). Menace means they need two blockers — your Ferocidon attacks through most defenses.
Engine: Goblin Guide
Your Hasty Scout
Goblin Guide (2/2 haste) attacks the turn it enters AND reveals their top card (they get a land if it's a land). You attack immediately on T1. Your payoff: 2 damage at haste before they can deploy blockers. Their free land is the downside — you accept it. You never wait to deploy Goblin Guide hoping for a better board state. T1 Guide attacks for 2, and 2 early damage is worth more than any land they receive.
Chapter 05

LINES &
TACTICS

"Never remove a blocker you can go over. Burn is always better spent on their face."
Line 01 — Only Use Burn on Creatures When Necessary
The primary mistake RDW pilots make is using burn on creatures they didn't need to. Before pointing burn at a creature, ask: can I attack profitably anyway? If yes, attack — save the burn for their face or a creature that actually blocks lethally. Wild Slash, Play with Fire, and Lightning Strike all kill most creatures in Pioneer. Use them on blockers only when you need to go through, or when the creature blocks enough damage to matter.
Line 02 — Kumano Faces Kakkazan Timing: Cast on T1 for Maximum Value
Kumano Faces Kakkazan is a saga: Chapter 1 (enters) deals 1 damage to each opponent and each creature they control. Chapter 2 (next upkeep) gives your next creature spell +1/+1. Chapter 3 (next upkeep) transforms into Etching of Kumano, a legendary creature 2/2 with "whenever a creature dealt damage this turn by a source you control dies, exile it." Always cast T1 — the 1 damage triggers spectacle for Light Up the Stage, and the exiling from Etching of Kumano prevents graveyard recursion (critical vs Phoenix/Greasefang).
Line 03 — Ghitu Lavarunner is a 2/2 Haste in Your Main Phase After One Spell
Ghitu Lavarunner has haste only if there are two or more instant/sorcery cards in your graveyard. But it also gets +1/+0 as long as there's at least one. In practice: if you cast any spell before attacking, Ghitu Lavarunner attacks as a 2/2 haste. This is the baseline assumption — always sequence Ghitu attacks AFTER casting a spell, even if it's just Wild Slash at a 1-toughness creature.
Line 04 — Wild Slash is Ferocious — Deal 3, Not 2
Wild Slash deals 2 damage and has Ferocious: if you control a creature with power 4 or greater, it deals 3 instead and can't be prevented. Bonecrusher Giant (4/3), Soul-Scar Mage after two prowess triggers (3/2+), and a Swiftspear after two spells (3/3+) all enable Ferocious. Check power before casting Wild Slash — an extra point of damage is often the difference between killing a creature or not. Also: the damage prevention clause matters vs Selesnya Company's protection spells.
Line 01
Your T1-T4 Sequence
You execute your curve on autopilot: T1 Goblin Guide or Monastery Swiftspear (attack immediately). T2 Eidolon of the Great Revel + one burn spell (attack with Swiftspear as 3/3 from prowess). T3 two burn spells + attack (Swiftspear at 4/4). T4 burn any remaining life total. You commit fully to your curve — you never hold spells hoping for a better line.
Line 02
Your Eidolon Priority
You deploy Eidolon on T2 before they have removal mana. You never wait for a "better" time — every turn Eidolon lives generates 2 damage per spell they cast. Your primary goal after deploying Eidolon: protect it by pointing their removal at your other threats. You never cast two spells in one turn when you can cast one and hold the other for T3 Eidolon backup.
Line 03
Your Burn-Face Protocol
You burn their life total, not their creatures, unless their creature blocks your best attacker AND blocking costs you more damage than the burn would deal. You calculate: if their 3/3 blocks your 4/4 Swiftspear, you're losing 3 damage next turn via their attack. Bolt their blocker instead. In every other case: bolt face.
Line 04
Your Kill Window Recognition
You identify your kill window before your attack step every turn. At 8 life: Swiftspear (3) + Goblin Guide (2) + Wizard's Lightning (3) + Burst Lightning (2) = 10, lethal. You count your burn in hand plus your attack damage before swinging. Never attack when counting would reveal you're one spell short — save the attack step for the turn you can definitively close.
Chapter 06

ROLE
ASSIGNMENT

"You are always the aggressor. Tempo, life total, and clock math — always your decision."
vs Combo
Race and Apply Eidolon
Against Greasefang, Lotus Field: race but add Eidolon. Every discard outlet, every setup spell they cast costs them 2 more life. Rampaging Ferocidon (SB) stops Lotus Field from gaining life. You often win game 1 before they can execute; game 2 Eidolon + Ferocidon is a hard lock on many combo draws.
vs Aggro
Kill Their Threats, Protect Clock
Against UR Cutter: kill their Stormchaser's Talent early, prevent the Monk token engine from firing. Searing Blood (SB) kills a 1-toughness creature AND burns them for 2 more if it dies. Soul-Scar Mage's -1/-1 counters permanently shrink their prowess creatures. Your clock is faster than theirs if you remove their Cutter.
vs Control
Eidolon Wins the Game
Against UW Control: play Eidolon on T2 and let them take 4-8 damage trying to answer it. Board in Chandra, Torch of Defiance — a threat they must answer but can't via counterspell without losing more life to Eidolon. Roiling Vortex (SB) prevents lifegain from Teferi, stops White Plains cycles. Every spell they cast is damage.
vs Control
Your Race and Burn Game Plan
Against UW Control: you ignore their creatures entirely and burn their face. You deploy Eidolon of the Great Revel on T2 — every Absorb they cast costs them 2 more life from Eidolon. You attack every turn. You never hold burn for their creatures when their life total is 8 or below. Roiling Vortex (SB) negates their Lyra Dawnbringer lifelink — deploy it before they cast their threat.
vs Greasefang
Your Race Line
Against Greasefang: you kill them before T3. Your T1-T3 goldfish must put them at 6 or below before their T3 Greasefang + Parhelion combo. Rampaging Ferocidon (SB) prevents all life gain — they can't gain life from Parhelion's lifelink. You deploy Ferocidon on your T3 to stop them from stabilizing if they do survive. You never slow down to interact — you race.
vs Aggro Mirrors
Your Eidolon Asymmetry
Against other aggressive decks: you deploy Eidolon of the Great Revel and gain the asymmetric advantage. Eidolon hits your own spells too — but your spells cost less on average than theirs. You calculate: if Eidolon costs you 2 damage per spell but costs them 2 damage per spell AND you're applying more spells per turn, you're ahead. You deploy Eidolon in aggro mirrors and win through the life damage race.
vs Selesnya
Your Ferocidon Line
Against Selesnya Company: Rampaging Ferocidon on T3 deals 1 damage for each creature entering (Collected Company puts 2 in — 2 immediate damage from Ferocidon). Your attack plus Ferocidon's combat damage plus each creature ETB damage means you win faster than their wide strategy closes. You never slow down to block their small creatures — you attack face and let Ferocidon damage stack.

P1 — vs Lifegain Strategies
Rampaging Ferocidon ×4
A 3/3 menace for {2R} that prevents ALL lifegain while it's on the battlefield. Every source of lifegain — Scavenging Ooze, Collective Brutality, Sheltered by Ghosts, lifelink creatures — is completely shut off. Additionally, whenever a creature enters the battlefield, Ferocidon can be used to deal 1 damage to that player. It's your best card vs Selesnya Company, Greasefang (Parhelion angels), and any deck that stabilizes via life recovery.

Roiling Vortex ×2
Enchantment that deals 1 damage to each player during their upkeep, AND prevents lifegain if you control Roiling Vortex and cast a red spell this turn. Shuts off Lotus Field's Abandon Hope lifegain, Teferi's life padding in control, and any other lifegain strategy. The passive upkeep damage is also relevant — 1-2 damage per turn adds up against opponents who stabilize at low life.

HEURISTICS

"Burn is always better pointed at their face unless the creature blocks lethally."
  1. 01Only use burn on creatures when they block lethally. Before burning a creature, ask: if I attack without removing it, do they block and prevent lethal? If yes, burn it. If no, burn them. Too many RDW games are lost from routing burn at irrelevant blockers rather than closing from 4 life.
  2. 02Ghitu Lavarunner attacks as a 2/2 after any spell. Cast at least one spell before attacking with Lavarunner each turn. One instant/sorcery in your GY gives it +1/+0; two gives it haste. The sequencing is: land, spell, Lavarunner attacks.
  3. 03Check power before casting Wild Slash — Ferocious gives 3 damage. Any creature with power 4+ enables Wild Slash to deal 3 instead of 2 and removes damage prevention. Bonecrusher Giant, a Swiftspear after two spells, or a Mage after three prowess triggers all enable this.
  4. 04Eidolon taxes you too — don't play it and immediately chain spells. Eidolon deals 2 to each player per spell. If you drop Eidolon and then cast Wild Slash + Lightning Strike, you take 4 damage. Plan your Eidolon turn carefully. Often: deploy Eidolon, attack, pass. Let them take damage trying to remove it.
  5. 05Wizard's Lightning costs {R} when you control a Wizard — always check. Soul-Scar Mage, Ghitu Lavarunner, and Bonecrusher Giant are all Wizards. If any are on the battlefield, Wizard's Lightning costs {R}. A {R} Lightning Strike is the most efficient spell in Pioneer.
  6. 06Kumano Faces Kakkazan on T1 triggers spectacle for Light Up the Stage on T2. The Chapter 1 of Kumano deals 1 damage to each opponent. This is enough for spectacle. Play Kumano T1, play Light Up the Stage for {R} T2. You'll be drawing 2 extra cards on T2 every time this sequence happens.
  7. 07Castle Embereth and Ramunap Ruins are always last resort, not primary damage. Don't tap them for mana early if unnecessary. In topdeck mode vs a stabilized opponent at 4-6 life: Castle Embereth gives all your creatures +1/+0 until EOT for {W}{R} (yes, it's on a Mountain but has the Plains requirement — note: actually Castle Embereth only requires mountains). Use Ramunap Ruins as your 22nd damage source when your hand is empty.
  8. 08Stomp's "prevent damage prevention" is relevant vs protection and lifegain. Bonecrusher Giant's Stomp prevents damage prevention. If they cast Sheltered by Ghosts or a protection spell, Stomp makes your burn deal full damage through protection for the turn. Time it correctly: cast Stomp, then burn through the protection.
  9. 09Rampaging Ferocidon shuts lifegain on the stack — it can't be responded to. Ferocidon says "players can't gain life." It's a static ability. Lifegain spells resolve but the life never changes. Opponents who activate a lifegain ability AFTER Ferocidon enters waste their resources — the life gain simply doesn't happen.
  10. 10Count to lethal before each combat. Your attack step is your turn's most important decision. Before attacking: count all your attackers' damage with realistic pump, check what burn in hand deals, factor in Ramunap Ruins activations. If you can't kill them this turn, determine exactly what cards you need to draw to kill them next turn. This math should take 10 seconds every combat.
  11. Your burn commitment threshold: The moment they drop below 10 life, you commit all burn to their face. At 9 life: Lightning Bolt (3) + any two attacks = lethal by T4. You never save burn for their creatures once you reach lethal range — identify the exact life total where your hand closes the game without another attack step, then dump your hand.
  12. Your threat density rule: You run 12+ creatures so you always have an attacker even after their removal. After they kill two of your threats: deploy the next one immediately. Every turn you spend not attacking is a turn they stabilize. You keep applying pressure until the game ends — there is no midgame pivot for this deck.

"4 Swiftspear, 4 Eidolon, and 4 of each burn spell is the core. Everything else is meta-adjustable."
Lock — Never Cut
4 Monastery Swiftspear
The single best threat in the deck. Haste + prowess makes it impossible to play around. Cutting even one reduces your T1 threat density enough to meaningfully slow the deck's kill clock.
Lock — Never Cut
4 Eidolon of the Great Revel
Too many matchups are decided by Eidolon alone. 4 copies ensures you draw it early and often. Some pilots cut to 3 to avoid the self-damage, but the win rate data supports 4 copies strongly.
Lock — Never Cut
4 Wild Slash + 4 Wizard's Lightning
Wild Slash's ferocious mode and Wizard's Lightning's {R} cost are unique to this deck. Both give you burn effects at an efficiency no other deck can replicate. Never cut these.
Flex 2–4
Bonecrusher Giant
2 is the reference number — a strong topdeck and answer to blockers, but the 3-mana cost makes it awkward in some draws. In creature-heavy metas (Selesnya) go to 3-4. In spell-heavy metas (control) trim to 1-2.
Flex 2–4
Light Up the Stage
2 main is a meta call. In open metas where games go to T4+, run 4 for more gas. In fast aggro metas where T3 kills are normal, 2 is correct — you rarely need the extra cards if you're winning that quickly.
Register When
Lifegain Is Minimal
RDW is at its best when opponent lifegain is minimal. If the meta is full of Selesnya Company, go up on Ferocidon (main?). If it's full of control with Teferi, bring Chandra. If it's Cutter and Phoenix, you're favored — just register the reference list.
Rule 01
Eidolon Commitment
You deploy Eidolon on T2 and never look back. Even if Eidolon damages you (from your own spells), the symmetry favors you because your spells are cheaper on average. A 3-spell turn under Eidolon costs you 6 life but deals you 6 life worth of damage too — on net, you're even on the Eidolon symmetry while your opponent takes the same.
Rule 02
Goblin Guide Blocking
You never block with Goblin Guide unless you're in lethal danger. Goblin Guide is an attacker — it applies clock. Using it as a blocker delays your damage output by one turn. You accept trading 2 or 3 life to keep Guide attacking. The only exception: blocking prevents lethal damage this turn.
Rule 03
Searing Blood Maximization
Searing Blood deals 2 damage to target creature — if that creature dies this turn, deals 3 to its controller. You cast Searing Blood on their creature when it's already damaged (from combat) to ensure the death trigger fires. Cast it during combat (in response to blocks) so the creature dies in combat and you get the 3 damage to them.
Rule 04
Ferocidon Deployment Timing
You deploy Rampaging Ferocidon on T3 before they can gain life from their key source. Against Greasefang: deploy Ferocidon before their Parhelion II enters play. Against Selesnya: deploy before their Company resolves. Ferocidon must be in play BEFORE their life gain source — deploying after misses the trigger.
Tune 01
Twinmaw Stormbrood Count
Twinmaw Stormbrood (2/3 haste Dragon: when it attacks, deals 1 damage to each creature opponent controls) is your anti-token piece. You run 2-3 copies in token-heavy metas (Selesnya Company). You cut Stormbrood in non-token metas where its combat ping doesn't kill anything. The ping is relevant only when they have multiple 1/1 tokens.
Tune 02
Searing Blood Meta Slot
Searing Blood ({R}{R}: 2 damage to creature; if it dies this turn, 3 damage to controller) converts your creature removal into additional face damage. You run 2-3 copies in metas with many blockers that must die before you can attack through. You cut Searing Blood in metas where their creatures rarely survive to trigger the 3-damage follow-up.
Tune 03
Roiling Vortex Count
You maximize Roiling Vortex (3-4 copies) when your meta has multiple lifegain decks. Against a meta with 30%+ lifegain decks: 4 Vortex. Against a meta with 10%: 2 Vortex. You never run zero when the meta has any significant lifegain presence. Lifegain makes your burn math impossible — Vortex is mandatory in those matchups.
Searing Blood Setup
Deal 2 Plus Get 3 More
Searing Blood deals 2 damage to their creature. If that creature dies this turn: deals 3 damage to its controller. You set up the 3-damage trigger by damaging their creature in combat first, then casting Searing Blood — their 2-toughness creature is already damaged, so 2 more kills it and triggers the 3 damage.
Ferocidon Menace Value
Two Blockers Required
Rampaging Ferocidon has menace — needs two blockers every combat. Against wide boards: they might lack two blockers for Ferocidon while also blocking your other threats. You attack with Ferocidon into boards where they can't profitably double-block it while also blocking your other threats.
T1 Creature Non-Negotiable
Mulligan for T1 Play
Your only T1 play is a creature. You mulligan any hand without one. The clock starts on T1 — a missed T1 means one fewer attack step and approximately 2 fewer damage over the course of the game. Two fewer damage often equals one fewer kill turn.
Swiftspear Pump Discipline
Burn Before Attacking Always
You cast burn spells BEFORE attacking with Monastery Swiftspear. Each spell gives Swiftspear +1/+1 (prowess) until EOT. You cast Lightning Bolt → Swiftspear is 2/3 → declare attack. You never attack first and then pump — the pumped stats apply to the current combat.
Eidolon Deployment Window
T2 Before Their Removal Mana
You deploy Eidolon of the Great Revel on T2 before they have interaction mana. Against UW Control: they have {W}{U} on T3 for Absorb. Against Lotus Field: they have mana online T3. Your window: T2 Eidolon before they can answer it profitably.
Ferocidon Placement
Before Their Lifegain Source
Rampaging Ferocidon must be in play BEFORE their lifegain source triggers. T3 Ferocidon before T4 Greasefang crews Parhelion: Parhelion's lifelink generates 0 life. If Parhelion attacks first and you deploy Ferocidon: you missed the Parhelion lifelink trigger.

Know Cold
Lethal Math by Turn
T3 lethal requires ~15-18 damage. Your standard sequence: T1 Swiftspear (attacks 1), T2 spell + Eidolon + Swiftspear attacks for 3 = 4, T3 three spells = 9+ damage. Total: 13-15. Wizard's Lightning for {R} is the card that tips you over lethal. Run these scenarios before each round starts.

Eidolon Tax Per Matchup
UR Cutter casts ~6-8 spells per game (cantrips + Cruise) = 12-16 from Eidolon. Greasefang runs 8-12 spells including discards = 16-24. Control runs 10+ spells = 20+. Your Eidolon is worth more damage than many of your burn spells. Protect it if they try to remove it without taking meaningful damage.

Testing Framework

Block 01
T4 Goldfish Kill
Practice your T4 goldfish. T1: Goblin Guide (attack for 2). T2: Monastery Swiftspear + burn spell (attack for 6+2=8). T3: burn + attack (16). T4: burn for 4 = lethal. Know the sequence cold.
Block 02
Swiftspear Pump Sequencing
Practice casting burn BEFORE attacking with Swiftspear. Cast Lightning Bolt (Swiftspear 2/3), cast Burst Lightning (Swiftspear 3/4), then attack. Never attack first then cast burn.
Block 03
Eidolon Damage Tracking
Practice tracking Eidolon damage. Every cantrip, every removal spell they cast deals 2 damage. Count: their hand has 3 spells of MV â‰¤ 3 = 6 damage from Eidolon triggers before their next draw step.
Block 04
Burn Math at Low Life
Practice the burn math at low life totals. At 4 life: Lightning Bolt (3) + Burst Lightning (2) = 5, lethal. Know the exact burn hands that close from low life without needing another attack.
